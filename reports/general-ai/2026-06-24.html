◆ Frontier models — all three in a holding pattern

Imminent Window open since Mon, June 22

GPT-5.6: window's open, card's still missing, June 25 now floated

Three days into its predicted launch window and GPT-5.6 still isn't out. Leaks now point at a June 25 ship for a Pro variant, with the same rumored spec sheet as last week: a context window pushed toward 1.5M tokens, a raised reasoning-effort budget, and built-in Playwright web automation. Prediction markets sit around 90% on a ship by June 28.

The caveat hasn't changed: no official announcement, no system card, no API model string as of today. Everything is leaks and prediction-market money.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on until a real model string and card exist. Keep your concierge and receptionist stacks model-pluggable so you can A/B GPT-5.6 the day it's callable — and don't quote a client a capability you've only read about in a leak.

Preview GA window June 23–30

Gemini 3.5 Pro is inside its GA window — and still isn't public

Google said Gemini 3.5 Pro would land "next month" at I/O on May 19; trackers now put the general-availability window at June 23–30. As of today it remains in limited Vertex AI enterprise preview, not in the Gemini app, AI Studio, or the general API. The headline specs are a 2M-token context window — the largest in any production frontier model — and a Deep Think reasoning mode gated to Google's $250/month Ultra tier. Prediction markets give it roughly 50–55% odds of shipping before June 30.

So what for TSD: A 2M-context Gemini matters for long-document RAG — handbooks, catalogs, the kind of corpus your Concierge demos chew through. Watch for the public API drop before month-end; until it's generally available it's not something you can build a client deliverable on.
Swisher Post — still missing its window · Tech Times — specs
Still dark Day 12 of suspension

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are still offline — the paywall date came and went

June 23 was supposed to be the day Fable 5 free access converted to paid usage credits ($10/$50 per million in/out). It's a non-event: both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 have been offline for every user since June 12, when a US export-control directive ordered them suspended over a narrow potential jailbreak. Anthropic has said it disagrees with the standard and is working to restore access; as of today there's no return date and no new movement.

So what for TSD: Same lesson, second straight week — never hard-pin a client deliverable to the newest release. Your production stacks belong on stable, available models (the Opus/Sonnet line), behind an abstraction layer that lets the model float underneath. The "best" model is the one your client can actually call.

◆ Tooling & the money behind it

Dev tools Usage billing since June 1

The AI coding market is reshuffling — usage billing in, Claude Code up

The coding-tool landscape keeps consolidating around three names — Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, and Cursor. As of June 1 every Copilot plan moved to usage-based billing with monthly AI-credit allowances ($15 on Pro, $70 on Pro+, $200 on Max). Trackers cite Copilot's share sliding from ~67% to ~51% while Cursor crossed $2B ARR and Claude Code grew roughly 6x. In a JetBrains poll of devs with 10+ years' experience, 46% picked Claude Code for daily work versus 9% for Copilot.

So what for TSD: You already live in Claude Code, so the share shift is validation, not a to-do. The actionable bit is the billing model: everyone's metering tokens now. Build the same discipline into your own client work — track which AI calls cost what, so a Managed-AI retainer is priced on real usage instead of a guess.

Funding signal As of June 2026

The agent-startup funding squeeze is getting talked about out loud

Even as the frontier labs raise on trillion-dollar valuations, analysts are flagging the layer below: a meaningful share of early-stage AI-agent startups are projected to run out of capital by late 2026, squeezed between high model-token costs and slow enterprise deployment cycles. Money in 2026 has shifted away from thin "application wrappers" toward orchestration engines, security layers, and interoperability — the unglamorous plumbing.

So what for TSD: This is the case for being a profitable services shop, not a venture-funded wrapper. You sell working outcomes to SMBs who pay you, with near-zero burn. When wrapper startups fold, the businesses that bought from them still need someone local who can build and maintain — that's the lane you already own.
