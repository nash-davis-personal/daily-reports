◆ Models and pricing

Release Oct. 7

Claude Haiku 5.5: $0.10/$0.50, adjustable effort, fastest Claude at standard speed

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on Oct. 7. Prompts under 100,000 tokens cost $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output; above that threshold the whole request bills at $0.50 and $2.50. Cache reads are $0.01 per million in the lower tier. Anthropic says about 90% of Haiku 4.5 requests fall in the lower bracket and estimates workloads cost roughly 75% less than on Haiku 4.5 once the new tokenizer, which uses somewhat more tokens for the same work, is counted. Standard rates match OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna, whose surcharge starts later at 272,000 tokens.

It is the first Haiku with adaptive thinking and effort levels from low through max, with medium as the default. Vercel's listing describes it as built for summaries, context compaction, database queries and classification, as a subagent for larger Claude models, and as the fastest Claude at standard speed for live customer support and browser use. VentureBeat reports it beats Haiku 4.5 on knowledge-work and coding tests, trails Sonnet 5.5, and leads GPT-6 Luna on selected evaluations. Benchmarks here are vendor-reported.

So what for TSD: Test Haiku 5.5 on TSD Concierge and the text side of TSD Front Desk this week using real transcripts. Keep RAG prompts under 100K tokens or the price jumps fivefold. Measure tokens per conversation before and after, since the tokenizer change means the rate card overstates the saving.

Preview Oct. 6

Mistral Large 4: a trillion parameters, open weights due Oct. 31

Mistral put Large 4 into public preview on Oct. 6. It is a natively multimodal model with one trillion total parameters and 49 billion active per token, and a 1M-token context window. Mistral says the open weights follow on Oct. 31. One roundup reports it was trained on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in European data centers and is being red-teamed with security firms and state authorities before the weights ship. It is already callable through Vercel AI Gateway.

So what for TSD: Nothing to change today. If a client ever needs EU data handling or a self-hosted option, an open-weight frontier-class model is now a real answer to that question. Revisit after Oct. 31 when the license terms are public.

◆ Agents and builder tooling

Standard Oct. 6

Meta, Walmart and Stripe back an open standard for letting personal agents into websites

TechCrunch reports that Meta, Walmart, Stripe, Sierra, Genesys, Rocket, NiCE and Decagon are backing an open standard for how personal AI agents identify themselves and transact with businesses. The aim is to tell agents acting for a real customer apart from abusive bots. The backdrop is that many large sites turn agents away today, some on purpose and some by accident through CAPTCHAs: the article lists Amazon, Delta, United, Yelp, eBay, Zillow, Pizza Hut and Adidas among them. A separate roundup says OpenAI and Anthropic are not among the participants.

So what for TSD: Client sites should be easy for an agent to read and act on: clean booking links, prices and hours in structured data, no CAPTCHA on the path to an appointment. That is a checklist you can run on every TSD site and sell to prospects. Booking Bridge is the natural place to add agent-friendly endpoints once the spec is public.

API Oct. 7

OpenAI Decisions API: typed answers with probabilities

OpenAI's Decisions API is in public beta and reachable through Vercel AI Gateway as of Oct. 7. You send one shared input plus typed questions, and the model (GPT-6 Luna Decisions) returns yes/no predicates, a choice from a list, or a score, each with a probability. It is aimed at routing, triage, guardrails and rubric scoring. Vercel also added confidence-based fallbacks, which escalate a decision to a stronger model when the first one is unsure. Pricing is unsettled: one roundup cites $0.10 per million input tokens, while another analysis says OpenAI has not published a rate for this endpoint.

So what for TSD: This is the shape of every "is this lead urgent, and who gets it" step in Front Desk and Booking Bridge. You can get the same result with Haiku 5.5 and structured output, so pick on measured cost and accuracy. Hold off on building against the Decisions endpoint until OpenAI posts a price.

◆ Money and business

Funding Oct. 6

Lambda raising up to $4B; most of its backlog is one customer

AI cloud provider Lambda is raising up to $4 billion at a $14.5 billion pre-money valuation, co-led by Coatue and Blackstone, ahead of an IPO now planned for 2027. Its contracted backlog grew from $15 billion in June to $50 billion in September, and $35 billion of that is a single Anthropic commitment signed in late August.

So what for TSD: More Anthropic compute is the supply side of the Haiku price cut. Expect small-model prices to keep falling, so write client contracts around outcomes and a flat monthly fee, and keep the model line item out of the quote.
TechCrunch
Business Oct. 6

HubSpot cuts about 660 jobs and reorganizes around "delivering outcomes" with AI

HubSpot said on Oct. 6 it is laying off nearly 660 employees, about 7% of its workforce. CEO Yamini Rangan told staff the company has shifted from building software that helps customers grow to delivering outcomes for them with AI. She said the cuts are about a flatter organization and are not driven by AI efficiencies. HubSpot expects $65 million to $75 million in restructuring costs.

So what for TSD: The largest SMB CRM vendor is now selling the same promise TSD sells: results, done for the customer. That validates the Managed AI retainer pitch and raises the bar on it. Lead with what a HubSpot seat cannot do: custom work on the client's own site, with source code they own.
