◆ The frontier moved on price

New Sep 2, 2026

Gemini 3.8 Flash ships at 3.7 Flash prices and beats Opus 5 on part of the board

Google's third Flash-tier release in six weeks costs $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens, introductory through December 31, 2026, with standard pricing of $1.50 and $7.50 starting January 1, 2027. Cached context reads are $0.075 per million. Context window is 1M tokens, and independent measurement puts throughput at 306–313 tokens per second depending on the thinking tier, the fastest anyone has clocked.

The launch table has it ahead of Claude Opus 5 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89.4% vs 89.1%), Vals Finance Agent v2 (61.4% vs 58.6%), LVBench (87.8% vs 75.4%), CharXiv Reasoning (86.2% vs 83.7%) and HLE-Verified (54.9% vs 54.4%). It loses on Terminal-Bench 4.0 (19.1% vs 51.8%), OSWorld-2.0 (59.0% vs 75.4%), DeepSWE v1.1 (71.0% vs 74.0%) and GDPVal-AA v2 (1545 vs 1824). Opus 5 lists at $5 input and $25 output. The pattern in that split is length: the longer and more open-ended the task, the better Opus still looks.

So what for TSD: Route the volume work in Concierge and Front Desk to 3.8 Flash and keep Claude on the long agent loops. The RAG lookup, the classification step, the transcript summary, the "is this caller asking about hours" branch — those are one-shot calls where 3.8 Flash costs a seventh of Opus and answers faster. Model the cost per task rather than per token before you switch anything, and note the January 1 doubling now so it does not surprise your margins mid-retainer. It is already live on Vercel AI Gateway, so the swap is a model string.

Capability Sep 2, 2026

Google, Anthropic and OpenAI all staked out cyber positions in one day

Google announced Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber, which it calls its most capable cybersecurity model, distributed through the Fairwind program to trusted defenders in government, healthcare and telecom, with more than 650 partners including CrowdStrike, Datadog and Palo Alto Networks. Google's framing leans on vulnerability fixing over discovery.

Anthropic now permits Fable 5.1 to identify software vulnerabilities and keeps Mythos 5.1 behind trusted-access programs for cybersecurity and life-science work, with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards pairing zero data retention against misuse detection. OpenAI says Astra meets the Critical cybersecurity threshold in its Preparedness Framework: 100% on ExploitBench, two zero-days discovered and weaponized during evaluation, a complete browser-to-host sandbox escape, and 91.5% of jailbreak attempts declined against 59% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Access runs through the Daybreak Blue tester program.

So what for TSD: Every SMB client site you run is now in range of an attacker with a model this good, and the defensive tooling is gated to enterprises with partner relationships. That asymmetry is your pitch and your obligation. Audit dependency versions on every client property this month, turn on automated patching where the host supports it, and put "we patch your stack" into the Managed AI retainer as a line item rather than an unpaid favor.

◆ Where the money is going

Funding Sep 2, 2026

Cognition is closing ~$1B at a $47B valuation on $900M+ of annualized revenue

Bloomberg reports Cognition, the company behind the Devin engineering agent, is raising roughly $1 billion at about $47 billion, with the final size possibly larger against nearly $10 billion of investor interest. Its valuation was $26 billion three months ago. Annualized revenue is above $900 million, up from $492 million in late May.

The revenue number is the part worth staring at. Coding agents are not a promise anymore, they are a business doing close to a billion a year, and that curve went nearly 2x in a quarter.

So what for TSD: Buyers of software services have now seen the price of "AI writes the code" collapse in public. Stop selling hours of implementation and start selling the thing an agent cannot do: knowing which SMB workflow is worth automating, owning the outcome, and being the local person who picks up the phone. Your money-back guarantee and source-code-on-day-one terms are worth more in this market than a lower hourly rate.

◆ Rules of the road

Policy Sep 2, 2026

New York City barred student-facing generative AI through eighth grade

The largest US school district put a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI, including companion chatbots, for 2-K through eighth grade in the 2026-27 school year, affecting roughly 600,000 students. Screen time on individual devices is capped at 30 minutes in grades 3–5 and 45 minutes in 6–8, with individual devices barred in pre-K through second grade.

Teachers may still use generative AI for lesson planning and administrative work against district safety standards. Carve-outs cover assistive technology for students with disabilities, multilingual learners and career-readiness programs. Five high school pilots get access to a vetted, limited tool set capped at five classes per school.

So what for TSD: A district this size sets the template other districts copy, and the template is "adults may use it, students may not, and the tool must be vetted." If any TSD product ever touches a minor, the compliance bar just got written for you. Nothing to build today, and worth knowing before an education-adjacent prospect asks.

Agents Sep 3, 2026

Per-action authorization is showing up as its own product

JetStream released Clearance, a reasoning engine that evaluates and authorizes every agent action before it executes and blocks dangerous sequences such as exfiltration patterns, rather than recording them after the fact. AccuKnox shipped AgentZ the same week, a model-agnostic platform bundling sandboxes, role-based access, runtime credential injection and audit traces. Genesys added an AI Control Plane for policy-aware sequencing in contact-center agents.

So what for TSD: When a client asks what stops your AI receptionist from doing something expensive, the honest answer needs to be a mechanism. Write down the exact list of actions Front Desk and Booking Bridge are allowed to take, enforce it in code before the tool call, and log every attempt. That is a one-evening change and it closes a real objection.
