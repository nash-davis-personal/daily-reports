◆ The agent layer becomes the product

New Jul 9, 2026

OpenAI folds Codex into ChatGPT and launches ChatGPT Work

The standalone Codex desktop app is gone, merged into a single ChatGPT desktop app; the old ChatGPT desktop app is renamed ChatGPT Classic. Riding on top is ChatGPT Work, described by OpenAI as an agent that operates across your apps and files, works for hours on a complex project, and returns a finished artifact — an Excel model, a Word document — rather than a chat transcript.

The substantive addition is a Unified Plugins Directory: fifteen third-party integrations at launch, invoked by @ mention, spanning Google Drive, SharePoint, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Gmail, Outlook, Salesforce, Adobe, Zoom, LinkedIn, GitHub, Canva and Dropbox. An "Auto-Review" layer inspects proposed actions and blocks risky ones before execution. It is available immediately in the desktop app, rolling out on web and mobile to Pro, Enterprise and Edu first, with Plus and Business following.

So what for TSD: This is OpenAI selling the thing you sell. An SMB owner who wires ChatGPT Work into Gmail and Drive gets a decent chunk of what a generic "AI automation" retainer promises, for a seat fee. Your defensible ground is what a plugin directory cannot touch: the client's phone line, their booking system, their own website's content, and the fact that they own the source code. Stop pitching "AI that works across your apps" — that's a commodity as of yesterday. Pitch Front Desk answering the phone at 7pm and Concierge knowing their specific catalog.

Context Jul 7–9, 2026

Anthropic got to the same place from the other direction

Two days before ChatGPT Work, Anthropic put Claude Cowork on web, iOS and Android in beta for Max subscribers — the same premise, arrived at from the coding-agent side. Sessions sync across devices, scheduled work runs in the background with no device online, and the phone notifies you when Claude needs permission to proceed. Anthropic extended its doubled Cowork usage limits through August 5 to mark the rollout, and cited usage data showing most Cowork users are not coding.

So what for TSD: Both leaders now agree the unit of value is a delegated task, not a conversation. That is the exact shape of a Managed AI retainer. Reframe the retainer around outcomes a client can name — "the phone gets answered, the quotes get drafted, the follow-ups get sent" — because that is the language the category is standardizing on, and it is language you can price against.

◆ Meta arrives, cheaply

New Jul 9, 2026

Muse Spark 1.1 gives Meta a coding model and, finally, a developer API

Alexandr Wang's Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Spark 1.1, calling it Meta's strongest model for agentic and coding work and claiming it rivals GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8 across many agentic evals. It is multimodal — reasoning, coding, computer use, document analysis, image, video and audio — and it arrives with the new Meta Model API, the company's first serious pitch at outside developers.

Pricing is the story: $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 per million output. That undercuts Grok 4.5 ($2/$6) and sits far below Opus 4.8. Meta's own agentic-eval claims have no independent verification yet.

So what for TSD: It was already on Vercel's AI Gateway within a day, so trying it costs an afternoon, not a migration. Route one internal, low-stakes, high-volume job at it — draft-generation inside this very briefing pipeline, or first-pass classification in Concierge — and measure. Do not put it in front of a client until someone other than Meta has benchmarked it.

◆ The receipts on Grok 4.5

Benchmark Jul 8–9, 2026

Independent testing lands: fourth place, and a hallucination rate that doubled

Yesterday's briefing flagged Musk's "Opus-class" framing as a marketing claim awaiting evidence. The evidence arrived. Artificial Analysis scores Grok 4.5 at 54 on its Intelligence Index — fourth, behind Fable 5, GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8. Pricing is $2/$6 per million tokens with cache hits discounted 75%.

The catch is on AA-Omniscience, which rewards correct answers and penalizes confident wrong ones. Grok 4.5's accuracy rose from 35% to 52% over its predecessor, and its hallucination rate rose from 25% to 54%. A bigger model that knows more and is also far more willing to assert something false. Separately, xAI's own token-efficiency claims lean on benchmarks partly derived from Cursor session data that the model also trained on — an overlap nobody has resolved.

So what for TSD: Cheap and fast, and it will lie to your client. A 54% hallucination rate disqualifies it from anything customer-facing — Front Desk quoting a price, Concierge answering a product question. If you use it at all, use it where output is verified before it reaches a human. The general lesson worth keeping: wait 48 hours for independent numbers before you believe a launch-day claim. That discipline just paid.

◆ Rules arrive

Policy Jul 6, 2026

Illinois signs the strongest state AI safety law in the country

Governor JB Pritzker signed SB 315, the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act. It applies to "large frontier developers" — those above $500 million in annual revenue — and requires them to publish and follow safety plans, submit to annual independent third-party audits (a first in the nation), and report critical safety incidents to the state within 72 hours, or within 24 hours where there is imminent risk of death or serious injury. It creates confidential whistleblower channels for employees. Penalties run $1 million for a first violation and $3 million after. It passed with bipartisan support and takes effect January 1, 2027.

So what for TSD: The revenue threshold means this lands on OpenAI and Anthropic, not on you. What it gives you is a sentence for the skeptical owner: the models you'd be building on are the ones now subject to independent safety audits and mandatory incident reporting. File it next to the government-preview note from yesterday. Watch for a state following Illinois with a threshold low enough to catch deployers rather than developers — that would be the version that touches your contracts.
