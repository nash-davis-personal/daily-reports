◆ Models and tools you can build with

Model Nov 12

Gemini 4 Hydrogen: Google's fast tier gets a real coding jump and a higher price

Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday as the everyday model in the Gemini 4 family, below Carbon and Argon. Coverage describes it as the successor to Gemini 3.8 Flash and the new default for AI Mode in Search. It is rolling out in the Gemini app, Search, AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini for business and Antigravity, and it is on the free API tier with the usual limits.

The numbers are Google's own: 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1 against 18.2% for 3.8 Flash, and 2.5x the speed at the default setting. The price is flat across the 1M-token window at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 output, with cached input at $0.075. Two details matter for a budget. Output pricing includes thinking tokens, and Hydrogen defaults to the high thinking level. And 3.8 Flash stays at $0.75 and $3.75 through Dec 31, so Hydrogen costs twice as much per token today. Google has not published batch prices or paid-tier rate limits, and secondary coverage says the official pricing page had not listed the model at launch.

So what for TSD: Run your Front Desk and Concierge eval set against gemini-4-hydrogen through Vercel AI Gateway this weekend, with thinking set to low and to high, and compare cost per finished call against what you run now. The per-token price went up, so the only number that counts is cost per completed task. If it wins, the swap is a model string. If you still have anything on 3.8 Flash, put Dec 31 on the calendar and recheck the price then.

Security agents Nov 12

OpenAI launches Daybreak Red Team and Defense Factory

At its "Cyber at the Frontier" event OpenAI released two security products under the Daybreak name. Daybreak Red Team, in early access, runs teams of agents that probe web applications, confirm or reject findings from scanners and bug bounties, and retest after a patch. The operator sets which apps and endpoints the agents may touch, and the work runs in isolated environments with a review layer OpenAI calls Guardian.

Daybreak Defense Factory, described as a research preview delivered as a plugin, takes findings from Red Team or from existing tools, traces them through a connected GitHub repo and drafts patches for an engineer to review. The Register identifies the underlying model as GPT-6 Cyber. OpenAI has published no pricing and named no launch customers. Every source found is same-day coverage; OpenAI's own page was not retrieved in this pass.

So what for TSD: Two things. First, the same capability is available to attackers through open tools (yesterday's AntiFox item), so the patch lag on client sites matters more each month. Second, "an agent team attacked this site and here is the report" is a deliverable a small business owner understands. Request early access with a TSD demo site as the target and see what the report looks like before deciding whether it belongs in a retainer.

◆ The companies

Leadership Nov 12

Nvidia confirms Jensen Huang has resigned; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO

Nvidia announced Thursday morning that Jensen Huang resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, after 33 years. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, agreed to serve as interim CEO. Harvey C. Jones was named board chair, a role the company has not had before. The board is starting a search and will consider internal and external candidates. The release carried no statement from Huang.

This closes the question yesterday's edition left open, when the board was reported to be meeting on his removal. News coverage ties the exit to the board's investigation, opened Nov 8, into reports about his personal conduct; CNN reports he has been called to testify before Congress. The stock fell more than 13% in early trading and closed down 4.34%. AMD and Broadcom each rose more than 5% on the day. Nvidia reports quarterly results on Nov 17, now with the CFO presenting as chief executive.

So what for TSD: Nothing you ship changes. The product roadmap and the supply contracts are the same today as on Monday. What to watch is whether a prolonged search slows GPU supply decisions, because that is the input behind every token price you pay. Nov 17 earnings is the first read.

IPO Nov 11–12

Anthropic's IPO order book is reported at about six times the shares on offer

Bloomberg reported that demand for Anthropic's offering is running at roughly six times the 825 million shares being marketed; earlier reports said "several times." The filed range is $70 to $78, which puts the IPO at about $64 billion at the top and about $90 billion including a private placement. The Financial Times reported that Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley expect the price could land above the range, and one report says bankers are weighing a valuation of $1.5 trillion or more.

What is confirmed and what is reported should stay separate. The range and share count are in the filing. The demand figure, the higher price talk, the Nov 18 pricing and the Nov 19 first trade are all press reports. No amended range has been filed and Anthropic has not commented. Indications of interest in the last week of a large deal are also routinely inflated by funds asking for more than they expect to receive.

So what for TSD: This is your main model vendor about to become a public company with quarterly numbers to hit. Expect two effects over the next year: more pressure on API pricing and enterprise packaging, and more disclosure about roadmap and uptime. Neither changes this week's work. If you want attention for anything you have built on Claude, the week of Nov 16 is when people will be searching the name.

◆ Risk and rules

Lawsuit Nov 12

Four nonprofits ask a California court to freeze frontier model development

The Alliance for Secure AI, the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen and Heat Initiative sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI division in San Francisco Superior Court on Thursday. The complaint, reported at 63 pages, alleges the companies violate California's unfair competition law by endangering public health and interfering with the use of property such as computer systems.

The plaintiffs want an injunction barring each company from developing or deploying systems more capable than its latest model. Secondary reports add that the suit also asks to bar using AI systems to autonomously run research and development of future models; that detail comes from less established outlets and should be checked against the complaint. No judge has ruled, no development has stopped, and the companies had not responded publicly at the time of the reports.

So what for TSD: Low odds of touching your work, and the cost of being ready is small. Today's models are explicitly outside the request, so everything you have deployed keeps running under any outcome. The habit worth keeping regardless is the one you already have on AI Gateway: every product names a fallback model from a second vendor.

Breach Disclosed Nov 11–12

Notion says a breach at Fireworks AI exposed customer AI chat transcripts

Notion disclosed that attackers who broke into its inference vendor, Fireworks AI, reached a dataset containing Notion AI chat transcripts from fewer than 1% of workspaces. The data came from Notion's Insights feature, and Notion says most of the transcripts could not be tied to a specific author or workspace. Fireworks alerted Notion to an exposed dataset on Nov 2 and confirmed on Nov 8 that customer data had been accessed. Notion says it cut traffic to Fireworks, deleted vendor-held data and halted training runs by Nov 3.

The dispute is about retention. Notion says Fireworks had contractually committed to zero data retention and that "data was retained from the Fireworks endpoints that we used." Fireworks says it has detected no impact to its Inference, Training SDK and Nexus services, which are zero-retention by default, and that the intrusion came through a late-October breach at a third-party cloud provider that reached its development systems, including billing records, secrets, datasets and partial model weights. The two accounts do not fully line up and nothing independent settles it. Notion also says Insights needs stored conversations, so its own zero-retention promise never covered that feature.

So what for TSD: A zero-retention clause covered the inference endpoint and missed the analytics feature next to it. Check your own products the same way: for Front Desk and Concierge, write down every place a caller's or visitor's words are stored (your Supabase tables, the voice vendor's recordings, model-provider logs, any eval or analytics dataset) and how long each keeps them. If a client asks "where do my customers' conversations go," you want that page already written.
