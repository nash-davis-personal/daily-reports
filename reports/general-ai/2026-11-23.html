◆ Labs and policy

Governance Nov 22

OpenAI's chief scientist proposes perplexity as the measure for limiting AI development

TechCrunch reports that Jakub Pachocki published a post arguing that benchmarks built on downstream skills have gone stale, while perplexity, a raw measure of how well a model predicts text, has stayed informative. The post puts GPT-4 at about 0.91 and says losses below 0.8 coincided with transformative capability in cybersecurity and mathematics. Sam Altman called it the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.

This is a proposal from one lab. No regulator or rival has adopted it.

So what for TSD: Nothing to change in your products. Keep it in mind when a client asks whether AI rules are coming: the labs are now proposing the yardsticks themselves, which usually comes before rules.
TechCrunch
Agents Nov 21 to 22

OpenAI's agents are reported to finish more long research tasks, and an always-on agent is in limited testing

An agent-news roundup dated Nov 22 says OpenAI's agents completed 46% of research assignments that take a person two to four hours, up from 23% in March, and 35% of four to eight hour assignments, up from 13%. The same roundup says a persistent agent feature called Dots is in limited testing with some ChatGPT Plus users. I could not open a primary OpenAI source for either claim, so treat both as secondary.

So what for TSD: Half of multi-hour tasks still fail. For client automations, keep jobs short, checkable and logged, and price a human review step into anything that runs for more than a few minutes.

◆ The money and the hardware

Costs Nov 21 to 22

Server makers are reported to be raising prices on signed AI orders, and Musk warns of a cash shortage in 2027

Two market roundups cite a Financial Times report that Dell, HPE and Supermicro are asking 25% to 45% above contract price at delivery, with Lenovo also named, as memory costs keep rising. HPE's chief executive is reported to have said memory and storage prices will stay elevated into 2027. HPE reports results today at 5 p.m. ET and Dell reports tomorrow, so both claims can be checked against the companies' own words this week.

Separately, Business Insider reports that Elon Musk wrote on X over the weekend that getting compute running reliably is very hard and that the cash shortage next year will be severe.

So what for TSD: Model prices fell last week, and the hardware underneath them is getting more expensive. Do not promise clients that AI costs only go down. Put a usage line in every Managed AI retainer so a vendor price change passes through.

Deals Nov 21

Amazon is reported to be in final talks to buy Decart for about $7.5 billion

The Financial Times, as relayed by Business Insider, says Amazon is in advanced talks to buy the real-time video generation startup and that signing could come within days. Decart's May funding round valued it near $4 billion. Nothing is signed and neither side has commented.

So what for TSD: Real-time generated video is heading into a big cloud's catalog. That matters for TSD Marketing's ad-video work in a year, and it changes nothing this week.

◆ Builder tools

API Nov 22

Google previews Tool Retrieval in the Gemini API

According to a MarkTechPost write-up summarized in a Nov 23 digest, the feature holds back each tool's parameter schema until the model decides it needs that tool. It runs server-side or client-side and is in preview in the Interactions API v1beta only. I could not open the original article, so check Google's docs before building on it.

So what for TSD: Front Desk and Concierge each carry a handful of tools, so the saving is small today. It becomes relevant once one agent holds twenty or more tools, such as a booking agent wired to several client systems.
