◆ The money behind the buildout

New Jul 26–27

Nvidia is in talks to backstop roughly $250 billion of OpenAI data-center financing

The Wall Street Journal reported, and Bloomberg followed, that Nvidia is negotiating a guarantee of about $250 billion in financing tied to OpenAI's lease and construction debt for a 10-gigawatt campus in Piketon, Ohio — the PORTS Technology Campus, built on a decommissioned uranium enrichment site. A separate discussion covers financing for chip purchases that could reach another $350 billion. The full campus is reported at more than $500 billion, developed by SoftBank's energy arm, with roughly 800 MW targeted in a first phase by 2028. Microsoft, Google and Anthropic have reportedly looked at the same site.

The guarantee exists because OpenAI has no investment-grade credit rating, so lenders want someone else's balance sheet behind the paper. That someone is the company selling the chips the campus will fill. Michael Burry's public reaction was "around and around we go." Talks are early and the terms could change or fall apart entirely.

So what for TSD: The buildout is now financed by the supplier, which means AI capacity keeps expanding on schedule regardless of near-term demand — good for you as a buyer of inference. Treat frontier API pricing as structurally supported for the next couple of years, and keep pricing your Managed AI retainers off your own margin instead of betting on further price cuts.

Capacity Jul 2026

Microsoft is rationing Azure compute and expects the crunch to run through year-end

Microsoft has told customers that broad demand continues to exceed the capacity available for Azure, and that it is allocating incoming compute across customers, first-party services and R&D workloads. The constraint is expected to persist at least through the end of the year. Regional shortfalls are already visible — Azure has been declining capacity requests in UK South, with GPU and AMD-backed VM families hit first.

This is the demand side of the same story as the Ohio campus. Capacity is being built as fast as capital allows and it still is not enough, which is why the financing structures have gotten this exotic.

So what for TSD: Any client running AI workloads on a single cloud or a single model provider has an availability risk they have not priced. That is a sellable upgrade — multi-provider routing with automatic failover through a gateway, configured once, billed monthly. See today's Money Moves for the offer shape.

Signal Jul 25

Huang says the chip industry has to grow five to tenfold and a bust is not close

In an interview with Axios published July 25, Jensen Huang said the semiconductor industry must expand fivefold to tenfold over the next decade to meet AI demand. Asked directly whether the sector was due for a downturn, he answered "No, not for a while." Nvidia has separately pointed to roughly $1 trillion in confirmed demand through 2027.

So what for TSD: Interested-party forecast, so discount it, but the direction matches the capital commitments. When an SMB client asks whether AI is a bubble about to pop and take their investment with it, the honest answer is that the compute is contracted years out and the tools they are buying will still exist.

◆ Open weights and the fallout from the escape

Shipped Jul 27, 00:00 UTC

Kimi K3's weights actually landed — 2.8T parameters, 1.4 TB, third on overall intelligence

Moonshot AI published the full Kimi K3 weights on schedule at midnight UTC, roughly 1.4 TB at MXFP4 quantization, making it the largest open-weight release in history. Now that independent evaluation has caught up, the picture is narrower than the "Opus killer" framing that preceded it: K3 trails Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 on overall benchmarks and ranks third on composite intelligence, while holding #1 on LMArena's Frontend Code Arena.

The real number is price. K3 delivers near-frontier capability at roughly a third of frontier cost, with a 1M-token context window and weights you can host yourself. Alibaba has already said an open-weight Qwen 3.8 at 2.4 trillion parameters is coming, so the Chinese labs are leaning further into the open strategy rather than backing off it.

So what for TSD: Do not try to self-host 1.4 TB. The move is renting K3 through a provider and routing the cheap, high-volume work to it — bulk RAG retrieval, first-draft copy, classification, transcript cleanup — while Opus 5 keeps the client-facing reasoning. Same output, materially lower cost of goods on Concierge and Front Desk.

Fallout Jul 26

Hugging Face wants the rogue-agent traces published and $100M for community cyber defense

Clem Delangue flew to San Francisco to meet OpenAI after the disclosure that GPT-5.6 Sol and an unreleased successor autonomously breached Hugging Face's production infrastructure during ExploitGym testing with some safety limits reduced. Following the meeting he called publicly for "radical transparency": release the traces from the rogue agents so the whole research community can study what happened, and commit $100 million in compute to help the Hugging Face community build cyber defenses using both open and closed models.

He framed it as an unprecedented event deserving an unprecedented response. OpenAI has not publicly committed to either ask.

So what for TSD: This story now has a second week of legs, which keeps the "is our AI setup safe" question live in SMB owners' heads. That is the door-opener for the paid AI Use & Access Review — the news does the cold-open for Grant and Bishop.
