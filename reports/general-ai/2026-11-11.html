General AI ◆Wednesday, November 11, 2026 ◆Covers Nov 7 – Nov 11 (five days, since the last edition)

Fable 5.2 ships cheaper and faster, Anthropic starts its IPO roadshow, OpenAI lets businesses claim a profile inside ChatGPT, and stolen AI keys cost Railway customers $740k.

The frontier moved twice in two days: Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 landed Monday at a lower price with a 2.5x Fast mode, and Gemini 4 Carbon went generally available Tuesday at introductory pricing that ends Nov 30. The money side was louder. Anthropic opened a Nasdaq roadshow that could raise about $64 billion plus $26 billion in private placements, OpenAI closed $11 billion at a valuation below its ask, and lenders started selling data-center loans at a discount. ◆ Frontier models

Model release Nov 9

Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2: a price cut, a Fast mode, and a 17-point jump on agentic coding

Anthropic released both models Monday. They are the same underlying model: Fable carries the cyber and biology safeguards and is open to everyone; Mythos is limited to organizations in the Cyber Verification and Life Sciences Verification programs. Standard pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, down from $10/$50 on Fable 5.1, with outputs up to 30% faster. An optional Fast mode generates tokens up to 2.5x faster at $16/$80 and is available in Claude Code, the Claude Platform, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry. The model is on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure from day one.

The posted numbers: FrontierCode 2.0 49.1% (Fable 5.1 was 32.4%, Opus 5.5 45.5%), FrontierSWE v2 69.9%, OSWorld 2.1 89.3%, Remote Labor Index 41.3% against 17.9% for 5.1. Gemini 4 Carbon still beats it on AutomationBench (53.9% vs 51.4%). The showcase claims are the kind you remember: 1,000 subagents built a bootable operating system from an empty folder over eight days, and about 1,000 agents formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours. Safety-classifier blocks are down about 55% overall. Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 as the daily driver and Fable for the hardest work. Paid plans get one to three usage resets, and Fable counts for up to 50% of weekly limits on Pro and standard Team seats.

So what for TSD: Your Claude Code sessions just got a cheaper top model and a Fast mode. Use Fable 5.2 for the gnarly client migrations and Opus 5.5 for everything else, and claim the usage reset before Dec 10. The 1,000-agent builds are a sales story too: "a one-person shop that ships like a team" now has a public benchmark behind it.

Model release Nov 10

Gemini 4 Carbon is generally available: $2/$10 through Nov 30, then $4/$20, and a /swarm preview in Antigravity

Google opened Gemini 4 Carbon to paying API developers in AI Studio and to AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode. It sits above Gemini 4 Argon, which Google still recommends for most work, and spends more tokens reasoning. Introductory pricing matches Argon at $2 per million input and $10 per million output until Nov 30, after which Carbon costs $4/$20. Google claims #1 on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at over 2x lower cost per task than the next external model, a 92% pass rate on a 375-task agentic honesty benchmark (Argon: 70%), and a simulated-attack breach rate of 0.8%. Antigravity's /swarm preview turns one prompt into a coordinated agent team with Carbon doing architecture and auditing while lighter models run tasks in parallel workspaces. Vercel added Carbon to AI Gateway the same day.

So what for TSD: Three weeks of frontier-model pricing at half price. Run your TSD Concierge RAG eval set against Carbon through Vercel AI Gateway before Nov 30 and decide whether it becomes the fallback model; the number you want is cost per answered question, with Claude as the control.

Product Nov 10

Claude Sheets and Whiteboards in beta, Claude Data goes GA, and connected apps now open inside Claude

A busy product day the morning after the model launch. Claude Sheets (beta) builds spreadsheets with tabs, formulas, formatting and drop-downs and exports to .xlsx or CSV. Claude Whiteboards (beta) is a shared brainstorming board that exports to a doc or deck or moves to Lucid, Miro or Zoho. Claude Dashboards was renamed Claude Data and is generally available with certified queries, live sharing and partner connectors (Amplitude, Mixpanel, PostHog, Sigma); it needs a data connector. All three are on paid plans across web, desktop and mobile; Enterprise plans have Whiteboards and Sheets off by default and Data turns on Nov 19. Separately, connected apps such as Asana, Canva, Dropbox, Vercel and Webflow can now open beside a conversation and Claude can act inside them with approval, and MCP Apps is in public preview for developers. Claude Meetings was announced as coming soon. The Claude Marketplace now lists more than 9,000 connectors and plugins.

So what for TSD: Claude Data plus a Supabase or PostHog connector is a credible "weekly numbers" dashboard for a client with no extra build; test it on your own metrics first. MCP Apps in preview is worth a look if you want TSD Concierge or Front Desk to render a real UI inside Claude for a client's team.

◆ The business of AI

IPO Nov 10

Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow: ANT on Nasdaq, $70–$78, 825 million shares, and $26 billion of private placements from its chip and cloud partners

Anthropic's own notice is short: it has applied to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ANT, expects $70.00 to $78.00 a share, and is offering 825,000,000 shares (801,351,354 from the company, 23,648,646 from existing holders) with a 30-day option on 123,750,000 more. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley distribute the prospectus. At the top of the range the base offering is about $64 billion; the registration is not yet effective. CNBC reports, citing sources, pricing on Nov 18 and first trade Nov 19. Separately, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC agreed to buy $26 billion of Class N stock in a private placement at the IPO price; one report puts Nvidia and Google at $10 billion each. A Reuters story on Nov 10 citing a $69.3 billion target was walked back the same day after the company told bankers the figure was inaccurate. The FT also reported Anthropic committed about $3.4 billion for 1.7 GW of gas generators via OnePWR orders in September.

So what for TSD: Nothing to trade here, and this is not advice. The practical read is that your core vendor is about to be a public company with quarterly numbers to hit, which usually means more promos (the usage resets are one), more enterprise packaging, and price stability on the API. Keep your prompts model-agnostic anyway.

Product Nov 10

OpenAI's "Businesses in ChatGPT": claim a free profile, upload a product feed, see your mentions and clicks, and (if you sell online in the US) publish a Business Agent

OpenAI opened a beta that lets a business claim its listing inside ChatGPT, correct details, and upload product feeds with prices and stock. The Business Hub shows mentions, profile views and website clicks over 7- and 28-day windows plus "discovery topics," the themes of conversations where the site gets cited. Eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses can build and publish a branded Business Agent inside ChatGPT and can pay to sponsor it as the destination for ChatGPT Ads. Claiming is free: search the business name, verify by phone, text or DNS, pass review, then confirm identity with a government ID before editing or publishing. Shopify merchants can connect a store to speed verification. OpenAI says user conversations are not shared with businesses and that insights are aggregated above undisclosed thresholds. Clicks are not confirmed visits. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT weekly. The same day OpenAI acquired travel-agent startup Odessia (terms undisclosed) and Bloomberg reported it wired an $11 billion first close at a $1.25 trillion valuation on Nov 6, below the $1.4 trillion it originally sought, with the round now targeting about $20 billion.

So what for TSD: This is a productized service you can sell this week: claim and verify the ChatGPT profile, upload the product feed, fix the Google Business Profile and site schema while you are in there, then a monthly "AI visibility" readout from the 28-day mentions and clicks. Your import/ceramics retail client is exactly the US ecommerce case that can get a Business Agent. Money Moves has the pricing.

Capital Nov 9

Lenders are selling AI data-center loans at a discount, and Ares says it will not finance GPUs it cannot depreciate

Semafor reports that investors are shopping whole portfolios of data-center loans at steep discounts, with GPU-backed financings hit hardest because lenders fear the loan terms will outlast the chips. Bloomberg reported ING is looking to sell $500 million of data-center loans including a $25 million piece backing Stargate. Goldman Sachs and Apollo have begun marketing a $40 billion chip financing for SpaceX, described as the largest of its kind, and the article frames its reception as a test. Ares CEO Michael Arougheti said on an earnings call that Ares will pass on GPU deals: "I, for one, personally do not know how to underwrite the decline and depreciation curves on that type of technology." The same week Google's eighth-generation TPU line added two systems (8tx with confidential computing on AMD hosts, 8i Max with two chips per unit), both without pricing or a ship date before 2027, and Nvidia reportedly required overseas cloud partners to reserve 51% of capacity for non-Chinese customers.

So what for TSD: The compute buildout is now financed by people asking what a GPU is worth in three years, which means model prices keep falling (see Fable and Carbon above) and the risk sits with the lenders. Build on the price curve, do not build a business that needs it to stop.

◆ Security for people who build on AI

Breach Nov 9–10

Railway: a 26-hour break-in, decrypted env vars for up to 100,000 workspaces, and $740,000 of someone else's AI usage. Fireworks AI: a second breach reaching datasets and partial model weights

Railway's Nov 10 incident report says attackers exploited an unknown bug in its build infrastructure on Oct 20, escaped filesystem isolation, copied internal source code, then used a misconfigured trust relationship to get credentials that decrypt customer environment variables. They were out by Oct 21. Railway puts the maximum exposure at 1.85% of workspaces (about 100,000) and says 1,852 users had linked-account tokens, including GitHub OAuth tokens and AI agent keys, exposed and reset. The stolen secrets were used for roughly $740,000 of unauthorized AI usage, mostly on AI provider keys; Railway says it will cover charges providers refuse to refund. Wiz links it to a campaign it calls "Black October" that also hit Northflank, OpenCode, Warp and Lightning AI. Railway says the attackers ran AI agents probing many routes in parallel.

Fireworks AI, meanwhile, disclosed that a second intrusion through a third-party cloud provider on Oct 23–24 reached development machines and exposed billing and account records, and for a limited set of accounts stored secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights. Payment card numbers were not stored. Notion said some unattributable Notion AI chat sessions may have been exposed. Two customers told Forbes they moved business away. Fireworks has revoked keys and told customers to rotate third-party credentials.

So what for TSD: Your Anthropic, ElevenLabs and Gemini keys are the asset now. Today: set spend caps on every provider, turn on Anthropic's IP allowlist for server keys, rotate anything that has ever lived in a hosting provider's env store, and put a monthly rotation on the Monthly Security line. Then sell the same checklist to every client whose site has an AI feature.
