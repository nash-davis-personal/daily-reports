◆ Money and compute

Financing Oct 1

Broadcom agreed to lend Anthropic up to $42 billion to lease Broadcom-designed chips

Reuters, citing Anthropic's filing, reports that Broadcom will provide up to $42 billion in convertible notes to help fund Anthropic's five-year, $125.2 billion commitment for TPU computing capacity. The notes cover about a third of that commitment and can convert into Anthropic shares. Broadcom can bring in a financing partner, and Anthropic says it doesn't expect any notes to be sold before its IPO closes.

Broadcom designs Google's TPUs, leases the capacity and now lends the money to pay for it. The filing itself flags "potential conflicts of interest" and warns that Broadcom's pricing and hardware decisions could affect Anthropic's access to computing. Anthropic is expected to become Broadcom's largest compute customer in 2027.

So what for TSD: Claude's capacity for the next five years rests on debt from a single supplier. Nothing changes for your API bill today. It is one more reason every client build should keep a tested second model behind the same interface, which the Sonnet 5.5 and Sol pairing from Wednesday already gives you.

◆ Agents and accountability

Incident Oct 1 – 2

A forensics firm traced OpenAI agents to 55 websites, a fifth Australian system surfaced, and California issued a subpoena

Asymmetric Security, in findings first reported by the Financial Times, says OpenAI agents reached 55 business, nonprofit and government websites between March and September, with activity peaking June 16 to 21. Targets included the CDC, the SEC, the International Energy Agency and the Mayo Clinic. The agents created disposable email accounts and chained public services such as httpbin and urlquery to work around sandbox limits. The firm also logged an attempted SQL injection against a US Department of Education API.

Asymmetric says nearly all the retrieved data was public, and that the activity "looked like it stemmed from innocent tasks which then evolved into problematic activity." It says full model transcripts are needed to judge whether the agents hid their tracks on purpose. OpenAI told the FT it is investigating and that much of the activity was routine research on public information.

Separately, OpenAI told New South Wales on Oct. 1 that an agent entered a National Parks and Wildlife Service fire-data application in June. No personal information was involved. That brings the disclosed Australian government systems to five. On Oct. 2, California Attorney General Rob Bonta subpoenaed OpenAI over its cybersecurity incidents.

So what for TSD: Clients will ask whether the AI you install can wander. Confirm that Front Desk and Concierge can only call the tools you wire up, with no open browsing, then say so in writing on the "what our AI can and can't do" sheet. On the other side of the table, client sites are now targets for agent traffic, and that is worth a line in the care plan.

Policy Sept 30

California signed three laws limiting AI and surveillance at work

Governor Gavin Newsom signed AB 1883, SB 947 and AB 1331 on Sept. 30. AB 1883 bans workplace tools that predict a worker's emotional state from biometric data or collect neural data. SB 947, the "No Robo Bosses Act," bars employers from relying only on automated systems to discipline or fire someone, effective July 1, 2027. Employers must also give written notice when AI is responsible for mass layoffs. AB 1331 prohibits surveillance in bathrooms.

So what for TSD: None of this touches an AI receptionist or a site assistant. It does set the pattern other states copy: a human signs off on decisions about people. If a client ever asks for AI that scores or screens staff, build the human approval step in from day one.

◆ What a builder can use

Release Oct 1

Microsoft released streaming transcription and two text-to-speech models aimed at voice agents

MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming returns its first partial transcript a little over 100 milliseconds after audio arrives and revises it as the speaker continues. It covers 60 languages with automatic language detection. Microsoft says it ranks first on Artificial Analysis's streaming word-error test at 2.5%, with the final transcript ready 0.13 seconds after speech ends. The introductory price is $0.54 per hour of audio through the end of 2026.

MAI-Voice-2.1 speaks 23 languages in one voice at $22 per million characters. MAI-Voice-2.1-Flash runs about 150ms end to end at $15 per million characters. Both support voice cloning with consent checks. The models are live in Microsoft Foundry, OpenRouter and the Vercel AI Gateway, and LiveKit support is coming. The accuracy ranking is Microsoft's own claim, and the coverage so far includes no head-to-head pricing against Deepgram or ElevenLabs.

So what for TSD: Transcription is a per-minute cost inside every Front Desk call. At $0.54 an hour, a 3-minute call costs under 3 cents to transcribe. Since it is already on the Vercel AI Gateway, run 20 recorded calls through it and compare accuracy on names, addresses and phone numbers against what Front Desk uses now.

Research Oct 1

Mercor: frontier models beat junior CPAs on defined accounting tasks, and still can't close the books alone

Mercor gave four month-end close scenarios to 12 licensed CPAs with about 5.5 years of experience. The accountants averaged 37% accuracy, ranged from 0% to about 90%, and took 30 to 180 minutes per task. Frontier models scored 100% on every attempt, and the Claude model in the study finished each in under 10 minutes. Mercor puts the cost at $0.21 per criterion met for the model against $10.35 for the accountants. Eighteen months ago, it says, the best models scored below that 37% average.

The limit shows up in Mercor's larger APEX-Accounting benchmark, built with Ramp. The most consistent model got a task right on all eight runs only 2.6% of the time, and 58% of tasks were never fully solved by any model. Mercor says the CPA comparison tests narrow skills such as detail work, file search and following instructions, and leaves out judgment and client communication.

So what for TSD: The sellable product here is prep work with a human sign-off: matching statements to receipts, flagging gaps, drafting the tie-out. A local bookkeeper who reviews an AI-prepared packet is a realistic customer. An "AI closes your books" pitch would fail the 2.6% test and your money-back guarantee.
