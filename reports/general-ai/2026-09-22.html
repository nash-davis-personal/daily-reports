New September 21–22

Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro is the top open-weights model in the world

Xiaomi released MiMo-V2.6-Pro and the cheaper MiMo-V2.6-Flash as omnimodal models with open weights under an MIT license. On Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index, Pro scored 46 — the highest open-weights score recorded, up from 26 for V2.5-Pro, and tied with xAI's Grok 4.7 released the same weekend. It sits above DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 39 and V4.1 Pro at 36.

The coding and agent jumps are the part worth reading twice. DeepSWE v1.1 went from 19.0 on V2.5-Pro to 71.9 on V2.6-Pro. AutomationBench went 16.0 to 53.1, MiMo Code Bench 40.4 to 63.2. Architecture is mixture-of-experts: 1.02T total parameters with 42B active for Pro, 309B total with 15B active for Flash.

So what for TSD: An MIT-licensed model in the same benchmark tier as a flagship closed model changes the floor on what a client concierge or receptionist costs to run. Worth an evaluation pass on MiMo-Flash for the high-volume, low-stakes parts of TSD Concierge — the RAG retrieval-and-summarize hops — while the customer-facing reasoning stays on Claude. Licensing is the point: MIT means a client can own the deployment outright, which lines up with the "you own the source code day one" pitch.

New September 21

Grok 4.7: a bigger base model at Grok 4.6 prices

xAI released Grok 4.7 on September 21 — 2.1 trillion parameters, tuned for coding, long-horizon reasoning and professional knowledge work, with a 500K-token context window and a June 2026 knowledge cutoff. List pricing held at $2.00 per million input tokens, $0.50 cached, $6.00 output. Requests at or above 200K prompt tokens bill the entire request at $4 / $1 / $12.

On xAI's own comparison table it beats Grok 4.6 on all seven benchmarks shown and GPT-5.6 Sol Max on four. Claude Fable 5.1 Max still leads it on CursorBench, Terminal-Bench, GDPval and HealthBench; Grok leads on EEBench and the Harvey legal benchmark. Access is through the xAI API, Grok Build, and Cursor.

So what for TSD: Fable 5.1 Max still wins the two benchmarks that map to how Nash actually works — CursorBench and Terminal-Bench — so there's no reason to move the primary coding loop. The real read is on pricing: a 2.1T model at $2/$6 sets the number clients will eventually expect to pay for token-heavy features. Price the Managed AI retainers with room for that, and keep the model behind an adapter so a swap is a config change.

◆ The buildout, announced end to end

New September 21–22

Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900, a 20GW target and a 10-trillion-parameter roadmap

At Alibaba Cloud's Apsara conference, T-Head unveiled the Zhenwu V900 accelerator — three times the performance of the M890 launched in May, with mass production and commercial release slated for Q1 2027. Alibaba says existing Zhenwu chips are already deployed by more than 650 customers across automotive, finance, energy and manufacturing. CEO Eddie Wu called it the most powerful AI chip in China.

The rest of the roadmap: Alibaba Cloud's global datacenter capacity is targeted above 20 gigawatts by 2032, Qwen 4 is in training, and the Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 series are aimed at 5 to 10 trillion parameters — two to four times today's flagship Qwen 3.8 Max at 2.4T. Wu framed the goal as longer-horizon task completion and a path toward artificial superintelligence. Tencent shipped its Hy Image 3.5 preview the same day and its Hong Kong shares closed up more than 7%.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on this week, but it's the supply side of the price curve Nash is selling into. Chinese labs releasing frontier-class weights under permissive licenses while building their own silicon means inference prices keep falling on a schedule set by someone else. Build client offers on outcomes — answered calls, booked appointments, recovered leads — rather than on tokens, so a 50% price drop shows up as margin instead of a renegotiation.

◆ Agents keep meeting security

Breaking September 21

A local zero-day in Muse for Mac reaches the user's iPhone

Patrick Wardle of the Objective-See Foundation published a proof of concept — "not-a-mused" — four days after Meta shipped the Muse Mac client. Muse carries an undocumented setting, endo_voyager_dictation_endpoint, that any process running as the current user can rewrite without additional macOS permissions. Change it and the app's dictation traffic goes to a server the attacker controls, carrying the dictated audio, the prompts, and the authentication token on the user's Muse account.

With that token, Wardle's PoC queried Muse's device inventory and invoked commands on a linked iPhone, retrieving its location and scanning nearby Bluetooth devices — all through the Muse client, invisibly. This is not remote exploitation; the attacker needs local code execution first. What it demonstrates is the multiplier: once an agent holds broad cross-device permissions, ordinary local malware inherits them.

So what for TSD: The general pattern is the lesson for anything TSD ships with tool access. Never store a long-lived credential where a config value can redirect where it gets sent; pin endpoints in code rather than in mutable settings; scope agent tokens to the single account and single action they need. If any client site runs a Claude-backed agent with write access to a booking system or inbox, check today that its endpoint and key can't be repointed by editing a stored value.

Policy September 22

Twenty countries and the EU asked the UN for an AI standards institution

Released through Finland's presidential office on September 22 as leaders gathered for the UN General Assembly, the joint statement calls for common standards, shared reporting of serious safety incidents, and an international institution that would "set standards, enable verification, and convene states when capability thresholds are crossed." Signatories include Germany, Canada, Australia, Singapore, the UAE, South Africa, Norway and Kenya, plus the European Commission.

The two countries that build the frontier models — the United States and China — did not sign. The statement names no deadlines and no enforcement mechanism, and stays open for further endorsement.

So what for TSD: No compliance work this year. Longer term, "shared incident reporting" and "verification" are the words that eventually turn into disclosure obligations for anyone deploying an AI system that talks to customers. Cheap insurance now: keep a dated log of which model version answers each client's calls and chats, and keep transcripts. If that ever becomes mandatory, TSD will already have it and competitors won't.
