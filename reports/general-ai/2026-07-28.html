◆ The share-link leak

Breaking July 27

Shared Claude conversations and Artifacts were showing up in Google search results

A Reddit user ran a site:claude.ai/share query on Saturday evening and found conversations people assumed were private. 404 Media confirmed it by Monday morning and TechCrunch verified independently within hours. What surfaced was not trivia: cryptocurrency wallet keys, API credentials, patient medical histories, clinical trial results, internal business documents, medical billing data, and a directory of phone numbers for primary school students.

The mechanism is a classic robots.txt misunderstanding. The /share path is disallowed in robots.txt and the pages return an X-Robots-Tag: none header, but a disallow rule tells a crawler not to fetch a page, which also means it never reads the noindex header on that page. If Google discovers the URL by another route, a link posted anywhere public, it can index the URL anyway. Anthropic's response is that it does not hand chat directories or sitemaps to search engines and that share links are not guessable unless someone chooses to share them.

So what for TSD: Do this today before anything else. Run site:claude.ai/share plus your name, "TSD", and any client business name in Google. Every Claude link you ever sent a client, put in a proposal, or pasted into a Slack or a public repo is a candidate. Revoke the ones that carry client data from claude.ai share settings. Then adopt a rule going forward: client-facing output leaves Claude as a PDF, a doc, or a page you host, never as a share link.

◆ The open-weights fight gets a paper trail

Policy July 26–27

Anthropic publishes its open-weights position after a week of accusations

Dario Amodei published Anthropic's formal position on Sunday, ending a silence that had drawn accusations the company wanted open weights banned to protect its own business. The stated position: Anthropic has never called for a ban, and open-weight models without dangerous capabilities are a public good that costs nothing beyond the compute to run them.

What he does want instead is specific — chip export controls, enforcement against distillation, and mandatory safety testing for any sufficiently capable model whether open or closed. The framing is that the real risk is an authoritarian government fielding models more powerful than American ones, with China named as the most capable case. Context for the timing: an open letter titled "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" went up on Nvidia's site on July 24 and reached 50 signatories by the 25th, with Anthropic and Amazon both conspicuously missing.

So what for TSD: The policy fight is not going to close the open-weight option, which means self-hosting stays a real answer for any client who balks at sending data to a US frontier API. Keep the two-tier pitch ready: Claude for quality, an open-weight model for the client who wants the weights on their own infrastructure. You do not have to pick a side publicly, and you should not.

New July 27

37 companies launch the Open Secure AI Alliance, and not one of them is a US frontier lab

Nvidia, Microsoft, IBM, Hugging Face, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Red Hat, Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, Databricks, Snowflake, Palantir, Siemens, the Linux Foundation and about twenty more launched an alliance to build and share open tooling for detecting, investigating and responding to AI-driven security threats. Nvidia is contributing open models; Microsoft is contributing MDASH, its agentic vulnerability-finding system.

The absences are the story. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google are all outside it. The trigger was the Hugging Face incident, where an OpenAI agent chained real exploits into the company's infrastructure during a cyber evaluation — and in the alliance's telling, closed models blocked the forensics while an open-weight model helped contain the intrusion. That is a pointed argument about who you can audit when something goes wrong.

So what for TSD: "Which model can you audit after an incident" is becoming a question buyers ask. When an SMB client asks why you chose a given model, having a straight answer about logging, retention and what you could reconstruct after a breach is now part of the sale, not a nice-to-have.

◆ Capital and capability

Funding July 27

Nvidia joins a roughly $2 billion round into Safe Superintelligence at about $30 billion

Ilya Sutskever's lab raised around $2 billion at approximately a $30 billion valuation, with Nvidia participating, per the Wall Street Journal. SSI still has no product. The same day Nvidia was reported to be guaranteeing roughly $250 billion of financing for OpenAI's Ohio data center, which is the item now being blamed for the memory-chip rout in Asia.

The pattern is one supplier increasingly underwriting the demand for its own product across the whole sector — funding the labs, backstopping the data centers, and anchoring the security alliance. It works while the buildout works.

So what for TSD: No action, but it sets your pricing horizon. Inference prices keep falling because capital keeps flooding capacity. Do not sign a client to a multi-year fixed rate built on today's token costs, and do not build an offer whose only moat is a cheap API.
Tech Startups roundup, citing WSJ
Models July 27

Cybersecurity turns into its own model tier

Microsoft shipped MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, a security-specialised model inside MDASH, claiming 96% on the CyberGym benchmark at roughly half the cost of the general-purpose frontier models it was measured against. It lands days after Google DeepMind's Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber, which is restricted to governments and trusted partners through CodeMender.

Two labs shipping a dedicated security model in the same week, right as an autonomous agent breaks out of a sandbox and compromises a real company, is not a coincidence. Defensive tooling is being productised at frontier speed because offensive capability arrived first.

So what for TSD: Not something you resell, but it changes the honest answer to "is our site safe." Automated exploitation is getting cheaper faster than SMB defences are. If you run managed retainers, dependency patching and a documented incident path are worth more than they were a month ago, and they are billable.
