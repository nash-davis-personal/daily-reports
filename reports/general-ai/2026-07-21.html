◆ AI security became a product category this week

New Jul 19–21, 2026

Microsoft is launching Project Perception before month-end and aiming it squarely at Anthropic's Mythos

Project Perception is a cybersecurity platform that scans enterprise code, infrastructure and deployed AI systems for vulnerabilities and then suggests or applies the patch. The architectural bet is multi-model routing: each task goes to whichever model handles it most efficiently, drawing on Microsoft's own models, OpenAI's, and Anthropic's inside one product. Cheap models take the bulk of the volume and expensive reasoning capacity is reserved for the edge cases.

That routing is the whole pitch. Anthropic's Mythos-class security offering set the capability bar; Microsoft is attacking the price of running it continuously rather than the quality of any single finding. The product sits under Hayete Gallot, the EVP for security who came back to Microsoft from Google Cloud in February. Launch is reported for the end of July, so specifics and pricing are not public yet — treat the cost claim as a claim.

So what for TSD: The thing enterprises are about to buy for six figures is a scanner that reads a codebase and reports what's broken. You can sell the SMB version of that today with Claude and a repo — a fixed-fee "AI security & dependency audit" on a client's existing site, delivered as a written report with prioritized fixes. It's the cheapest possible foot-in-the-door for a rebuild pitch, it needs zero new infrastructure, and Grant and Bishop can sell it without understanding a line of it.

New Jul 20, 2026

Neo raised $100M across a combined seed and Series A to secure AI-written applications

Announced July 20, backed by Andreessen Horowitz, Bessemer Venture Partners, Craft Ventures and Merlin Ventures. A hundred million dollars pre-product-maturity, in the same week Microsoft aims a security platform at Anthropic, tells you where the smart money thinks the bottleneck is: not writing the code, securing it.

Same day, Infinity raised $15M in seed for AI infrastructure and inference software. The AI funding tape is still heavily weighted toward infrastructure and security rather than applications.

So what for TSD: Confirmation of the wedge above. When VCs put $100M into "the AI wrote it, now who checks it," the SMB-scale version of that anxiety is a client who paid someone for a site and has no idea whether it's safe. That's a sales conversation, not a technical one.

◆ Open weights on a countdown

Breaking change Jul 24, 2026

DeepSeek retires deepseek-chat and deepseek-reasoner in three days

Both legacy model aliases become fully inaccessible after July 24, 2026 at 15:59 UTC. This is a deprecation deadline, not a launch — some coverage this week has been conflating it with a DeepSeek V4 general-availability date, and V4 still has no confirmed GA. V4 has been in preview since April 24.

So what for TSD: Grep every project, script and n8n/agent config for those two strings before Friday. If a client automation you built quietly falls back to DeepSeek for a cheap step, it fails silently on Friday afternoon and you find out from the client.
DeepSeek API docs — V4 preview & deprecations
New Jul 27, 2026

Moonshot publishes Kimi K3's full 2.8-trillion-parameter weights on Monday, under a modified MIT license

Moonshot split the K3 launch in two. The model went live July 16 through the Kimi app, Kimi Work, Kimi Code and the Kimi API. The weights — the largest open-weight release in existence — plus a technical report covering architecture, training and evaluation land July 27 under a modified MIT license. K3 has since taken the top spot on a major coding leaderboard.

The market already priced the announcement: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell into a bear market on July 17, down more than 20% from its June peak, in the worst weekly rout for chipmakers since April 2025. Moonshot has separately paused new Kimi K3 subscriptions on capacity grounds, which is its own signal about what US export controls are doing to Chinese inference supply.

So what for TSD: You will not self-host a 2.8T model, and you shouldn't try. What matters is the second-order effect: a permissively licensed frontier-class model puts a hard ceiling on what any vendor can charge per token, and that ceiling keeps falling. Price your Managed AI retainers on the value of the workflow, never on a per-message inference margin, because the margin is going to zero.

Preview Jul 19, 2026

Alibaba previewed a 2.4T multimodal Qwen3.8-Max and published nothing verifiable with it

The Qwen team previewed Qwen3.8-Max-Preview on July 19 at the World AI Conference: 2.4 trillion total parameters in a sparse mixture-of-experts design, and Qwen's first model above a trillion parameters to go multimodal. The team described it as "second only to Fable 5" among the systems it benchmarked.

That claim shipped without a benchmark table, a model card, a license, a standard per-token price, or any independent testing. Access is live through Token Plan, Qoder and QoderWork at 10% of standard pricing. Open weights are promised with no date.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on, and that's the point. When a lab announces a ranking without a benchmark table, the correct response is to keep building on the model you can actually evaluate. File it and move on.

◆ Distribution, not capability

Feature Jul 14–20, 2026

Anthropic put Claude in every US K-12 classroom it can reach, and brought Fable back to paid tiers

Claude for Teachers launched July 14: verified US K-12 educators get free premium Claude access, a teaching-skills library, and a Learning Commons connector that grounds lesson plans in academic standards for all 50 states plus curricula like OpenSciEd and Illustrative Mathematics. Terms are FERPA-aligned and were co-developed with the American Federation of Teachers. Sign-ups run through June 30, 2027. It's individual educators only for now; a schools-and-districts offering is still unannounced.

Separately, Fable 5 returned to Max and Team Premium on July 20, and the free-access window that had been open closed July 19. Claude Code's boosted limits hold through August 19.

So what for TSD: Two things. Anthropic is buying distribution with free tiers while the model tiers shuffle underneath — expect your own limits and model availability to keep moving, so never hard-code a model ID in a client deployment without a fallback. And if you want a zero-cost referral surface in Gastonia and Charlotte, teachers who just got free Claude are the single most connected group of local adults you can reach.
TechTimes — Claude for Teachers
New Jul 20, 2026

SAP closed its Prior Labs acquisition and put over €1B behind models that read tables instead of text

SAP committed more than €1 billion across four years to tabular foundation models — systems pretrained on table-shaped structured data rather than language. Prior Labs' TabPFN series was published in Nature. On spreadsheets and databases, this class of model outperforms language models and competes with traditional machine learning without the feature-engineering overhead.

So what for TSD: Worth knowing before you build the next client dashboard. When a client asks for forecasting or anomaly detection over their sales table, the answer is increasingly a tabular model, not a prompt wrapped around a CSV. Nothing to buy yet — this is a "don't reach for an LLM by reflex" note.
