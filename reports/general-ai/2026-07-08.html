◆ Anthropic goes multi-surface — and federal

Feature Jul 7, 2026

Claude Cowork lands on web and mobile — and most of it isn't coding

Anthropic rolled Claude Cowork out to web, iOS, and Android for Max users, with sessions that move across devices and keep running in the cloud after the laptop closes, pausing only to ask for a decision or permission. Chat and Cowork now share one home tab, one sidebar, one search.

The more useful disclosure was the usage data: more than 90% of what Cowork does is not software development. The biggest category (33.4%) is business-process work — pulling scattered updates into one report, building onboarding checklists, reconciling spreadsheets — followed by content and copywriting at 16.4%. That is squarely the day-to-day of the finance, HR, admin, and marketing staff at the SMBs Nash sells to.

So what for TSD: This is a productizable service, not just a model update. "We set up an AI coworker that runs your reporting, onboarding, and reconciliations" is a cleaner pitch to a 15-person business than anything about coding. Build one Cowork-driven workflow (weekly ops report, new-hire checklist) as a demo Grant/Bishop can show, and price it as a Managed AI retainer.
VentureBeat — Cowork mobile/web + usage data
New Jul 8, 2026

Claude for Government opens in beta on FedRAMP High

Anthropic opened Claude Code and Claude Cowork in public beta inside Claude for Government Desktop, delivered through a FedRAMP High authorized environment and built on the same application commercial customers use. It's the clearest sign yet that the coding-agent and knowledge-work-agent stack is being trusted with regulated, sensitive workloads.

So what for TSD: Not a direct sale — Nash isn't chasing federal contracts — but it's a credibility artifact. "The same Claude tooling we build your site and receptionist on just cleared FedRAMP High" is a real trust line for a nervous SMB owner who thinks AI is a toy. Keep it for objection handling.

◆ The price floor collapses

Shift Jul 7, 2026

Chinese models now take up to 46% of US developer usage

CNBC reported that as OpenAI and Anthropic prices climb, US companies are increasingly routing work that doesn't need the very best model to the cheapest one that's good enough — and Chinese open models are winning that trade. On OpenRouter, the four most-used models are now all Chinese, with DeepSeek still the single most popular.

So what for TSD: Nash's margin lever. For high-volume, low-stakes tasks (first-pass RAG retrieval, draft copy, classification inside Concierge/Front Desk), a cheap open model can cut inference cost hard while Claude handles the calls that matter. Worth a routing layer — but keep the client-facing default on a name they trust.
CNBC — Chinese models gain ground as costs surge
Capability Jul 2026

GLM 5.2 lands within a point of Opus 4.8 on agents — at a fifth of the cost

Z.ai's GLM 5.2 came within roughly one percentage point of Opus 4.8 on a closely watched agentic benchmark at about one-fifth the price, and it posted the fastest adoption of any model Vercel tracked in 2026 — daily token volume up ~27x and customer count up ~80x in its first full week. It's regarded as especially strong for software development and app-controlling agents.

So what for TSD: The "agent that does real work" is no longer premium-priced. If a GLM-class model runs Nash's booking or scraping agents at a fifth the cost with near-frontier quality, per-client automation margins improve fast. Prototype one internal agent on it and measure the quality gap before betting a client workflow on it.
Interconnects — GLM-5.2 is the step change for open agents

◆ The money moving behind it

New Jul 2026

Microsoft stands up "Frontier" — a $2.5B unit to deploy AI for enterprises

Microsoft launched Microsoft Frontier Company, a new consulting organization putting $2.5B and 6,000 industry and engineering experts behind one job: helping enterprises actually plan and deploy AI. The signal is that the bottleneck has moved from model quality to implementation — big companies are paying humans to make the technology land.

So what for TSD: Microsoft just validated Nash's whole business at enterprise scale — the money is in deployment, not the model. He does the same thing for the businesses too small for Microsoft to notice. Borrow the framing: "the AI is the easy part; getting it working in your shop is the job." That's the TSD pitch.
