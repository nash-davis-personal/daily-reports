◆ Models you can actually use got better and cheaper

New Jun 30, 2026

Claude Sonnet 5 ships with a native 1M-token window and promo pricing

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5 and made it the default across Claude Code, with a native 1M-token context window and promotional pricing of roughly $2 in / $10 out per million tokens running through August 31. That is frontier-level reasoning at a mid-tier price, with enough context to hold an entire client site, docs set, or codebase in a single call.

For anyone building RAG or agent features, the win is the pairing: a bigger window that used to mean the priciest models, now at a promo rate that makes it cheap to prototype and cheap to run in production for a while.

So what for TSD: This is the model to rebuild the TSD Concierge and Front Desk on right now. A 1M window lets a concierge ingest a client's whole website and document set at once, and the promo pricing gives you a hard deadline to ship a premium "Managed AI Concierge" tier before Aug 31 and lock in margin.

New Jun 29, 2026

Wix's Base44 launches its own model, Base1, to cut cost and own the stack

Base44, the vibe-coding platform Wix acquired, started rolling out Base1, a proprietary model trained on tens of millions of real app-building interactions on the platform. Founder Maor Shlomo framed it as cheaper inference than frontier models like Claude Opus, better tuned for app generation, and — the real point — defensibility from owning distribution, data, and infrastructure end to end. Base44 is at ~$150M ARR, still trailing Lovable's ~$500M.

So what for TSD: Two lessons. First, the "own your stack" argument is exactly the pitch to SMB clients — a site and AI they own beats renting a platform that can change terms on them. Second, cheaper app-gen tooling keeps lowering your build cost per client, so lean into fixed-price rebuilds.

◆ The top of the stack goes behind a gate

Breaking Jun 26–27, 2026

GPT-5.6 and Anthropic's Mythos released only to government-approved partners

OpenAI limited its new GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, Luna) to a preview for roughly 20 government-approved companies after the Commerce Department asked it to hold a public release pending a cybersecurity review. Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 are under the same regime — Anthropic had already suspended both in mid-June under an export-control directive. OpenAI said publicly it does not think this kind of case-by-case government access should become the long-term default; both labs are now working with the White House on a repeatable framework.

So what for TSD: The models you sell on — Sonnet 5, Opus, GPT-4-class — are unaffected and publicly available, so nothing you ship today breaks. What's worth watching: a future where the strongest models require "approved partner" status could fragment what's buildable, and it may become a talking point with security-conscious clients.

Trend Jun 2026

Coding agents keep eating the work: Polaris in Copilot, Devin writing its makers' code

Microsoft is replacing GPT-4 Turbo with its own in-house coding model, Project Polaris, as the default for every GitHub Copilot subscriber starting in August, running on Microsoft's Maia chips with a three-month fallback to GPT-4 Turbo. Separately, Cognition — makers of the Devin agent — closed a $1B round at a $26B valuation and says Devin now writes ~89% of the code its own engineers commit. The direction is one-way: AI-built software is becoming the norm, not the exception.

So what for TSD: Your edge isn't that you use agents — everyone will. It's that you point them at real SMB problems and deliver a finished, owned product. Package that as a fixed-price "rebuild your outdated site in a week" offer Grant and Bishop can pitch.
