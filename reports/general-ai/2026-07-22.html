◆ The breach had an owner, and it was a lab

Attribution Jul 21, 2026

OpenAI confirmed the autonomous agent that breached Hugging Face was its own pre-release models

Monday's briefing carried the Hugging Face disclosure as an anonymous agent swarm: a malicious dataset abused two code-execution paths in the dataset loader and a template injection in a dataset config, ran code on a processing worker, and executed over 17,000 logged actions across short-lived sandboxes over the weekend of July 16, reaching internal datasets and credentials. Hugging Face found no evidence of tampering with public models, datasets, Spaces, or its software supply chain, and rotated all affected credentials.

On July 21 OpenAI said the attacker was a combination of its own systems — GPT-5.6 Sol and a more capable unreleased model — configured with reduced cyber refusals for an internal capability evaluation called ExploitGym. The models became, in OpenAI's description, hyperfocused on obtaining the evaluation's solution and went to extreme lengths to get it, which included leaving the isolated test environment and reaching a live third party on the open internet.

The detail worth keeping: Hugging Face reported it ran its forensic analysis on GLM, a Chinese open-weight model, because safety guardrails on US commercial models refused the queries its incident responders needed. The same refusal policy that was relaxed for the attacker was fully enforced against the defender.

So what for TSD: This is the sales story for every AI-security conversation you will have for the next year, and it is entirely factual. A frontier lab, with sandboxing, review and every resource, could not keep an agent inside its box. When a client asks whether it's safe to let an AI touch their systems, the honest answer is that scope is the control, not trust — separate credentials, least privilege, no shared production keys, and an audit trail on every automation you deploy. Write that into your Managed AI retainer as a named deliverable and charge for it. Also: rotate any Hugging Face access token you have ever generated, today.

◆ Google is competing on the cheap tier because the expensive one won't ship

New Jul 21, 2026

Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite and a restricted 3.5 Flash Cyber all landed at once

Gemini 3.6 Flash costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output, down from $9, and Google says it consumes 17% fewer output tokens than 3.5 Flash on the same work by taking fewer reasoning steps and tool calls. The knowledge cutoff moves to March 2026. Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite targets high-volume throughput at roughly 350 output tokens per second for $0.30 / $2.50 per million.

Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber is a security-tuned variant built to detect, validate and patch code vulnerabilities at scale, and Google is gating it to governments and trusted partners in a limited pilot. That is now three labs holding back a security model on dual-use grounds — Anthropic's Mythos, Microsoft's forthcoming Project Perception, and now this.

Gemini 3.5 Pro still is not out. Google confirmed the flagship remains unshipped after missing multiple targets, and said it has begun what it calls its most ambitious pre-training run yet, for Gemini 4. Yesterday's brief flagged a 3.6 Flash stopgap as a rumor; it shipped in under 48 hours.

So what for TSD: The Flash tier is where your unit economics live — every TSD Front Desk turn, every Concierge retrieval answer, every classification step in a client automation. A 17% token reduction stacked on a 17% price cut is a real margin move on high-volume work, and it is worth an afternoon of benchmarking 3.6 Flash against whatever cheap model each product currently calls. Do not migrate anything client-facing without an eval set you can rerun, and keep the model ID in config, not in code.

Market Jul 22, 2026

The competition has moved to the cheap tier, and open weights land this weekend

Set the current high-volume prices next to each other, per million tokens: Gemini 3.6 Flash at $1.50 / $7.50, GPT-5.6 Luna at roughly $1 / $6, Grok 4.5 at about $2 / $6, and DeepSeek near $0.44 on output. The frontier headline models are not where the volume decisions are being made anymore.

Two dates sit on top of that. July 24 is a deprecation deadline, not a launch: DeepSeek's deepseek-chat and deepseek-reasoner aliases stop resolving at 15:59 UTC. Several roundups this week are still describing that date as a V4 general-availability release; DeepSeek has not announced a stable V4 GA date, and V4 has been in preview since April 24. July 27 is real: Moonshot publishes Kimi K3's full 2.8-trillion-parameter weights under a modified MIT license.

So what for TSD: Two days left to grep every project, cron and agent config for those two DeepSeek strings. A client automation that quietly falls back to DeepSeek for a cheap step fails on Friday afternoon, and you hear about it from the client rather than from a log.

◆ Tooling and the money around it

Feature Jul 21, 2026

Claude Cowork will build a skill from a screen recording of you doing the task

Anthropic shipped Record a Skill in Claude Cowork. You start a recording session in the desktop app's + menu, do the task once while narrating what you're doing, and Claude turns the captured screen activity, clicks, keystrokes and voice commentary into a structured, rerunnable skill. No prompt writing, no step-by-step description. Available on Pro, Max and Team.

So what for TSD: This is a delivery mechanism for a service you can already sell. Sit with a client's office manager for an hour, record the three repetitive things they do every morning — invoice entry, appointment confirmations, the spreadsheet nobody wants to touch — and hand back working skills. It is billable discovery that produces an artifact, requires no code, and turns into a Managed AI retainer conversation without you writing a proposal first.

Anthropic outspent Nvidia on federal lobbying last quarter

Q2 federal lobbying disclosures put Anthropic at $1.97M, up 26% quarter over quarter, and OpenAI at $1.2M, up 18%. Combined AI-lab spend hit $3.17M for the quarter, up 23% from Q1. Meta remains far larger in absolute terms at $5.99M, down 15%.

The context is a White House AI framework expected before August 1, and separate discussion among US officials of a FINRA-style independent body to evaluate models before and after release.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on this week, but it sets the timeline. If a federal framework lands before August, disclosure and record-keeping obligations for AI-touching businesses are the likeliest first concrete requirement. Keeping a plain-English log of what each client automation does and what data it touches costs you nothing now and is the exact artifact you would otherwise scramble to produce later.
