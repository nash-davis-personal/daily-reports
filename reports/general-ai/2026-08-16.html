◆ The cheap tier is where the capability landed

New Aug 13, 2026

Gemini 3.7 Flash ships for coding and agents at a 50% introductory price

Google DeepMind put Gemini 3.7 Flash into general availability three weeks after 3.6 Flash, and the jumps are on the benchmarks that matter for agent work. FrontierCode 1.1 went from 34.4% to 43.6%. DeepSWE v1.1, a long-horizon software-engineering test, went from 48.6% to 65.3%. Terminal-Bench 2.1 came in at 85.8%, close behind GPT-5.6 Terra's 87.4%, and Code Arena's web-development Elo moved from 1,538 to 1,588.

Pricing is the actual story. Google is charging $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output through December 31, 2026, then $1.50 and $7.50 from January 1. That is a two-model gap collapsing into a discount window: for four and a half months, a model that scores within two points of a frontier coding model on terminal work costs Flash money. It is live on the Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity and Android Studio, and Vercel put it on the AI Gateway at the same 50% off.

So what for TSD: This is the model to run the boring half of your products on. TSD Concierge does retrieval-and-answer, Booking Bridge does structured extraction, and neither needs Opus-class reasoning per turn. Route those to 3.7 Flash before December 31, measure the quality delta on 20 real transcripts, and if it holds you have cut the per-conversation cost of your Managed AI retainers without touching the offer. Set a calendar reminder for mid-December: the price doubles on January 1 and your margin math changes with it.

New Aug 14, 2026

Alibaba open-weights Qwen3.8-27B: Apache 2.0, 262K context, one GPU

The Qwen team released Qwen3.8-27B at 15:00 UTC on August 14 with open weights under Apache 2.0. It is a dense 27.78-billion-parameter multimodal model taking text, images and video, with a native 262,144-token context window that extends to a million. The point of the release is that it targets a single consumer GPU rather than a rack.

The agentic scores are what make it notable. Terminal-Bench 2.1 went from 63.4 to 73.0 over the prior generation, DeepSWE 1.1 from 13.3 to 42.2, OSWorld-Verified from 63.9 to 84.3, and SWE-MM from 25.7 to 38.6. On Terminal-Bench it beats Meta's 30B Muse Glimmer by more than 20 points. Reported SWE-Bench Pro is 61.7%.

So what for TSD: This is the model that makes "it runs on your own hardware, we never send your data anywhere" a truthful sentence at an SMB price point. That is a real objection you hit with medical, legal and financial clients. Do not rebuild delivery around it — your speed comes from Claude Code — but it is worth having a one-page answer ready when a prospect asks where their customer data goes, because now the answer can be "a box in your office" without you eating a quality cliff.

◆ Where the money actually is

Business Aug 14, 2026

OpenAI's enterprise revenue passed consumer, two quarters ahead of plan

CFO Sarah Friar told investors on August 14 that business revenue has overtaken ChatGPT consumer revenue. "We entered the year at 60-40, but enterprise has accelerated much faster than expected and those lines have now crossed," she said. She had previously guided to parity by the end of 2026.

The supporting numbers: roughly $40 billion in annualized recurring revenue, up from about $20 billion at the start of the year, with a 20% month-over-month jump in July alone. Revenue chief Denise Dresser departed after eight months and was replaced by Dali Rajic.

So what for TSD: The company with the best consumer AI brand in the world makes most of its money selling to businesses, and it got there by accelerating past its own forecast. That is your market thesis with a $40 billion receipt attached. Give Grant and Bishop this one line for cold calls and the top of proposals: the AI money is being made inside businesses, not by consumers playing with chatbots, and the businesses buying it are not tech companies.
CNBC (Aug 14) · Unite.AI
Product Aug 15, 2026

OpenAI tells European free-tier users that ads arrive later this month

OpenAI Ireland sent a notice on August 15 to Free and Go plan users across the European Economic Area and Switzerland saying ads are coming to ChatGPT later in August. Paid tiers — Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu — stay ad-free. Ads appear as a sponsored section below the model's main answer, keyed to what the user asked about.

Targeting starts contextual only, with personalized advertising requiring opt-in consent, which is the GDPR constraint. The UK has had ChatGPT ads for a while; France, Germany and Ireland were announced as future markets without dates. The notice also updated OpenAI's European privacy disclosures to cover ad personalization, mirroring an April 30 change on the US side.

So what for TSD: Sponsored placements inside assistant answers are the next search-result real estate, and your SMB clients will eventually ask how to appear in them. Nothing to sell today. What to do today is keep instrumenting how often client sites get cited by assistants, because when the paid version of that surface opens up, the shop that already has the organic baseline is the one that gets the budget.

◆ Models that find bugs in your tools

Security Aug 14–15, 2026

GLM-5.3 found a potentially serious vulnerability in Cursor

Yesterday's briefing covered GLM-5.3's release and its safety gating. The follow-on is more concrete: given a complex reverse-engineering task, the model surfaced what security researcher Joshua Saxe described as a potentially serious vulnerability in Cursor. It was disclosed privately, and the Cursor team is working with Z.ai on a fix with details to follow once users are protected.

Z.ai says it added vulnerability-discovery environments to the post-training mix expecting the model to get better at finding flaws, and instead watched capability progress further down the exploitation chain. CyberGym went from 77.2% on GLM-5.2 to 84.5%. ExploitBench, which measures reasoning through and executing an exploit, roughly doubled from 24.4% to 54.4%.

So what for TSD: Update Cursor when the patch lands — that is the two-minute action item. The larger point: models that find exploitable bugs in developer tooling are now open-weighted, so the window between a flaw existing in something you ship on and someone finding it is closing. Pin your dependencies, keep an eye on the advisory feeds for your stack, and stop treating "nobody would bother looking at a small agency's site" as a security posture.
