◆ China shipped its biggest model, and you can already call it

New August 3

Qwen3.8-Max went wide at 2.4 trillion parameters, with open weights promised next week

Alibaba made Qwen3.8-Max broadly available today. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 2.4 trillion total parameters that activates about 95 billion per request, handles text, images, video and documents, and takes up to a million tokens of context. Alibaba is pitching it at long-horizon autonomy — the company's framing is tasks that run over days, like reproducing a research paper or working through a chip design.

The part that matters more than the parameter count: Alibaba says open weights follow next week. A 2.4T MoE with 95B active is not something a solo builder self-hosts, but open weights mean a dozen inference providers will be serving it cheaply within days of release. The model was previewed July 19, shortly after Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 open-weight launch, so the two biggest open-weight releases of the year are now landing two weeks apart.

So what for TSD: Wait for the open-weight drop before rebuilding anything. The realistic play is price arbitrage on Concierge and the briefing engine: once third-party providers are serving Qwen3.8-Max, benchmark it against your current summarization and RAG calls on a real client corpus and keep whichever wins on cost per acceptable answer. Do that as a measured swap on one workload, not a stack migration.

◆ The models started doing the mathematics, and the credit question arrived with them

Breaking August 1–2

OpenAI announced Astra by publishing ten open problems it had already solved

OpenAI published ten results in mathematics and theoretical computer science produced by an internal version of Astra, the model it calls its next major release. The problems span group theory, von Neumann algebras, high-dimensional geometry, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography and extremal combinatorics, and each had been open at least a decade. One result constructs a non-sofic group, a long-standing question in group theory.

The verification story is what separates this from previous claims. Every result ships with a Lean 4 certificate on GitHub under Apache 2.0, and the repository reports a "sorry" count of zero, meaning no step in any formalized proof was left unproven. A machine can check the whole thing without trusting OpenAI. Fields medalist Timothy Gowers said he would recommend one of the proofs to a top journal without hesitation. The compute cost to find all ten was around $2,000 at current API rates.

So what for TSD: Steal the format. The reason this announcement landed is that the claim came with an artifact anyone can verify independently. Every TSD proposal should carry the same shape — a working demo on the client's own content or phone number instead of a capability list. "Here is the thing, check it yourself" beat a benchmark table this weekend and it beats one in a sales meeting.

New August 3

Two research groups solved the same quantum-crypto problem with the same model, hours apart

Seyoon Ragavan, an MIT PhD student, and University of California cryptographers Prabhanjan Ananth and Amit Sahai independently pointed GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra at the unclonable-encryption problem, open for about six years. Both got there. Both credited the model with the core construction and proof ideas, both had human experts refine and verify the result, and both submitted preprints to arXiv on the same day, three hours apart.

Neither team knew about the other. The interesting question is no longer whether the model can contribute a real idea — it is what "independent discovery" and priority mean when the bottleneck stops being insight and becomes who queued the job first.

So what for TSD: The moat is not the idea any more. If a frontier model will hand the same construction to anyone who asks, the durable advantage is distribution, relationships and speed to a shipped thing. That argues for pointing Grant and Bishop at more Charlotte SMBs now rather than polishing another product spec.

◆ Agent plumbing

Research August 2

Meta's fix for agents that forget the task is a second agent whose only job is remembering

Meta AI researchers published work on a proactive memory agent that runs alongside the acting agent and selectively injects short, memory-grounded reminders into its context. The problem it targets is specific: on long tasks, the decision-relevant state — requirements, environment facts, failed attempts, open subgoals — gets scattered across a growing trajectory and eventually buried or pushed past the context window, so it stops influencing decisions exactly when it matters.

The design choice worth noting is that the memory agent pushes rather than waits. Most memory systems retrieve on request, which fails when the acting agent has already forgotten there was something to ask about.

So what for TSD: This is the failure mode in Front Desk on long calls — twelve turns in, the assistant has lost the constraint the caller gave in turn two. You do not need Meta's architecture to copy the idea: keep a short structured state object (name, service, constraints, quoted price) updated every turn and re-inject it verbatim into each prompt. It is an afternoon of work on an existing product.
