◆ The agent-platform wars go enterprise

New Jul 22, 2026

OpenAI launches Presence for "trusted" enterprise agents

OpenAI introduced Presence, a fully managed platform for building, governing and improving production voice and chat agents. Agents answer questions, use company systems, take approved actions and escalate to humans, all under company-defined policies, guardrails and escalation rules. It is sold through Forward Deployed Engineers and select systems integrators — not self-serve — and is in limited general availability for eligible enterprises. OpenAI says its own implementation already resolves 75% of inbound English phone support without a human.

So what for TSD: This validates the exact category TSD Front Desk sits in — an AI that answers the phone and takes action. OpenAI is aiming at large enterprises with white-glove deployment; it will never hand-hold a Gastonia HVAC shop. That unserved local SMB is Nash's lane. Use "even OpenAI is betting on this" as social proof in the pitch.

◆ Cheaper closed models, faster open ones

Release Jul 21, 2026

Google ships three Gemini Flash models; 3.5 Pro still MIA

Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite and a governments-only 3.5 Flash Cyber. Gemini 3.6 Flash runs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 output (down from $9), uses roughly 17% fewer output tokens than the prior Flash, and extends its knowledge cutoff to March 2026. The flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro remains unreleased after repeated delays, and Google says it has begun its "most ambitious pretraining run yet" for Gemini 4.

So what for TSD: Cheaper, fresher Flash is a direct cost cut for RAG products like TSD Concierge. Lower per-token cost plus 17% fewer output tokens means fatter margins or a cheaper entry tier to win price-sensitive SMBs. Worth benchmarking Concierge against 3.6 Flash this week.

Open weights Jul 24–27, 2026

Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 keep the open frontier moving

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 — a 2.8-trillion-parameter model with a 1M-token context window and native vision — has its weights promised for July 27; Moonshot suspended new K3 subscriptions over capacity. DeepSeek's V4 stable release is slated for July 24. The open-weight tier keeps closing the gap with frontier closed models on capability and context.

So what for TSD: Optionality. As these weights land, self-hosted or ultra-cheap inference becomes viable for pieces of the stack where privacy or COGS matter, without locking every client into one closed vendor's pricing.

◆ Tooling & money

Tooling Jul 22, 2026

Claude Cowork adds screen-recording-to-skills

Anthropic's Claude Cowork gained a feature that converts a screen recording of a workflow into a reusable skill — record yourself doing a task once, get an automation you can re-run.

So what for TSD: Nash already lives in the Claude ecosystem. Record a repetitive client task, get a reusable skill, and package it as a deliverable — a clean way to turn a one-off automation into a productized setup fee.

Capital keeps pouring into agents and infra

Nous Research raised a $75M Series B at a $1.5B valuation (Robot Ventures leading); Bespoke Labs raised $31.75M Series A for agent training. Infra player Fireworks AI is reported raising $1.5B. Global venture capital hit roughly $510B in H1 2026, and AI-agent startups pulled in about $1.8B across 12+ deals in July, with developer tooling a leading category.

So what for TSD: The money is validating agents and dev tooling as the durable layer — the same layer Nash sells from. It also means better, cheaper building blocks keep shipping; ride them rather than compete with them.
