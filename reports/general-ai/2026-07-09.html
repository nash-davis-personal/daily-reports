◆ OpenAI ships GPT-5.6

New Jul 9, 2026

Sol, Terra, Luna go public — with a pricing ladder aimed at real workloads

OpenAI launched GPT-5.6 as a three-model family: Sol (the frontier variant, tuned for biology, chemistry, and cybersecurity, with a stronger Sol Ultra), Terra (GPT-5.5-competitive at roughly half the cost), and Luna (smallest and fastest). Per-million-token pricing is Sol $5/$30, Terra $2.50/$15, and Luna $1/$6. On Terminal-Bench 2.1, Sol Ultra leads at 91.9% and plain Sol at 88.8%, ahead of GPT-5.5 (88.0%) and Anthropic's Opus 4.8 (78.9%).

The release also ships more predictable prompt caching — explicit cache breakpoints and a 30-minute minimum cache life — with reads still getting the 90% discount and writes billed at 1.25x the uncached input rate.

So what for TSD: Terra is the interesting one. A GPT-5.5-class model at $2.50/$15 is a real "cheap but good enough" tier for the high-volume, low-stakes calls inside Concierge and Front Desk — first-pass retrieval, draft copy, classification — while a top model handles the ones that matter. The caching changes directly cut cost on repeat-context flows like RAG. Treat this as a margin lever, and keep the client-facing default on a name they already trust.

Context Jul 9, 2026

A 12-day government-gated preview cleared the way

GPT-5.6 became broadly available only after roughly 12 days inside a voluntary, government-coordinated safety preview with a set of vetted partner organizations — the result of a June executive order asking major developers to submit leading models for government evaluation. OpenAI said the gated approach should not become "the long-term default," and did not detail whether weights or architecture changed before launch.

So what for TSD: Not a sale, but a credibility artifact. For a nervous SMB owner who thinks AI is a toy, "the newest models now go through a government safety review before public release" is a real trust line. Keep it in the objection-handling kit alongside the FedRAMP note from yesterday's briefing.

◆ The field goes three-wide

New Jul 9, 2026

Grok 4.5 goes public hours before GPT-5.6

SpaceXAI released Grok 4.5 to the public on July 9, a 1.5-trillion-parameter model on its V9 foundation with supplemental training data from Cursor. Elon Musk framed it as an "Opus-class" model — roughly comparable to Anthropic's previous-generation Opus 4.7, but faster and cheaper — though SpaceXAI has published no independent benchmark evidence for the claim. Separately, SpaceX holds an option to acquire Cursor outright for $60B this year.

So what for TSD: Another cheap, capable option to route non-critical work to — but the "Opus-class" label is a marketing claim until independent numbers land. Don't wire a client workflow to it on the press release; prototype on an internal agent, measure the quality gap, then decide.
explainX — Grok 4.5 public launch
Shift Jul 9, 2026

First day three labs have new flagships public at once

With Grok 4.5 and GPT-5.6 both shipping July 9 and Anthropic's Sonnet 5 already the default for all users (with Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 available), this is the first day three frontier labs have brand-new flagship models publicly available simultaneously. Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro remains the lone anticipated model still in preview.

So what for TSD: Model choice is now a commodity menu that turns over monthly. The durable edge is not picking "the best model" — it's building behind a thin abstraction so a client's Concierge or Front Desk can swap providers in an afternoon when the price/quality math shifts. Sell the integration and the ownership, not the model.
