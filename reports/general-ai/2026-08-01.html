◆ A model that produces new mathematics

Breaking August 1

An internal Astra model closed ten long-open problems, with Lean certificates for all of them

OpenAI published ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science produced by an internal version of Astra, the model family it describes as its next major one. The problems had resisted human progress for at least a decade each, most of them far longer. The list includes improved asymptotic upper bounds on high-dimensional sphere-packing density reaching the Cohn–Elkies threshold, a construction of a non-sofic group, and a counterexample to Connes's rigidity conjecture, plus results in coding theory, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography and extremal combinatorics.

The verification story matters more than the headline. Each result was formalized in Lean 4 and published as a machine-checkable certificate in a public repository, alongside a walkthrough of the model's reasoning. Sébastien Bubeck put the framing bluntly on X, and University of Manchester mathematician Thomas Bloom called the results big news. The honest caveats came from OpenAI itself: humans worked with the model to formalize the proofs and write the papers, no Millennium Prize problem fell, and researcher Noam Brown noted they did not spend much compute per problem, which cuts both ways. Astra is built to coordinate multiple agents over hours or days, and OpenAI names compounding errors over long runs and accuracy decay as context grows as the open weaknesses.

So what for TSD: The transferable idea is the certificate, not the mathematics. Astra's results are credible because a compiler checks them, not because a lab says they are good. Build that pattern into what you sell: every agent output that matters should come with a cheap, automatic verifier — a schema validation, a test run, a database constraint, a second model checking the first against source text. That is the difference between "the AI wrote it" and "the AI wrote it and here is proof it is right," and it is the thing an SMB owner will actually pay a retainer for.

Policy August 1

The federal frontier-model review framework hit its build deadline today

Executive Order 14409, signed June 2, gave agencies 60 days to produce two things, and that clock ran out August 1. The first is a classified benchmarking process, led by the NSA with CISA and Treasury, that determines which systems count as "covered frontier models" — the reporting centers on a model's ability to find and exploit software vulnerabilities on its own. The second is a voluntary framework through which developers engage the government before releasing a covered model, giving agencies roughly a 30-day window to examine a system before it reaches trusted partners such as critical-infrastructure operators.

Two details are worth holding onto. The criteria are classified and stay classified, so no one outside the process will know what capability level trips the designation. And "voluntary" is doing real work in that sentence: OpenAI has said Astra will go through the review, and both OpenAI and Anthropic have publicly backed the approach, which is how a voluntary framework becomes the industry default without a statute.

So what for TSD: No compliance burden lands on you — this governs frontier labs, not people building on their APIs. The practical effect is timing. A pre-release government window means the gap between "a lab has a capability" and "you can call it from an API" gets longer and less predictable, so stop building client roadmaps that assume the next model ships on the old cadence. Quote work against what is generally available today.

◆ The money behind the models

Deal July 31

Amazon completed its $50 billion OpenAI investment and took roughly 5%

Amazon disclosed in an SEC filing that it finished the $50 billion investment first announced in February, unlocking the remaining $35 billion tranche early after OpenAI hit undisclosed performance milestones. The money went in across the year — $15 billion in Q1, $13.7 billion in Q2, and $21.3 billion after June 30 — and leaves Amazon holding close to 5% of OpenAI ahead of a public listing OpenAI confidentially filed for in June.

The compute side of the deal is the larger number. OpenAI and Amazon expanded their cloud arrangement by an additional $100 billion over eight years, and OpenAI committed to consuming 2 gigawatts of AWS Trainium capacity, including Trainium4 chips expected in 2027. Amazon is now a significant shareholder in both OpenAI and Anthropic.

So what for TSD: Watch the Trainium commitment rather than the equity. Two gigawatts of custom silicon under an OpenAI workload is a bet on driving inference cost down, which is the same trend that produced last week's 80% cut on the cheap GPT-5.6 tier. Your token line stays on a downward path, so price retainers on the value of the outcome and let falling inference cost widen the margin instead of pricing at cost-plus and handing the savings back.

Business model July 31

China's AI companies are ending the free-for-everyone era and moving to tiered enterprise pricing

Semafor reported that Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance are pushing into business AI in search of revenue that actually arrives. ByteDance restructured to pull its workplace tool closer to the Doubao chatbot and now runs a tiered pricing system that looks a lot like the Silicon Valley one. China Telecom launched separate packages for casual users, developers and businesses, calling token operations its central strategic focus.

The context is that the Chinese AI race spent years buying market share with free access, shopping vouchers and cash giveaways, and the companies still are not profitable. The pivot to enterprise is what happens when that stops working.

So what for TSD: Two labs on two continents concluding the same thing — consumer AI subsidies do not pay, business workflows do — is the strongest available signal that your positioning is right. You sell AI that does a specific job for a business with a number attached. Keep doing that. It also means the cheap Chinese open models you might lean on for non-critical tasks will keep getting maintained, because someone now has a revenue reason to maintain them.

◆ Models that move

Model July 30

Gemini Robotics 2 controls a humanoid from feet to fingertips

Google DeepMind announced Gemini Robotics 2, a vision-language-action model that drives an entire humanoid robot — walking, balancing, crouching, and manipulating objects with five-fingered hands. The 2025 model handled the upper body only. The system pairs a high-level reasoner, Gemini Robotics ER 2, that decomposes multi-step tasks with action models that execute whole-body movement, demonstrated on Apptronik's Apollo 2 with Sharpa hands. DeepMind also showed coordinated work across different robot platforms.

The benchmark numbers are the useful part. Unscrewing a light bulb, which needs the whole body to cooperate, hit 92% success. Across the full task set, success ranged from 32% to 92%. Access is rolling out to early partners and developers, and DeepMind is explicit that delicate manipulation remains a long-term goal.

So what for TSD: Nothing to sell here this year, and that is the point — a 32% floor is not a product. Track it as the clock on your client base. The trades and service businesses you sell websites and phone agents to are the last place physical automation lands, which means your software-side moat there is good for several more years. Revisit when the low end of that range is above 90%.
