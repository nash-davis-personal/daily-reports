◆ Your stack

Funding + launch Oct 2

Supabase raised $150M, agreed to buy Turso and launched Supabase Compute for long-running agents

Singapore's GIC led the $150 million round, joined by Alphabet's CapitalG, IronArc and SquarePeg. It comes four months after a $500 million Series F; no new valuation was stated. Supabase also agreed to acquire Turso, the SQLite-compatible database built for spinning up huge numbers of small, isolated databases, for an undisclosed sum. Turso founder Glauber Costa becomes Head of Agentic Services. Both companies say nothing changes for existing users: Supabase stays on Postgres, Turso stays open source, and Turso users get a migration path to Postgres when they outgrow SQLite.

The third piece is Supabase Compute, hosted sandboxes for agents that run for long stretches. No pricing has been published in the coverage so far. Supabase says it now adds more than 1 million users and 4 million databases a month, and that agents or AI tools create 70% of those new databases. Those are company figures with no stated counting method.

So what for TSD: Your backend vendor just raised again and is building agent hosting next to your data, so Supabase is a safe bet to keep standardizing on. Watch for Compute pricing. If it is reasonable, it could run Front Desk's after-call follow-ups and Booking Bridge reminder jobs in the same project as the client's data, which avoids Vercel function timeouts. Turso's one-database-per-agent model also fits giving each client's Concierge its own isolated store. Wait for the docs before moving anything.

◆ The security cost of agents

Platform Oct 2

Apple is tightening macOS Full Disk Access, citing AI agents

Full Disk Access lets a Mac app read files, mail, messages and browsing history. Apple says "some developers are using Full Disk Access in ways that could put users at risk, exposing everything on their systems" without users' full understanding, and that agents will increase that risk substantially. New controls will make sure people who "genuinely wish to grant an app this extraordinary level of access" can do so only through "very explicit user action." Apple gave no macOS version or date.

The statement follows an Inc. columnist's report that Meta's Muse agent knew the contents of his private messages without his permission, which Meta disputes, and a Wired report on a flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app that could have exposed user data.

So what for TSD: Your own coding agents and Claude Code probably run with broad disk access on your Mac. Expect an extra permission prompt after a macOS update, and use the change as a reason to check which apps have Full Disk Access today. For clients, this is the argument for the narrow-tools design you already use: an agent that can only call the tools you wired up won't be on the receiving end of a platform crackdown.

Report Oct 1 – 2

Microsoft's Digital Defense Report: AI has handed attackers the near-term advantage

The 2026 report covers July 2025 through June 2026 and draws on what Microsoft says are 165 trillion security signals a day. Its headline finding is that the median time from a vulnerability being found in the wild to being weaponized has fallen below 24 hours. Finding and exploiting bugs used to take experts; in many cases, Microsoft says, it now comes down to "simply writing a prompt." Tracked CVEs for 2026 are on pace for a record of about 72,000. Phishing was the way in for 23% of the intrusions Microsoft investigated, and Chinese, Russian and North Korean state-linked groups already use AI in live operations.

So what for TSD: A one-day exploit window means "we patch monthly" no longer protects a client site. Turn on Dependabot or Renovate auto-PRs for every client repo and let Vercel preview deploys catch breakage. Then sell that as a line item in the care plan. Quoting this report gives the price a reason.

Patch Oct 2

GitLab and AWS patched critical flaws in their AI agent platforms

GitLab fixed CVE-2026-90970, rated 9.9, in its self-hosted AI Gateway. A signed-in user with Duo Agent Platform access could craft a custom flow that escapes the prompt-template sandbox and runs commands on the gateway host. Fixed versions are 19.2.4, 19.3.2 and 19.4.1; CISA listed exploitation as "none" on Oct. 2.

AWS disclosed four flaws in its open-source Loom agent orchestration platform and SageMaker Unified Studio. The worst, CVE-2026-103956, is rated 10.0: on a Loom deployment without an identity provider configured, any network client could take full admin control of the agent control plane, register malicious tool servers and pull stored integration credentials. AWS recommends upgrading to Loom 1.7.0, rotating OAuth2 client secrets and checking CloudTrail.

So what for TSD: You don't run either product, so there is nothing to patch. The lesson carries over: the dangerous parts of an agent system are the template layer and the tool registry. Never let client-supplied text reach a template engine unescaped, and keep the list of tools Front Desk and Concierge can call hard-coded on the server.

◆ Labs and hardware

People Oct 1 – 3

OpenAI fired three staff over sharing information with an outside evaluator, and a safety lead reportedly resigned

OpenAI said it "parted ways with three individuals for violating our policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information." The Register describes them as two safety researchers and a program manager who shared sensitive information with an outside organization that evaluates AI systems. OpenAI cited "other misconduct beyond that disclosure" without details and said the employees "were not dismissed for raising AI safety concerns."

Business Insider then reported that David Robinson, who led safety transparency work including system cards, has resigned. Secondary coverage counts it as at least the sixth or seventh senior safety departure in about two years; that tally is not from OpenAI. This lands one day after California's attorney general subpoenaed OpenAI over its agent incidents.

So what for TSD: It doesn't affect anything you build. It does add to the story clients are reading about OpenAI this week, and it supports defaulting to Claude in proposals while keeping GPT-6.1 Sol as the tested fallback.

Hardware Oct 2

Nvidia launched a 64GB DGX Spark at $4,999 and raised the 128GB model to $6,950

The new 64GB desktop runs models up to about 100 billion parameters and is pitched at local agent development. Two units can be linked. Acer, ASUS and other partners start selling it Oct. 23. The 128GB model launched at $3,999, rose to $4,699 earlier this year and is now $6,950, close to 75% above launch. Nvidia blames tight memory supply. The new half-memory model costs more than the original 128GB box did a year ago.

So what for TSD: Skip it. Local inference hardware is getting more expensive while API prices keep falling, so the math for a solo studio still favors paying per token. If a client asks for "private AI on our own box," this is your price anchor: $5,000 or more before any work starts.
