◆ The money moved into applied voice AI

New Jun 24, 2026

Assort Health hits a $1.2B valuation on AI for the medical front desk

Assort Health closed a $120M Series C led by Menlo Ventures, vaulting the healthcare-voice-AI company to unicorn status. Its product is, in plain terms, an AI agent that answers clinic phones, books and reschedules patients, and handles the administrative grind of patient access. The round is one of the largest applied-AI raises of the month and lands while no big lab has shipped a new frontier model.

The signal is the category, not the company: investors are now paying unicorn prices for "AI that runs a specific industry's phone line." That is the exact shape of product a small studio can build on existing voice models without training anything.

So what for TSD: This is the clearest validation yet for TSD Front Desk. A $1.2B raise for a clinic phone agent means the buyer skepticism is fading — the market believes. Repackage Front Desk for one Charlotte vertical (dental, vet, or med-spa), put a clinic-specific demo line in front of Grant, and ride the category instead of explaining it.

New Jun 24, 2026

Coval raises $28M to test voice agents before they go live

Coval, which builds testing and evaluation tooling for voice and chat agents, closed a $28M Series A (Norwest, Base10 Partners, Twilio Ventures, Y Combinator, Swift Ventures), bringing its total to $31M. The thesis: as more businesses ship phone agents, somebody has to prove the agent behaves before it talks to a customer.

So what for TSD: Two voice-AI rounds in one day is a trend, not a coincidence. Steal Coval's discipline even if you don't buy it — a written test script of 15–20 tricky caller turns (wrong number, angry customer, after-hours, "is the doctor in") run before every Front Desk go-live becomes a selling point and a churn-killer.

◆ The frontier is still frozen

Breaking Day 14

Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 stay offline under the export-control ban

Anthropic's two most capable models remain suspended for all users, two weeks into the June 12 US government export-control directive. Because access can't be gated by nationality in real time, the simplest compliance move was to pull both models entirely. Reporting points to a July 8 ID-verification step and an August 1 governance deadline as possible restoration triggers, but nothing is confirmed.

So what for TSD: Keep all TSD client work on Opus 4.8 and Sonnet 4.6 — they're stable and unaffected. Don't design anything around Fable/Mythos availability until a real restoration date lands; the ceiling you can ship on hasn't changed in two weeks.

Watch Jun 26, 2026

Google has four days to make its own Gemini 3.5 Pro deadline

At I/O on May 19, Sundar Pichai committed to general availability of Gemini 3.5 Pro in June. It's still preview-only with four days left in the month. Prediction markets put the June miss as the likely outcome. Meanwhile ByteDance shipped Seed 2.1 Pro and Turbo on June 24 — the contrast between "promised and not shipped" and "quietly shipped" is the story of the month.

So what for TSD: Don't pin a client roadmap to an unreleased model. The pattern this quarter is slips and bans, so build on what's live today and treat any new flagship as upside, not a dependency.
