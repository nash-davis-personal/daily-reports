◆ The Fable 5 blackout and its fallout

Breaking Offline since Jun 12 · still down Jun 15

The most capable public model on earth is still switched off for everyone

Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5 — the first publicly available model in its Mythos class — on June 9, free to Pro/Max/Team/Enterprise users through June 22. On June 12 the US Commerce Department ordered it suspended under an export-control restriction barring access "by any foreign national." Because Anthropic can't reliably separate foreign nationals from other users in real time, it disabled Fable 5 and its restricted twin Mythos 5 for every customer worldwide. As of June 15 both remain down with no restoration date, and refunds are being processed.

This is the first time an export-control directive has pulled a live, publicly deployed AI model. Anthropic disagrees the underlying jailbreak risk is significant — it says the same weakness is common industry-wide — but complied while it works to restore access.

So what for TSD: This is concentration risk made real. If TSD Front Desk or Concierge calls a single model endpoint, one order or outage can take a client's AI dark with zero notice. Add provider fallback (Claude → an OpenAI/Gemini backup) with automatic failover, and make "always-on" a selling point. Note for your own builds: Opus 4.8 stayed up and is unaffected.

New Jun 10

The leaked 120K-char system prompt is a free agent-design reference

Within roughly a day of launch, jailbreak researcher Pliny the Liberator published what he claims is the full Fable 5 system prompt — all 120,040 characters — to X and GitHub. Anthropic hasn't confirmed it, and no one outside the company can verify it's complete or unmodified, so treat it as unofficial. But analysts are reading it as a detailed look at how a frontier lab instructs a model for long-running, multi-hour agent work: tool use, memory, refusal handling, and self-correction all spelled out in natural language rather than hard-coded logic.

So what for TSD: Read it as a reference, not gospel. The structure — how it scopes tools, handles ambiguity, and recovers from failure — is directly transferable to how you write system prompts for your own Concierge and receptionist agents. Don't copy proprietary text; do steal the architecture.

◆ Safety, scale, and the money behind it

Signal Jun 4

Anthropic asks for a coordinated global pause — and shows why

Anthropic's "When AI Builds Itself" (authored by Marina Favaro and Jack Clark) argues that systems are nearing the ability to recursively improve themselves faster than humans can oversee, and proposes a coordinated global pause — explicitly not a unilateral halt, since it would need multiple frontier labs across the US and China to agree under verifiable rules. The load-bearing data point: more than 80% of code merged into Anthropic's production systems as of May 2026 was written by Claude, and its engineers now ship roughly 8× more code per quarter than in 2021–25.

So what for TSD: The same trajectory that makes your AI offers valuable is the one regulators may try to slow. Two practical reads: (1) capability is compounding fast, so don't over-engineer around today's model limits — they'll move; (2) access can get politically fragile (see Fable 5), so build for model portability.

Context Jun 2026

Goldman pegs AI capex at $7.6T — and it's now a debt story

Goldman Sachs estimates cumulative AI infrastructure investment of about $7.6 trillion from 2026 to 2031 — roughly a quarter of annual US GDP — across compute, data centers, and power. The financing is shifting from cash to credit: Nvidia is in market this week with its first corporate bond sale since 2021, targeting $20B and already drawing ~$85B in orders (see today's Technology brief).

So what for TSD: This much committed capacity means inference keeps getting cheaper per token over time. When you price fixed-fee AI features for clients, budget for falling unit costs — you can promise more usage at the same price and let margin improve as token costs drop.
