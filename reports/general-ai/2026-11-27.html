◆ Research claims

Claim Nov 23

Anthropic and OpenAI each post a proof that VP ≠ VNP, and neither has been checked by a person

VP versus VNP is the algebraic cousin of P versus NP, open since Leslie Valiant posed it in 1979. Anthropic's GitHub repository formal-math/vp-vnp holds a 70-page paper credited to Claude as sole author and a Lean 4 formalization spread over 324 files. OpenAI published its own paper on the same problem later that day; coverage says the two arguments differ in important ways.

The repository itself says no human mathematician has checked the proof. Lean confirms the formal steps, but whether the formal statement says what the paper claims was judged only by AI readers. That gap is exactly where past "AI solved it" claims have fallen apart.

So what for TSD: Keep it out of sales copy until mathematicians weigh in. The part worth stealing is the method: make agents hand back something a machine can check (tests, type checks, a schema) instead of prose you have to trust. That is how TSD should frame AI work for skeptical clients.

◆ Builder tools

Beta Nov 24

Claude can install an artifact as a desktop app on Mac and Windows

Anthropic is rolling out "Install as app" in beta in the Claude desktop apps. An artifact (the small interactive tools Claude builds in chat) can be installed, run and shared as its own desktop app. Coverage so far is light on limits such as offline use, data storage and who can open a shared app, so check those before promising anything.

So what for TSD: A cheap new deliverable: a quote calculator, an intake checklist or a price lookup built as an artifact and installed on the client's front-desk machine. Test one internally first and write down what breaks before Kirk quotes it.

Open model Nov 27

Nvidia publishes training data and a fine-tuning recipe for Nemotron 3.5 Parse

Nemotron 3.5 Parse turns page images into structured text with layout classes, bounding boxes and reading order. Nvidia lists it at about 631 million parameters, a ParseBench score of 69.05 against 64.46 for Parse 2.0, and 14.21 pages per second in BF16, all its own numbers. A new Hugging Face post adds synthetic chart and table datasets, a fine-tuning recipe and a speculative-decoding helper.

So what for TSD: Concierge lives or dies on clean source documents. A small parser that reads tables and reading order could take the manual cleanup out of loading a client's PDF menus, price sheets and spec sheets. Worth one afternoon test on the import retail client's catalog PDFs.
