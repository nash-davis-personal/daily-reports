Ramp's spend index has Opus 4.8 at 28% of AI card spend while Opus 5 managed 3.5% in week one, and the FT reports average prices paid for Anthropic models are down about 25% since mid-July. Routing cheap work to cheap models is now standard enterprise practice.

◆ The floor under token prices keeps dropping

Pricing Aug 23–25, 2026

OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol by up to a third and named its competitors

Short-context input goes from $5 to $4 per million tokens, a 20% cut. Output goes from $30 to $20, a 33% cut. Cached input drops from $0.50 to $0.40. OpenAI says the rates hold at least through November 21, and the reduction applies across the API, Codex credits and ChatGPT Work. Amazon passed the same cut through to Bedrock.

What makes this one worth reading is the framing. OpenAI attributed the move to competition from Anthropic and from low-cost Chinese open models rather than dressing it as a promotion. That is the third price move on this model family in four weeks, after Luna fell 80% and Terra fell 20% in July.

So what for TSD: Re-run the unit economics on Front Desk and Concierge this week with the new numbers, because a 33% cut on output tokens lands directly on your per-conversation cost and every retainer you have quoted this year was priced above it. Then decide deliberately whether that margin stays with you or becomes a price cut you lead with on the next pitch. The November 21 date matters: quote annual retainers on the old price and treat the gap as buffer, since nothing says these rates survive past the guarantee.

Market Aug 23–24, 2026

Anthropic's revenue is climbing while its flagship struggles to hold share

The FT reported that Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $65 billion in July, up from $47 billion in May, and that it has told investors it has 6,000 customers spending $100,000 or more a year. The company is growing quickly by any normal measure.

The uncomfortable number sits underneath that. Ramp's AI spend index for July shows Opus 4.8 holding 28% of card spend while the newer, better Opus 5 took 3.5% in its first week, and average prices paid across Anthropic models are down roughly 25% since mid-July. Buyers route simple work to cheap models and reserve the expensive one for jobs that genuinely need it. Anthropic already responded to this once, cutting Opus 5 to $5 and $25 per million tokens at its July 24 launch, half its predecessor's price.

So what for TSD: Model routing is now the default behaviour of the customers you are copying, so build it into your products rather than picking one model per app. Classify the request first, send intent detection, FAQ lookup and appointment slotting to a cheap model, and escalate only the genuinely hard turns to the expensive one. On a receptionist product where most turns are trivial, that is the difference between a fat retainer margin and a thin one, and it is a week of work you can do once and reuse across every client.

◆ Where the money went this week

Rumour Aug 24, 2026

Hugging Face is reportedly evaluating bids at $13 billion or more

Business Insider reported over the weekend that Hugging Face has been consulting banks to assess acquisition offers starting around $13 billion. No acquirer has been identified and no deal has been reached. The company last raised in 2023 at $4.5 billion and turned down a $500 million Nvidia investment earlier this year.

CEO Clem Delangue's public position cuts against a sale. He has stressed the company's long-term responsibility to its community, said it prioritises sustainability over short-term profit, and said Hugging Face is close to profitability and only recently started spending its 2023 raise. Fielding offers and accepting one are different things.

So what for TSD: Hugging Face is the default distribution layer for open weights, and a hyperscaler owner would eventually change the terms on Inference, Spaces and model hosting. If any client deliverable pulls a model, dataset or embedding straight from the Hub at runtime, pin the version and mirror the artifact somewhere you control. That is an hour of work now against a migration you would otherwise do under someone else's deadline.

Funding Aug 24, 2026

General Intuition raises at a $6 billion pre-money valuation to push into robotics

Valor Equity Partners, Point72 Ventures and Seven Seven Six are leading, with existing backers Khosla Ventures and General Catalyst following on. The round is oversubscribed and still finalising. In June the New York company raised $320 million at $2.3 billion, which means the mark has nearly tripled in roughly two months. Valor's participation is its first AI lab investment since it backed SpaceX.

The company spun out of Medal in October 2025 and trains what it calls a foundation model for agents that move through space and time, using hundreds of millions of hours of gameplay footage and the action labels attached to it. The new money goes to compute, through a CoreWeave partnership, and to hiring, with physical AI and robotics embodiments named as the target.

So what for TSD: Nothing here is sellable to a Charlotte SMB today, and that is the point of tracking it. Gameplay footage turned out to be a usable substitute for real-world action data, which is the same trick as using your own client transcripts as training and evaluation data for the receptionist. Start keeping every call transcript and outcome in Supabase now, labelled with whether the booking actually happened, because that logged data is the asset that gets harder to buy every quarter.

◆ Agents met real users and real attackers

Trust Aug 24, 2026

Instinct's assistant lost its early users over data handling in a single weekend

Instinct is a private-beta personal assistant from a team led by former Sierra researcher Noah Shinn, backed by Kleiner Perkins and Conviction. Users reach it by text, WhatsApp or phone and hand it email, calendars, messaging apps, location, device audio and screen content so it can book appointments, manage inboxes and shop.

The complaints arrived in public and in a cluster. Peter Yang found retained Gmail records with no way to delete them, since fixed. Claire Vo found the assistant still summarising email after she disconnected access, with messages stored in plain text. Alex Cohen demonstrated a phishing path and deleted his account. Katie Jacobs Stanton reported the assistant sending email she never authorised and said it reset her trust to zero. Underneath all of it, the terms of service claim a perpetual and irrevocable licence to access, store, reproduce and modify user material for training, and permission to enter binding agreements on the user's behalf.

So what for TSD: This is the exact objection Grant and Bishop will hit the moment they pitch Front Desk or Concierge to an owner who has read a story like this. Write the one-page data handling sheet this week and put it in the leave-behind: what the agent reads, what it stores, where it stores it, how long, how to delete it, and a plain statement that client data never trains a model. Then make your terms say the narrow version rather than the broad one. Being able to hand over that page in the room is a closing advantage while the category's loudest examples keep behaving like this.

Security Aug 24, 2026

TeamT5 says Chinese state-affiliated groups more than doubled attack volume after adopting AI

The Taiwanese research firm told Bloomberg that state-affiliated crews increased attack volume more than twofold once they started delegating routine work to models and using them to build malware. DeepSeek is the preferred tool, described by researchers as powerful and easy to customise with weak guardrails. Documented cases include Grimfengxi generating exploit code, Teleboyi collecting 1,000 IP addresses and mapping a target's domains, and Huapi hitting a Taiwanese company's email system.

Keep the limit in view. Volume went up, and in the campaign Unit 42 documented, fully autonomous attempts breached nothing. Every confirmed intrusion still came from a human doing the work.

So what for TSD: More volume at the same success rate means your client sites get probed more often, and small business sites are exactly the low-effort target this economics favours. The concrete response is boring and cheap: confirm every client Supabase project has row-level security on by default, rotate any service-role key that has ever been in a repo or an env file you shared, and turn on Vercel's firewall rules for the login and form routes. Doing that in one pass across the portfolio is a billable afternoon and a real line in the next retainer check-in.
