◆ Governance moves to the front

Policy Jul 14 · manifesto

Hassabis calls for a US-led, FINRA-style body to screen frontier models

Demis Hassabis — Nobel laureate, Google DeepMind CEO — published a personal manifesto, "A Framework for Frontier AI and the Dawning of a New Age," proposing that the US stand up an industry-funded standards body modeled on FINRA, the private watchdog that polices Wall Street under SEC oversight. Frontier labs would voluntarily submit models up to 30 days before release for testing that probes dangerous cyber, biological, and "deception" capabilities, with a majority-independent board of Turing Award winners and other experts, plus industry, government, and open-source seats. He wants it operating before the end of 2026.

The tell isn't the specifics — it's who's saying it. When the head of a top-three lab argues out loud for mandatory pre-release screening of "all frontier-class models, no matter their country of origin or whether they are open or closed," the industry is signaling that self-policing is running out of runway.

So what for TSD: Nothing changes what you can ship today, but the direction matters. If pre-release model screening becomes real, expect slower launches and more documentation from the labs you build on — and more SMB clients asking "is this AI safe / compliant?" Start keeping a one-page note per client on which model powers their product and why; that answer becomes a sales asset the moment regulation gives it teeth.

◆ The talent-and-compute race

Hire Confirmed Jul 13

Anthropic pulls Monzo's Tom Blomfield onto its compute team

Tom Blomfield — co-founder and former CEO of UK digital bank Monzo — confirmed on X that he's joining Anthropic's compute team, taking leave from his general-partner role at Y Combinator. He'll work under co-founder and chief compute officer Tom Brown on the infrastructure that trains and serves Claude. It's the latest in a 2026 hiring run that already landed OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy (pre-training), DeepMind's John Jumper, and ex-Azure exec Eric Boyd.

Reading the pattern: Anthropic is loading its bench on the two axes that decide the next 18 months — pre-training research and raw compute logistics. Putting a serial operator on the compute team, not the model team, says the bottleneck they're most worried about is getting and running chips.

So what for TSD: The lab whose API your whole stack rides is aggressively hardening its supply of compute and talent. That's stability, not risk, for a shop standing on Claude — and a hint that Claude capacity and pricing keep improving over time. Keep building on it, and keep a fallback model wired into anything client-facing anyway.

◆ On deck & the model lull

Upcoming Expected Jul 17

Gemini 3.5 Pro is still two days out — hold model decisions

Google's next flagship, Gemini 3.5 Pro, is still targeted for July 17 after a full architectural rebuild that reportedly scrapped the 2.5 Pro base to chase better math reasoning, SVG generation, and image quality. Every headline spec — the rumored 2M-token context, pricing, benchmark scores — remains unconfirmed until Google publishes a model card. As of early July the public API still lists gemini-3.5-flash and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, not a GA 3.5 Pro.

So what for TSD: Same call as last week, now with a deadline: if you're about to lock a model into TSD Concierge or Front Desk, wait until Friday. A genuinely larger context window can let you stuff more of a client's catalog straight into the prompt and skip building a heavier RAG pipeline — that's a build you'd rather simplify than over-engineer.
