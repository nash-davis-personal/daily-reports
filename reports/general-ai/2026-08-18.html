◆ Agents on both sides of the exploit

Breaking Aug 17, 2026

Copilot Autofix introduced a shell-injection bug, and Wiz's agent used it to take a live credential

On June 18 a pull request merged into snowflakedb/snowflake-connector-net carried a commit co-authored by GitHub Copilot Autofix. The change touched .github/workflows/jira_issue.yml, a workflow that fires whenever anyone opens a public issue. Autofix removed the repository's existing sanitized-input pattern and substituted direct string expansion inside a run: block, which means the issue title landed unescaped in a shell command. That step also had JIRA_BASE_URL, JIRA_USER_EMAIL and JIRA_API_TOKEN in its environment.

Wiz's autonomous Red Agent found the workflow five days later. Its first payload failed. It read the failure, rewrote the payload, and base64-exfiltrated all three secrets to an out-of-band listener. The stolen token authenticated as an account with read access to Snowflake's engineering, security compliance and bug bounty projects. Wiz reported it on June 23, Snowflake patched the same day, rotated the token, and confirmed Wiz was the only third party to touch the endpoint during the window. The details went public on August 17.

So what for TSD: Two separate action items. First, go through every repo you own and confirm no workflow interpolates ${{ github.event.* }} directly into a run: block, because issue titles, PR titles and branch names are all attacker-controlled. Pass them through env: variables and quote them. Second, and this is the harder habit: an accepted AI fix is a code change from an untrusted author. You already read agent diffs on client work. Treat automated security fixes with the same suspicion, because this one deleted an existing protection and nobody caught it in review. If you ever pitch a client on agent-assisted delivery, this story is also the honest answer to "what could go wrong," and having the answer ready is worth more than dodging the question.

◆ Who is minding the frontier

Breaking Aug 17–18, 2026

OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team and split the work across product groups

The unit responsible for evaluating whether frontier models could assist with biological, chemical, nuclear or large-scale cyber harm was dissolved at the end of July, reported this week. Its mandate now sits with senior staff inside existing teams, split by subject area, with separate owners for biological risk and cyber risk. OpenAI has described the move as streamlining.

The pattern matters more than the single decision. Superalignment was dissolved in May 2024 after Jan Leike left, AGI Readiness was dissolved after that, and Preparedness is the third. Head of ethics Chloé Bakalar left in July, and chief futurist Josh Achiam and safety lead Johannes Heidecke have also departed. The restructuring is happening while the company is reported to be preparing for an IPO.

So what for TSD: This is a vendor-risk data point, not a moral one. Anthropic published a risk report three days ago that raised its own misalignment rating and named failure modes in its own models; OpenAI folded the team that would write the equivalent document. When an SMB client asks why you standardized on Claude for their receptionist or concierge, that contrast is a concrete, sourceable answer rather than a preference. Keep the model layer swappable anyway — your gateway abstraction is the insurance policy against any single lab's governance turning into your outage.

New Aug 17, 2026

A fake think tank was built to be cited by chatbots, and it worked

Politico traced an organization called the Hanover Institute to Piro Inc., a subcontractor working through Havas Media for the Israel Government Advertising Agency, funded at roughly $100,000 and registered under FARA. The mechanism is the interesting part. Hanover publishes unbylined "data reports" with footnotes and tables of contents, organized around precisely phrased questions of the kind people type into a chatbot, written in a flat neutral register. In Politico's own tests, both ChatGPT and Perplexity cited Hanover content.

Strip the geopolitics and what remains is a working recipe: format content to match the shape of a retrieval query, write it in the register a model rates as sourceable, and get quoted inside answers you never paid for a placement in.

So what for TSD: Answer-engine optimization has a demonstrated method now, and it is not a mystery. For every client, publish a page per question a real buyer would ask a chatbot — "how much does mobile detailing cost in Gastonia," "do salons in Charlotte take same-day appointments" — with the answer in the first two sentences, a specific number, and a citable source. That is a productizable add-on Grant and Bishop can sell as a monthly line item on top of a site build. The defensive half applies too: run your clients' brand names through ChatGPT and Perplexity monthly and see what gets cited, because someone in their category is going to figure this out.

◆ The money and the courts

Deal Aug 17, 2026

Nvidia is close to guaranteeing about $105 billion of debt for an OpenAI data center it will not own

Nvidia is nearing an agreement to backstop roughly $100 to $105 billion in credit so OpenAI can lease a data center campus in Ohio, where a developer builds, owns and operates the site under a 20-year lease covering up to 8 gigawatts. Nvidia is also putting about $1.5 billion into SB Energy. The number is a markdown from the $250 billion figure discussed in July, cut after investors raised concerns about Nvidia's exposure.

The reason the guarantee exists at all is that OpenAI has no investment-grade credit rating, so Nvidia's balance sheet stands in for one and lenders price the debt against the chipmaker instead of the borrower. The guarantee covers the lease and construction debt rather than the chips that fill the building.

So what for TSD: Nothing changes in your build this week, but it changes how you should read model pricing over the next two years. The compute buildout is increasingly financed by vendor credit rather than by customer revenue, which is what makes aggressive introductory pricing possible and also what makes it reversible. Quote managed AI retainers on a term you can requote annually, and keep your per-client token cost in a spreadsheet you actually look at, so a price move is a renegotiation instead of a surprise against your margin.

New Aug 17, 2026

An expert witness lost a $61 million case after his report turned out to be mostly ChatGPT

In the Watson Grinding explosion litigation in Harris County, Texas, an explosion that killed three people and destroyed about 200 homes, a 3M expert from Knighthawk Engineering produced a report that trial testimony pegged at 85 to 90 percent ChatGPT output. Among roughly 350 pages of prompts recovered in discovery was the instruction to "show how 3M is 0% at fault for the explosion at Watson Grinding."

The discovery method is the detail worth remembering. A plaintiffs' lawyer suspected the document was AI-generated, searched its text, and found the expert's chat sessions sitting on the open web where shared conversations are indexed. The deposition paused for three hours while they collected them. The jury returned more than $61 million and assigned 3M 30 percent of the fault. The report had been billed at over $80,000.

So what for TSD: Shared chat links are public documents. If you have ever used a share link to hand a client a draft, an audit, or a proposal outline, go find those links and kill the ones that contain client names, pricing, or internal reasoning. Beyond hygiene, this is the sharpest sales artifact you will get this month for the "we're worried about AI" objection: the failure was a person passing off unreviewed output under his own signature. Your pitch is that a human owns the output and reviews it, and now you have a $61 million example of what the alternative costs.

Context Aug 17, 2026

The DOJ has been investigating a16z for nearly a year over board seats at competing AI-data companies

Bloomberg reported a civil antitrust investigation into Andreessen Horowitz under Section 8 of the Clayton Act, which bars interlocking directorates between competing companies above certain size thresholds. The seats in question are Ben Horowitz at Databricks and general partner Martin Casado at Fivetran. No enforcement decision has been made.

The precedent is thin but real: five Thoma Bravo representatives left the boards of SolarWinds and N-able in 2022 and 2023 after the DOJ raised the same concern. Applying it to a venture firm rather than a private equity firm is the new part.

So what for TSD: No direct effect on delivery. It is worth tracking because interlocking boards are one of the mechanisms that keeps a handful of AI-infrastructure vendors coordinated, and you are a downstream buyer of that infrastructure. File it under reasons to keep switching costs low across every vendor in your stack.
