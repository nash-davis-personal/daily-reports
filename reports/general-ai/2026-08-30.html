◆ The bill for the training data

Breaking Aug 28–29, 2026

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell sued Anthropic, and named its founders

The two publishers filed late Friday in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, accusing Anthropic of a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping and downloading copyrighted works on a massive scale" to train Claude. The complaint covers tens of thousands of copyrighted compositions including lyrics and sheet music, a far wider set than the earlier lyrics cases, and pleads direct and contributory infringement. Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann are named as individual defendants alongside the company. An Anthropic spokesperson said the company disagrees with the claims and intends to defend itself robustly in court.

The legal ground here was laid in Bartz v. Anthropic, where the court held that training on copyrighted works could be lawful while acquiring them through piracy was not, and Anthropic paid $1.5 billion to settle with authors. Some of the same lawyers who brought the Concord and Universal action in January are on this one. With Sony and Warner in, the publishing arms of all three majors are litigating against the same defendant.

So what for TSD: This is a vendor-risk line in your client contracts, not a reason to switch models. Anthropic settled Bartz for cash and kept shipping; nothing here touches API availability or your Claude-backed builds. What it does touch is the "who indemnifies my client if the model is trained on stolen material" question a cautious SMB owner will eventually ask. Read Anthropic's commercial terms for the IP indemnity language once, write down the answer, and have it ready before a prospect asks — that is a two-hour job that makes you look like the adult in the room.

New Aug 26, 2026

The same three majors took equity in Stability AI two days earlier

Stability AI closed a $76 million Series B backed by Universal Music Group, Sony Music Group, Warner Music Group and Electronic Arts, with AMD Ventures and Pacific Alliance Ventures also in. It is the first time all three majors have taken an equity stake in the same AI company, and it brings Stability's total under CEO Prem Akkaraju to $232 million. Stability's pitch is licensed training data — the round follows Stable Audio 3.0, a music model family trained on licensed material and shipped as a DAW plugin.

Put the two events side by side and the rights holders' playbook is legible: fund the model that pays for its inputs, and litigate the one accused of taking them. That is a pricing signal for every generative-media vendor, and it is why "trained on licensed data" is starting to appear on sales pages.

So what for TSD: If you ship any generative image, music or voice asset into a client deliverable, know the provenance of the model you used and say so on the invoice line. For client-facing marketing assets, prefer tools with a stated licensed-data position — it costs nothing and removes an objection you would otherwise have to argue.

◆ The model supply behind your editor

Breaking Aug 28–29, 2026

OpenAI will stop supplying models to Cursor on November 12

OpenAI said Friday it plans to end its agreement with Cursor, proposing a shutoff date of November 12, 2026. Its stated reason is a change-of-control cancellation window triggered when SpaceX completed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, Cursor's parent, earlier in August. OpenAI said it cannot be confident SpaceX will use its technology within the terms of service, citing X's conduct under Musk's ownership. Musk replied on X that he "couldn't care less" and called Altman and Greg Brockman untrustworthy. Cursor co-founder Michael Truell said the company is engaged with OpenAI to resolve it.

The practical consequence lands on developers. Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown said Anthropic will increase compute to support Claude models inside Cursor, which makes Claude the default path for Cursor users who lose GPT access in November.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do today, and one thing to note. Your own harness is Claude Code, so this does not hit your delivery pipeline at all. It does prove the point you should already be making in sales: model access is a contract that can be cancelled by a third party's corporate action. That is the exact argument behind an "AI dependency sweep" for clients whose AI feature was built by somebody else on somebody else's key.

◆ Agents leave the screen

Feature Aug 27–28, 2026

The Model Hardware Standard is MCP for physical equipment

Anthropic opened the first phase of a research preview for the Model Hardware Standard, a shared spec for AI agents to operate physical devices safely. MHS introduces a standardised driver that translates between an operating system and a device using simple read and write commands, makes devices discoverable, and works with anything that has a programmable interface. It is model-agnostic, and any agent harness can reach it over standard protocols including MCP. Anthropic says integration time drops from weeks to hours or minutes, and it plans to open-source the spec.

The early numbers come from labs. Genentech automated a protein assay procedure with it; Carnegie Mellon researchers ran drug-discovery experiments roughly three times faster. Hugging Face is adding MHS support to LeRobot, its robotics library, and Raspberry Pi is enabling it across several products.

So what for TSD: The Raspberry Pi line is the one that matters for you. It puts agent-driven hardware inside a $60 hardware budget, which is the first time "an agent that reads a sensor and writes a row to Supabase" is a weekend build rather than an integration project. That is not a product yet. Park it as a Q4 experiment and keep shipping web and voice.
