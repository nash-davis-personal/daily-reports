◆ The coding-tool map redraws

Breaking June 16, 2026

SpaceX confirms $60B all-stock acquisition of Cursor

SpaceX confirmed it will acquire Anysphere, the company behind the Cursor AI IDE, in a $60B all-stock deal expected to close in Q3 pending regulatory approval. The move follows an option SpaceX took in April and hands xAI (which SpaceX absorbed in February) a serious foothold in agentic coding — one of the few places AI is already throwing off real enterprise revenue. Cursor runs at roughly $2.6B annualized.

So what for TSD: The major AI coding tools are now all owned by giants — Cursor (SpaceX/xAI), Copilot (Microsoft), Codex/Windsurf (OpenAI). Claude Code, the tool Nash runs his whole shop on, is the notable one still inside its model lab (Anthropic). Watch for pricing or roadmap shifts at Cursor; don't anchor delivery to any single vendor's terms.

Breaking June 18, 2026

Google retires the Gemini CLI for the Antigravity CLI

Google confirmed the Antigravity CLI replaces the older Gemini CLI as of today, giving the command-line a new interface built around the Gemini 3.5 agentic model family. It's part of Google's broader push to reshape its coding stack around agents rather than chat completions.

So what for TSD: If any TSD automation or scripts shell out to gemini, they'll break — migrate to the Antigravity CLI or pin the old binary. Minor footprint for Nash (the stack runs on Claude), but worth a one-line grep across the repos.

New June 2, 2026

Microsoft ships MAI-Code models to cut developer costs

At Build, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash, its first in-house model that turns written descriptions into source code for apps and sites — explicitly aimed at lowering developer costs and reducing reliance on OpenAI. It's another sign every major lab now wants its own code model rather than renting one.

So what for TSD: More cheap code-gen options is good for margins, but the real signal is commoditization — "we generate your website with AI" is table stakes now. TSD's edge stays the same: owned source code, a money-back guarantee, and a human who ships and maintains it.

◆ Agents & governance

Improve June 2026

Agent Development Kit reaches stable 1.0

The Agent Development Kit hit a stable 1.0 across Python, Go and Java (with TypeScript available), and the broader agent protocol stack — including MCP — is starting to look stable enough to build production systems against rather than rewrite every quarter.

So what for TSD: Nash already ships agents (this report engine, the receptionist, the concierge). Stable protocols mean less churn and a clearer story to sell clients: "your automation is built on standards, not a one-off script."

New June 17, 2026

G7 leaders meet the frontier AI CEOs

A G7 working lunch on June 17 brought together Sam Altman (OpenAI), Dario Amodei (Anthropic) and Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind) with G7 leaders, focused on youth safety and frontier-model risk. The session produced a voluntary-commitments framework rather than binding rules.

So what for TSD: Direction of travel is more disclosure and safety expectations, not hard regulation yet. Nothing to do today, but it reinforces the honest-AI posture Nash already takes with skeptical SMB clients.
