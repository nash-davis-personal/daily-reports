◆ The business of AI is now a services business

New Jul 15, 2026

Anthropic and Blackstone bet the next trillion-dollar category is implementation, not models

Anthropic, Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, Goldman Sachs and others launched Ode, a $1.5B company that puts elite software engineers physically inside enterprise clients to help them operationalize AI. It runs "Claude-first" but isn't Claude-only, employs ~100 engineers under CEO Chris Taylor (co-founder of Fractional AI, which Ode acquired), and squares off against OpenAI's own deployment arm plus Deloitte and Accenture.

The thesis, in Taylor's words, is that integration is "the most important product feature" companies will build over the next two years. Translation: the models are commoditizing and the money is moving to whoever makes them actually work in a business.

So what for TSD: This is validation of the exact business you're in, one tier down. If a $1.5B JV is being built to do AI implementation for the Fortune 500, the same demand exists for Charlotte SMBs who will never hire Ode — and you already have the delivery stack (Front Desk, Concierge, Booking Bridge). Position TSD explicitly as "AI implementation for small business," not "websites + AI features." Steal the frame.

◆ Builder tooling

Tooling Jul 15, 2026

Anaconda acquires Kilo Code, the 3M-developer model-agnostic coding agent

Anaconda bought Kilo Code, an open-source agentic engineering platform used across VS Code, JetBrains, web and CLI by more than 3 million developers, orchestrating roughly 10 trillion tokens a month. Kilo runs 500+ models, self-hostable, and auto-picks the best model per task. Anaconda says customers running agents through its platform are seeing 30–50% reductions in token consumption.

So what for TSD: The 30–50% token-cost angle is the real signal. Your margins on AI products live or die on inference cost. Kilo's model-agnostic, auto-route-to-cheapest-model approach is the pattern to copy in your own agents — don't hard-wire one model where a cheaper one clears the bar. Worth a look as a delivery tool too.
BusinessWire — Anaconda acquires Kilo Code

◆ Model & market watch

Funding Jul 15, 2026

DeepSeek is raising at a $74B valuation ahead of a planned 2027 IPO

DeepSeek is targeting a ~500B yuan ($74B) valuation and raising up to ~50B yuan, per Bloomberg reporting. Backers reportedly include founder Liang Wenfeng, Tencent, CATL, China's national AI fund, NetEase and JD.com. It closed its first external round in June at ~$63B and has begun planning a mainland IPO filing this year for a 2027 debut.

So what for TSD: DeepSeek's whole game is high capability at very low training/inference cost, and it's now capitalized to keep pushing. That's downward price pressure on the whole model market — good for your input costs. Keep an open-weight option in your stack for tasks that don't need a frontier model; it's where your margin is.

Hold Due Jul 17

Gemini 3.5 Pro is still one day out — every spec is still unconfirmed

Google's fully-rebuilt Gemini 3.5 Pro remains targeted for July 17. Reported features — a 2M-token context window, a "Deep Think" reasoning layer, ~$1.25/M input pricing — are all rumor until Google ships a model card and API docs. Only Gemini 3.5 Flash is generally available so far.

So what for TSD: Same call as yesterday: if you're choosing a model for a client build this week, wait one more day. A 2M-token window at low pricing would be genuinely useful for RAG/Concierge work — but don't design around a spec Google hasn't confirmed.
