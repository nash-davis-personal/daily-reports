◆ Open weights caught up to the closed labs

New Jul 16, 2026

Moonshot's Kimi K3 is the largest open-weight model ever — and it benchmarks like a frontier model

China's Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model (16 of 896 experts active) with a 1M-token context window. It scored 93.5% on GPQA Diamond and 91.2% on BrowseComp — the strongest published open-weight results on both — plus 88.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and a 57.11 Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index / 76.24 Coding Index. The API is live now; Moonshot says full downloadable weights ship by July 27.

The gap between open and closed weights has been the whole basis for paying frontier-model prices. K3 narrows it hard: a self-hostable model landing within striking distance of OpenAI and Anthropic on agentic and coding work.

So what for TSD: This is a margin story. For client work that doesn't need a frontier model — RAG lookups, classification, routine drafting inside Concierge or Front Desk — an open-weight option you can route to (self-hosted or via a cheap inference host) protects your unit economics. Don't hard-wire one premium model where a K3-class open model clears the bar. Bookmark the July 27 weights drop and price it against your current inference bill.

◆ Model & release watch

Hold Jul 16, 2026

Gemini 3.5 Pro misses a third deadline; Google may ship a stopgap

Google's rebuilt Gemini 3.5 Pro — scrapped and rearchitected after "structural failures in recursive tool-calling, SVG generation and mathematical reasoning" — has now missed a third launch target and is reportedly trailing GPT-5.6 on benchmarks, per TechTimes. The rumored specs (2M-token context, roughly $15/$60 per million in/out tokens) remain unconfirmed, and Google is said to be weighing a stopgap release rather than shipping the full rebuild.

So what for TSD: Same call as last week — don't re-plumb any client product around Gemini 3.5 Pro on the strength of a rumored date. Nothing is shippable until there's a model card and a price. Your near-term default stays Claude for delivery, with the open-weight option above for cost-sensitive paths.

◆ The politics & business of AI

Policy Jul 16, 2026

Anthropic and OpenAI take opposite sides on AI regulation ahead of their IPOs

With both companies eyeing Q4 2026 IPOs, Anthropic is publicly endorsing state-level AI-safety laws — California's SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act — arguing it already meets their documentation and risk-assessment requirements. OpenAI is lobbying the other way, for a single federal framework that would pre-empt the state patchwork.

So what for TSD: If state AI laws harden, the documentation-and-risk-assessment burden flows downhill to anyone deploying AI in regulated-ish contexts. For SMB clients that's mostly noise today, but "we build with a lab that meets the strictest state standards" is a real trust line you can use in a pitch. Keep basic model/data documentation on client AI deployments so you're never caught flat.
