◆ Two frontier-adjacent agent models in two days

Model Aug 12

Grok 4.6 lands at GPT-5.6 Sol's score and keeps Grok 4.5's price

xAI released Grok 4.6 roughly five weeks after 4.5, scoring 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — level with GPT-5.6 Sol and two points behind Claude Opus 5. Pricing stayed at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output, with a fast variant at double that. The context window is unchanged at 500,000 tokens.

The pitch is sustained project work over one-shot generation: researching an unfamiliar domain, implementing core functions, then iterating on feedback across a long session. It was callable the same afternoon in Cursor, Grok Build, the xAI API, OpenRouter, Cloudflare, and Vercel's AI Gateway.

So what for TSD: At $2/$6 with a 500K window, Grok 4.6 is now the cheapest credible option for the long-context grunt work in your pipeline — scraping and summarizing a client's entire existing site before a rebuild, or bulk-generating page copy from a content brief. It is already in the Vercel AI Gateway, so trying it against a real workload is a model-string change and a spend comparison, not an integration project.
xAI announcement · TestingCatalog · Vercel changelog
Model Aug 12–13

DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813 reached general availability with agent benchmarks nobody has checked

DeepSeek moved V4-Pro out of the preview it had been in since April and into general availability across its app, web interface and API. The GA build carries a 1,000,000-token context window and can produce up to 384,000 tokens of output, running in either thinking or non-thinking mode. The training emphasis is agentic: tool use, code execution, and multi-step workflows that run without a human in the loop.

DeepSeek's own numbers are 87.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1, 62.7 on DeepSWE and 61.5 on NL2Repo. Treat those as vendor-reported. Artificial Analysis puts the max-effort configuration at 53 on its composite index, one point above DeepSeek's own V4 Flash from July, which is a much smaller gap than the headline benchmarks suggest. A formal API version with Responses API support followed on August 13.

So what for TSD: The 384K output ceiling is the interesting number, because it is enough to have a model emit an entire small Next.js page set in one call instead of orchestrating twelve. Worth a bake-off against Sonnet 5 on a real Concierge ingestion job before you trust it with anything client-facing, given no outside party has reproduced the agent scores.
TechTimes · Artificial Analysis · Yahoo Tech

◆ The price of running an agent went down, and the unit changed

Pricing Aug 10

Anthropic made Sonnet 5's $2/$10 permanent and cancelled the September increase

Sonnet 5 launched on June 30 at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with the rate scheduled to rise to $3/$15 on September 1. Anthropic has now made the introductory number the standard price and cancelled the increase. Subscription plan prices are unchanged.

One caveat worth checking against your own bills: the newer tokenizer can push billable token counts up by as much as 35% on the same input, so a flat headline rate does not automatically mean a flat invoice. Verify against Anthropic's published pricing page rather than a third-party summary.

So what for TSD: A 50% cost increase you had penciled in for September is gone. That reprices every Managed AI retainer you were about to quote — the Concierge and Front Desk margins you modeled at $3/$15 now hold at $2/$10, which is the difference between a defensible monthly number and one you would have had to renegotiate in Q4. Re-run the retainer math this week and quote from the new floor.
Anthropic pricing docs · Sonnet 5 launch post · Finout on the tokenizer
Product Aug 11

Grok Bot sells persistent agents as monthly seats

xAI opened an early beta of Grok Bot, where each agent gets its own persistent cloud computer with a browser, filesystem and terminal. The agent signs into the customer's existing tools using the customer's own credentials and works through multi-step jobs while the user is away, surfacing only when something needs approval. The explicit design goal is operating software that has no clean API or MCP connector.

The pricing is the signal. It ships bundled into SuperGrok Heavy, Cursor Ultra at $200/month, and Cursor Teams Premium at $120 per seat per month. This is the first public product out of SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor's parent Anysphere, announced in June.

So what for TSD: Note what is being sold: a seat, priced like a junior employee, for work measured in outcomes rather than tokens. That is the shape of the Managed AI retainer you want to be selling to SMBs, and it is now market-validated at $120/seat. Also worth flagging to clients — an agent authenticating as a human user with real credentials is a security conversation you should have before a client's office manager sets one loose on their booking system.

◆ The money underneath

Check this Aug 13

The Anthropic profit number everyone repeated this week is a May projection

A wave of coverage on August 13 reported that Anthropic had booked its first operating profit: $559 million on $10.9 billion of Q2 revenue, up 130% from $4.8 billion in Q1, arriving roughly two years ahead of internal guidance. The underlying figures come from projections Anthropic shared with investors ahead of a funding round, first reported by the Wall Street Journal on May 20. Anthropic has not published audited quarterly results, and it is a private company with no obligation to.

The caveats that travel with the number are real: the company itself flagged that sustained profitability through the rest of the year is uncertain because of scheduled compute cost increases, and at least one analysis argues a compute discount flattered the quarter. Separately, OpenAI's public S-1 is expected on EDGAR in mid-to-late August after a confidential June 8 filing, which will be the first audited look at anyone's frontier-lab economics.

So what for TSD: Do not repeat the $559M figure to a client as fact. When a prospect asks whether the AI vendor you are building on will still exist in three years, the honest answer is that Anthropic's revenue growth is well-sourced and its profitability is a projection — and that you hand over source code on day one so they are not stranded either way. That answer closes better than a number you cannot defend.
CNBC, May 20 · Where's Your Ed At, skeptical read · OpenAI S-1 background
