◆ Models

New model Sept 30

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Argon, its first frontier model since Gemini 3 Pro, with access limited to cyber defenders

Google says Argon is rolling out first to trusted cyber defenders through its Fairwind program while it goes through the US government's voluntary pre-release review. Paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers come next, "as soon as possible," with no date. Pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output during an introductory period, with cached input 95% off, then $4/$20. The output limit jumps from 64K tokens to 1M. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 68% on CWE-bench v1.

Implicator reports Argon scored 53 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tied with GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1. 9to5Google, citing Bloomberg, says Google employees found it weaker on real coding work than the benchmarks suggest. Treat the coding claims as unproven until outside developers get keys.

So what for TSD: Nothing to switch to today because you can't get a key. When the API opens, the $2/$10 intro window makes Argon a cheap third backend to test behind the same provider switch as Sonnet 5.5 and Sol. Budget for the price doubling before you quote a client on it.

Voice Sept 29

Inception released Mercury Voice, a diffusion LLM built for phone agents

Inception Labs says Mercury Voice returns its first token in a median 320ms (750ms at the 95th percentile) while still doing reasoning and tool calls. List price is $0.40 per million input tokens and $1.50 per million output, half that during launch, which Inception puts at roughly $0.009 per conversation minute. Context is 128K. The endpoint is OpenAI-compatible and Inception lists LiveKit, Pipecat, Vapi and Retell as supported. Access runs through Inception's API for enterprise customers, with a sales contact for onboarding. The speed and cost figures are the vendor's own.

So what for TSD: Front Desk runs on Vapi, so this is a drop-in candidate for the LLM slot. Request access and run your receptionist eval calls against it. If it holds up on booking and transfer tool calls, you cut both dead air and per-minute cost on every client line.

◆ Regulation & safety

Policy Sept 30

The FTC opened a broad investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI labs

An FTC spokesperson confirmed the investigation to the AP and declined to give details. The Washington Post reports the agency plans formal information demands and compelled executive testimony at top developers, including Anthropic, OpenAI and the evaluation group METR, with demands likely in the coming weeks. The focus is consumer risk from AI agents that go beyond instructions, reach the internet and break into outside systems. It is the first official US enforcement action aimed at rogue agents. Neither company had commented as of Wednesday.

This lands one day after the voluntary White House pact covered in yesterday's edition. A pledge with no legal force now sits alongside an agency with subpoena power.

So what for TSD: The probe targets labs, so there's no compliance step for a small builder. It will put "is this AI safe?" in front of every SMB owner who reads the news. Have a one-page sheet ready for Front Desk and Concierge that lists what each agent can access, what it cannot do, and how a human takes over.

◆ The SMB and voice market

Business Sept 30

ElevenLabs doubled to a $22B valuation on voice-agent demand

A $300 million employee tender led by Wellington and T. Rowe Price values ElevenLabs at $22 billion, up from $11 billion in February. The company says its agents now handle more than 15 million conversations a week, three times February's volume, and that agent revenue more than tripled over the same stretch. Enterprise is 55% of revenue. Its customers include five of the ten largest insurers and four of the ten largest telecoms.

So what for TSD: This is third-party proof that businesses are paying for AI that answers the phone. Quote the 15 million weekly conversations in the Front Desk pitch. Big enterprises buy from ElevenLabs direct, which leaves the local trades and shops to whoever shows up and installs it for them.
ElevenLabs ↗ · Reuters ↗
SMB Sept 30

OpenAI partnered with America's SBDC to train small-business advisors on AI

OpenAI will train about 150 Small Business Development Center advisors through its Academy program, and those advisors will run hands-on workshops aimed at reaching at least 1,000 small businesses in person. Advisors and owners who finish a workshop get free ChatGPT Plus. The pilot covers a set of states that the announcement coverage doesn't fully list. OpenAI's companion report says about 4 million employees at small firms used its tools in the week of Sept. 9–15.

So what for TSD: SBDC workshops teach owners to chat with a model. They don't build a phone agent or a site assistant. North Carolina's SBDC network is the SBTDC, hosted by the UNC system, and you're a UNC student. Ask the Charlotte-region office whether NC is in the pilot and offer to be the local builder they refer owners to afterward.

Infrastructure Sept 30

Cloudflare opened a beta that lets sites charge AI agents per request

The Monetization Gateway lets a domain owner put a price on specific URLs, APIs, MCP tools or datasets. An agent that hits a priced route gets an HTTP 402, authorizes payment, and receives the resource in the same request flow with no checkout page. Settlement runs in USDC on Base through Coinbase's x402 facilitator. It is a closed beta for US sellers and buyers, applied for through the Cloudflare dashboard. Cloudflare has not published its fee.

So what for TSD: This is plumbing for selling data or tools to agents without building billing. No client needs it this month. Apply for the beta anyway so you understand it before a client with a valuable catalog or dataset asks how to get paid when AI reads it.
Cloudflare ↗
Research Sept 30

Anthropic: robots could do 74% of US physical tasks, and it pays to use them for 0.3%

Anthropic's new robot-exposure index finds robots can technically perform about three-quarters of physical job tasks in limited settings, which covers 34% of all working hours. Only 0.3% of tasks are cost-competitive with human labor today. At the historical 3% yearly price decline, reaching 10% would take about 40 years. Vehicle operators top the exposure list. Nursing and general repair sit near the bottom. Combined with language models, about 80% of job tasks have some exposure.

So what for TSD: The trades you sell to keep their hands-on work for a long time. Their office work is the exposed part: phones, scheduling, quotes, follow-up. That is the exact slice Front Desk and Booking Bridge cover, and this report is a credible source to cite when a contractor asks whether AI is coming for the business.
