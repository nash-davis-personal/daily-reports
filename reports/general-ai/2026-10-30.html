◆ Frontier models and builder tools

Model Oct 28–29

Google launches Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model, and gives it to cyber defenders first

Google says Carbon is built for sustained engineering and security work. Its own numbers: 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (finding real vulnerabilities), 71% on CWE-bench (writing working fixes), 54.8% on ExploitGym, and first place on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5 at 59. Introductory API pricing is reported at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output.

Access is limited to selected defenders through the Fairwind program, and Google is taking part in voluntary U.S. government testing. Ordinary developers can't call it yet. Gemini 4 Argon, released two weeks ago, stays the generally available model.

So what for TSD: Nothing to switch to today, since you can't get it. Watch the price: if Carbon opens up near $2/$10 it becomes a serious option for code review and security passes on client repos. Keep Claude as the default until then.
Google blog Ars Technica
Dev tools Oct 28

GitHub makes Copilot Fusion GA, and its Octoverse report says agents now start over 40% of pull requests

Fusion replaces the model picker with routing across Anthropic and OpenAI models: a single model, a cheap-first cascade, or a draft-and-review loop. Artificial Analysis scores it 2 points below Opus 5.5 in Claude Code on its Coding Agent Index at about half the cost per task. GitHub's speed and cost claims are its own.

Octoverse 2026 counts 17.9B pushes in the past year and says over 40% of September pull requests began as an agent's first draft, up from 17% in March. Enterprise Copilot is moving to usage-metered AI credits.

So what for TSD: The cost gap between "best model" and "good-enough routing" is now about 2x for nearly the same coding score. Use that framing when you price AI products: route routine client requests to cheap models and save the top model for the hard turns.
GitHub changelog Octoverse 2026 Artificial Analysis
Open model Oct 28

Nvidia releases Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe: free, open-weight speech-to-text for 40 languages

It transcribes recordings up to an hour long, separates up to eight speakers, and translates 31 languages to or from English. It leads the open-weight Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard at a 5.33% average word error rate, with about 4B active of 30B total parameters. It does not do live audio. Nvidia also released Granary v2, a 463,062-hour speech dataset.

So what for TSD: Good fit for Front Desk's after-call work: transcripts, speaker labels and Spanish-to-English call notes without a per-minute API fee. Live call handling still needs your current real-time stack.
Hugging Face TechCrunch

◆ Agents, security and the business of AI

Security Oct 26–28

A startup's AI agents broke into Anthropic's sales, HR and IT systems through third-party business software

Cathedral, a security startup, used AI agents inside Anthropic's bug bounty program and reached some internal systems through outside admin and sales software, per a draft report reviewed by The Washington Post. Anthropic says contact info for some customers and partners and basic account data were exposed, and that "Chats with Claude, source code, and model weights were never exposed."

The two sides dispute the details. Cathedral says it took under 12 hours. Anthropic says the probing ran several days, that Cathedral reported only one of two flaws, and that it went beyond the program's safe harbor. Anthropic shut the access points the same day and notified federal authorities.

So what for TSD: The weak spot was the SaaS bolted onto the company, which is exactly what your SMB clients run. Every Front Desk or Concierge install touches a CRM, calendar or inbox: keep those tokens scoped to the minimum and list them on the client's agent sheet.
The Decoder Logan Graham (Anthropic)
Business Oct 28

Anthropic gives Claude Enterprise customers reports on what their AI spending buys

New smart reports, in-app surveys and pooled team budgets show usage and outcomes by team. Anthropic says the data isn't used for training by default. TechCrunch cites Jellyfish data that coding-tool spend is up 10x since January, about 2% of engineering costs. The launch lands days after Anthropic's S-1; Bloomberg (via XTB) says the IPO roadshow could start the week of Nov 9 at the earliest.

So what for TSD: Buyers now expect a report showing what the AI did for them. Build a one-page monthly "calls answered, bookings made, questions handled" report into every TSD retainer before a client asks.
Anthropic TechCrunch XTB on S-1 timing
Agents Oct 28–29

Websites are being graded on whether AI agents can use them

Dedica launched a free checker that runs 125 tests for tasks AI browsing agents can't complete on a site (search, sign-up, booking) and suggests fixes. The same week Chrome's opt-in autofill began pulling confirmation numbers and order details from Gmail, and Vercel's Firewall started letting sites block AI crawlers and AI assistants separately.

So what for TSD: This is a sellable audit for every TSD site: make booking and contact flows finishable by an assistant, and set a deliberate crawler-versus-assistant policy. Run Dedica on two client sites this week to see what breaks.
