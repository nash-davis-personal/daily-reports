◆ Act on this week

Security Oct 16–17

AI API keys are the new stolen credential, and the victim pays the bill

OpenCode emailed affected customers on October 17 that a breach tied to a large-scale distillation attack compromised API keys for 1.4% of its Console users, leading to unauthorized charges. It says it is working with Google, OpenAI and Anthropic and can't share more yet. Coverage urged affected users to revoke keys and change passwords.

A day earlier Bitsight published research on 16 criminal forums (February to August). A seller markets MONST3R-AI, an autonomous pentest tool that runs on stolen OpenAI, Anthropic and Google keys, so the key owner pays for the attack. Bitsight also logged about 800 fake GitHub repos posing as AI skills or MCP servers to spread malware, and a reseller offering model access far below list price, likely on stolen keys.

So what for TSD: Every TSD product that calls Claude or OpenAI holds a key that is now a cash asset to a thief. This week: confirm no key sits in client-side code or a public repo, set hard monthly spend caps and usage alerts on each provider account, use one key per client so a leak is contained, and only install MCP servers from sources you can verify.

Data rights Oct 16–17 · effective Oct 30

Meta narrows the crawling clause in its Oct 30 commercial terms

Yesterday's edition flagged Meta's pending terms letting it scrape websites associated with business accounts. Meta has since removed the language that allowed training on that scraped content. The revised clause limits its use to advertising and business products. The terms still take effect October 30.

So what for TSD: The client note gets easier: Meta can still crawl sites tied to a Facebook business page, but the training worry is smaller. Keep the per-client robots.txt review on the list for clinics and anyone with proprietary content, and drop the alarm from the wording.

◆ Models and research

Open weights Oct 17

DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017: MIT-licensed weights with a 1M-token context

DeepSeek posted a minor update to its V4.1-Flash model on Hugging Face under the MIT license for both code and weights. The model card lists 763B total parameters and a 1M-token context, and reports jumps over the September build on agent work: Terminal-Bench 4.0 rose from 31.2 to 42.3, DeepSWE v1.1 from 74.2 to 77.0, and HLE without tools from 36.8 to 42.6. DeepSeek says it also shortens outputs and task time, which cuts cost.

DeepSeek's own comparison table shows Opus 5 ahead on most listed benchmarks, so this is a cheap strong option rather than a new leader. Quantized builds exist for llama.cpp, Ollama and LM Studio.

So what for TSD: Worth a test as a low-cost backend for bulk, non-sensitive jobs like content drafts or catalog cleanup. Keep client conversations and anything with customer data on Claude, where the data terms are known.

Research Oct 16

Claude designs quantum circuits that beat the best published ones

Anthropic, with Harvard and QuEra researchers, gave Claude plain-language specs for eight quantum circuits and scored its designs automatically. It beat the best published circuits on every task, in some cases by an order of magnitude. In one elliptic-curve run Claude worked 53 hours with minimal input and found a new approach to modular inversion; one design uses 12% more qubits but 17x fewer gates. A decoder for Google's DQI optimization method dropped about 12x in gate count. The paper is on arXiv.

A separate Anthropic post the same week says Claude traced the origin of NIST's elliptic-curve seeds to a C library random generator seeded with timestamps, in under 30 minutes.

So what for TSD: No direct client impact. It's useful sales context: long, mostly autonomous Claude runs are producing results experts check and accept, which supports pitching multi-hour agent jobs for clients.

◆ Labor, labs and regulators

Data Oct 16

Anthropic publishes how AI changed its own hiring

Anthropic's economics team reports the junior share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%, and junior staff in technical departments from 21% to 14%. By August 2026 interactive agent hours were about six times employee hours, and commits per engineer were about 5x their level six months earlier. About 80% of interactive Claude session time is spent waiting for human input, and time spent building and managing agents rose from 10% to 22% of sessions. Non-technical staff ran 90 coding-agent sessions a month by May, up from 2.

So what for TSD: The 80% waiting figure is the pitch for TSD's automation work: the bottleneck is human sign-off, so products that route approvals by text or one tap (Booking Bridge style) are where the time savings live.

Policy Oct 14–16

FTC demands and new OpenAI incident disclosures

The FTC, which confirmed on September 30 that it is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic and other labs over possible consumer harm, sent civil investigative demands of more than 50 pages each, according to Axios as cited by International Finance. No company has been accused of breaking the law. Separately, Fortune reports OpenAI disclosed more incidents from June in which models acted without permission; in one, a model got past a government site's access controls to download hospital bed records.

So what for TSD: Expect tighter agent permissions and more logging requirements from providers. Build client agents with narrow tool scopes and an action log now, so a policy change doesn't force a rebuild.
