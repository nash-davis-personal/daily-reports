◆ The open-weight frontier moved again, and it is permissively licensed

Model release Aug 28, 2026

Tencent released and open-sourced Hy4 preview: 770B parameters, 1M context, Apache 2.0

Tencent Hunyuan published Hy4 preview this morning as an open-weight model. The architecture is a mixture of experts: 770B total parameters with 49B active per token, 78 layers where the first is a dense feed-forward block and the remaining 77 route across 256 experts plus one shared expert, gated sparse attention with a cross-layer index cache, a 120,832-token vocabulary and a context window past one million tokens. The model card on Hugging Face lists Apache 2.0, which is the permissive end of the license range and materially different from the custom community licenses that have shipped with several other large open releases.

Tencent's self-reported numbers put it at the top of the open pack: 92.3 on GPQA Diamond, 65.7 on SWE-Bench Pro, 64.3 on DeepSWE and 82.9 on SWE-Bench Multilingual. The company also ran an internal blind evaluation with 163 domain experts across 203 engineering tasks, scoring Hy4 preview at 2.99 out of 4.00 against 2.92 for GLM-5.3 and 2.94 for Kimi K3. Treat that last one as vendor-run and directional. The API is live on Tencent Cloud TokenHub and OpenRouter at $0.834 per million input tokens, $2.501 per million output and $0.042 on a cache hit, and it is free inside Tencent's WorkBuddy and CodeBuddy for two weeks. Tencent's own write-up says the model contributed to its own development, proposing approaches and iterating on experiment results.

So what for TSD: Apache 2.0 at this capability level changes what you can promise a client. A custom community license makes a lawyer at a mid-sized business nervous and forces you into a hosted API; Apache 2.0 means you can quote self-hosting or on-prem for a client with data-residency worries and not be lying about the license terms. Practically, this belongs in the same routing tier you already use for cheap long-context work. Point one non-critical job at it through OpenRouter this weekend, a RAG summarization pass or a bulk classification run, and measure cost per completed job against your current default. The 1M context and the $0.042 cache-hit price are where the savings are, so structure the prompt with a big stable prefix.

◆ Anthropic gave agents two new surfaces to act on

Product Aug 27, 2026

Claude ships its own browser inside the desktop app

Anthropic built a Chromium browser directly into the Claude desktop app on Mac, Windows and Linux. When a task needs the web, the browser opens in a side panel and Claude loads pages, reads them, clicks and types, which lets it work against portals that have a login screen and no API. It replaces the Chrome extension for that job.

The isolation model is the part worth reading closely. The browser runs separately from your own, so Claude cannot see your tabs, bookmarks or saved passwords. Logins move over one site at a time from Chrome, Edge or Firefox, and banking and email sites are excluded from that import. It is on by default for Enterprise, Pro, Max and Team, with a setting to turn it off, and it rolled out to Enterprise first with the other tiers following this week.

So what for TSD: This kills a category of "we can't automate that" objections. Half your SMB prospects run something with a web portal and no API: a scheduling system, a supplier ordering site, a state licensing board, an old property-management dashboard. You can now demo Claude filling that form or pulling those numbers without asking the client to install a browser extension, which was always the awkward part of that conversation. Pick one existing client with a portal they hate, record a 90-second screen capture of Claude doing the boring part, and send it. That clip is worth more than any deck. Note the guardrail when you pitch it: separate profile, no access to their real browser, banking and email off the table. Say it out loud, because it is the first question they will ask.

Standard Aug 27, 2026

The Model Hardware Standard lets agents drive lab and factory equipment over MCP

Anthropic opened the first phase of a research preview for the Model Hardware Standard, a shared specification for AI agents to operate physical devices, starting with scientific research labs and advanced manufacturers. It works with any device that exposes a programmable interface, it is model-agnostic, and agents reach it through standard protocols including the Model Context Protocol. Anthropic describes agents running several instruments in parallel, microscopes, liquid handlers and robotic arms, reasoning through each step, updating parameters mid-run and recovering from some hardware errors without a human.

The claimed benefit is integration time falling from weeks or months to hours or minutes. AWS is supporting it through Strands Robots, Automata is adding it to its LINQ lab automation platform, Tecan is adding it for Fluent liquid handlers and Universal Robots plans support on its robotics platform. Anthropic says it eventually intends to open-source the standard so any device manufacturer in any industry can adopt it.

So what for TSD: Not a this-quarter revenue line, and worth watching anyway, because it is the same play that made MCP useful to you. Anthropic writes a spec, vendors implement it, and suddenly a category of integration you would have quoted at 40 hours becomes a config file. If MHS goes the way MCP did, the version of this that matters to a Charlotte business is equipment telemetry: a shop with CNC machines, a lab, a print operation, anything with a programmable controller and no software layer on top of it. File it, and check back when the spec is open-sourced. That is the moment the SMB opportunity actually opens.

◆ The industry asked for help with the problem it is shipping

Policy Aug 27, 2026

More than 100 companies signed an open letter on AI-enabled cyber attacks

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Microsoft joined more than a hundred other companies, including CrowdStrike, Okta and Fortinet along with financial institutions and internet infrastructure firms, on an open letter warning that AI-enabled cyber attacks "will become far more widespread and sophisticated as models around the world become increasingly capable." The letter names hospitals, water treatment plants and internet systems as the exposed surface, and asks for new defensive tooling, new cross-industry partnerships, and coordination between governments at local, national and international levels.

The letter arrives a day after OpenAI's own report on evaluation agents breaching Hugging Face infrastructure, and alongside the defensive products the same signatories sell: OpenAI's Daybreak, Anthropic's Mythos and Microsoft's Perception. TechCrunch's coverage points at the obvious tension, which is that the companies building the capability are also the ones selling the countermeasure.

So what for TSD: This is the top-of-funnel argument for the security review you should already be selling. When four of the largest AI companies in the world put their names on a document saying attacks are about to scale, you no longer have to make that case yourself in a sales conversation; you cite it and move to the fix. Pair it with yesterday's actively exploited CVEs and you have a two-paragraph cold email for Grant and Bishop that opens with a headline the prospect has probably already seen. Keep the ask small and fixed-fee. The retainer is the follow-on.

◆ The ship-it layer

Gateway Aug 27–28, 2026

Vercel added a free finance model, Cursor in the harness layer, and a Managed Agents recipe

Three entries worth knowing. Inclusion AI's Ling 3.0 Flash Fin is on the AI Gateway free through September 25, a finance-tuned variant with a 256K context, up to 32K output tokens, reasoning and function calling. The free model ID is inclusionai/ling-3.0-flash-fin-free, and Vercel notes it returns an error after the offer ends instead of silently switching you to paid, which is the correct design for a promo.

Cursor is now available in the AI SDK harness layer through @ai-sdk/harness-cursor using the HarnessAgent interface. And today Vercel published a walkthrough for running Claude Managed Agents with the Chat SDK, building a Slack research bot with one persistent session per thread, streamed briefs with sources, and no Slack credentials to manage yourself.

So what for TSD: The Managed Agents plus Chat SDK recipe is the one to actually read this weekend. A Slack research bot with a persistent session per thread and no credential handling is roughly the shape of an internal-knowledge assistant you could sell to any client with more than ten employees, and Vercel has done the plumbing. The Ling free window is a cheap way to test a finance or bookkeeping-flavored feature for a month at zero token cost, though a free tier that expires September 25 is a prototype budget rather than a production dependency, so do not put a client on it.
