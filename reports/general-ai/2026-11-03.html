◆ The frontier

Filing Nov 2, 2026

Anthropic's amended S-1 says it may soon hit its recursive self-improvement threshold

Anthropic's revised S-1/A, filed Monday, adds risk-factor language saying internal estimates "indicate that we may imminently meet" the Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI." It says no current model has been designated as exceeding that threshold, admits it has in the past not recognized capability jumps until after they happened, and says it is weighing RSP updates to keep humans supervising frontier model development.

TechCrunch notes the obvious tension: an S-1 is both a disclosure and a sales document. Treat it as Anthropic's own read of its pace, stated under securities-law liability, which makes it more than a blog post and less than proof.

So what for TSD: Plan for the Claude models under TSD Front Desk and Concierge to keep jumping faster than a yearly cadence. Keep prompts, evals and model IDs in one config per client so a swap takes an afternoon, and expect Anthropic's safety rules (like the new Claude Code limits below) to tighten as capability rises.

◆ Agents at work: who gets them, and what they cost

Data Nov 2, 2026

ElevenLabs: voice agents beat chat on any conversation longer than two minutes

ElevenLabs compared each production agent's voice results (phone and WhatsApp) against its own chat results, May to July 2026. Under one minute, chat won by about 9 points. Past two minutes, voice won by 10.9 points, a 31.7% relative lift, and successful long voice calls took 4.6 minutes against 7 for chat. Across 315 agents that moved to a faster LLM, a 16% cut in end-to-end latency went with a 26% lift in success, and calls abandoned in the first 15 seconds fell from 21.1% to 3.8%.

Caveats the post itself states: ElevenLabs sells voice agents, length is a rough proxy for complexity, and the latency link is correlation.

So what for TSD: This is sales ammunition for TSD Front Desk: complex calls (booking changes, quotes, complaints) are where voice earns its keep. Also check Front Desk's model latency this week. The 21% to 4% hang-up drop says speed is worth more than a smarter, slower model on the phone.

Enterprise Nov 2, 2026

Goldman Sachs limits Claude Code and other coding agents to engineers

Per Bloomberg's report on October guidelines, Goldman has cut most traders and bankers off from advanced coding tools, including Claude Code and GitHub's agents. They had used them for months to monitor trading flows and build content, and the CIO praised non-technical "vibe coding" in July. A spokesperson cited "productivity and risk management" and said experimentation is over.

So what for TSD: Even a bank with unlimited budget decided non-engineers shipping their own tools was a risk. That supports TSD's pitch to SMB owners: let a builder who owns the result do the AI work, rather than hoping staff vibe-code something safe.

Interview Nov 2, 2026

Garry Tan spends $100K to $200K a year on tokens and says personal agents are still too expensive

The Y Combinator CEO told Walter Isaacson his agents handle coding (23 Claude Code skills he published as "gstack"), interview prep, and personal admin with access to his email, Dropbox and Apple Notes. YC runs an internal agent, Stray Light, with a "company brain" covering every founder and application. Tan's view: agents should not be treated like traditional software, and they go mainstream only when costs fall.

So what for TSD: The "company brain" pattern (one agent with full context on every customer) is a sellable SMB product at a far smaller token bill. A Concierge variant pointed at a client's CRM, email and job history is the same idea scaled to a shop owner's budget.

◆ Abuse and trust

Investigation Nov 2, 2026

WIRED ties 400-plus ordinary-looking AI apps to a deepfake abuse network, and an infostealer uses an LLM to rank victims

WIRED found more than 400 apps and sites, including pet-portrait generators, that hid tools for sexually abusive deepfakes, with over 350,000 combined downloads; Apple pulled 19 after WIRED asked. Separately, Tom's Hardware reports a Windows infostealer that sends stolen data to Claude to rank a victim's assets by value, switches models if one refuses, then deletes itself.

So what for TSD: Clients will hear these stories and ask whether their AI is safe. Have a one-page answer ready: what data Front Desk and Concierge see, where it is stored, and who can read it. It closes deals with cautious owners.
