◆ The race for people and chips, not models

Talent Reported Jun 23–24

Anthropic keeps pulling senior researchers out of Google DeepMind

After hiring Nobel laureate and AlphaFold co-creator John Jumper earlier in June, Anthropic is reported to be set to take two more senior Gemini/DeepMind researchers — Jonas Adler (AI coding) and Alexander Pritzel (pretraining). It caps a 2026 hiring run that already includes Andrej Karpathy, while Noam Shazeer moves the other way to OpenAI.

Bloomberg and Fortune framed the departures as raising real questions about DeepMind's bench depth heading into the second half of the year.

So what for TSD: You build on Claude. A research bench that keeps getting deeper is a bullish signal for the model line you've bet your delivery stack on. Nothing to do today, but it lowers the risk of "what if Claude falls behind" when a client asks why you standardized on Anthropic.
Bloomberg ◆ Fortune
New Jun 24

OpenAI and Broadcom reveal "Jalapeño," their first custom inference chip

OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, a reticle-sized ASIC built specifically for LLM inference, taped out in roughly a nine-month cycle with a claimed ~50% cost saving versus typical AI GPUs and better performance-per-watt. Initial deployment is targeted for the end of 2026.

Custom silicon from the labs is the slow-burn story that eventually drags per-token API prices down for everyone building on top.

So what for TSD: Cheaper inference at the lab level is what makes your per-seat AI products — the receptionist, the Concierge — fatter-margin over time without raising client prices. File it as a tailwind on the economics of every Managed AI retainer you sell.
openai.com ◆ TechCrunch

◆ Agents are now a security surface

Security Jun 23–25

Five Eyes: AI agents inside critical systems are over-permissioned

The Five Eyes cyber agencies issued a joint statement warning that AI agents already operating inside critical infrastructure routinely hold far more access than they need, and that a single compromised agent can escalate a security incident into a major operational and financial crisis. Coverage cited roughly 1 in 8 enterprise breaches now involving an AI agent, and 78% of compromised agents found to be over-permissioned.

So what for TSD: This is your scoping checklist. Every agent you ship — the receptionist's booking access, the Concierge's data reach — should get the narrowest permissions that still work. "Least privilege by default" is a real selling point with SMB owners who are nervous about AI, and now you have a government source to point at.
The Register
GA Late June

Grok 4.3 reaches general availability on Amazon Bedrock

xAI's Grok 4.3 — a 1M-token context window, configurable reasoning effort, and a claimed lowest-hallucination-rate among frontier models — is now generally available on Amazon Bedrock. xAI also shipped a free Grok add-in for Microsoft Word.

So what for TSD: If you ever want a second model behind a client product without standing up a new vendor relationship, Bedrock now carries Grok next to Claude. Useful as a fallback or for A/B on a specific task — not a reason to leave the Anthropic ecosystem.
