◆ Models & capability

New model Jul 1–2

Google drops the floor on AI images and adds conversational video

Google released two generative-media models for developers. Nano Banana 2 Lite (gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image) makes a 1K image in about 4 seconds for roughly $0.034, built for high-throughput pipelines. Gemini Omni Flash generates and edits short video from text/image/video prompts with natural-language controls at $0.10 per second, capped at 10-second clips in this preview. Both are live in AI Studio, the Gemini API and Google Cloud.

So what for TSD: This is the strongest builder-relevant item in weeks. At 3.4¢ an image you can generate hero art, product shots, and section imagery for a whole client site for pocket change, and Omni Flash puts short social/ad clips in reach without a video shoot. Wire Nano Banana 2 Lite into your image pipeline as a low-cost default and test Omni Flash against your existing Veo flow for client reels.

Ecosystem Jul 2

Datadog acquires Adaptive ML as agent tooling keeps consolidating

Datadog acquired Adaptive ML, a startup focused on deploying specialized AI agents; terms weren't disclosed. It's another sign the "agent stack" is being rolled up by bigger platforms — observability, orchestration, and deployment are converging into suites rather than staying separate point tools.

So what for TSD: Don't over-commit to a single boutique agent tool that might get absorbed or sunset. Keep your own agent logic portable (your Claude + MCP setup already is) and lean on primitives you control. Watch which stack wins so you're recommending the durable one to clients.

◆ The business of AI

Cost Jul 2–6

Tesla caps employee AI spending at $200/week — the token bill is now managed

Starting July 6, Tesla limits employee third-party AI spend to $200/week after engineers were burning through thousands in tokens weekly; anything above needs manager sign-off, with a carve-out for xAI's Grok. Tesla joins Uber (which blew its whole 2026 AI budget by April, then capped at $1,500/month), Meta, Amazon, and Walmart. Notably, reporting says many Tesla engineers still reach for Anthropic's Claude over the in-house tools.

So what for TSD: Runaway token bills are now a named corporate fear. For SMB clients that translates to a real objection ("won't this AI thing cost me unpredictably?") and a real opening: sell fixed-price, predictable managed AI where you absorb and cap the token cost. Bake a monthly usage ceiling into every Managed-AI retainer so the client never sees a surprise.

Funding H1 2026

Record $510B in startup funding, and it's overwhelmingly AI

Global startup investment hit a record $510B in the first half of 2026 — more than all of 2025 ($440B). Over 70% went to AI, ~88% of AI capital went to US companies, and OpenAI + Anthropic alone took $217B (43% of all venture funding). Early-stage startups without a shipping product or a defense angle still struggled to raise.

So what for TSD: Two read-throughs. (1) The money is chasing foundation models and infra, not services — a solo shop selling done-for-you AI to SMBs isn't competing for that capital, it's riding the demand it creates. (2) A wave of newly funded startups needs sites, landing pages, and AI features fast; that's a client pool worth prospecting.
