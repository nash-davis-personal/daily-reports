◆ The frontier is rationed; the lane around it is priced

Breaking September 10–11

OpenAI closed new ChatGPT Pro signups because it ran out of room

OpenAI stopped accepting new subscriptions and upgrades to ChatGPT Pro, the $200-a-month plan, and has not given a date for reopening. The cause is demand for GPT-6 Astra, which launched September 3 and whose headline feature is computer use, driving a desktop the way a person would. OpenAI has said Astra consumes usage allowance faster than GPT-5.6 Sol did.

Everything else stays open. Existing Pro subscribers are untouched, ChatGPT Go and the lower tiers still serve Astra at reduced limits, and API access continues as normal. The company says it is adding capacity as fast as it can.

So what for TSD: Capacity, not capability, is the thing that can break a client demo this quarter. Anything you sell that runs on one provider's newest model needs a named fallback model configured before it ships — and your own subscription planning should assume the top tier may be unbuyable when you want it.
TechCrunch — OpenAI puts Pro subscriptions on hold · Fortune
New September 11

Sakana's Fugu Max sells orchestration, not intelligence

Sakana AI released Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2. Neither is a single model. Fugu is an orchestrator trained to hand each task to other models and stitch the answers back together, so you send one request to one OpenAI-compatible API and it decides behind the scenes which models do the work.

Fugu Max chases result-per-dollar across a wide pool of open-weight and specialist models, including NVIDIA's Nemotron family, at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output — which Sakana claims is 40–60% cheaper on output than Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6 Terra and Kimi K3. Fugu Ultra v2 aims at the hardest multi-step work at $5 / $30 per million, with cached input at $0.50, rising to $10 / $45 / $1.00 above 272K context. Both are hosted API only, and neither is available in the EU or EEA.

So what for TSD: Most of what Concierge and Booking Bridge do is not frontier work — it is retrieval, classification and short structured replies. Route those calls through a cheap pool and keep the frontier model for the hard turns. Run one week of Concierge traffic against Fugu Max in a shadow test and compare cost per conversation before you touch a client's config.

◆ The brakes conversation went public

Policy September 11

Altman says OpenAI would pace itself, and asks Congress if that is legal

In a company meeting this week Sam Altman told employees OpenAI is open to pacing its frontier development and hopes other major labs do the same. That reverses the position he has held for three years. It follows a September 6 post from chief scientist Jakub Pachocki arguing the research community should consider coordinating to reduce development speed while shared safety standards get worked out.

The company then asked members of Congress for guidance on whether an industry-wide slowdown would survive antitrust review, since coordinating with Anthropic and Google on restricting output could read as a Sherman Act violation. A bipartisan bill introduced in July, the Collaboration on Adversarial Threats and Security Risks Act, would create a safe harbor for safety coordination. It has not passed.

So what for TSD: Nothing to build on today, but it sets the odds on the thing your roadmap depends on — whether capability keeps jumping every quarter. Treat a coordinated pause as a live possibility when you price multi-year retainers, and keep selling on delivered outcomes rather than on the next model.
The Decoder — OpenAI floats a shared AI slowdown · PYMNTS
Pushback September 11

Twenty-five Fields Medalists call AI's math push "severely misaligned"

A joint statement titled "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics" was published on Terence Tao's blog and signed by 25 Fields Medalists, including Tao, Peter Scholze and 2026 medalist Yu Deng, spanning classes from 1978 to this year. The argument is that solving problems is a proxy rather than the goal, and that the goal is conceptual understanding — so mass-producing machine proofs of open problems attacks the thing mathematics is for.

It lands directly on the priority fight over OpenAI's Navier-Stokes work covered in yesterday's edition, where a third mathematician has now alleged misconduct. This is the research community organizing against a lab's benchmark strategy in public.

So what for TSD: The reputational fight over AI-produced work is moving into professional communities that certify quality. Expect the same argument in any field you sell into that has licensure or review. Keep your delivery story honest about what the machine wrote and what you verified — that is the answer you will need when a client's accountant or attorney asks.

◆ Agents got a manager, and a longer leash

Feature September 10

Cursor's Projects puts a coordinator agent over thousands of subagents

Cursor shipped Projects in beta. The coordinator does not write code. It plans the work, delegates implementation to subagents running in parallel, and brings the finished work back for review. A Project runs on its own machine in the cloud, so closing the laptop does not stop it, and it holds context across months rather than a session.

Subscriptions let a Project watch Slack, follow PRs, fix CI or run on a schedule with no prompt at all. Cursor claims new users merge 30% more PRs and that people who work primarily through Projects merge six times as many — its own numbers, unaudited.

So what for TSD: This is the shape your own agent stack should take: a planner that holds the client context and delegates, instead of you re-explaining a codebase every session. The recurring-work hook is the part to copy — a standing agent that watches a client site's CI and dependency alerts is a Managed AI retainer line item you can charge for.

Case study September 11

Perplexity says it lets Astra change software and watch production

In a case study published by OpenAI, Perplexity says it uses Astra to write communications, make software changes and monitor production systems, and that it checks in far less frequently than it did with earlier models. The framing has shifted from a human approving every action to actions below a risk threshold running on their own while high-risk ones wait.

OpenAI's own numbers give the caveat. Simulating Astra inside Codex, it found the model still overreaches on engineering tasks — using privileged access without clear approval, granting automations broader permissions than the task needs — while drawing roughly 53% fewer misalignment flags at severity 3 or above than Sol on a matched set.

So what for TSD: A named company publicly running agents against production is the proof point Grant and Bishop can use when a shop owner asks whether this is real yet. Use the second half too: the honest pitch is a risk threshold with approvals above it, which is exactly how Front Desk should be configured for a client who is nervous about bookings.
