◆ Anthropic and the agent layer

Launch Oct 21

Claude Duet: a background agent that asks before it acts

Duet runs on Claude Opus 5.5, watches connected apps like email and calendar, and prepares work such as scheduling and invoicing. It sends a morning brief and checks in every few hours, by text or voice. Anything that deletes, pays, signs or sends waits for approval. It is a research preview behind a waitlist for US Pro and Max subscribers on iOS, Android and claude.ai; Team and Enterprise are not included yet.

Background activity counts against the plan's usage limits, and there is no separate price. Anthropic says it can still misjudge what is urgent.

So what for TSD: This is the consumer version of what TSD sells as a front desk. Join the waitlist on Nash's Max account, run it on TSD's own inbox and calendar for a week, and note exactly where the approve-before-send pattern saves time. That pattern is the pitch line for client builds.

Standard Oct 23

Agent Skills joins MCP at the Agentic AI Foundation

Anthropic is donating the Agent Skills format to the Agentic AI Foundation, where it already placed the Model Context Protocol. Claude's developer account says more than 70 agent platforms have adopted Skills since December 2025, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code.

So what for TSD: Skills Nash writes for TSD now travel to other agents, which makes a packaged client skill (intake, booking rules, brand voice) a portable deliverable. Write client playbooks as Skills by default.

Starting next week, auto mode is the default across web, desktop and mobile for Pro, Max and Team. Automated checks block risky actions, and users can switch back to manual approvals.

◆ Security: AI finds the holes, and AI walks through them

Security Oct 21–23

PHP2Shell and Fortinet show AI bug hunting has outpaced maintainers

Two critical PHP core flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, CVSS v4 9.2) were found with AI help by Calif, OpenAI and Wiz; PHP shipped fixes Oct 21. Fortinet pushed a hardening release Oct 22 crediting AI-assisted discovery by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceX, with CVE details held until Nov 10. Separately, Anthropic published research using Claude to formally verify security properties across 123 open-source libraries, turning up bugs that had sat undetected for years.

So what for TSD: Security audits are now cheap to run and expensive to ignore. A short AI-assisted dependency and config review is a sellable add-on to every TSD maintenance retainer.
Wiz on PHP2Shell ↗ Anthropic research ↗ Cybernews on Fortinet ↗
Threat Oct 21

Microsoft: a China-aligned group used an agentic pentest tool in the ConnectWise compromise

Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise supply-chain compromise to Storm-0940, which used ARTEX, an LLM-driven penetration-testing framework, to automate activity and abuse application credentials across Microsoft 365 environments. CyberScoop puts the targeted tenants above 2,250; Microsoft did not say how many were compromised. The Neuron also flagged a researcher who found one attacker using agents to break into more than 100 small online shops.

So what for TSD: Small business sites are now worth automating attacks against. Rotate API keys on client projects, make sure Supabase RLS is on everywhere, and keep service keys out of any client-side bundle.
Microsoft Security blog ↗ CyberScoop ↗
Finance Oct 22

Banks try to tell a customer from the customer's agent

Axios reports Morgan Stanley and Bank of America are working to contain risk from unvetted third-party AI agents that already have access to customer accounts. The core problem is attribution: whether an action reflects what the customer asked for or an agent acting past it. 1Password also announced a credentials platform for agents, built with Anthropic and OpenAI.

So what for TSD: Any client AI build that touches money or accounts needs a clear audit log of who asked for what. Build that log into TSD's receptionist and booking products now so it shows up in the demo.

◆ Money and the big labs

Funding Oct 22

OpenAI reportedly seeks up to $30B at $1.25T; Meta's capex bill bites

CNBC and TechCrunch report OpenAI is negotiating a raise of up to $30B at a $1.25T pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and UAE investors; terms are not final and the figure sits below the roughly $1.4T floated earlier this month. Meta fell about 7% after posting its first negative free cash flow since its 2012 IPO and lifting 2026 capex guidance to $135B to $145B. Reuters reports DeepSeek is closing roughly $15B with Tencent among backers.

So what for TSD: The labs are raising and spending at a pace that keeps API prices falling for small builders. Nothing to act on this week beyond pricing client AI features on current rates and rechecking them quarterly.
