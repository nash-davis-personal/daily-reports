◆ AI on the phone line

New September 24

Google is testing Gemini calls to businesses on a user's behalf

Gemini can now phone a business to check whether something is in stock, book a reservation, reschedule an appointment or put an item on hold. The user watches a live transcript and can take over the call at any point. The test is small: US Pixel 11 owners on a paid Gemini plan, using the beta Phone app.

Google's post doesn't say how the assistant identifies itself on the call or whether a business can opt out of receiving these calls.

So what for TSD: Every client running TSD Front Desk will eventually field calls from other people's AI. Test the receptionist against a scripted bot caller this month (terse requests, no small talk, a hold request) and make sure it books cleanly and logs the call as AI-originated. "Handles human and AI callers" is a line Grant and Bishop can use now, while it's still novel.
TechCrunch — Google tests letting Gemini make phone calls
Business September 24

ElevenLabs is at about $600M ARR, and its CEO wants AI callers to say so

In a TechCrunch interview, CEO Mati Staniszewski said the company, reportedly valued at $22B since July, is running at about $600M in annual recurring revenue, 45% of it from small businesses, developers and creators. He will trade margin for market share and expects an IPO "in the next years."

He said "there should be disclosure at this time" that a caller is talking to AI, and expects that norm to fade within five years. Customers can pick which model does the reasoning: open-source models hold up on simple informational calls, while financial-services clients prefer frontier models.

So what for TSD: Two things to steal. First, open every Front Desk call with a one-line AI disclosure by default, since the biggest vendor in the category is on record for it. Second, split the model by call type: a cheap model for hours, directions and FAQs, Claude only when the call turns into booking or money. That's the per-minute margin lever on a Managed AI retainer.
TechCrunch — Twenty minutes with the CEO of ElevenLabs
Consumer September 23–24

Meta's Muse agent is the top free app, and Zuckerberg rejects a coordinated slowdown

Muse, launched September 8, is the #1 free app on iOS and Google Play. It books travel, manages email and cancels subscriptions. At Connect, Meta showed a palm-sized "Muse Charm" device and said Muse is coming to its glasses and to Mac.

In an NBC interview, Zuckerberg said "I don't think that we need some kind of industrywide coordination," breaking with Amodei, Altman and Musk, and argued that commercial and liability pressure already push labs toward safety.

So what for TSD: Your SMB owners now have a consumer agent on their own phone doing bookings and email. Expect "can an agent just run my scheduling?" in discovery calls. Have a two-minute Booking Bridge demo ready that answers yes, for their business, on their calendar.
NBC News — Zuckerberg interview TechCrunch — Everything new coming to Muse

◆ Who writes the rules

Policy September 24

AI lab CEOs asked the UN Security Council for global oversight

Dario Amodei told the Council that AI handled poorly "could be a risk to humanity as a whole." Sam Altman said the big decisions can't be made "by labs in San Francisco alone." Hugging Face's Clem Delangue and Yoshua Bengio also spoke. For the US, Michael Kratsios said the administration "totally reject[s]" any international body taking central control.

Separately, at the White House summit Xi said the US and China have the "capability and responsibility" to keep AI under human control. No AI agreement was announced; the two sides plan to meet in Shenzhen in about two months on guardrails.

So what for TSD: Nothing changes for a small shop this quarter. The practical takeaway is that disclosure and audit trails are where rules land first, so keep call logs and AI disclosures on every client deployment. It costs nothing now and becomes a selling point later.
Al Jazeera — AI leaders tell UN the industry needs global regulation
Reported September 24

Google, OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly plan a frontier-AI standards body

The Information, citing unnamed sources, reports a planned "Standards Authority for Frontier AI" (SAFA) modeled on FINRA: shared standards and benchmark testing across labs. Former White House AI adviser Sriram Krishnan was approached to be CEO, and Arati Prabhakar and Condoleezza Rice were approached for roles. Launch could come at the end of 2026 or early 2027. None of the companies has confirmed it.

So what for TSD: Unconfirmed, so file it as context. If it happens, a common safety test regime makes "built on a certified frontier model" a credible line in SMB proposals, the same way "PCI-compliant payments" is today.
BankInfoSecurity — Frontier AI standards body plan

◆ How software gets built now

Business September 24

Lovable's revenue run rate crossed $600M

The vibe-coding platform went from $500M in June to over $600M now. Its co-founder says people at two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies use it, and apps built on Lovable draw nearly 1B combined monthly views. It has raised over $700M in eight months, most recently $400M at a $13.3B valuation in August.

So what for TSD: The floor on "a website" keeps dropping toward free. Price and pitch outcomes instead: the site plus the receptionist plus follow-up, owned source code and the money-back guarantee. Also a lead source: SMB owners who half-built something on Lovable and got stuck are a warm market for a fixed-price "finish and harden it" job.
TechCrunch — Lovable's annualized revenue crosses $600M
Signal September 25

DHH says he's done writing code by hand

In his Rails World 2026 keynote, the Ruby on Rails creator said he hasn't hand-written code since about March, and that doing so "no longer makes economic sense" for most programmers. Secondary coverage says agents now write 80–100% of his code; that figure wasn't checked against the talk itself.

So what for TSD: The loudest skeptic of hype in web dev just endorsed the way Nash already works. Use it: "we build with AI agents and a human owns every line" is now a mainstream position, and it explains why a one-person studio can quote team-sized scope.
The Decoder — DHH is done writing code by hand
