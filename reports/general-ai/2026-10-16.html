◆ Models and pricing

Claude Oct 15–16

Claude Opus 5.5 fast mode is open to every paid tier

Anthropic's fast-mode docs now say Opus 5.5 needs no beta header or access request. Organizations on the Start, Build and Scale usage tiers get a default fast-mode rate limit; the Evaluation tier has to move up first. Pricing is $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, with prompt-caching and data-residency multipliers stacking on top. Opus 5 and 4.8 stay in research preview at $10/$50.

The fine print matters: the speedup is in output tokens per second, not time to first token. Switching between fast and standard speed misses the prompt cache, and fast mode isn't available on the Batch API or with a Priority Tier commitment.

So what for TSD: Don't put it behind TSD Front Desk; voice calls live and die on time-to-first-token, which fast mode doesn't touch. It does fit the RAG concierge's long answers and any client-facing agent that streams a lot of text. Test it on one high-value flow, and keep cheap models on everything else.
Claude docs: Fast mode
Open weights Oct 15

Meta Muse Spark Open: 816B parameters you can download

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 42 billion active per request, a 1-million-token context window and native text, image, video and audio input. Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model so far but put no benchmark numbers on the launch page. Weights ship under the Muse Model License, which limits commercial use. It runs locally on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with MLX, and hosted endpoints are live at Baseten, Fireworks, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter, Together and Vercel's AI Gateway.

So what for TSD: Read the license before anything client-facing touches it. The practical use for now is a fallback route in AI Gateway and a test of the privacy pitch ("your data never leaves a box you own") for a medical or legal client who asks.
Meta research blog Vercel changelog
Open weights Oct 15

Nvidia Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Microsoft's MAI-Base-2

Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a 120B mixture-of-experts model with 12B active, a 1M-token context and tool calling across text, images, documents and video, plus 74B and edge variants, under the OpenMDW license. Nvidia claims up to five times the throughput of comparable open models on one GPU and wins over Claude Opus 5 and GPT-6.1 Sol on five specialized tasks; those are its own benchmarks.

Separately, Microsoft AI said it finished pre-training MAI-Base-2, a 2.8-trillion-parameter base model (80B active), and claims 4–12x better pre-training compute efficiency than DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base on private tests. It's a base model, so no product yet.

So what for TSD: Nemotron's document-reading strength is the interesting bit: invoices, intake forms and scanned PDFs are a common SMB pain. Worth a quick eval against Haiku 5.5 on a real client document set before you quote any doc-processing job.

◆ Oversight and security

Policy Oct 15

The FTC sends OpenAI and Anthropic 50-page records demands

Axios reported that the FTC issued separate civil investigative demands, about 50 pages each, to OpenAI and Anthropic covering security incidents, safeguards, marketing claims and privacy policies. The same day the White House's new Super Intelligence task force began a 120-day agenda that includes a report on cyber, critical-infrastructure and biosecurity risk and protocols for labs to report breaches; how it gets enforced is still open. This follows the eight state lawsuits against OpenAI covered yesterday.

So what for TSD: Marketing claims are in scope, which flows downhill. Keep TSD's AI product pages to claims you can prove (what the receptionist does, what it doesn't, where data goes), and keep the AI use and safety page on every product current.
Axios Semafor
Security Oct 14–15

AI models find the holes; Bank of America hires 1,000 people to close them

Bank of America brought on 1,000 outside contractors to patch weaknesses that AI models, including Anthropic's Mythos, exposed in its own systems and vendor software. They join about 3,000 existing remediation staff, and CEO Brian Moynihan put the cost in the same bucket as anti-money-laundering spend. On the vendor side, Cohere signed a letter of intent to acquire AISLE, an AI vulnerability scanner that found 18 of the 24 cURL flaws patched in October, to form a Cohere Cyber unit. And GitHub made Agentic Autofix and AI Scan for pull requests generally available.

Also on Oct 15, an open letter signed by about 420 researchers asked labs to keep models with advanced hacking ability off the internet during initial testing.

So what for TSD: Finding bugs is now cheap and fixing them is the bottleneck, at banks and at a 10-person dental office alike. Turn on GitHub AI Scan on TSD client repos this week, and treat "we scan and patch your site every month" as a real line item in the retainer.

◆ Business

Ads Oct 15

ChatGPT ads triple their advertiser count as prices fall

The Information reports OpenAI now has about 60,000 advertisers, up from roughly 20,000 in late August, after cutting prices. Its ad team is about 140 people, nearly half hired in the third quarter. One agency said some business-software clicks cost about $5 on ChatGPT against $30–$50 on Google, while noting results vary.

So what for TSD: One agency's number, so treat it as a lead. If it holds for local services, a small ChatGPT ads test is something TSD can set up and report on for a client already paying Google $40 a click. Try it on TSD's own lead gen first.
