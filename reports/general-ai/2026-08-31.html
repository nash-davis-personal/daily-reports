◆ What Claude costs you from tomorrow

Pricing Effective Sept 1, 2026

The Sonnet 5 increase that was on the calendar for tomorrow is off

When Sonnet 5 launched in June, Anthropic priced it at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens and labelled that introductory pricing through August 31, 2026, with a rise to $3/$15 booked for September 1. The pricing documentation now carries a note stating the $2/$10 rate is the standard price and the September 1 increase will not occur. Batch pricing stays at $1/$5, and a 5-minute cache read stays at $0.20 per million.

The tokenizer caveat is unchanged and still matters more than the headline rate for code-heavy work. Claude 4.7 and later models use a newer tokenizer that produces roughly 30% more tokens for the same text, so a Sonnet 5 request is not directly comparable to a Sonnet 4.6 request at $3/$15 on token count alone. Measure a real workload rather than the sticker price.

So what for TSD: Every Concierge and Front Desk conversation you priced against a September 1 cost increase just got cheaper than your model assumed. If you built margin headroom into a retainer quote for the hike, that headroom is now real profit — and if you deferred a Sonnet-backed feature waiting on the price, ship it this week.

Limits Effective Sept 14, 2026

Claude Code's weekly limits: a 25% raise that lands 17% below today

Anthropic is permanently raising standard weekly Claude Code limits by 25% for Pro, Max, Team and seat-based Enterprise plans from September 14. The measurement point is the original pre-boost baseline. A temporary 50% increase has been running through the summer and expires on the same date, so on a baseline of 100 the sequence is 150 today and 125 from September 14 — a 17% reduction against current allowance.

Both figures are arithmetically correct and they describe different reference points. The framing drew enough criticism that Anthropic deleted the original announcement and reposted it leading with the change rather than the increase, with employees conceding the messaging could have been clearer.

So what for TSD: You have two weeks of the higher ceiling left. Front-load the token-heavy work — the multi-agent report runs, any large refactor of the client sites, the podcast pipeline experiments — before September 14, and check whether your current plan tier still covers a normal delivery week at 125.

◆ The agent reality check

Reported Aug 26, 2026

Meta scrapped Project OT after the agents failed to deliver

Reuters reported that Meta executives built a restructuring plan codenamed Project OT — Organization Transformation — at a January executive retreat, envisioning AI agents absorbing routine work and small teams of senior staff supervising them. Some team headcounts would have fallen by as much as 60%. Zuckerberg halted the plan on the evening of May 19, hours before the first round was due, and the second wave planned for November was dropped.

The internal numbers are the part worth keeping. Code changes to Meta's AI platforms rose 220% year over year while new features reaching users rose 36%. Technical and security incidents climbed 40%, and the time employees spent responding to them rose 70%. Employee sentiment fell 19 points after staff concluded that keystroke and mouse-click monitoring was training their replacements. In July, Zuckerberg said agentic development over the prior four months had not accelerated the way Meta expected.

So what for TSD: This is the best sourced counter-argument yet to "AI will replace your staff," and it comes from the company with the most compute to throw at the problem. Use it in sales: TSD sells agents that absorb a specific bounded job — answering the phone, booking the slot, answering the FAQ — not headcount replacement. That framing now has Meta's own telemetry behind it.

Deployment Aug 30, 2026

Caterpillar says the model was never the bottleneck

Caterpillar CTO Jaime Mineart told TechCrunch at the Ai4 conference that the company's autonomous mining fleet has moved more than 11 billion tons of material and travelled over 385 million kilometres autonomously, which he put at more than twice the autonomous mileage of the entire automotive industry, without a reported injury. Caterpillar now sells automated haul trucks, drills, underground loaders and dozers alongside a software command centre and fleet management.

The company is moving that playbook into quarries and construction sites, which are less controlled than a mine. Internally it uses AI to scan sites and build digital twins, and agents to modernise legacy code and catch defects earlier. Mineart's framing: the hard part of physical AI is incorporating the technology into the customer's jobsite and workflows.

So what for TSD: Same lesson at your scale. The Front Desk demo is never what loses a deal; the integration into how the shop actually takes calls is. Budget your delivery hours toward workflow fit and staff handoff, and say so in the pitch — one of the largest heavy-equipment makers on earth naming the same bottleneck is useful cover.

Funding Aug 28–29, 2026

DeepSeek is closing about $7.4 billion at a $74 billion pre-money valuation

DeepSeek is finalising a round of roughly $7.4 billion at about $74 billion pre-money, targeting a close by the end of August. That lifts the valuation from over $50 billion in June. Existing shareholders Monolith Management, Shixiang and battery maker CATL are participating, alongside several funds backed by Chinese local-government financing platforms. Proceeds are earmarked for model R&D and compute infrastructure.

A full raise at that pre-money implies roughly $81 billion post. Reporting points to a possible IPO filing as early as the end of 2026 and a debut on Shanghai's STAR Market in 2027, which would make it the first frontier-scale lab outside the US to go public.

So what for TSD: No action today, but watch the price floor. DeepSeek's whole strategy has been undercutting Western per-token pricing, and $7.4 billion of compute money points the same way. If you ever need a cheap fallback model behind Concierge, that option is getting better funded rather than going away.
