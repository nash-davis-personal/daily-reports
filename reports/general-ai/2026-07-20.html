◆ The economics of finding bugs just broke

New Jul 20, 2026

Searchlight Cyber found the WordPress wp2shell RCE with an off-the-shelf model and about $25 of compute

Adam Kues at Searchlight Cyber pointed GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra at a stock WordPress instance with no special harness. It extracted the administrator email address within minutes. Roughly four hours later it had established that a read-only SQL injection in the author__not_in parameter could be escalated to full admin access without ever cracking a password hash.

That chain is half of wp2shell — the pre-auth RCE (CVE-2026-63030 and CVE-2026-60137) that WordPress patched on July 17 in 6.9.5 and 7.0.2, and that started seeing in-the-wild exploitation once a proof of concept went public. Searchlight framed the result against the roughly $500,000 exploit brokers will pay for a WordPress pre-auth RCE.

So what for TSD: Two jobs. First, audit today — any client site you built, inherited, referred out, or are mid-migration on needs a WordPress version check and a post-patch compromise check, because exploitation began before most shops patched. Second, this is the cleanest cold-open you will get this quarter: the security floor under WordPress is dropping because attackers now rent a model instead of hiring a researcher, and "your site is a monthly CVE subscription" is a true, checkable claim you can lead a Next.js migration pitch with.

Breaking Jul 16–19, 2026

Hugging Face disclosed an intrusion that ran end-to-end as an agent swarm

A malicious dataset exploited a remote-code dataset loader plus a config template injection to execute code on a processing worker, then escalated privileges and moved laterally across internal clusters over a weekend. Forensics recovered more than 17,000 recorded attacker events spread across a swarm of short-lived sandboxes. Limited internal datasets and service credentials were touched; Hugging Face verified that public models, datasets, Spaces and the software supply chain were clean.

One detail worth sitting with: Hugging Face had to run the forensic analysis using the open-weight GLM 5.2 on their own infrastructure, because commercial API guardrails refused to accept the malicious artifacts as input.

So what for TSD: The guardrail detail is the practical lesson. If you ever need to analyze something genuinely hostile — a suspicious upload, a poisoned document in a Concierge RAG corpus — the commercial APIs will refuse it and you will need a local open-weight fallback. Worth knowing before you need it at 2am. More immediately: anything that ingests client-supplied files into a RAG index is an untrusted-input path, and Concierge should treat uploaded documents as data rather than instructions.

◆ The disclosure clock is running

Compliance Jul 20, 2026

The European Commission published Article 50 transparency guidelines, two weeks before the rules bite

The Commission filed "Guidelines on transparency obligations for providers and deployers of AI systems" on July 20. The guidelines themselves are non-binding, but they are the first Commission instrument covering the full scope of Article 50, and the underlying obligations become applicable on August 2, 2026.

Two duties matter for anyone shipping conversational AI: telling users they are interacting with an AI system, and machine-readable marking of synthetic content.

So what for TSD: This lands squarely on TSD Front Desk. Hawaii's Act 248 already pushed the same disclosure duty in the US, and the EU version makes it the de facto global template. Build the "you're speaking with an AI assistant" disclosure into the default greeting now rather than retrofitting it per client, then put it on the sales sheet — "disclosure compliant by default" is a free trust differentiator with SMB buyers who are nervous about the legal surface of an AI answering their phone.
