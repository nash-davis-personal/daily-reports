◆ Policy & governance

Policy EO 14409 · Jun 2 → framework due Aug 1

A voluntary US review regime for frontier models is close

The Trump administration is in the final stretch of talks with OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic on a voluntary framework for pre-release testing of frontier models, with an announcement reported as soon as this week. It flows from the June 2 executive order "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," which directs Treasury, NSA, and CISA to stand up a classified benchmarking process by August 1 that designates "covered frontier models," lets developers engage the government to determine a model's status, and provides access for up to 30 days before public release.

The sticking point in negotiations has been the threshold: labs want a bar high enough that only truly frontier systems trigger review, while officials have pushed for a lower bar that captures more releases. The framework is expressly voluntary and pre-clearance is prohibited.

So what for TSD: Nothing to patch or change. But this is the first real US governance scaffolding around the models Nash resells, and it is a selling point, not a threat — when a skittish SMB owner asks "is this stuff safe and here to stay," the honest answer is that the frontier vendors are now moving toward federal pre-release review. Keep the framing factual and vendor-neutral.

◆ Coding & agents go downmarket

Tooling GA Jul 1 · Business/Enterprise Jul 7

GitHub Copilot puts its first open-weight model in the picker

GitHub made Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.7 Code generally available in the Copilot model picker, the first open-weight model offered as a selectable option there. It is MIT-licensed with the full weights on Hugging Face; GitHub simply runs a hosted copy on Azure for teams that would rather not manage infrastructure. Architecturally it is a 1-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts that activates about 32B parameters per token, so it aims for large-model capability at roughly small-model compute cost. It rolled out to Pro/Pro+/Max first and reached Business and Enterprise on July 7.

The meaningful shift is not the single model. It is that open-weight coding models have crossed from specialist infrastructure into a mainstream developer surface, sitting one dropdown away from the closed frontier options.

So what for TSD: A cheaper coding tier Nash can reach for on price-sensitive client builds, while Claude stays the default where agentic reliability is what he is actually selling. More concretely, it is a pricing lever: offer a budget "built on an efficient open model" tier and a premium "built on Claude" tier so more prospects can say yes.

Capital Week of Jul 6

The capital is buying agent labor, not just chips

Two funding signals point the same way. Microsoft said it will invest $2.5B and embed roughly 6,000 industry and engineering experts alongside customers to co-design and continuously improve their AI systems — an attempt to productize the human labor it takes to make enterprise AI actually work. Separately, 8090 Labs closed a $135M Series A led by Salesforce Ventures to scale a "Software Factory," an agentic coding system aimed at regulated sectors like healthcare and aerospace where audit trails and guardrails matter.

So what for TSD: The money is validating the exact thing Nash sells at SMB scale — a person plus agents delivering working systems, not a raw model. Microsoft renting out "forward-deployed engineers" to the Fortune 500 is the enterprise version of TSD's whole pitch. Use that as air cover in sales calls.
