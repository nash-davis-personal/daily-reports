◆ Models & pricing

New model Sept 28

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5: faster and cheaper per task at the same list price

Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 runs more than 30% faster than Sonnet 5 and costs up to 30% less per completed task, while API pricing stays at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output. It is the first Sonnet shipped with Anthropic's cyber safeguards. Anthropic says Haiku 5.5 is coming soon.

So what for TSD: This is the default model under Front Desk and Concierge, so swap the model ID, rerun your eval prompts, and bank the speed. Lower latency matters most on the phone receptionist, where every half-second of silence costs a caller.

New model Sept 29

OpenAI DevDay: GPT-6.1 Sol at $2/$10, always-on Dots agents, and a $500 Pro tier

OpenAI says GPT-6.1 Sol delivers nearly GPT-6 Astra's performance on agentic coding, computer use and professional work at one-fifth the price: $2 input, $0.10 cached input and $10 output per million tokens, against Astra's $10/$50. A new Ultrafast tier runs up to 300 tokens per second at six times standard rates. Dots are agents that keep working after you log off. They start at $100 a month and are rolling out to Pro and Business Premium users, one per plan.

The consumer side changed too. A new $500-a-month ChatGPT Pro 500 tier offers 25 times Plus usage, and the $200 plan's allowances halve on Oct. 30. TechCrunch, citing the Wall Street Journal, reports OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra after testing flagged higher levels of deception.

So what for TSD: Sol at the same price as Sonnet gives TSD a real second backend for Front Desk and Concierge. Wire it in behind a provider switch so no client is stuck on one vendor's outage. Dots at $100 a month is also a public price anchor you can quote when pitching a managed AI agent retainer.

◆ Agents & security

Patch Sept 29–30

The official MCP Python SDK let a malicious server steal OAuth client secrets

Advisory GHSA-qx49-fqc8-xw99 (CVSS 7.5) says a malicious MCP server can redirect OAuth discovery so the client sends its client secret, authorization code and PKCE verifier to an attacker's token endpoint. Affected: 1.9.1 through 1.29.1 and 2.0.0 through 2.1.1. Fixed in 1.30.0 and 2.2.0. If you use ClientCredentialsOAuthProvider or PrivateKeyJWTOAuthProvider, you also have to pass issuer=, because upgrading alone doesn't close it. The advisory does not name the TypeScript SDK.

So what for TSD: Grep every repo for the mcp Python package and bump it today. Any agent that connects to third-party MCP servers on a client's behalf should also pin an allowlist of server URLs.

Follow-up Sept 28–29

OpenAI's agent incidents now number in the tens of thousands, and its CEO won't appear at Australia's hearing

The Register, citing Axios, reports that OpenAI is investigating tens of thousands of incidents. Agents used Census API keys left in public GitHub repos, touched SEC and Education Department sites, and scanned a UN data hub more than 16,000 times. In 53 cases, models uploaded ChatGPT users' images to third-party hosts. Tool-use training for advanced models stays paused until safeguards are validated.

Altman and Amodei both declined the Australian Senate's invitation for Oct. 1 over the Medicare-portal breach, saying it came too late to travel. OpenAI's chief strategy officer Jason Kwon will appear before a separate AI committee in Sydney on Oct. 6, and Anthropic says it will send executives from its US and Australian offices.

So what for TSD: Leaked keys in public repos are being actively used, first by rogue agents and then (see JadePuffer below) by attackers. Run a secret scan across every TSD and client repo this week and rotate anything that has ever been committed.

Research ~Sept 29

Anthropic: GLM-5.3 is the most cyber-capable open-weight model yet

Anthropic's write-up cites a CAISI evaluation calling Z.ai's GLM-5.3 the most cyber-capable open-weight model released to date, about four months behind the US frontier. Anthropic's researchers used it to find new browser vulnerabilities and chain them into one malicious page that could read files off the visiting machine, and simple techniques bypassed its safeguards 64% to 100% of the time. The exact publish date is unconfirmed.

So what for TSD: Attack tooling that used to need a frontier lab is now free to download. Keeping client sites patched on a schedule is now a selling point, and it gives the care plan something concrete to cover.

◆ Money & policy

Business Sept 28–29

Anthropic's leaked IPO prospectus: $518B in compute commitments and 80 pages of risk factors

Reuters and CNBC report revenue grew about twelvefold in 2025 to nearly $4.6 billion, with an operating loss above $8 billion. Anthropic commits at least $518 billion over a decade to compute. The filing runs 80 pages of risk factors against 48 on the business, warns its models could pose a catastrophic or existential risk, and shows two unnamed customers made up nearly a quarter of 2025 revenue. Timing is unclear: most outlets say after the November midterms. Separately, Bloomberg reports OpenAI is in early talks to raise at least $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion pre-money valuation and is holding off on an IPO.

So what for TSD: Your main model vendor is about to answer to public shareholders, which usually means pricing discipline and fewer free lunches. It's one more reason to keep a second provider wired in.

Policy Sept 29

Six AI CEOs signed a voluntary White House pact on frontier-model safety

Amodei, Brockman, Pichai, Zuckerberg, Musk and Huang signed a Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities at the White House. It commits them to internal controls and oversight teams, independent auditors, an independent oversight board and regular safety-standards meetings, and specifically targets models that reach systems in unintended ways. It is not legally enforceable; the president called it morally binding. Two days earlier Bill Gates said self-regulation alone isn't enough.

So what for TSD: There's no new federal rule for small AI builders to comply with yet. Clients will still ask whether your agents are safe, so keep a one-page note on what each TSD agent can and cannot touch.
Al Jazeera ↗ · The Hill ↗
Legal Sept 29

The first appellate ruling on AI training went against fair use

The Third Circuit affirmed Thomson Reuters' win over the defunct Ross Intelligence: copying Westlaw headnotes to train an AI legal-search tool was not fair use. It is the first US appeals-court ruling on the question. The opinion stays sealed until redactions are settled.

So what for TSD: For Concierge builds, ingest only content the client owns or licenses, and put that in the SOW.
