◆ Anthropic is buying the physical layer

New Aug 10

Theseus Infrastructure puts someone else's balance sheet under Anthropic's compute

Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management and GIC announced a platform called Theseus Infrastructure that will develop, own and operate purpose-built US data centers and lease them to Anthropic under long-term agreements. Macquarie-managed funds and GIC fund the majority of the equity for each project and own the platform. Anthropic is the anchor tenant, not the owner.

Anthropic also committed to pay 100% of grid-upgrade costs at each site and to cover consumer electricity price increases tied to its own demand. That is a direct answer to the local-government backlash that has now produced restrictions in more than 500 US towns and counties. No dollar figure was disclosed for the venture itself.

So what for TSD: Anthropic is locking in capacity through 2048, which is the strongest available signal that Claude API pricing and rate limits are not about to get worse. Building TSD Front Desk and Concierge on Claude is a safer multi-year bet today than it was last week.
Macquarie Group announcement · HPCwire
New Aug 10

Riot Platforms leases 191MW to Anthropic for 20 years and $9.1 billion

Riot Platforms disclosed a 20-year agreement covering 191 megawatts of IT capacity at its Rockdale, Texas campus, running through June 2048. Bloomberg reported the counterparty is Anthropic. Two five-year extension options would take the potential contract value to $16.1 billion. Delivery is phased: the first 96MW targeted for December 2027, the full 191MW by June 2028.

Riot is funding an estimated $2.1B–$2.3B buildout with a $573 million interim facility from Morgan Stanley. The stock rose more than 20% after hours Monday and traded up around 22% pre-market Tuesday, from a $19.40 close toward $23.65. This is Riot's second AI lease this year after an AMD deal in January.

So what for TSD: Nothing you can buy or ship, but it dates the capacity. Compute that Anthropic is signing for now does not arrive until late 2027. Price and capacity relief on the API is a 2028 story, so plan margins on today's token costs.

◆ A swarm of subagents did real mathematics

Research Aug 10

Claude raised a Riemann-zeta lower bound from 41.6% to 67.2%

Anthropic published a run in which an unreleased research version of Claude improved the proven lower bound for the proportion of Riemann zeta zeros lying on the critical line, from 41.6% to 67.2%. The work took two Claude Code sessions over roughly a day and a half: about 60 subagents, 31 million output tokens, 2,400 shell commands, and 54 arXiv papers reviewed.

The mechanism matters more than the number. Claude did not invent new mathematics. It connected recent work by Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya and Turnage-Butterbaugh with a Bombieri paper from 2000, across sub-fields that had not been joined. Two Anthropic mathematicians verified the result and Claude produced a formally verifiable proof. Anthropic states plainly that this technique is not expected to prove the full hypothesis.

So what for TSD: This is the strongest public evidence yet that long-horizon subagent orchestration beats single-context prompting on hard problems. The same shape — many subagents, one shared artifact, a verification pass — is exactly how your daily-reports engine already runs. Push more client work through that pattern instead of one big prompt.

◆ A model built to find holes

New Aug 11

GPT-5.6-Cyber found two chainable zero-days in Chrome's V8 engine

OpenAI released GPT-5.6-Cyber, a version of GPT-5.6 Sol purpose-trained for authorized vulnerability research, exploit validation and red teaming, with materially fewer refusals on security work. It helped find two previously unknown V8 flaws that chain together to corrupt memory and escape the heap sandbox. Both were disclosed to Google; one high-severity issue, an optimizing-compiler error that skipped a safety check during integer conversion, is CVE-2026-15903.

OpenAI also reports hundreds of other findings across desktop and mobile operating systems and databases, including a privilege-escalation chain in a widely used mobile OS. Access is gated: Daybreak Blue for approved defenders on general models, Daybreak Red for this one.

So what for TSD: The gap between "a model can audit code" and "a model can write the exploit" just closed for anyone who gets vetted. Assume the same capability leaks to attackers within months, and stop shipping client sites with unauthenticated write endpoints. Today's Metabase breach in the Technology brief is what this looks like when it lands.
