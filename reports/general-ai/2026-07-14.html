◆ Frontier models & distribution

Release Jul 14 · Microsoft 365 Copilot

GPT-5.6 becomes the default model inside Office

OpenAI's newest frontier family, GPT-5.6, is now the preferred model in Microsoft 365 Copilot — powering drafting in Word, analysis in Excel, decks in PowerPoint, and the Chat and Cowork surfaces. GPT-5.6 is three models under one banner: Sol (flagship), Terra (a cheaper capable tier), and Luna (the fastest, most cost-efficient option). The rollout follows the staggered, customer-by-customer access OpenAI adopted after this year's cybersecurity review.

The story here is reach, not a leaderboard. Putting a fresh frontier model in front of every Office seat resets the baseline for what a "normal" business user expects a document or spreadsheet to do on its own.

So what for TSD: Your SMB clients now have a frontier model bundled into software they already pay for. Stop selling "AI writing help" — that's commoditized. Sell the things Copilot can't do: an AI phone receptionist that answers on their number, a concierge trained on their catalog, booking that writes back to their systems. Lead with the workflow they own, not the model.

Upcoming Expected Jul 17

Gemini 3.5 Pro is expected this week — hold model decisions

Google's next flagship, Gemini 3.5 Pro, is reported to land July 17 with a 2-million-token context window, a "Deep Think" reasoning mode gated to a $250 Ultra tier, and roughly $1.25 input / $10 output per million tokens. The timing lines up with Shanghai's World AI Conference. Treat the specs as reported, not confirmed, until the model card is out.

So what for TSD: If you're about to lock a model into TSD Concierge or Front Desk, wait three days. A 2M-token window changes how much of a client's catalog or knowledge base you can stuff into context without a heavier RAG pipeline — that can simplify a build you'd otherwise over-engineer.

◆ The compute war

Business Reported Jul 13–14

Anthropic chases custom Samsung silicon and an October IPO

Anthropic is negotiating with Samsung to build a custom AI chip tuned to its Claude models, and is reportedly preparing an S-1 for an IPO as early as October 2026 — backed by locked-in compute deals and a reported $47B annualized revenue run-rate. Owning more of the silicon stack is how a lab escapes both GPU scarcity and Nvidia's margin.

So what for TSD: The lab whose API you build on is heading for public markets and its own hardware — that's stability, not risk, for a shop standing on Claude. It also signals the price of Claude intelligence keeps trending down over time as Anthropic controls more of its cost base. Keep building on it.

Constraint Reported Jul 14

Google rationed Meta's Gemini access — compute is the bottleneck

Google capped the amount of Gemini capacity it would sell Meta after Meta requested more than was available. When one hyperscaler tells another "no, we're out," the signal is clear: the 2026 constraint on AI is data-center capacity and power, not model quality. It's the same scarcity Anthropic is trying to engineer around with its own chip.

So what for TSD: Capacity crunches show up as rate limits and latency spikes on the APIs you resell to clients. Build retry logic and a fallback model into anything client-facing (Front Desk especially), so a provider throttling event never becomes a "your AI receptionist is down" call.

◆ Cheap capability from China

Release Jul 13–14

ByteDance ships Seedream 5.0 Pro image generation

ByteDance released Seedream 5.0 Pro, its latest image model, pushing further into a visual-AI market Chinese labs increasingly lead on price. Paired with TikTok-scale distribution, the pressure on Western image tools is on cost, not just quality.

So what for TSD: Client image work — product shots, social creative, hero images — keeps getting cheaper to produce. That's margin you can either pass along as a cheap add-on or bundle into a site build. Test Seedream on a real client asset before you quote the next design job.
