◆ The middle tier moved

Model release June 30, 2026

Claude Sonnet 5 runs near-Opus quality at agent-scale prices

Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5 as its most agentic Sonnet yet — it plans, drives browsers and terminals, and runs autonomously at a level that needed a much bigger model a few months ago. Anthropic says its performance is close to Opus 4.8. It's the default model on Free and Pro, available on Max/Team/Enterprise, and now the default in Claude Code with a native 1M-token context. Launch pricing is $2 per million input / $10 per million output through August 31, then $3/$15.

So what for TSD: This is the most directly useful item you'll see this week. Re-point the Concierge and Front Desk agent loops at Sonnet 5 and benchmark against your current tier — if it holds Opus-class quality at $2/$10, your per-conversation cost drops hard and your margin on a Managed AI retainer goes up without changing the price. Do the swap in a branch and diff a few real transcripts before Aug 31 while the intro rate is live.

Rollout Early July 2026

Google starts shipping Gemini 3.5 Pro with a 2M-token window

After missing both its May and June general-availability targets, Gemini 3.5 Pro is now in expanded Vertex AI enterprise preview and beginning a gradual developer-platform rollout. It carries a 2-million-token context window — the largest of any production frontier model — with a "Deep Think" reasoning mode gated to the $250/month Ultra tier and standard pricing around $1.25/$10 per million tokens.

So what for TSD: The 2M window matters for one thing you actually do: dumping an entire client site, catalog, or knowledge base into context instead of chunking it into a RAG pipeline. For a small Concierge build, "just put the whole thing in the prompt" can be cheaper to build and maintain than a vector store. Keep Gemini 3.5 Pro as a candidate for context-heavy demos, but don't rebuild anything until it's out of gated preview.

◆ OpenAI: pricing set, doors still shut

Pricing July 6, 2026

GPT-5.6 pricing confirmed, but the lineup stays government-gated

OpenAI confirmed pricing for its GPT-5.6 tiers: Sol at $5/$30 per million tokens (frontier reasoning and long-horizon agents), Terra at $2.50/$15 (GPT-5.5-class at ~2× lower cost), and Luna at $1/$6 (fastest and cheapest). As of July 6 the models remain in a limited preview restricted to roughly 20 government-vetted partner organizations, pending a White House voluntary-standards framework expected around July 7 that would define the conditions for a broad release.

So what for TSD: You can't touch GPT-5.6 yet, so it changes nothing in production this week — but the pricing tells you where the market is settling. Terra at $2.50/$15 is aimed squarely at Sonnet 5's $3/$15. When both are generally available, run the same Concierge eval against each; pick per-client on cost and quality rather than defaulting to one vendor.

Voice Early July 2026

gpt-realtime-2.1 cuts voice latency at least 25%

OpenAI released gpt-realtime-2.1 and a mini variant for low-latency voice and multimodal experiences, claiming at least 25% lower p95 latency across its Realtime voice models plus better speech recognition, noise handling, tool use, and instruction-following.

So what for TSD: This is a Front Desk item. Latency is the whole game on a phone receptionist — a caller notices a half-second of dead air. If your Vapi stack can route to gpt-realtime-2.1, A/B it against the current voice model on interruption handling and time-to-first-word before you assume Claude or your existing pipeline wins on feel.
