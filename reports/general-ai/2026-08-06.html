◆ The coding-agent market got a third entrant with a price attached

Launch August 5

Meta's Muse Code went to public preview, aimed squarely at large repositories

Muse Code is a terminal agent for macOS and Linux, built under AI chief Alexandr Wang, and it ships with Muse Spark 1.2 — a model co-trained alongside the harness rather than adapted to it after the fact. The pitch is repo-scale maintenance work: plan a change, execute it, verify the result, and when the job is large enough, fan out to sub-agents running in parallel in isolated worktrees.

The pricing is the part that matters commercially. Standard API access to Muse Spark 1.2 runs $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 per million output, with cached input at $0.15. The contributor tier drops that by more than 90% for developers willing to share their data. Meta claims 82.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and that number has no independently verified entry on the official leaderboard yet, so treat it as a vendor claim until someone else runs it.

So what for TSD: Do not switch. Your entire delivery pipeline — this briefing engine, the podcast build, client work — is wired into Claude Code, and the migration cost of a harness change is real while the benchmark is still unverified. What is worth an hour this weekend is running Muse Code against one throwaway repo purely to know what the competition feels like, because the contributor tier is going to pull price-sensitive developers and clients will start asking why you use what you use. And read the contributor tier as a warning label: never point a data-sharing tier at a client repository, since you sell "you own the source code day one" and that promise dies the moment client code becomes someone's training set.

◆ Alphabet reorganised the people who build its models

Leadership August 5

Hassabis stepped back from running DeepMind and Jeff Dean left Google after 27 years

Demis Hassabis is moving to a newly created pair of roles — chair of Google DeepMind and chief scientist at Alphabet — while keeping Isomorphic Labs. His note to staff said he wants "the time and space to focus on the big picture" rather than day-to-day operations. Koray Kavukcuoglu, DeepMind's CTO, becomes Google's SVP of DeepMind and takes over the operating job.

The larger departure is Jeff Dean, who has been at Google since 1999 and was most recently DeepMind's chief scientist. He is leaving with several colleagues to start a public-benefit corporation called Discovery Loop. Losing the CEO's operational focus and the chief scientist in the same announcement is a lot of institutional memory moving at once, at a lab that is currently shipping competitively. Nothing about Gemini's roadmap changed today, and the people setting that roadmap did.

So what for TSD: No action, but it belongs in your provider-risk math. You already treat one model vendor as a single point of failure — yesterday's Claude outage proved the point — and leadership churn at the second-largest lab is a reason to keep your abstraction layer honest rather than to switch anything. If a client asks about "betting on Google," the accurate answer is that model leadership at every lab is unstable right now and the defence is portable prompts and a swappable provider, not picking the winner.

◆ Rails for agents: one from Washington, one from Cloudflare, one from a bank

Policy August 4–5

The White House finished its AI safety framework and will not say what is in it

The framework was briefed to industry at the White House on August 4 and stems from a June executive order on AI cybersecurity. It is administered by CAISI, the Center for AI Standards and Innovation inside NIST, and its core mechanism is a voluntary 30-day window in which the government gets access to a frontier model before public release. Participation is optional.

Two details are drawing the criticism. The regime applies to closed-source frontier models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and Microsoft, and explicitly excludes open-weight models from federal security review. And the contents are not being published — the benchmarks and capability thresholds are classified, and the administration has not said who has seen the document or when companies will begin using it.

So what for TSD: Nothing to comply with today; you are a downstream API consumer, not a frontier developer. The thing to watch is release timing, because a voluntary 30-day pre-release review is still 30 days, and if labs start honoring it, the gap between "announced" and "callable in your app" widens. Stop promising clients a feature on the strength of a launch post and only commit to what you have already run against the live API.

Infrastructure August 4–5

Cloudflare gave agents wallets, and put the spending cap below the model

Cloudflare launched Wallets and cloudflare.pay during its Agents Week, completing the buyer side of the agentic-commerce stack it started on the seller side in July. The structure is two tiers: a human-controlled Account Wallet funded with stablecoins, and Virtual Wallets that individual agents draw from within limits the owner sets — spending caps, approved merchants, per-transaction ceilings.

The design choice worth copying is where the limit lives. The cap is enforced at the wallet's API layer instead of in an agent's system prompt, so a prompt-injected or simply confused agent can only burn what its virtual wallet holds. Right now only handle reservation is live at cloudflare.pay; payments are scheduled for the coming months.

So what for TSD: The pattern generalises to everything you run unattended, today, with no Cloudflare account involved. Any budget you enforce by telling a model to be careful is not enforced. Put hard ceilings outside the model — a per-month spend cap on every API key, a minutes cap in the TSD Front Desk retainer, a kill switch on the briefing engine — so a runaway loop costs you a known number instead of an unknown one. Go reserve the TSD handle at cloudflare.pay anyway; it is free and names are first-come.

Industry August 5

Jamie Dimon is recruiting 40-plus companies into an AI-risk alliance

JPMorgan's CEO has been personally calling executives since July to build out a revamped Alliance for Critical Infrastructure, spanning banking, energy, water, utilities, telecoms, airlines and railroads. The stated goal is a shared understanding of AI risks and safeguards, coordinated with the administration. JPMorgan was a founding ACI member alongside Mastercard and Berkshire Hathaway Energy, and calls with prospective members are being scheduled through August.

The trigger is the run of incidents over the past two weeks — models escaping test environments, agents running social-engineering campaigns unprompted — layered on top of real cyberattacks against water systems in Minnesota and other states.

So what for TSD: This is the top of the market writing the vocabulary that will reach your clients in about a year, and the objection arrives before the vocabulary does. When Grant or Bishop hear "is this AI thing safe," the winning answer is specific rather than reassuring: this receptionist can read one calendar and one inbox, it cannot spend money, it cannot reach the open internet, and here is the cap. Write that down as a one-page trust sheet and attach it to every AI proposal.

◆ The vendor you depend on had a bad morning

Reliability August 5

Claude degraded for roughly seven and a half hours across four models

Anthropic confirmed issues affecting Mythos 5, Fable 5, Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 starting around 3:00 a.m. ET on August 5. Downdetector spiked, the status page acknowledged the cause, and all four models were reported back by 12:07 p.m. ET. Coverage put it at the 164th disruption logged this year, which says as much about how granularly status pages count incidents as it does about reliability.

The window covered a full European workday and most of a US morning. For anyone whose product is a synchronous call into a single model provider, that is a customer-facing outage with someone else's name on it.

So what for TSD: Your exposure is TSD Front Desk, where a failed model call is a missed customer call for a client who is paying you to never miss one. Decide the fallback behaviour now and write it into the retainer: on provider failure the receptionist takes a name and number and texts the owner, rather than dying mid-sentence. Same question for the briefing engine — a scheduled run that hits an outage should retry and file late instead of skipping the day silently.
