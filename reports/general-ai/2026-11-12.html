◆ Tools you can build with

New API Nov 11

Google's Gemini Decisions API prices a routing call at $0.10 per million tokens

Google released a new endpoint and model, gemini-decision-1, built for closed-set questions. You hand it context and a question, and it returns a yes/no, a choice among 2 to 255 options, or a rubric score, each with a confidence from 0 to 1. It scores the candidate answers in a single forward pass instead of generating text, which is why output is free and input costs $0.10 per million tokens. There is a free tier in AI Studio.

It accepts text, images, audio, video and PDFs up to 256K tokens and up to 1,000 questions in one request. Google states 150 to 250 ms end to end for contexts up to 16K tokens. Vercel added it to AI Gateway the same day; Google Cloud support is listed as coming soon. Simon Willison notes the design is close to Jev's decision model ($0.042 per million input) and says the price matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna decision model. Google's benchmark charts were not readable in this pass, so no accuracy claim here.

So what for TSD: Front Desk and Concierge both make a pile of small decisions per turn: is this an emergency, which department, did the retrieved chunk answer the question, should this hand off to a human. Those calls currently ride on a full chat model. Run a one-hour bake-off this week: pull 100 real intent and grading calls from your logs, send them through gemini-decision-1 on AI Gateway, and compare agreement, latency and cost against what you pay now. The confidence score is the part to keep even if you stay on Claude for the talking, because it gives you a clean threshold for "transfer to the owner."

New tool Nov 11

AWS Emulator: a free local AWS that agents wrote and maintain

AWS published an official emulator, written in Rust, that the AWS CLI, the SDKs and coding agents can hit at 127.0.0.1:9297 at no charge. Linux gets RPM and Deb packages; on macOS and Windows you build a Docker image yourself, since AWS has not published one. AWS says agents running on Bedrock AgentCore generate and maintain the emulator from the AWS API models, with a separate adversarial security agent reviewing changes.

The limits are stated plainly: no real IAM policy evaluation, a single account, and any operation that is not implemented returns HTTP 501. A GeekWire headline puts coverage at more than 200 services with no human-written code; the AWS post gives no service count, so treat that number as unconfirmed.

So what for TSD: Your stack is Vercel and Supabase, so this is not a daily tool. It matters as a marker: a hyperscaler just shipped a production developer product and said out loud that agents built it and agents keep it current. If a client project ever needs S3 or SES, test against this locally and stop paying for a sandbox account.

Policy Nov 11

United Airlines moves to block third-party AI agents from booking flights

United is updating its booking and ticketing policies to bar "unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants," and says it will block them on its own site and app and through travel agencies and aggregators. The reasons it gave: exposure of customer credentials and payment details, unintended purchases or mileage redemptions, and conflict with federal check-in rules. No effective date was given. CBS notes Southwest has gone the other way and lets ChatGPT users see its inventory.

So what for TSD: This is the first large consumer brand to write "no outside agents" into its terms, and others will copy the language. Keep your products on the safe side of that line: Front Desk and Booking Bridge act for the business that hired you, on systems the business owns or has an API key for. Say that sentence in sales calls. Do not build anything that logs into a third party's consumer site with a customer's password.

◆ The buildout and the money

Developing Nov 11–12

Reports: Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Jensen Huang

The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove its founder and CEO, and CBS confirmed the report with two sources. The reporting ties the move to the independent investigation the board opened Nov 8, which concerns an alleged relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence believes was working for China; the New York Times reported a second alleged relationship with a direct report. Bloomberg, as relayed by Fortune, says the board met Wednesday evening and has hired an outside firm to identify CEO candidates, with an interim CEO under consideration and no one named.

The stock fell about 8% in extended trading Wednesday and was down 6.9% premarket at 6:23 a.m. ET Thursday, per CNBC. Bernstein cut its price target from $400 to $200. Two senators asked the administration to preserve documents on Nvidia's chip exports. As of this filing Nvidia has announced nothing, and Q3 results are still set for Nov 17. Everything above is sourced reporting about a decision that has not been made public.

So what for TSD: Nothing changes in what you build or what an API call costs this month; Anthropic's own roadshow says Claude now runs on four chip families. The exposure is second-order: Nvidia is one of the seven firms buying into Anthropic's IPO placement, and a leadership fight at the center of the supply chain is the kind of thing that moves the whole AI trade the week before that IPO prices. Watch it, do not act on it.

IPO Nov 11

Anthropic's filing: nearly $900B in commitments, and a model that ported itself to AMD in three days

CNBC went through the S-1 on Wednesday. As of September, Anthropic had nearly $900 billion in future spending commitments, about half due by the end of 2030 and about $70 billion due within the next year, including up to $120.5 billion with SpaceX through 2029. For the first nine months of 2026 it reported adjusted operating income of $4.08 billion, a GAAP operating loss of $1.26 billion, and a GAAP net loss above $104 billion, most of it a $94 billion noncash charge tied to securities issued to partners such as Google and Amazon. Pricing is still expected Nov 18 with trading on Nasdaq under ANT on Nov 19.

Simon Willison transcribed the roadshow video. Two details stand out: Claude runs on four chip families (AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia and AMD), and Anthropic says Mythos ported Claude to AMD GPUs in three days with almost no human help. Separately, TechCrunch relayed a Bloomberg report that OpenAI's Q3 revenue was about $10 billion, up from $6.7 billion in Q2, against Anthropic's $17 billion for the same quarter. The OpenAI figure is from anonymous sources and unconfirmed by the company.

So what for TSD: Your main supplier is profitable on an adjusted basis and carries commitments worth roughly eleven times its run rate. Read that as price pressure in both directions: scale lets it cut prices (Fable 5.2 just did), and the commitments mean it cannot give capacity away forever. Keep model IDs in config, keep AI Gateway in front, and keep your retainers priced on outcomes so a token price move in either direction is your margin and your client never notices.

Earnings Nov 10–11

CoreWeave grew 168%, raised prices twice, and is asking customers to prepay

CoreWeave reported Tuesday night and the stock rose 24.71% Wednesday, its best day since April 2025. Q3 revenue was $3.66 billion, up 168% from a year earlier, with a net loss of $709 million and $846 million of interest expense in the quarter. Backlog stands at $132.5 billion, it raised its 2026 revenue forecast, and it set capital spending at $37 to $39 billion. TechCrunch reports it expects about $9 billion in customer prepayments over the next three months and is signing shorter contracts. It raised prices about 25% in July and roughly another 10% later in the quarter.

So what for TSD: GPU rental prices went up this quarter while token prices at the model labs went down. The labs are absorbing that gap for now. If you ever quote a client a fixed multi-year AI cost, do not; quote a monthly retainer with a usage cap and a 12-month price review.
