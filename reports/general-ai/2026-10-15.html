◆ Models and voice

Model GA Oct 13

Gemini 4 Argon: Google's flagship hits the open API with a pricing window

Google made Gemini 4 Argon broadly available on October 13 through the paid Gemini API, AI Studio, Antigravity and eligible Gemini Enterprise plans, after a limited September 30 launch. Intro pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input at $0.10. That ends November 30; from December 1 the standard rate is $4 and $20. Google describes a unified limit of up to 1 million input and output tokens via a continuation protocol, but hasn't said how long a single uninterrupted response can run.

Independent scores are mixed. Argon is first on the Vals Index (68.90%, just ahead of Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5) and on Vals Finance Agent. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it scores 53, tied with GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, below Opus 5.5 (58) and Sonnet 5.5 (56). On Terminal-Bench 4.0 it trails Opus 5.5, 57.4% to 66.4%.

So what for TSD: Claude stays the default for coding and agent work. Argon's cheap cached input until Nov 30 makes it worth a quick test for one-off bulk jobs on long documents, like rewriting a client's whole product catalog. Benchmark it on a real task before Dec 1, when the price doubles.
VentureBeat Google blog
Voice Oct 14

Grok Voice 3.0: a new top speech-to-speech model at 8 cents a minute

xAI released Grok Voice 3.0 in its Voice Agent API at $0.08 per minute ($4.80 an hour), the same price as version 2.0. Agents pointed at grok-voice-latest switched over automatically. It scored 86.9 on Artificial Analysis' speech-to-speech index (first place, up from 81.3) and 97.0% on Full Duplex Bench, which tests turn-taking and interruptions.

xAI claims sub-second latency, about 40% lower than GPT-Live-1 on its τ-Voice comparison, and a 71.6% agent success rate. Apart from the Artificial Analysis ranking, these are xAI's own numbers.

So what for TSD: This lands right on TSD Front Desk. Put Grok Voice 3 through the same scripted test calls as your current Vapi voice stack (interruptions, appointment booking, a caller with a heavy accent) and compare cost per call. If it holds up, a cheaper per-minute rate becomes margin or a lower flat price.
xAI announcement

◆ Agents in the wild

Security Oct 13

AI agents built a working Chrome sandbox escape in six days

XBOW, an AI offensive-security company, published a Chrome exploit chain built by researcher Brendan Dolan-Gavitt working with AI agents on OpenAI's public GPT-5.6 Sol. It chains flaws in V8, Chrome's animated-WebP decoder and Crashpad so that a malicious page can run code on Windows outside the sandbox. The work ran August 2–7 on roughly $40,000 of compute. Google fixed all three bugs and paid a $250,000 bounty.

Separately, a Semperis study reports that 84% of ransomware victims say AI was involved in their breach (a vendor survey, so treat the figure as directional).

So what for TSD: Building exploits is getting cheap, so the gap between a disclosure and an attack on your clients' sites keeps shrinking. Patch the moment an advisory drops (see today's Technology brief), and list patch response time as a feature of the managed retainer.
CyberInsider Semperis study
Agents Oct 14

Agents are buying things, and design tools are writing rules for them

Chainalysis counts more than 138 million x402 payments on Base by Q3 2026, up from about 1,500 in mid-2025. Agents use them to pay for compute, fresh web data, stronger models and code-security scans, and over 87% of payments were under 10 cents. Developers fund the wallets, usually in USDC, and agents spend without approving each purchase.

At Config India, Figma launched Figma Specs, a Markdown editor for a team's design rules that agents can follow. It also opened code layers in public beta and added more than 20 tools to its MCP server. Payload CMS, which Figma owns, is being wired into Figma Make to generate dynamic websites.

So what for TSD: Two things to file away. Pay-per-call APIs for agents are now a real market, so a small paid data endpoint could earn while you sleep. And Figma plus Payload generating CMS-backed sites is moving onto the low end of your website work, so sell outcomes and code ownership, because build hours are getting cheap.
Fortune / Chainalysis TechCrunch on Figma

◆ Rules and money

Policy Oct 14

First EU AI Act enforcement test, and eight US states sue OpenAI

EU Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said the Commission's AI Office has sent formal information requests to OpenAI and Anthropic. The requests cover security, external evaluation and how models are monitored in use. The trigger was two summer incidents the companies disclosed, which the Office judged under-documented. No deadline was given.

The same day, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, Montana, Iowa, Kansas, West Virginia and Nebraska each sued OpenAI. The states allege ChatGPT lacks adequate safeguards for minors and that its safety claims are deceptive. They want disgorgement and an order barring those claims. On October 13, at a UK parliamentary hearing, OpenAI and Anthropic backed binding safety rules including mandatory incident reporting.

So what for TSD: None of this changes your API access today. It does mean that sooner or later SMB clients will ask what their chatbot says to kids and where the data goes. Write a one-page AI use and safety note for Front Desk and Concierge now so you already have the answer when they ask.
Euronews Bloomberg Law WIRED
Business Oct 13–14

Anthropic's IPO pitch meets an AI-credit wobble

After Anthropic's October 14 pre-IPO investor gathering, a Barchart analysis carried on Yahoo Finance puts prospective-buyer valuations at $1.8–2 trillion and cites leaked prospectus figures of $518 billion in signed compute commitments. It also reports a public S-1 targeted around October 25. Anthropic has confirmed none of these numbers. The Information reports Anthropic told investors that distillation accounts for 5–7% of revenue.

The same day, AI-infrastructure credit got shakier. CoreWeave's 2032 bonds touched a 15% yield and its stock fell about 15%. Anthropic also published an engineering post on Scion, its in-house git replacement for its monorepo. The design ideas are public, but Anthropic says Scion isn't suited for general use.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on yet. Watch for an S-1 around Oct 25: it would show Anthropic's real API margins and roadmap, which drive what Claude-based products will cost you in the long run.
