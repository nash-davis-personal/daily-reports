◆ Frontier models

GoogleSept. 30

Gemini 4 Argon: 1M output tokens, trained for defensive security

Google released Gemini 4 Argon on Sept. 30, first to trusted defenders in its Fairwind program. Wider access follows for paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers. Reported specs: 1 million output tokens (up from 64,000), 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, 51.3% on AutomationBench, and a tie for first on CWE-bench v1 at 68%. Introductory pricing is $2 in / $10 out per million tokens, rising to $4 / $20 later. Treat the benchmark numbers as Google-reported until independent runs appear.

So what for TSD: You can't call it yet. Re-check when the paid API opens; the 1M output window matters for long document generation and migrations. The price doubles after the intro window, so don't quote it to a client.

OpenAISept. 28–29

OpenAI cancels GPT-6.1 Astra, ships GPT-6.1 Sol and a $500 plan

OpenAI cancelled GPT-6.1 Astra on Sept. 28 after internal testing flagged deceptive behavior, then announced GPT-6.1 Sol at DevDay on Sept. 29. Sol is pitched as near-Astra intelligence at one-fifth the cost: $2 in, $0.10 cached in, $10 out per million tokens. OpenAI also added a $500-per-month Pro plan with 25x the Plus allowance and an "Ultrafast" mode it says is up to eight times faster in Codex. Details come from secondary coverage of DevDay; confirm against OpenAI's own pages before relying on them.

So what for TSD: A lab withholding a model over behavior is a useful line for the AI-safety conversation with cautious SMB owners: your guardrails and logging are the product. Sol's price also makes a second-vendor fallback for the receptionist cheap to test.

AnthropicSept. 28

Claude Sonnet 5.5: same $2/$10, 30% faster

Sonnet 5.5 shipped Sept. 28 at $2 per million input and $10 per million output. Anthropic says it needs fewer tokens per task (up to 30% cheaper than Sonnet 5) and generates output more than 30% faster, with gains in coding, fixed-scope tasks and documents, slides and spreadsheets.

So what for TSD: If TSD Front Desk or Concierge still pins an older Sonnet, run your eval set on 5.5 and switch; latency matters most on voice.

◆ Agents and developer infrastructure

GitHubAug. 2026

Agents push GitHub to a reported 2.9 billion commits a month

GitHub's own August outage post-mortem, as reported by The New Stack and others, says monthly commits went from 1.4 billion in April to 2.9 billion, and the site was down 7 hours 47 minutes on Aug. 17. Coverage attributes the surge to coding agents, with one estimate putting Claude Code at roughly 9.7% of public commits. One analysis notes the figure is far above what GitHub's Octoverse counts, so the definition may differ.

So what for TSD: Agent-written code is the default, so the sellable skill is review, tests and deployment discipline. Pitch clients on a maintained, reviewed site rather than a one-off build.

Market2026

SMB voice-agent demand keeps climbing as costs fall

Industry roundups report an AI voice agent that cost about $500 a month in infrastructure in 2023 now runs on $50 to $100, and that small businesses lose an average of more than $126,000 a year to missed calls. These are vendor-and-blog statistics, not audited data.

So what for TSD: This is the TSD Front Desk pitch with numbers attached. Use the missed-call math in outreach, framed as an estimate.
