◆ The labs are walking down to the metal

Breaking Aug 21, 2026

Anthropic put the founder of Google's TPU program on its compute team

Amir Salek started Google's custom-chip effort and ran the TPU business until 2022, delivering the first seven generations of those chips. Before Google he spent eight years at Nvidia, where he founded and led a system-on-chip design group working on GPU and Tegra parts. Since 2022 he has been a senior managing director at Cerberus Capital Management investing in semiconductors, AI and aerospace. At Anthropic he reports to James Bradbury.

Anthropic has not said it is building a chip. It does not have to. Hiring the person who took a hyperscaler from zero to seven shipped generations of custom silicon is the expensive part of starting, and it follows the AMD Instinct MI450 arrangement, the Google and Broadcom compute expansion, and a Broadcom-led debt hunt north of $60 billion for Anthropic-bound chips. Every frontier lab is discovering that the model is the easy half and the substrate is the hard half.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do today, but it sets your two-year assumption: the labs are fighting to get token costs down structurally, which is why per-token prices keep falling even as the hardware gets more expensive. Price your Managed AI retainers on your labor and your outcome, never on a token cost you are quietly betting stays flat.

Breaking Aug 22, 2026

Nvidia told customers AI server prices rise more than 15% early next year

Server manufacturers assembling systems for Microsoft, Google and Oracle have been notified of increases above 15% on machines shipping early next year, with the exact number depending on the chip generation and memory configuration. Vera Rubin and Grace Blackwell systems are both in scope. GB200 NVL72 racks already run $2.8–3.4 million each; pre-shipment inquiries on Vera Rubin NVL72 systems have come in at $5–7 million.

The cause is DRAM. Accelerators are only as useful as the memory paired with them, and Samsung, SK hynix and Micron have not scaled output fast enough to meet AI infrastructure demand. Nvidia carries roughly 75% gross margin and is the most valuable listed company in the world, and it is still choosing to pass the cost along rather than eat it.

So what for TSD: This is the floor under inference pricing eighteen months out. Any retainer you sign with unlimited AI usage baked in at today's rates is a bet you are making without being paid for it — put a usage ceiling and an annual repricing clause in the contract now, while it costs you nothing to ask for.

◆ The cheap end of the frontier keeps getting cheaper

New Aug 20–22, 2026

A free 1M-context model called ox-alpha is on OpenRouter and nobody has claimed it

It surfaced August 20 on OpenRouter and OpenCode under the provider name "stealth": a 1,048,576-token context window, text, image and video input, function calling, and $0 for input, output and cache reads during a preview stated to last about a week. No lab has publicly claimed it.

Community fingerprinting has converged on Z.ai. On August 22 researchers reported serving-layer evidence — a Java stack trace, an identical 1214 error code, and 30 of 30 tokenizer matches against GLM-5.3 — pointing at Zhipu. Treat the attribution as strong circumstantial evidence rather than confirmation; the operator remains unconfirmed as of this filing.

So what for TSD: A free million-token window is a cheap week of R&D. Dump an entire client codebase or a full document corpus into it and see whether your Concierge RAG pipeline is even necessary at that context length. Do not route client production traffic through an unattributed endpoint — you cannot tell a client where their data went.

New Aug 21, 2026

DeepSeek's V4-Flash-Vision-Exp claims two wins and two losses against Opus 4.8

DeepSeek shipped an experimental multimodal variant of V4-Flash on its API platform, keeping the text model's reasoning and general knowledge and adding image understanding. On the numbers it published, it scores 35.0 on ZeroBench — 100 deliberately brutal image-analysis tasks — against Opus 4.8's 34.0, and 27.3 on Agents' Last Exam, a set of 1,000-plus multi-step tasks requiring app interaction, code and media interpretation, against 25.7.

The caveat matters more than the numbers. DeepSeek evaluated using its internal Harness Minimal Mode, and none of it has been independently reproduced. Coverage generally read the result as a 2-2 split rather than a clean win.

So what for TSD: Vision at DeepSeek prices makes document-intake automation sellable to SMBs that would never pay frontier rates — think invoice, work-order and intake-form extraction for a trades client. Prototype against it, keep Claude on anything a client sees, and never quote a benchmark a vendor scored on itself.

◆ Agents, and whether they are working

Feature Aug 20, 2026

Antigravity now runs inside VS Code, JetBrains and Zed

Google put its autonomous coding agent into other people's editors. The VS Code extension is live in Microsoft's marketplace on macOS, Linux and Windows; JetBrains support covers IntelliJ IDEA, PyCharm, WebStorm, GoLand, CLion and Rider from version 2026.2.1; Zed is included; and a Visual Studio 2026 build is in preview for .NET work. The extensions give you an agent panel, inline diffs, plan inspection and multi-step delegation without relocating a project into the Antigravity 2.0 desktop app.

Eligible Gemini Enterprise customers can attach the agent to existing Google Cloud projects and governance controls, which is the part aimed at buyers rather than builders.

So what for TSD: The IDE lock-in play is over and harness quality is the whole competition now. Stay on Claude Code for long autonomous sessions, but it is worth one evening running Antigravity against a Next.js repo you know cold — if it handles a migration you would have done by hand, that is billable hours back.
The New Stack — Google's coding agent escaped its own IDE Google Cloud blog
Data Aug 22, 2026

About 90% of executives say AI has not raised their productivity

An Atlanta Fed study covering roughly 6,000 executives found the overwhelming majority report no measurable productivity gain from AI investment so far. Roughly a third of leaders use AI tools personally, and those who do average about 90 minutes a week. The researchers attribute most of the productivity improvement seen since 2021 to remote work and to headcount reduction in sectors like technology rather than to AI.

One finding is worth more than the headline: staff attitudes toward AI correlated with company productivity indicators, while management optimism about AI showed no statistically significant relationship to them. Companies are still citing AI in layoff decisions regardless.

So what for TSD: This is your best cold-open with a skeptical SMB owner — the spend is not landing for most companies, and you can say so before they do. It also tells you where the money is: adoption and workflow change, which is exactly what a Managed AI retainer sells, rather than another tool license.
