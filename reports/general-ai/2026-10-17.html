◆ What hits SMB clients

Data rights Oct 16 · effective Oct 30

Meta's new terms reach every website linked to a business page

Meta's updated commercial terms, effective October 30, let it use scrapers to collect content from websites "associated with" business accounts on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. Three lawyers who read the terms for Nieman Lab said the language could support training Meta's AI on that content, and that there's no obvious opt-out short of dropping the business account. One cautioned a court might not enforce it, but said publishers should assume it would. Meta notified users through a blog post and Business Suite earlier this month.

Meta's own crawler docs still say its bots respect robots.txt, and Meta-WebIndexer isn't on its list of agents allowed to bypass it. So the terms and the technical controls point in different directions for now.

So what for TSD: Almost every TSD client has a Facebook business page linked to their site. Before Oct 30, check each client's robots.txt for Meta's crawler agents and make a deliberate call per client. Most local businesses want to be found by Meta AI; a clinic, law office or anyone with proprietary pricing or content may not. Send clients a two-line note so they hear it from you first.
Nieman Lab Meta crawler docs
Commerce Oct 15–16

Google Universal Cart: one checkout across stores, inside Search and Gemini

Google launched Universal Cart in the US. Shoppers can hold products from multiple retailers in one cart inside Search and the Gemini app, get price-drop alerts, and pay through Google Pay in a few taps or hand the cart to the merchant's own site. Launch partners include Nike, Sephora, Target, Ulta, Walmart, Wayfair and select Shopify sellers; each remains the seller of record and handles support. Checkout runs on Google's Universal Commerce Protocol. Canada, Australia and the UK are next; YouTube and Gmail come later.

So what for TSD: For any client that sells online, product data quality is now a sales channel. Clean Merchant Center feeds, accurate structured data and Shopify's Google channel are what get a product into an agent's cart. That's a sellable audit for retail clients, and a reason to keep custom storefronts emitting full product schema.

◆ Labs and money

Business Oct 15–16

Anthropic's IPO valuation talk drops to $1.5 trillion

Reuters reported that investors at Anthropic's investor day discussed a $1.5 trillion valuation for its planned November share sale, down from roughly $2 trillion expected earlier. Anthropic told investors Q3 revenue was $16.8 billion, up about 45% from Q2's $11.6 billion and profitable on an adjusted basis; growth was about 147% the quarter before. Formal marketing could start the week of November 9 with a debut targeted before Thanksgiving. A separate FT report, relayed by newsletters and not independently checked here, says Anthropic offered Blackstone a yield above 10% to anchor part of a $60 billion debt package. All figures come from private meetings; the S-1 will be the first audited look.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do today. Two things to watch: a public Anthropic has to show margin, which usually means pricing and plan changes for API customers, and growth at 45% a quarter still means more capacity and model releases. Keep TSD products model-portable through AI Gateway so a price move is a config change.
Reuters via AI Weekly Implicator
Agents Oct 16

Perplexity open-sources SPACE, an agent containment sandbox

Perplexity released SPACE as open source, a sandbox for running AI agents with hard containment. It reports nine frontier models made 118 escape attempts with zero successes, its own test. CoreWeave, Akamai and Crusoe will offer it to customers. It lands a week after Anthropic said it cut live internet access from all internal agent evaluations, and amid a White House push for labs to report breaches.

So what for TSD: If TSD ever runs agents that browse or execute code for a client, the bar is moving to "contained by default." Vercel Sandbox already fills that role in your stack; SPACE is worth a read for the threat model, not a migration.
Semafor via AI Weekly
Research Oct 16

Terence Tao and 24 Fields Medalists criticize OpenAI's math push

The New York Times reports 25 Fields Medal winners, including Terence Tao, called OpenAI's approach to AI mathematics detrimental to the field. OpenAI has claimed a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem; the proof has not been verified.

So what for TSD: A reminder to treat lab capability claims as marketing until someone outside checks them. Same rule for TSD's own product pages.
