◆ Frontier models

Imminent Window opened Mon, June 22

GPT-5.6 enters its predicted launch window — but it isn't out yet

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 hit the front edge of the window traders had bet on: Polymarket put 83% odds on a ship between June 22 and June 28, on roughly $960K of volume. Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has called it a "meaningful improvement" over GPT-5.5. Leaks point to a context window pushed toward 1.5M tokens, a training cutoff into May 2026, and — most usefully — a redesigned reward-audit pipeline meant to fix the alignment failure OpenAI documented in its April "Where the Goblins Came From" post-mortem.

The hard caveat: as of today there is no official OpenAI announcement, no system card, and no API model string. Everything above is leaks and prediction-market money. Don't plan around a date OpenAI hasn't given.

So what for TSD: If a 1.5M-token, better-aligned GPT-5.6 lands this week, it widens your model bench for non-Anthropic builds and for clients who specifically want OpenAI. Keep your concierge/receptionist stacks model-pluggable so you can A/B it the day a real model string appears — and don't quote a client a capability until you've seen the card.

Still dark June 22 billing milestone

Fable 5 and Mythos 5 stay offline on the day they were supposed to start charging

Anthropic had originally said Fable 5 would be free in Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise plans through June 22, with usage credits kicking in June 23. That flip is now moot: both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 have been offline for every user since June 12, after a U.S. export-control directive ordered them suspended for any foreign national, including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees. As of today there's no new development — the directive stands and the models are still pulled.

So what for TSD: Your production stacks should be on a stable, available model (Opus/Sonnet generations), not anything that depends on Fable 5 or Mythos 5. This is the second straight week the frontier's top model is a model nobody can call — a reminder to never hard-pin a client deliverable to the newest release. Build the abstraction layer; let the model float underneath.
Anthropic — statement on the directive
Preview As of June 21

Gemini 3.5 Pro is still enterprise-preview only ahead of its June deadline

Google promised Gemini 3.5 Pro "by next month" at I/O, with a 2M-token window and a Deep Think reasoning mode. As of June 21 it remains in limited preview for select Vertex AI enterprise customers — not in the public Gemini app, AI Studio, or the general API. The clock is the story: Google's own June window closes June 30.

So what for TSD: A 2M-context Gemini at Google's API prices would matter for long-document RAG (handbooks, catalogs) like your concierge demos. Watch for the public API drop before month-end; until it's generally available it's not something you can quote.
Vertex AI release notes

◆ Builder tooling

Dev tools 3.8 — June 18; 3.7 — June 17

Cursor ships an /automate skill, cloud subagents, and a 90-second code reviewer

Cursor's June cadence has been heavy: version 3.8 (June 18) added an /automate skill plus new GitHub and Slack triggers; 3.7 (June 17) added cloud-environment setup and cloud subagents that run their own VMs; and a June 10 update has Composer 2.5 powering Bugbot with average review times down to ~90 seconds from ~5 minutes. The 3.7 line also added /review with selectable Bugbot and Security Review agents before you push.

So what for TSD: Even if you live in Claude Code, the pattern is the signal — agents are moving from "write the code" to "review and ship it for you." A pre-push security/bug review agent is exactly the discipline a one-person shop needs. Borrow the workflow: run an automated review pass on your own repos before client deploys, regardless of which tool you use.
