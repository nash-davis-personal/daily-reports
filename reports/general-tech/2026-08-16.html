Critical Aug 12–14, 2026

CVE-2026-58231: max-severity RCE in SAP Commerce Cloud, exploited in the wild

The flaw is an improper authorization bug in SAP Commerce Cloud's Data Hub Adapter extension. An unauthenticated attacker can abuse a default authentication client and submit crafted input to functions that lack sufficient validation, ending in remote code execution. CVSS 10.0. SAP shipped the fix on its August 12 patch day under security note 3771065 and told customers to apply it immediately.

Threat-intelligence firm Defused reported exploitation attempts against its honeypots three days later, on August 14. There is no public proof-of-concept, which is the detail worth sitting with: attackers diffed the patch and built a working exploit inside 72 hours. SAP's August patch day carried 28 new security notes in total, including a separate 9.6 in S/4HANA and Commerce Cloud.

So what for TSD: No direct action — you do not run SAP. The transferable lesson is the clock. Three days from public patch to live exploitation, with no PoC, is the new normal for anything internet-facing with a default credential path. Go audit every client project for default or shared service credentials still sitting in an env file, and make "we patch your dependencies monthly" an explicit, billable line in the Managed AI retainer instead of something you do for free when you remember.
BleepingComputer (Aug 14) · Onapsis — SAP Patch Day analysis · SecurityWeek
Clear Aug 13–16, 2026

Nothing new to patch on Next.js, React, Vercel or Supabase

Checked and clean for this window. No new security advisory landed on the Next.js repository, React Server Components, the Vercel platform or Supabase between August 13 and 16. The most recent coordinated Next.js security release remains the May 2026 batch of 13 advisories covering denial of service, middleware and proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning and XSS — including CVE-2026-23870, the high-severity Server Function DoS affecting React 19.x and App Router deployments on Next 13 through 16.

Next.js 16.3 remains the current feature line, carrying Instant Navigations, a faster dev server, and a set of agent-facing additions: bundled docs via AGENTS.md, first-party Skills for multi-step workflows, an Agent Browser with React introspection, and actionable errors with paste-ready prompts.

So what for TSD: Nothing to patch this weekend. If you have not already confirmed every client deployment is past the May 2026 advisory line, that is the one to verify — run npm audit across the client repos once and be done. The 16.3 agent tooling is worth an hour of your time: AGENTS.md plus first-party Skills means Claude Code can drive Next.js scaffolding with less hand-holding from you, which is delivery hours back.

◆ Platform changes on your stack

Platform Aug 13–14, 2026

Vercel ships Encrypted Client Hello, one-click Node 20 upgrades, and more agent plumbing

Encrypted Client Hello is now supported across the Vercel CDN for domains managed by Vercel DNS. ECH encrypts the server name in the TLS handshake, so a network observer sees a connection to Vercel rather than to a specific client site. It is on for eligible domains without configuration.

The rest of the week's changelog is developer-experience work: a dashboard control that upgrades projects off deprecated Node.js versions to Node 20 in one click, Grok Build added to the AI SDK harness layer, a meta adapter that lets any Agent Client Protocol harness plug into the AI SDK, Exa joining the Agent Marketplace for grounded search, and DeepSeek V4 Pro switching to updated weights on the AI Gateway with no code change.

So what for TSD: Two concrete jobs. First, run the Node 20 one-click upgrade across every client project still on a deprecated runtime this week — deprecated runtimes eventually stop building, and you do not want to discover that during a client emergency. Second, ECH is a free privacy line for proposals to clients who care about what their traffic reveals; it costs you nothing and it is already on.

◆ Capital, roads and orbit

Markets Aug 13–14, 2026

AMD raised $4.75 billion in its largest-ever dollar bond sale

AMD came to market with a four-part unsecured senior note offering maturing in 2029, 2031, 2033 and 2036, initially targeting $4 billion to $5 billion and pricing at $4.75 billion. Bank of America, JPMorgan, Barclays and Wells Fargo led the sale, which was expected to settle August 17. Proceeds are earmarked for general corporate purposes including debt repayment.

Context: AMD guided third-quarter revenue above estimates earlier in the month and said data-center sales would more than double by 2027. The bond sale is part of a broad wave of chip and hyperscaler borrowing to fund AI capacity — roughly $220 billion raised by tech companies for infrastructure so far in 2026.

So what for TSD: Chipmakers are funding AI capacity with debt rather than cash flow, which means the buildout now has interest payments attached to it. That does not change what you ship this month. It does mean the price of inference is eventually going to reflect somebody's coupon payments, so keep the usage ceilings in your retainer contracts and do not price a fixed monthly on the assumption that token costs only fall.
Bloomberg · Reuters via Yahoo Finance
Approval Aug 15, 2026

California cleared Waymo to charge for driverless rides across 18 counties

The California Public Utilities Commission approved Waymo on August 15 to operate and charge for fully driverless rides in 18 counties: Alameda, Contra Costa, Marin, Napa, Sacramento, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, Solano, Sonoma, Yolo, Los Angeles, Orange, Riverside, San Bernardino, San Diego and Ventura.

The permit covers freeways, highways, city streets, rural roads, parking lots, driveways and rail crossings, day and night, in rain, fog and hail. It opens Sacramento and San Diego as new markets and clears the full Bay Area and Los Angeles. Waymo says rollout will be gradual. As of May its service area was about 1,400 square miles across 11 cities with roughly 3,000 electric vehicles.

So what for TSD: Watch this as a regulatory template, not a product. A state utility commission just granted blanket operating authority for an autonomous service across half its population, and that same approval logic is what will eventually govern autonomous agents making commitments on a business's behalf. When a client asks whether an AI receptionist can legally book and confirm work for them, the answer is trending toward yes — write the audit logging into Front Desk now so you can prove what it said.
Electrek · Engadget
Record Aug 15, 2026

SpaceX launched two Falcon 9s 38.5 minutes apart

Saturday evening, a Falcon 9 lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 9:12 p.m. EDT carrying eight satellites for Globalstar, deploying all eight over roughly six minutes starting 56.5 minutes after liftoff. At 9:50 p.m. EDT — 38.5 minutes later — a second Falcon 9 launched USSF-366 for the US Space Force from Vandenberg. That is a new record for the shortest gap between two SpaceX launches.

So what for TSD: No client action. Worth keeping in your head as a benchmark for what a well-built pipeline looks like: two independent launch campaigns on opposite coasts, coordinated to under 40 minutes apart, because the process was designed to be repeatable rather than heroic. That is the same argument you make when a client asks why you template your deployments.
