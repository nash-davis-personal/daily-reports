Case study Sept 4

The Next.js team published its issue-triage agent in full

The backlog peaked at 3,109 open reports in January 2025 and was still 2,244 on August 10, 2026, against about 36 new reports a week. Auto-closing on inactivity had helped but was closing real bugs, because a timestamp cannot tell a fixed issue from an ignored one. So the team built closability, a research agent on eve, Vercel's open-source agent framework, running each investigation in a fresh sandbox loaded with the Next.js repo, Node, Playwright and Chromium. Per issue it reads the conversation, checks supported versions, searches related PRs, commits, releases and docs, attempts a reproduction on the reported version plus latest stable plus canary, and then explicitly looks for evidence contradicting its own conclusion. It returns structured JSON with a confidence score, a primary reason, an evidence list and reference links.

They ran it on GPT-5.6 Luna at max reasoning effort. Investigations averaged 30 minutes, so they scaled to 200 concurrent eve sessions. Results went into a Close Queue that maintainers actually read before acting. By September 4 that produced 1,462 closures and a backlog under 1,000, even with 218 new reports arriving: 37% already fixed, 19% duplicates, 16% expected behavior, 6% no longer reproducible, 5% unsupported, 17% other. Two design decisions carry the whole thing. The agent is read-only outside its sandbox — it cannot comment, close, push or deploy — and it is configured to ignore instructions found in issue text or repo content, which is the prompt-injection defense. They also shipped a GitHub Action letting anyone reopen a wrongly closed issue before the sweep started. The ongoing version reviews up to 100 stale issues every Monday, and full auto-close is limited to cases where closability scores 80+ and a second adversarial agent independently agrees, capped at 25 a week.

So what for TSD: This is the blueprint for a productized service, and it is free. The pattern — research agent proposes, human disposes, second agent argues the other side, hard cap on autonomous actions, read-only outside the sandbox — is exactly what an SMB needs for inbox triage, quote requests, warranty claims or review responses. Steal the shape wholesale: confidence score, evidence list, human queue, reopen path. It is also the answer to "what if the AI does something wrong" that you can point at a real company running it in public.

Breaking Sept 6–7

About 4,000 BTC left Blockstream's Liquid federation and the attackers want a patch first

Liquid, Blockstream's bitcoin sidechain, is paused and its bridge nodes are disabled after roughly 4,000 BTC — about $320 million — moved out of the federation wallet backing L-BTC on September 6. The treasury held over 4,200 BTC before; Blockstream's proof-of-reserves page now shows a little over 200. The mechanism was a consensus bug in Elements, the software underpinning Liquid: the withdrawal transaction looked valid under the buggy rules, so the federation's 11-of-15 multisig HSM signers signed it. The security model held exactly as designed and produced the wrong answer.

The party holding the coins immediately signed an on-chain message reading "we are whitehats. contact us on chain," and has said it will return most of the funds once the underlying vulnerability is fixed and every node is patched. Blockstream has since sent a PGP-signed on-chain reply: "Bridge nodes are patched, safe to return the funds." As of Monday the return had not completed.

So what for TSD: Not your market, but the lesson is portable and worth one line in your next security conversation. Multi-party approval does not protect you when the thing being approved is validated by broken code — every signer independently reached the same wrong conclusion. Applied to client work: if your booking or payment flow validates the same input the same way at every layer, you have one check, not five.

◆ Hardware and platforms

New Sept 7

Huawei's tri-fold ships on a domestic chip built by stacking, not shrinking

Huawei launched the Mate XT2 Ultimate Design in Guangzhou on Monday, its third tri-fold, with an inward-folding mechanism, a 10.2-inch 3K 120Hz main display, a 5,600 mAh battery and three 50MP rear cameras. It goes on sale in China September 12 at 19,999 yuan (about $2,980) rising to 24,999 yuan (about $3,725).

The interesting part is the silicon. The Kirin 9050 Pro uses what Huawei calls LogicFolding, a 3D stacking architecture that puts components vertically instead of laying them out on one layer, and Huawei claims a 42% performance gain over the original Mate XT from two years ago. That is a company routing around process-node restrictions with packaging rather than lithography, and getting a flagship-class part out of it.

So what for TSD: Nothing to buy, but keep the foldable form factor in the back of your mind on layout work. Tri-folds are still a rounding error in traffic, and a site that breaks between 1000 and 1400px CSS pixels is a real bug on far more common tablets. If your responsive testing stops at 768 and 1024, add a wide-tablet breakpoint to the checklist.

Business Sept 6

Apple is hunting App Store margin, and its store chief left over it

Mark Gurman reported that Apple, under new CEO John Ternus and services chief Eddy Cue, wants to raise App Store margins and pull more recurring revenue out of the platform. The specifics have not been disclosed and could land over the coming weeks or months. Gurman reports the push was a factor in Phil Schiller, the longtime executive running the App Store, stepping away from the role, on the view that the changes would further irritate developers, governments and regulators. The same newsletter noted Tim Cook's new pay package as executive chairman — roughly $2 million salary plus about $45 million in stock for 2027 — reads more like a CEO package than a retirement one. Apple has a product event scheduled for September 9.

So what for TSD: A reason to keep steering SMB clients toward web-first. Anything you ship as a responsive web app with a home-screen install avoids store fees and store policy entirely, and that argument gets stronger if Apple raises its take. If a client is set on a native app, price the store cut into the proposal now rather than after Apple announces.
