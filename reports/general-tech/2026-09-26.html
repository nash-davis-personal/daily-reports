◆ Your stack

Security Release Sept 30

Next.js will fix nine vulnerabilities on Wednesday, one of them critical

The Next.js team gave advance notice (posted Sept 23) of a scheduled release on Sept 30: one critical, two high, five medium and one low. Versions 16.3.7 and 15.5.27 ship alongside full advisories with affected versions. This is separate from last week's out-of-band 16.3.6 fix for the next/og remote-code-execution bug.

So what for TSD: Patch instruction: on Wednesday, bump every client site to 16.3.7 (or 15.5.27 on the 15 line), run the build, and push. Today, list every repo and its Next version so it's a one-hour job, and confirm last week's 16.3.6 bump landed everywhere.

Security September 25

About 16,000 Supabase databases are exposing personal data

UpGuard found publicly readable tables across roughly 16,000 Supabase-hosted databases, including names, addresses, phone numbers and user passwords: private chats on an adult streaming site, thousands of license plates from a US valet service, and contacts from an immigration service. The pattern is tables reachable with the public anon key and no row-level security.

Supabase's CISO Bil Harmer called projects "secure by default" and security a shared responsibility. Follow-up coverage ties many of the exposed apps to AI coding tools.

So what for TSD: Audit every client Supabase project this weekend: RLS enabled on every table in exposed schemas, no permissive policies, the service key never in the browser. Then sell that same audit (see Money Moves).

◆ Security, beyond your stack

Threat September 25

Kiteworks told customers to shut down servers ahead of a possible zero-day attack

The file-transfer vendor (formerly Accellion) said it received "credible threat intelligence from law enforcement" that an attacker may target customer systems, and asked for a six-hour shutdown while it investigates. It says all known bugs are fixed in 9.5.1 and worries about flaws it doesn't know about yet. Heise first reported an attack could come this weekend.

So what for TSD: Accellion's 2021 breach hit dozens of organizations. If a client sends files through Kiteworks, tell them today to follow the vendor notice and move to 9.5.1.

Breach Week of Sept 18–24

ShinyHunters claims it took FBI employee and applicant records

The group says it used a zero-day in the FBI's Oracle PeopleSoft system to pull 2–3TB of data, including names, home addresses, phone numbers, birth dates and spouse details. The claim is the group's own; treat the scope as unconfirmed.

So what for TSD: Another reminder that stolen PII fuels targeted phishing. Keep MFA on every client admin login you manage.

Compute September 25

Anthropic will pay Akamai $11.6B over seven years, mostly for CPUs

The deal could grow to about $20B, is the largest in Akamai's history, and comes with a warrant for up to about 5% of Akamai stock. Akamai expects about $5.5B in related capex; first services are due late Q2 2027. Akamai rose about 15%, and CoreWeave and Cloudflare ticked up.

So what for TSD: Agent workloads are driving demand for general-purpose compute at the edge. Expect more agent-hosting options from CDN-style providers you already know.

Space Sept 28

Starship Flight 14 is set for its first orbital attempt Monday

SpaceX targets Sept 28 with a 75-minute window from 7:15 a.m. CT at Starbase, with backups Sept 29–30. Ship 41 will carry 26 upgraded Starlink V3 satellites, the first operational payload for Starship.
