Status Next.js 16.2.6 · May 7

No new critical advisory this week — confirm everyone's already patched

There's no fresh critical Next.js, React, or Supabase advisory since the May coordinated release (16.2.6 patched 13 issues across middleware bypass, DoS, SSRF, cache poisoning, and XSS). The December RSC remote-code-execution chain (CVE-2025-55182 / CVE-2025-66478) is also old news — fixed months ago. So nothing new to chase; the risk this week is a client site still sitting on an unpatched older build.

So what for TSD: Run npm ls next react across every client repo and confirm each is on a patched 16.2.x (or has the relevant 15.x/16.0.x fix). Any site below the May release is exposed to known, public exploits — patch those before doing anything else.

◆ The AI buildout becomes a credit story

Markets Jun 15

Nvidia raises $20B in bonds — its first sale in five years, $85B in orders

Nvidia kicked off its first investment-grade bond offering since 2021 (when it raised $5B), targeting at least $20B across seven tranches maturing as far out as 2056, with Goldman, JPMorgan, and Morgan Stanley as bookrunners. Demand was enormous — roughly $85B in orders — and proceeds go to general corporate purposes including refinancing. It's the clearest sign yet that the AI datacenter boom is now being financed with debt, not just cash flow, alongside Goldman's $7.6T 2026–31 capex forecast.

So what for TSD: Long-dated debt funding this much compute means inference prices keep trending down for years. Price client AI features (Concierge, Front Desk) assuming token costs fall — promise generous usage in fixed plans and let margin expand as the cost curve drops.

◆ Chips & security

Geopolitics Jun 2026

China publicly questions the security of Nvidia and AMD AI chips

Chinese regulators raised security concerns over Nvidia and AMD AI accelerators, the latest move in an escalating chip-export standoff that already includes US export controls (and, this week, the export-control pull of Anthropic's Fable 5). The throughline: AI compute is now a national-security asset, and access to both chips and models is increasingly governed by policy, not just price.

So what for TSD: Indirect, but real — model and chip availability can shift on policy. Keep your client AI stack portable across providers so a single export action can't strand a deployment.
