◆ Your stack

All clear Jul 26, 2026

No new advisory to patch, and Vercel keeps shipping platform features

Nothing new has landed on Next.js, Vercel or Supabase security since the July 20 monthly release, which carried four high-severity and five medium-severity fixes covering Server Actions denial of service, middleware and proxy bypass, SSRF, cache confusion of response bodies, image-optimization DoS via SVG, unauthenticated disclosure of internal Server Function endpoints, and unbounded Server Action payloads on Edge. If any TSD or client project is still behind that release, it is the one thing to fix this week.

Feature side, Vercel shipped several things worth knowing about. WAF for Blob went to beta on July 24, letting you deny, challenge and rate-limit blob traffic with no code changes. Workflow steps now run up to 30 minutes on extended function durations. Claude Opus 5 landed on AI Gateway with BYOK, no markup on provider pricing and automatic failover. On July 23 the dashboard gained interactive terminals into running Sandboxes with shell access and file transfer, feature-flag evaluation metrics, and flag revision history in the CLI.

So what for TSD: Two concrete moves. Run npx next@latest --version across every live project and confirm each one is on or past the July 20 release, because the middleware-bypass and unauthenticated-endpoint-disclosure items in that batch are exactly the class of bug that turns into an incident on a client site. Then turn on WAF for Blob anywhere you serve client uploads or generated media — it is a rate-limit you get for free, and "we rate-limit your file endpoints" is a line worth having in the Managed AI retainer.

Exploit Jul 24, 2026

Public exploit code lands for a GitLab flaw patched six weeks ago

Researchers at depthfirst published working exploit code on July 24 for a GitLab vulnerability the company patched on June 10. The gap between a quiet patch and a public exploit is the window where unpatched self-hosted instances get found, and six weeks is a long time for a self-managed install to sit untouched.

So what for TSD: You are on GitHub, so this is not your exposure. It is a sales observation worth reusing: any SMB running self-hosted developer or business software inherits a patching obligation nobody at the company owns. That is the argument for managed hosting and a retainer, and it is more persuasive with a dated example than with a general warning.

Market Jul 23, 2026

DRAM spot prices are up roughly 700% in a year and manufacturers keep raising

Memory prices surged again on July 23 on AI data-center demand and constrained supply, lifting SK Hynix, Micron and SanDisk. A July 2026 Bloomberg report puts DRAM spot prices up nearly 700% over the past year. Samsung is pushing Q3 DRAM average selling prices up as much as another 20%, a third straight quarter of double-digit increases, and SK Hynix is reported to have scrapped long-term contract price caps. Samsung's DRAM ASP rose about 90% quarter over quarter in Q1 2026 and another 50–60% in Q2; SK Hynix posted roughly $38 billion revenue and a near-72% operating margin for the quarter ended March 31.

The cause is allocation, not a factory fire. HBM for AI accelerators carries better margins than commodity DRAM, so capacity moved. Samsung and SK Hynix have warned the shortage could run past 2027, with some analysts pointing beyond 2030, and Chinese suppliers are filling the gap — CXMT is now the world's fourth-largest memory producer.

So what for TSD: This is a quoting problem before it is a news item. Any project where you spec hardware for a client — a front-desk tablet, a kiosk, a shop workstation, a mini PC running a local display — is looking at materially higher device costs through the rest of the year, and phone and laptop prices follow. Put a 30-day expiry on hardware line items in proposals instead of eating the delta. It also strengthens the pitch for cloud-first: a Vercel and Supabase build has no memory bill of materials at all.

Capex Jul 24, 2026

Alphabet burned free cash flow to buy AI capacity, and Intel raised its own spend

Alphabet posted roughly negative $5.8 billion in quarterly free cash flow, a direct consequence of AI infrastructure spending, and raised its 2026 capital expenditure forecast to $195–205 billion. Intel lifted its 2026 capital spending plan from $18 billion to $20 billion after strong quarterly results and AI infrastructure demand. Both point the same direction: the largest balance sheets in tech are choosing capacity over cash generation.

So what for TSD: No trade here, and you are not the buyer of any of this. What it tells you is that per-token prices are being subsidized by an enormous capital build that nobody wants to admit might overshoot. Enjoy the cheap inference while it lasts, and avoid designing a client product whose unit economics only work if API prices keep falling. Price your retainers off today's cost with room, not off an assumed discount.

Funding Jul 23, 2026

Etched raises $300M at $10.3B on a transformer-only inference chip

Etched closed a $300 million Series C led by Sequoia at a $10.3 billion valuation, with a16z, Jane Street and SK Hynix participating. Its Sohu chip is a transformer-specific ASIC on TSMC 4nm that the company claims delivers a 20× inference speedup over Nvidia's H100. Etched was founded by three Harvard dropouts in 2022 and recently opened an 80,000-square-foot facility near its San Jose headquarters. Separately, AMD and Cerebras announced a partnership letting customers split inference workloads between AMD Helios systems and Cerebras hardware through Cerebras Cloud.

So what for TSD: Inference is where the silicon fight has moved, and that is the half of the market you actually buy. More competition below Nvidia means the per-token cost of the AI features in Front Desk and Concierge trends down over the next few years without you doing anything. Nothing to act on this week, but it supports pricing AI retainers on value delivered rather than on a cost-plus markup that will erode.
