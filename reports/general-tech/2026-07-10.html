◆ Security — patch this first

Critical Jul 8, 2026

GhostLock: any local user to root in five seconds, on essentially every distro

Researchers at Nebula Security disclosed CVE-2026-43499, a use-after-free in the kernel's cleanup path for priority-inheritance futex operations. It has been present in every mainstream distribution — Ubuntu, Debian, RHEL, Fedora, Arch — since a kernel change in May 2011. Exploitation requires nothing but a local login and CONFIG_FUTEX_PI=y, which is on by default everywhere Nebula checked. Their exploit is 97% reliable and takes about five seconds. It also escapes Docker and Kubernetes containers, which makes shared and cloud infrastructure the priority. Google paid the team $92,337 through kernelCTF. CVSS 7.8.

The upstream fix landed in April (commit 3bfdc63936dd), before anyone knew what it was. Distribution rollout has been uneven: as of early July, Ubuntu had patched its newest releases and some cloud kernels while 24.04, 22.04 and 20.04 LTS were still listed as vulnerable or in progress. A follow-up, CVE-2026-53166, fixes a crash the original patch introduced. No exploitation in the wild has been observed, but the exploit is published, so that window is closing.

So what for TSD: Your client sites on Vercel and hosted Supabase are somebody else's kernel — nothing to do there. What you must check today is anything you actually run: a VPS, a self-hosted Supabase instance, any Docker host, any box where a client or contractor has a shell. Run apt update && apt upgrade and reboot into the new kernel, then confirm with uname -r against your distro's advisory. Rebooting is the part people skip; a patched package on a running old kernel is still exploitable. If you have a client on their own Linux server, this is a legitimate, non-salesy reason to call them today, and it is exactly the kind of call that turns into a managed-hosting conversation.

Fix Jul 9, 2026

Vercel redacts Sensitive Environment Variable values from build logs

Build logs now mask the values of variables marked Sensitive. It is a small change against a common failure: a build step echoes its environment, or a framework prints config on error, and a service-role key ends up sitting in a log that more people can read than you think.

The same week, Vercel's AI Gateway added Grok 4.5 (July 8) and then Muse Spark 1.1 and GPT-5.6 Sol, Luna and Terra (July 9). Also shipped: 10x faster flag evaluation in the Flags SDK, and project settings management from the Vercel CLI.

So what for TSD: Two jobs. First, go mark your Supabase service-role keys and any API secrets as Sensitive in each project — the redaction only protects variables you have flagged. Second, the Gateway now carries every model from yesterday's briefing behind one interface, which is the abstraction layer worth standardizing on. One integration, swap the model when the price or quality math moves.

Breaking Jul 1–10, 2026

Supabase: Postgres 14 is gone, and supabase-js is dropping old TypeScript

Support for Postgres 14 ended July 1, and projects still on a deprecated version are being automatically upgraded to the latest available Postgres. On July 10, Supabase posted a deprecation notice that @supabase/supabase-js will require TypeScript 5.0. Its July developer update also announced an OpenCode integration, alongside work across realtime, GraphQL, wrappers and Multigres.

So what for TSD: An automatic major-version Postgres upgrade is the kind of thing that silently breaks an old client project on a Tuesday. Inventory which client databases were on 14, and smoke-test each one's auth and queries. Then check the TypeScript version pinned in any project you haven't touched in a year, before the next npm install decides for you.

◆ The buildout

New Jul 9, 2026

Meta puts its own AI chip into production in September, chasing 14 gigawatts

Per an internal memo reported by Reuters, Meta will begin manufacturing "Iris," a data-center accelerator designed in-house under its MTIA program, in September. TSMC fabricates it; Broadcom is the design partner. Testing reportedly took six weeks and surfaced no major issues — notable for a silicon effort that has struggled for more than five years.

Iris is the first of four planned generations, with Meta intending to ship a new chip roughly every six months through 2027, against an industry norm of a year or more. The company is targeting 7 gigawatts of compute capacity in 2026 and 14 gigawatts in 2027.

So what for TSD: Custom silicon is why Muse Spark 1.1 can be priced at $1.25/$4.25 and why the price floor keeps falling. You are a buyer in this market, not a seller. The planning assumption for any AI product you quote: inference cost drops meaningfully every few months. Price your retainers on the outcome delivered, never on a token-cost passthrough, and let the falling floor become your margin instead of your client's discount.

Deep tech Jul 7–8, 2026

Oratomic raises $300M to skip straight to fault-tolerant quantum

The neutral-atom startup closed a $300 million Series A co-led by ARCH Venture Partners, Spark Capital and Khosla Ventures, with Bezos Expeditions, Index, General Catalyst, Bain Capital and Scott Aaronson participating. Oratomic traps individual atoms in reconfigurable optical tweezers, letting qubits physically move mid-computation. Its pitch, from research with Caltech, is that a utility-scale fault-tolerant machine may need around 10,000 reconfigurable neutral-atom qubits rather than the millions usually assumed.

So what for TSD: Zero near-term relevance to anything you ship, and that is the honest read. Worth one line of attention only because the "millions of qubits" assumption is what has kept post-quantum cryptography off everyone's roadmap. If a credible path to 10,000 holds up, TLS and key-rotation timelines for client infrastructure move from theoretical to schedulable — years out, not months. Nothing to do today.
