◆ Your stack, this morning

Pricing September 9

Vercel moved Password Protection to $20 per project per month

Pro teams used to buy password protection once at the team level and apply it everywhere. As of September 9 it is billed per project at $20 a month, enabled or disabled from Security > Deployment Protection inside each project. Turning it off on a project stops future charges for that project. Vercel paired the change with a reminder that protecting production deployments remains free on every plan.

The practical read: the feature got more flexible and, if you had it switched on broadly, more expensive. A studio with five client projects sitting behind a shared password now has a $100/month line item where it previously had one flat charge.

So what for TSD: Open the Vercel dashboard today and walk every project under Security > Deployment Protection. Turn password protection off anywhere it was habit rather than a client requirement — demo sites, stale previews, anything already gated another way. Where a client genuinely needs it, that $20 belongs in their monthly retainer line rather than your overhead.

Billing September 8

Flat Rate CDN went generally available for Pro teams

Flat Rate CDN swaps usage-based CDN billing for a fixed monthly bill covering fast data transfer, blob data transfer, CDN requests and observability events. Pro teams get 1M requests and 1TB of transfer in the base tier, with higher tiers for more. New Pro teams have it on by default; existing teams opt in through Billing settings.

Predictability is the whole pitch. Usage-based CDN charges are the line that spikes when a client's site gets picked up somewhere, which is exactly the month you do not want a surprise invoice to explain.

So what for TSD: Pull last three months of CDN usage from Billing and compare against the flat tier. If your portfolio sits under 1M requests and 1TB — likely, for a book of SMB sites — opting in converts a variable cost into a fixed one you can price retainers against with confidence. Do the comparison before switching; the flat rate is not automatically cheaper, it is automatically predictable.
Vercel changelog — Flat Rate CDN
Improve September 8

Vercel Sandbox routing got 18x faster by moving to regional replicas

Sandbox now resolves public domains from regional replicas rather than a centralized store. Median routing latency dropped from 62ms to 3.4ms, with p99 improvements up to 112x in Sydney and 146x in Cape Town. The change is automatic on all sandbox domain requests, with no pricing change.

Same window, Vercel added GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst to the AI Gateway, following GPT-6 Astra on September 4 and Ling 3.0 Flash Sante on the free tier.

So what for TSD: If you have been avoiding Sandbox for anything user-facing because of cold routing latency, the objection is gone — it is now viable for agent-run preview environments and client-facing code execution demos. The AI Gateway additions mean you can A/B image models behind one key instead of wiring a second provider.

◆ Security

Security September 8

Researchers used AI to build a zero-click WeChat worm in days

Security firm Calif built a proof-of-concept worm exploiting a flaw in WeChat's call stack, using AI tooling to find the vulnerability and weaponize it within days. Reporting on the work described a worm that could have spread across the platform in hours had it been released. Tencent patched the affected versions.

Set this next to Google's threat tracker from the same day and the pattern is consistent: the discovery-to-exploit interval is collapsing on both sides of the fence.

So what for TSD: Patch windows that used to be measured in weeks are now measured in days. Set Dependabot or Renovate to auto-open PRs on every client repo and commit to shipping security patches within 48 hours as a stated line in your retainer — it is a real differentiator against the "we built it and left" shops you compete with.

Breach September 8

220 million traveler records sat behind default credentials

A public Advance Passenger Information System database linked to Vietnam exposed roughly 220 million passenger and crew records — passport numbers, names, dates of birth, nationalities and flight details spanning 2017 through 2026. Researchers reached the system through a cloud-access path protected by default credentials.

No exotic exploit, no zero-day. The largest traveler-data exposure of the year came from a credential nobody changed.

So what for TSD: Audit every client Supabase project for service-role keys sitting in client-side code, and every third-party integration for a default or shared credential you set up during a build and never rotated. This is a fifteen-minute sweep that prevents the single most embarrassing kind of incident on a site you own the reputation for.

Policy September 8

China set a 9,800 EFLOPS compute target for 2030 behind a $532B program

MIIT's five-year plan commits 3.8 trillion yuan — about $532 billion — across 2026 to 2030 to quadruple national AI compute from roughly 2,450 EFLOPS as of July 2026 to 9,800 EFLOPS. Alongside it, Huawei is testing a Kirin 9050 Pro built on a LogicFolding architecture that stacks logic vertically to gain performance without access to leading-edge nodes.

On the Western side of the same race, Intel disclosed it has processed more than one million 300mm wafers on High-NA EUV in under two and a half years, and ASML is designing larger High-NA photomasks — roughly 40% more productivity — to allow bigger AI processor dies. Arm launched Neoverse CSS N4, an 8-to-128-core platform for TSMC N3P aimed at cloud providers building custom server silicon.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on this week, but this is the supply side of the token prices you build margin on. More global inference capacity through 2027 means the per-call cost of Front Desk and Concierge keeps falling while your retainer price stays put. Price contracts on today's cost rather than on a hoped-for drop, and pocket the difference.
