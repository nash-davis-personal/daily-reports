◆ Patch this first

Exploited Aug 11

CVE-2026-68820: a socket-driver bug attackers are already using for SYSTEM

Microsoft shipped fixes for 421 CVEs on August 11. The one under active exploitation is CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free in afd.sys, the kernel-mode driver behind the Windows Sockets API. A crafted application triggers a race condition and elevates to SYSTEM. Researchers point at nation-state activity based on the history of similar driver bugs, which in practice means it will be commodity tooling soon.

Also worth pulling out of the pile: CVE-2026-62832, a publicly disclosed User Profile Service elevation-of-privilege; remote code execution in Windows DNS, Deployment Services TFTP, Microsoft QUIC and HPC Pack; and CVE-2026-62911 in Exchange Server. The breakdown is 236 Windows fixes, 98 in Office, 30 in SharePoint, 26 in developer tools, 17 in Azure. Separately, CISA added CVE-2026-8037, a command injection in Progress LoadMaster, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and the SharePoint auth-bypass disclosed August 6 as SCTPhantom (CVE-2026-64564) is still worth confirming as patched on any client running SharePoint on-prem.

So what for TSD: Update your own Windows machines and say the words "Patch Tuesday, elevation of privilege, patch now" in your next client check-in. For clients on Microsoft 365 with an on-prem Exchange or SharePoint box still running, this is a billable morning of work you can offer today. On the web side there is nothing to do, which is the useful half of the answer.

◆ Changes on the stack you ship

Feature Aug 10

Vercel Functions accept Bun.serve as an entrypoint, WebSockets included

The Bun runtime for Vercel Functions now takes Bun.serve() as a function entrypoint. You write a server.ts at the project root with a routes map, set "bunVersion": "1.x" in vercel.json, and the server you ran locally deploys as-is with no framework wrapper. Add a websocket handler and call server.upgrade(request) inside fetch to promote matching requests.

The pricing detail is the part that matters. WebSocket connections run on Fluid compute with Active CPU pricing, so you are billed for time spent processing messages rather than for holding the connection open.

So what for TSD: This removes the reason you have been avoiding realtime on client projects. A live booking-availability feed, an agent status stream in Concierge, or a "your quote is ready" push no longer needs a separate always-on server, and an idle connection at 2am costs nothing. Try it on one internal project before you put it in a client quote.
Vercel changelog · WebSockets docs
Breaking Week of Aug 9

Self-hosted Supabase now defaults to Envoy instead of Kong

Supabase made Envoy the default API gateway for self-hosted deployments this week, replacing Kong. Anything depending on Kong's HTTPS listener, a custom kong.yml, or the kong service name by hostname will break on upgrade unless you explicitly opt back in. Separately, since August 5 specifying an explicit version when creating or updating a Postgres extension on Supabase is deprecated.

Hosted Supabase projects are unaffected by the gateway change.

So what for TSD: Your client projects are on hosted Supabase, so this is a no-op today. It matters the first time a client asks to self-host for data-residency reasons, which given the AI-provenance rules in this morning's General AI brief is a conversation that is coming. Note the extension-version deprecation in any migration scripts you reuse.

◆ Capital is buying the physical layer

New Aug 10–11

Nvidia lined up six asset managers to mobilize over $500 billion for AI compute

Nvidia signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to establish independent AI compute infrastructure financing platforms, targeting more than $500 billion of third-party capital over time. Nvidia is not lending. The six partners underwrite and deploy capital through their own platforms, and Nvidia connects its customers to that capital. The buildout stays off Nvidia's balance sheet.

The intent is to make GPU compute a financeable asset class in the way commercial real estate or toll roads are — something you borrow against on a long amortization. Jensen Huang called the chips an "investable asset" on CNBC. Deals are expected to reach market within months.

So what for TSD: Nothing to buy. The read is that inference capacity keeps expanding on someone else's balance sheet, which supports the case that per-token prices trend down over the next two years. Keep quoting Managed AI retainers on today's costs and let the margin improve underneath you.
Nvidia newsroom · CNBC
New Aug 11

Sony and TSMC put $4.7 billion into a Japanese image-sensor venture

Sony Semiconductor Solutions and TSMC agreed to form Advanced Vision Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp in Kumamoto, next to TSMC's existing JASM plant. Sony is the controlling shareholder at ¥465 billion (about $2.92 billion) including a cash-and-asset transfer of its newly built Kumamoto factory; TSMC puts in ¥282 billion. Sony leads core sensor technology, product planning and design; TSMC supplies the advanced process. Volume production targets 2029, with additional funding assumed to depend on Japanese government support.

So what for TSD: A 2029 production date on smartphone camera sensors is background, not action. File it as evidence that the chip supply chain keeps concentrating in Japan and Taiwan, which is the real variable behind hardware costs on anything you spec for clients.
Sony Semiconductor · Reuters via Investing.com

◆ Robots and aircraft change hands

Markets Aug 10–11

Unitree's Shanghai listing drew about $1.2 trillion in orders

Unitree Robotics priced its STAR Market IPO at 150.8 yuan on August 6, selling 40.45 million new shares — 10% of enlarged share capital — to raise 6.1 billion yuan (roughly $904 million) at a valuation near $9 billion. Retail demand ran to about 8,288 times the offer, roughly 9.8 million orders and 8.1 trillion yuan of subscription value, with a lot-winning rate around 0.018%. It is the first pure-play humanoid robot company to list on a mainland Chinese exchange.

So what for TSD: Nothing actionable, and the valuation math (219× earnings) is its own warning. The useful signal is that "humanoid robotics" now has a public comparable, which means the AI-adjacent hardware narrative has somewhere concrete to point. Expect it in client conversations as a reason they think everything is about to change.
Reuters via Yahoo Finance · Bloomberg
M&A Aug 10–11

Archer is buying Wisk, Insitu and SkyGrid from Boeing for equity

Archer Aviation signed definitive agreements to acquire three Boeing subsidiaries: Wisk Aero's autonomous eVTOL program, Insitu's uncrewed aircraft business, and SkyGrid's airspace-management software. Boeing takes a stake of nearly 20% in Archer, agrees to invest up to $55 million in an upcoming Archer round, and keeps cross-licensed access to Wisk's autonomous flight systems for its own commercial and defense platforms. Closing is expected by the end of 2026 subject to regulatory review.

So what for TSD: Consolidation in a capital-hungry sector, paid in stock rather than cash. Nothing to act on, but it is a clean example of the pattern where the incumbent trades an unprofitable program for a stake in whoever might finish it.
TechCrunch · CNBC
