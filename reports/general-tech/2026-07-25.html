◆ Your stack this week

Stack watch Jul 25, 2026

No new advisory to patch — Supabase and Vercel keep shipping features

Nothing new landed this week that forces an emergency update on Next.js, React, Tailwind, Vercel or Supabase. The July Next.js security release — the eight-CVE batch, four high-severity, hitting 16.2 and 15.5 — is already out and covered in the last several briefings; if you've upgraded, you're current. On the feature side, Supabase's July developer update added binary payloads to Realtime Broadcast, a MongoDB foreign-data wrapper in Wrappers v0.6.2, and an OpenCode integration; Vercel continued its full-stack push (project-level "Services," Dockerfile support, agent tooling, AI Gateway and AI SDK updates).

So what for TSD: Confirm every client project is on the patched Next.js line — that's the one non-negotiable. Otherwise this is a build week, not a fire drill. Supabase's MongoDB wrapper is worth a look if any client data lives outside Postgres, and Vercel's project-level Services model can simplify how you ship multi-part client apps with atomic rollback.

◆ The chip cycle turns

Earnings Jul 23, 2026

Intel posts its fastest growth in 15 years — and the stock falls anyway

Intel reported Q2 revenue up 25%, its fastest quarterly growth in almost 15 years, helped by AI-infrastructure demand for its server chips. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.42 (GAAP was a $2.16 loss). It also confirmed 18A had landed its first commercially recognized external customer in volume — a major cloud provider — a real milestone for its foundry ambitions. Despite the beat, the stock kept sliding, down roughly 28% on the month after a 278% first-half surge, as investors rotated out of the chip sector.

So what for TSD: Nash doesn't buy chips, but the froth coming off the semiconductor trade is an early tell that the AI-infrastructure boom has a valuation ceiling. Practically: watch for it to ripple into cloud and inference pricing over the next few quarters — cheaper compute would help COGS on Concierge and Front Desk, a capex pullback could tighten it.

Geopolitics Jul 2026

Taiwan indicts an ex-TSMC manager over alleged chip-secret theft for China

Taiwan indicted a former TSMC manager for allegedly stealing chip secrets on behalf of China — reportedly the first case tying a TSMC manager to a Chinese semiconductor materials-analysis company. It lands as India commissioned its third semiconductor plant, another sign that governments now treat advanced-chip capacity as strategic infrastructure to protect and duplicate.

So what for TSD: Background risk, not an action item. The takeaway for a builder is durability: assume the chip and compute supply chain stays politically contested, so keep client stacks portable across model and cloud vendors rather than betting a client's whole product on one provider's chips or pricing.
