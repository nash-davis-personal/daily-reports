◆ Your stack and dev tools

Security status checked Oct 4

No new advisories for Next.js, React, Tailwind or Supabase; 16.3.8 and 15.5.27 remain the patched versions

The Next.js advisory page still tops out at the Sept. 30 batch: the high-severity SSRF in Image Optimization, the critical next/og remote-code-execution fix from Sept. 22, and three moderate/low cache and Draft Mode leaks. Nothing has shipped above those since. React's latest is still 19.3.0 (Sept. 9), Tailwind sits at 4.3.3 (July 16), and Supabase's changelog has nothing new since the Oct. 1 OrioleDB beta. Vercel's changelog is quiet aside from the already-known First Input Delay sunset on Nov. 1.

So what for TSD: Same open item as the last few days: confirm every client repo reads 16.3.8 or 15.5.27 and close it out this weekend. Nothing new to pull in, so this is just a verification pass, not a scramble.

◆ Platforms

Platform Oct 4

Google shipped Googlebook, a ChromeOS-Android fusion laptop OS, with real hardware on sale today at $899+

Googlebook OS is built on the Android stack with ChromeOS's desktop shell and multi-window support bolted on, and it drops Windows-app compatibility that Chromebooks had been adding. Five launch laptops went on sale in the US today — Acer Googlebook 14 from $899, Dell XPS Googlebook from $999 or $1,199, HP and Asus Googlebook 14 at $1,299, and Lenovo Googlebook 15 at $1,299 — running Intel Core Ultra or Snapdragon X Elite chips, up to 2.8K OLED touchscreens, and Gemini baked into the OS (Magic Pointer, Magic Cues, Rambler voice-to-text). Google is promising 10 years of direct OS updates, Pixel-style, and is pushing Play Store "desktop" app categories alongside the browser. UK, Ireland, France, Germany and Australia get it Oct. 5. Google says current Chromebooks keep support, but court filings suggest ChromeOS gets phased out over several years.

So what for TSD: Google's own pitch is that "70% of time on laptops is in a browser," so your Next.js sites and PWAs still work fine on day one — add a Googlebook-class Chrome build to your QA matrix and confirm your PWA install prompts fire correctly on it. Longer term, budget Chromebooks (common in SMB front-desk and school-adjacent client setups) are on a slow path out; don't spec new client hardware around ChromeOS-only assumptions going forward.

◆ Money, chips and the AI buildout

Big tech Oct 2

Anthropic is targeting a pre-Thanksgiving IPO while the FTC investigates it and Oura's own listing stays shelved

Dario Amodei is pushing to list before Thanksgiving despite fading IPO sentiment generally: smart-ring maker Oura postponed its $2.1 billion Nasdaq debut on Sept. 29 citing market uncertainty, and another recent offering priced below range. A leaked Anthropic filing shows a roughly $8 billion operating loss against fast-growing revenue, and the FTC is reportedly reviewing both Anthropic and OpenAI over consumer-risk concerns. Anthropic confidentially filed its draft S-1 back in June; lead underwriters are reported as Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Citigroup.

So what for TSD: No direct action, but it's a signal the capital behind Claude's pricing is under real market scrutiny right now. Keep a second model wired in behind every client agent as a fallback, and don't assume current Claude API pricing is locked in past year-end while this IPO and the FTC review play out.
SiliconANGLE ↗ · CNBC (Oura) ↗
Chips & storage Oct 2

Toshiba's plan to double hard-drive output wiped 11% off Seagate and 7% off Western Digital in a single session

Nikkei Asia reported Toshiba will spend roughly ¥60 billion ($400 million) to double its HDD production capacity by fiscal 2027, aiming to grow its market share from about 10% to 30% to fill an AI-driven chip-memory supply gap. Seagate and Western Digital, whose stocks had run up 240% and 170% this year on the thesis that tight hard-drive supply would keep AI-datacenter storage prices high, dropped 11% and 7% respectively on the news. Morgan Stanley, Citi and Rosenblatt called the selloff overdone; the broader memory sector (Micron, SK Hynix) was unaffected.

So what for TSD: If more HDD supply eventually eases storage pricing, that flows through to cloud storage costs over time — worth revisiting Supabase storage tiers and backup-provider pricing in a quarter or two, but nothing to act on today.

◆ Security beyond your stack

Crypto infra Oct 2

Core Lightning warns attackers are actively targeting Bitcoin nodes that haven't patched to 26.06.8

Blockstream's Core Lightning team issued an urgent advisory saying attackers are scanning for and hitting nodes still running v26.06.7 or earlier. Version 26.06.8, shipped Sept. 22, closed three vulnerability classes, including a channel-closing flaw that could force operators to forfeit funds through Bitcoin's penalty mechanism, plus bugs that could crash nodes or exhaust memory. No confirmed fund losses have been disclosed, but the gap between patch and active exploitation was about 10 days.

So what for TSD: TSD doesn't run Lightning infrastructure today, but if a future client project ever adds Bitcoin/Lightning payment rails, treat node patching exactly like a Next.js advisory: subscribe to release notes and patch within 48 hours, not weeks.
