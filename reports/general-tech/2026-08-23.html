◆ Patch this week

Patch Ships Aug 26, 2026

Next.js 16.3.3 and 15.5.24 fix one critical vulnerability on Wednesday

Vercel's pre-announcement, published August 20 by Josh Story, Karim Rahal and Sebastian Silbermann, confirms a scheduled security release on August 26 addressing one critical severity issue. Both 16.3.3 and 15.5.24 publish alongside the full advisory covering impact, affected versions and upgrade instructions. This is the security-release program Vercel announced in July: you get warning that something critical is coming, and no detail about what it is until the fix exists.

Practically, that means the exploit window opens the second the advisory goes live, because the advisory is the disclosure. Anyone watching for a Next.js critical will be reading it at the same time you are.

So what for TSD: Put Wednesday morning on the calendar now. Inventory every client site's Next major today — 16.x goes to 16.3.3, 15.x goes to 15.5.24 — and stage the bumps so Wednesday is `npm i next@x.y.z`, push, verify the Vercel deploy went Ready. Anything still on 14 or below gets no patch at all, which is the upgrade conversation you should be starting with that client this week.

Advisory AA26-231A

CISA, NSA and the FBI: Siemens S7 PLCs are being actively targeted with AI-written exploits

The joint advisory describes threat actors running reconnaissance and capability development against US-based Siemens S7 Series programmable logic controllers. They use internet scanning services to locate exposed controllers running outdated firmware or otherwise poorly protected, then deploy AI-generated exploitation scripts disguised as legitimate monitoring tools. CISA's framing was unusually direct: this is an active threat, not a theoretical one.

Named sectors are critical manufacturing, energy, water and wastewater, chemical, food and agriculture, and commercial facilities. Recommended actions are the unglamorous ones — inventory your S7 units, patch them, get them off the public internet, tighten access control, and actually watch the logs. The notable detail for everyone else is the method: AI-generated attack tooling has moved from demo to field use.

So what for TSD: Not your gear, but it is your pitch. If any Charlotte-area manufacturing, water or food-processing prospect runs Siemens controllers, this advisory is a legitimate reason to call on Monday — and the general lesson generalizes to what you do sell: anything of a client's that answers on the public internet is being scanned by tooling that now writes its own exploits.

◆ Platforms changing the rules under you

New Aug 20, 2026

Google shipped a button that lets readers make a site a preferred source

Publishers can now embed an interactive Preferred Sources button on their own pages. A reader clicks it, the site is registered as a preferred source on their Google account, and they are dropped back exactly where they were. The payoff is placement: preferred sources surface more in Top Stories, AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google's Mrinalini Loew said more than 600,000 unique sources have been selected to date.

Google is pitching this as an answer to AI-driven traffic loss, which is a fair description of the problem it created. A companion Discover change is also rolling out, letting users tap a three-dot menu and describe in their own words what they want more or less of.

So what for TSD: This is a real, cheap SEO deliverable you can add to any content-producing client — a blog, a local news-ish site, a trades company that publishes. Embedding the button is a fifteen-minute job and it is the kind of concrete "we did a thing Google just launched" line that justifies a retainer month.

Feature Aug 20, 2026

ChatGPT will read, search and send your Apple Messages if you give it the keys

OpenAI's Apple Messages plugin for the macOS app covers iMessage, SMS and RCS: search conversations, summarize threads, draft replies, send. It runs on Apple silicon Macs only, not Intel, and it is available on every tier including free, inside ChatGPT Work and Codex modes. Pinned-chat syncing between Mac and iPhone improved in the same update, along with read-only snapshots for local Codex threads.

The price of entry is Full Disk Access plus contact-name and automation permissions. Sends require per-message approval by default, and you can make that permission persistent for a specific chat, which is the setting worth thinking about twice.

So what for TSD: Full Disk Access on the machine you do all client delivery from is a real blast radius — client credentials, contracts, and every Supabase key you have ever pasted live on that disk. If you want it, put it on a separate account. And it is a preview of the SMS-assistant feature clients will start asking Front Desk for.

◆ Hardware, vehicles and consolidation

Recall Aug 21, 2026

Tesla is recalling about 3 million vehicles in China over door handles

The recall covers Model 3, Model Y, Model S and Model X vehicles built in China or imported between March 4, 2019 and April 29, 2026. Tesla's notice states that severe collisions can cause the vehicle's low-voltage system to fail, and that the resulting door-handle failure could stop occupants getting out quickly and impede rescuers from outside. It is the largest vehicle recall ever recorded in China, and part of a country-wide push against hidden and flush door handles.

The remedy is software plus stickers: an over-the-air update to the window control software so windows automatically lower when a crash is detected, and warning labels affixed to recalled vehicles. Deficient driver-monitoring systems are covered in the same action.

So what for TSD: The interesting part is regulatory, not automotive — a design choice that shipped for seven years got reclassified as a safety defect, and the fix was pushed over the air. Any product you build with a remote-update path inherits that same expectation: if you can fix it remotely, you will be expected to.
