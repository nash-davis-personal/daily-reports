◆ Your stack

Status as of Nov 27

No new advisory for Next.js, React, Tailwind or Supabase

The Nov 3 Next.js security release (16.4.5 and 15.5.29, seven fixes, the top one a cross-tenant cache disclosure when unstable_cache and use cache are nested) is still the latest security line. npm's latest tag is 16.4.6, a Nov 9 bug-fix release on top of it. The separate React Server Components RCE from Oct 14 (fixed in React 19.0.9, 19.1.10, 19.2.9 and 19.3.1) also stands; upgrading Next.js itself is the safer route because it vendors the Flight runtime.

Supabase CLI 3.4.0 (Nov 24) now starts new projects with a commented JSON config. Nothing to patch.

So what for TSD: Nothing new to do. If any client repo is still below Next.js 16.4.5 or 15.5.29, that is the one item on the list; run npx next --version across repos before Monday.

◆ Security

Breach Nov 24–25

Hackers reportedly breached a CIA analysis system, with reports linking the Artex AI framework

Reporting says an open-source intelligence analysis system at the CIA was breached and that the intrusion also reached systems at the ODNI and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency. Reports say only unclassified information was exposed. One outlet ties the attack to Artex, the open-source AI penetration-testing framework released in July and since forked more than 12,000 times. The agencies have not published details, so treat the attribution as reported.

Microsoft has already linked Artex to state and criminal groups that use it for scanning, password spraying and OAuth application abuse, in some cases moving from a first foothold to cloud admin rights in under three hours.

So what for TSD: Artex automates the cheap attacks that hit small businesses: sprayed passwords and rogue OAuth apps. For every client on Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, confirm MFA is on for every account and review which third-party apps hold mail or drive access.

Patching Nov 25

Ubuntu is criticized for taking nine or more days to ship critical kernel fixes

A post on the oss-security list lays out Ubuntu taking nine or more days to ship fixes for several critical Linux kernel CVEs. Separately, sudo-rs output on Ubuntu 26.04 broke Ansible's password-prompt detection; Ansible Core 2.22.0 fixes it.

So what for TSD: Low exposure while client apps run on Vercel and Supabase. If any client keeps a self-managed Ubuntu box (a mail relay, an old WordPress host), put unattended upgrades and a livepatch check on it.

◆ Commerce

Retail Nov 25–27

Adobe expects $12.9 billion online today and $15.1 billion on Cyber Monday, mostly on phones

Adobe forecasts US online sales of $12.9 billion for Black Friday, up 9.2%, and $15.1 billion for Cyber Monday, which would be the first US online day above $15 billion. Its Thanksgiving forecast was $6.9 billion with a record 63% on phones. Actual spending from Nov 1 to Nov 22 was $82.5 billion, up 9.1% and ahead of the full-season forecast of $275.1 billion. Buy now, pay later is forecast at a record $21.3 billion for the season.

So what for TSD: Phones carry this weekend. Check the mobile product and checkout pages on every client store today; a slow image or a broken pay button costs more on Monday than any other day of the year.
