◆ Your stack: patch and check

Security Nov 3, 2026

Next.js's November security release ships today with seven medium-severity fixes

Announced Oct 27, the scheduled release lands today and covers seven medium vulnerabilities; full advisories with affected versions and upgrade steps are published alongside it. As of this filing the Next.js blog had not posted them. This follows the Oct 14 out-of-band update, which fixed a critical remote-code-execution bug in image optimization (patched in 16.4.1 and 15.5.28).

So what for TSD: Patch instruction: tonight, check every client repo is at least on 16.4.1 (or 15.5.28), then bump to today's patched version once the advisory posts, run the build, and push. Log each site and version in the client's care notes; that log is the proof a site-care plan sells.

Next.js blog: upcoming release ◆ Oct 14 update
Breaking change Effective Nov 10, 2026

Supabase disables a subset of TLS 1.2 ciphers on project HTTP APIs next Tuesday

Supabase's changelog says clients that don't support modern standards "will fail to connect" to Auth, Data APIs, Storage, Realtime and Edge Functions after Nov 10. It puts affected traffic below 0.0001%, so browsers and current Node runtimes are fine. Risk sits with old embedded devices, legacy kiosks or very old server runtimes calling Supabase directly.

So what for TSD: A five-minute check: confirm no client integration (an old POS, a kiosk, a legacy PHP webhook) calls a Supabase project directly. If one does, test it against a modern TLS endpoint before Nov 10.

Supabase changelog
Breach Nov 2, 2026

Nvidia's Brev platform may have exposed SSH keys for customer cloud machines

Tom's Hardware reports Nvidia told Brev customers about unauthorized activity spanning 16 days in October that may have exposed SSH keys used to reach customer VMs. Nvidia says it found no evidence of misuse so far.

So what for TSD: If any GPU or dev box was ever spun up on Brev, rotate those keys today. Generally, keep deploy keys per project so one leaked platform never opens every client.

◆ Big tech earnings

Earnings Nov 2, 2026

Apple's first quarter under John Ternus: $114.5B revenue and a record fiscal year

For the quarter ended Sept 26, Apple reported revenue of $114.5B (+12%), net income of $30.7B, or $2.10 a share, and iPhone revenue of $57.6B (+17%), a September-quarter record despite supply constraints, with the iPhone Duo and iPhone 18 Pro in the lineup. MacRumors tallies Mac +23.7%, Services +9.8%, Wearables −7.9% and iPad −10.4%. Shares slipped after hours, per StockTitan's wrap.

So what for TSD: Mac growth plus a strong iPhone cycle means client customers are browsing on new, fast devices. Design and test for the newest iPhone screen sizes first; most SMB site traffic is mobile.

Earnings Nov 2, 2026

Palantir's revenue jumps 96% and commercial customers now outspend the US government

Palantir's Q3 release shows revenue of $2.313B, up 96% and above $2B for the first time, GAAP net income of $1.128B, a 62% adjusted operating margin and $1.216B of adjusted free cash flow. US commercial revenue passed US government revenue for the first time. Consensus had been about $2.0B. The stock still fell after hours.

So what for TSD: The fastest-growing enterprise AI vendor's growth is now ordinary businesses paying to wire AI into operations. That demand exists at SMB scale too, at prices TSD can serve.

◆ Chips and the money behind the buildout

M&A Nov 2, 2026

Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at about $115B

Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares each, about $1,132 a share and a 23.9% premium; Marvell holders end up with about 67% of the combined company. Marvell targets more than $1B in yearly cost savings from a "full-stack connectivity" portfolio of chips and optics for AI data centers. Both stocks fell sharply on the day (Lumentum −18.6%, Marvell −12.7%).

So what for TSD: No direct action. The signal is that moving data between AI chips is now worth a $115B bet, so compute supply for the APIs TSD rents keeps growing.

Financing Nov 2, 2026

Oracle sells $10B of stock and $10B of notes to fund its AI cloud

Oracle filed concurrent $10B common-stock and $10B senior-note offerings (floating and fixed notes maturing 2029 to 2046). It already carried $125.3B of debt as of Aug 31. The shares fell 8.8% on the day.

So what for TSD: Hyperscalers are now paying up to borrow for AI capacity. If that money gets tighter, API prices stop falling as fast; keep client AI pricing with margin for that.
