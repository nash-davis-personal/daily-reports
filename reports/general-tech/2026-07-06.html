◆ Security — the one to patch

Advisory Early July 2026

"Bad Epoll" gives unprivileged users root on Linux and Android

CVE-2026-46242 is a race-condition use-after-free in the Linux kernel's epoll subsystem that lets an unprivileged local process escalate to root. It was demonstrated in Google's kernelCTF with high reliability, and the impact extends to Android devices. Any server you run that a low-privilege process can touch is in scope.

So what for TSD: Your client apps live on Vercel and Supabase (managed, patched for you), so the direct exposure is low — but if you run any Linux box you SSH into (the desk Mac's remote host, a VPS, a self-hosted job), run your package manager's update and reboot into the patched kernel this week. Confirm your Supabase/hosting providers have rolled the fix; managed platforms usually do it silently.

Stack watch Next.js 16.2.10 · Jul 1

Next.js, Vercel, and Supabase are quiet on security this window

No new Next.js security advisory dropped over the holiday. The most recent releases (16.2.10 and 15.5.20 on July 1) were housekeeping — republishing a WASM package that had been missing since 16.2.4. The security release you should already be past is 16.2.6 (May 7), which fixed 12 advisories including DoS via Server Components and middleware/proxy bypasses. The 16.3 preview is adding Turbopack persistent build cache and first-party Skills.

So what for TSD: Nothing urgent, but confirm every client repo is on 16.2.6 or later — that's the line that matters. If any project is still on 16.2.4/16.2.5, bump it while it's quiet rather than during the next scramble.

◆ The compute buildout keeps getting heavier

Chips July 2–3, 2026

Anthropic in talks with Samsung for 2nm custom AI chips

Anthropic has entered discussions with Samsung to manufacture custom AI silicon on a 2nm process. It follows OpenAI's own inference-chip reveal last week — every major lab is now trying to own its compute stack down to the wafer to escape Nvidia pricing and supply.

So what for TSD: More custom silicon means cheaper, more plentiful inference over the next 18 months — good for your unit economics on every AI product. No action, but it's why betting your retainers on per-seat AI value (not per-token markup) is the durable model.

Infrastructure Early July 2026

Google's power use jumps 37% as a Virginia data center gets cancelled

Google reported a record 37% surge in electricity consumption driven by AI data-center expansion. At the same time, QTS cancelled a Virginia data center project amid community opposition — the first signs that local politics and the grid, not chips, are becoming the binding constraint on the buildout.

So what for TSD: This is the macro backdrop, not a to-do. But it's a useful honesty point with skeptical clients: AI has real physical costs and limits, which is exactly why a focused, cheap, single-purpose tool (a receptionist, a concierge) beats "put AI on everything." Sell the scoped win.

IPOs & funding Early July 2026

China's physical-AI companies line up for public markets

Unitree Robotics was approved for a $619M IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market, and Kling's AI-video unit raised $2.8B at roughly an $18B valuation ahead of a Hong Kong listing. Capital is flooding into robotics and generative video — the "physical AI" and media-generation categories are where the next wave of money is going.

So what for TSD: Generative video is the adjacent category worth watching for your business. You already run ffmpeg pipelines and have shipped AI UGC ads for Gallant. As Kling-class video keeps getting cheaper, a productized "AI video ad" add-on for existing clients is a real upsell — no new core skill required.
