Advisory Release Jul 20 · Next.js 16.2 / 15.5

A Next.js security release lands Sunday — get your upgrade path ready now

Next.js has a scheduled security release targeted for July 20, shipping patch releases for the 16.2 and 15.5 lines and fixing multiple issues — reported as four high and five medium severity. Per the Next.js security-release program, CVE details are published only once the patch is out, so the specifics land Sunday. This follows May's cluster of Server Components / App Router / Middleware fixes (DoS, SSRF, XSS, cache-poisoning), so the surface area is familiar.

Nothing to install yet — the point today is to be ready. Know which sites run 16.x versus 15.x, confirm your proxy.ts deployments are on a pinned version you can bump fast, and clear a maintenance window for Monday.

So what for TSD: Direct patch instruction. Inventory every client site by Next.js major/minor today. When the release drops July 20, bump 16.x sites to the 16.2 patch and 15.x sites to 15.5, redeploy on Vercel, and smoke-test. This is also a clean reason to ping every client with a "we already patched you" note — free trust, and a natural lead-in to a managed-maintenance retainer.

Zero-day Jul 14 · Patch Tuesday

Microsoft fixes a record ~570 flaws, two already being exploited

Microsoft's July 2026 Patch Tuesday is its largest ever — roughly 570 CVEs by most counts (some firms tally up to 622 including Chromium and mid-month updates), with three zero-days. Two are under active attack: CVE-2026-56155, an Active Directory Federation Services flaw that grants admin privileges, and CVE-2026-56164, an unauthenticated network-exploitable SharePoint privilege-escalation bug. A third, CVE-2026-50661, is a publicly disclosed BitLocker security-feature bypass. The release also rolls out a Kerberos encryption overhaul.

So what for TSD: You don't run AD FS or SharePoint, but plenty of your SMB clients' back offices do — and the AD FS bug is exactly how a small business gets its whole Microsoft 365 tenant taken over. If you touch any client's Windows/365 environment, tell them to apply July updates now, and never stand up a client integration against an unpatched on-prem SharePoint.

◆ The compute buildout

Cloud Jul 2026

Meta Compute enters the cloud market to sell AI power

Meta launched Meta Compute, a cloud business that sells GPU capacity, hosted Llama models, and AI-agent tooling to outside customers — its first real move to monetize AI infrastructure beyond advertising. Reports say Meta plans to lean on its custom MTIA chips and supply chain to undercut AWS and Azure by 20–30%. The launch rattled AI-compute stocks and puts a fourth serious bidder in a market that was effectively AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud.

So what for TSD: More cloud competition is good for a solo shop's cost base — cheaper GPU and hosted open models over time. You won't move off Vercel/Supabase for client sites, but a cheaper hosted-Llama tier is worth watching for the batch/back-office AI jobs where you don't need frontier Claude and want to protect margin.

Semiconductors Jul 14

A memory supercycle is lifting chip and cybersecurity stocks

AI demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM), DDR5, and storage is driving a capital-spending boom: DRAM equipment spending is forecast to hit ~$37B in 2026 and 3D NAND ~$14B. On July 14, cybersecurity and AI-chip stocks rallied (partly on strong signals around IBM) while some software names sold off — a market rotating toward the hardware and security layers of AI rather than the app layer.

So what for TSD: The second-order effect reaches you: a memory supercycle means RAM, SSDs, and cloud instances get more expensive before they get cheaper. If you're spec'ing a self-hosted or VPS piece for a client, lock pricing and lean on serverless (Vercel/Supabase) where you're not eating hardware inflation directly.
