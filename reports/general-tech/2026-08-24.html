◆ Your stack, this week

New Aug 24, 2026

Vercel Sandbox is globally available across four regions

Vercel shipped it this morning: Sandbox now runs in iad1 (Washington DC), sfo1 (San Francisco), cle1 (Cleveland) and cdg1 (Paris). You select the region per sandbox, and you can configure failover so a region problem does not take the workload down with it.

Sandbox is the isolated ephemeral compute Vercel sells for running code you do not trust — user submissions, agent output, arbitrary builds. Single-region meant every execution paid the latency of one fixed hop and inherited that region's failure domain. Four regions with explicit placement is the version you can put in front of paying customers.

So what for TSD: This is the missing piece for any TSD product that runs code a client typed or an agent wrote. Put east-coast client workloads on iad1 and stop eating cross-country latency on every execution, and set failover before you sell it rather than after an incident. If you ever wanted to offer a client a "paste your data and we'll transform it" tool, the safe execution layer for that is now GA-grade.

Patch Aug 26, 2026

Wednesday's Next.js release fixes one critical vulnerability

Third mention in this briefing and the last one before it lands. Under the security release process announced in July, Next.js pre-announced on August 20 that August 26 brings a single critical severity fix, published as 16.3.3 and 15.5.24 with the full advisory covering impact, affected versions and upgrade instructions. Nothing about the vulnerability itself is public until Wednesday.

The whole point of the advance notice is that teams stage the upgrade before the disclosure gives attackers a map. That window is today and tomorrow.

So what for TSD: Today, run the inventory: every client site, which Next major, which exact minor. Anything on 16.x goes to 16.3.3 Wednesday and anything on 15.x goes to 15.5.24, so confirm now that each project can take a patch bump without a build break. Do the boring dependency check today so Wednesday is a version bump and a deploy rather than a day of debugging.

◆ Search moved and nobody sent a memo

Search Aug 18–21, 2026

Google's August 2026 spam update rolled out and completed in two and a half days

The rollout started Tuesday August 18 at roughly 12:30 pm ET and Google confirmed completion on August 21 at 4:50 am ET. It applies globally and to all languages. This is the third spam update Google has announced in 2026, after March and June, and unusually there was no companion blog post and no new spam policies announced with it — a routine enforcement pass against the guidelines already published.

This one slipped past the last few editions of this briefing, which is worth saying plainly. Spam updates do not get the coverage core updates do, and they still move rankings.

So what for TSD: Pull Search Console for every client property and compare the week of August 21–27 against the two weeks before. If a site dropped, the update finished on the 21st, so the damage is already fully applied and any recovery pitch you make is honest. If nothing moved, that is a concrete line for the next check-in: "Google ran a spam update on the 18th, I checked your traffic on the 24th, you held." Clients pay retainers for exactly that sentence.

◆ Silicon and orbit

Chips Aug 19, 2026

Samsung raised advanced foundry prices by up to 15%, and TSMC may follow in January

Samsung raised prices on new foundry orders by up to 15%, concentrated on its advanced 4nm (SF4) and 5nm (SF5) nodes, with the increases taking effect in July. Chinese and US customers absorbed 10–15% relative to the prior month; Taiwanese customers saw 5–10%. AI demand has filled the 4nm lines beyond what Samsung can accommodate, and industry estimates frame this as the turn for a foundry business that has lost money since 2022. TSMC is separately reported to be considering increases of up to 10% across all nodes starting January 2027.

Read alongside Saturday's news that Nvidia told server partners AI system prices rise more than 15% early next year on DRAM costs, and a pattern is visible: every layer between sand and a token is raising price at once, and none of them are absorbing it.

So what for TSD: Same conclusion as the Nvidia story, now with a second data point behind it. Do not sign a Managed AI retainer with uncapped usage priced off today's inference costs. A usage ceiling plus an annual repricing clause costs nothing to ask for at signing and is nearly impossible to add later.

Space Aug 21, 2026

SpaceX flew its 100th orbital mission of 2026

Starlink 15-20 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 12:25 am PDT on August 21 carrying 29 Starlink satellites. Booster B1100 was flying its ninth mission, having previously launched the NROL-105 national security payload and seven earlier Starlink batches. It landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You about eight and a half minutes after liftoff — the 220th landing on that vessel and the 653rd Falcon booster landing overall.

Elon Musk says the constellation is now past 11,000 satellites in orbit. Hitting 100 orbital missions before the end of August puts the year's cadence ahead of any prior year.

So what for TSD: Nothing to build, but it is a live sales fact for rural Gaston and Cleveland County prospects. Any trades business outside cable coverage that has been told it cannot run a real booking system or a cloud POS is working from a 2021 assumption about connectivity, and that objection is now answerable.
