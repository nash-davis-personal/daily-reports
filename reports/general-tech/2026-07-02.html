Action Jul 1–9, 2026

Supabase deprecated Postgres 14 and is changing self-hosted + logging defaults

Supabase's changelog lands three dated changes in a one-week window. Postgres 14 is deprecated as of July 1, and any project still on a deprecated version gets auto-upgraded to the latest available Postgres. The week of July 6, the default self-hosted config for API_EXTERNAL_URL changes to include the /auth/v1 path prefix. And for new projects created after July 9, the Postgres log_connections default flips from on to off.

So what for TSD: Check every client Supabase project's Postgres version this week — a surprise auto-upgrade can break a live client site if you're pinned to old behavior. If you run any self-hosted Supabase, the API_EXTERNAL_URL path change will bite auth flows; update it before the 6th.

Patch CVE-2026-44578

Next.js WebSocket SSRF — a standing patch item for any self-hosted deploy

CVE-2026-44578 (published May 11, CVSS 8.6) lets an unauthenticated attacker use a crafted WebSocket upgrade request to make a self-hosted Next.js server issue an internal GET to any host reachable on port 80 — cloud metadata endpoints, internal APIs, admin panels — and read the response. It affects the built-in Node.js server on 13.4.13+ through <15.5.16 and 16.0.0 through <16.2.5. Roughly 79,000 exposed instances were still reachable in scans. Vercel-hosted apps are not affected.

So what for TSD: Your Vercel-hosted client sites are in the clear. But if any prospect or client runs Next.js on their own box (a common find when you audit a lead's site), this is a clean, credible reason to reach out: "you're one version behind on a high-severity CVE — I can patch it." Upgrade path is 15.5.16 or 16.2.5.

Markets Jul 7, 2026

SpaceX joins the Nasdaq-100 three weeks after a record IPO

SpaceX, which went public June 12 in the largest IPO ever at a $1.77T valuation and closed its first day up 19%, joins the Nasdaq-100 on July 7 under a special rule Nasdaq adopted for faster inclusion. The IPO raised roughly $86B, explicitly earmarked in part for orbital AI-compute infrastructure to serve xAI. It's now among the most valuable US-listed firms.

So what for TSD: Mostly a signal, not an action — but "space company raising $86B to build AI data centers in orbit" is the clearest sign yet that AI compute, not launch, is where the money is pooling. The buildout keeps pulling capital toward anything AI-infra adjacent.

Chips Q1/H1 2026

The semiconductor supercycle keeps setting records — and dragging the tape when it wobbles

Global chip sales hit $298.5B in Q1 2026, up 25% year over year, with the industry tracking toward roughly $975B for the full year on AI-infrastructure demand. Supply stays tight through at least year-end. The flip side showed on July 1: the Nasdaq slipped 0.66% as chipmakers cooled after the strongest quarter since 2020, a reminder of how concentrated the market has become in a handful of AI names.

So what for TSD: Tight supply and rising compute cost is why cheaper model tiers (Terra, Sonnet 5 promo) matter for your margins — inference isn't getting free, so price AI retainers with headroom for token costs.
