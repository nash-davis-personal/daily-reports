◆ Your stack

Behavior change July 30 → effective Aug 10

Vercel will stop stripping Server-Timing headers, and eight other changes landed the same day

The one with a deadline: starting August 10, 2026, Server-Timing response headers pass through to the client and land in the browser's Performance API. That is useful if you want real backend metrics in your RUM data. It is a leak if any of your routes emit timing entries named after internal services, database queries or third-party calls, because those names become visible to anyone who opens devtools on a client site.

The rest of the July 30 batch is upside. Deployments are up to 7 seconds faster — as much as 5 seconds of platform overhead removed automatically, plus about 2 more on the latest CLI. mcp-handler 2.0 supports the new stateless MCP protocol, so an MCP server no longer needs Redis behind it. The @vercel/sandbox SDK can now run multiple isolated agents in one sandbox using separate Linux users and groups. Turborepo and Remote Cache added OIDC so CI workflows can authenticate without long-lived tokens. On AI Gateway: GPT-5.6 Luna is 80% cheaper and Terra 20% cheaper, Sol's fast mode runs 2.5× quicker at double the price, and two new models arrived — MiniMax H3 for 2K text-and-image-to-video, and Thinking Machines' Inkling Small, an open-weights multimodal model with a 1M-token context window and controllable thinking effort.

So what for TSD: Do the Server-Timing audit this week, before August 10 does it for you. Grep every client project for Server-Timing and for any middleware or observability wrapper that sets it, and rename or drop anything descriptive. Then take the free wins: bump the Vercel CLI on every project for the deploy speedup, and if you have been putting off shipping an MCP server for Concierge because of the Redis dependency, that excuse is gone.
Vercel changelog — July 30
Clear July 29–31

No new advisory on anything you ship — the week's KEV addition was network gear

Nothing new to patch on Next.js, React, Tailwind or Supabase since yesterday's edition. The most recent CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities addition, on July 29, was CVE-2026-20316, a hard-coded password in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center. Two older entries carried federal remediation deadlines of July 30: an OS command injection in Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator On-Prem, and a sensitive-information exposure in Fortinet FortiOS that lets an unauthenticated attacker bypass patches with crafted HTTP requests. Next.js remains on the monthly security cadence, with the July 20 release covering nine CVEs fixed in v16.2.11 and v15.5.21.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do on your own code. The relevant question is whether any client runs their own firewall or SD-WAN appliance, because those three CVEs are all edge devices and all actively exploited. If a client has an IT vendor, one email asking whether they are patched is worth sending, and it costs you nothing to be the person who asked. Confirm every client project is on Next.js 16.2.11 or 15.5.21 if you have not since July 20.

◆ Big tech reported, and the market split

Earnings July 30

AWS grew 37%, its fastest in eighteen quarters, and Amazon guided capex to $220 billion

Amazon reported Q2 net sales of $200.6 billion, up 20% from $167.7 billion, against consensus near $196.5 billion. Operating income rose 43% to $27.5 billion. AWS did $42.2 billion, up 36.7% year over year — an annualized run rate of $169 billion, well past the 31% analysts expected — with $16.6 billion of operating income at a 39.4% margin. Andy Jassy said the AI and chips businesses each cleared $25 billion in run rate. Trailing-twelve-month purchases of property and equipment hit $169 billion, up 64%, and Jassy put full-year capital spending at roughly $220 billion. The stock rose more than 10% after hours.

So what for TSD: Cloud reacceleration at this scale means the compute glut people predicted has not arrived, so do not plan around inference prices collapsing further on their own. The useful read for you is the direction of Amazon's spend — custom silicon and AI capacity — which is why the OpenAI and Anthropic price cuts keep coming. Budget client AI features on today's prices and treat every cut as margin, rather than pricing a retainer on a discount that has not happened yet.
CNBC Amazon press release
Earnings July 30

Apple posted its best June quarter ever and fell on a Services miss

Apple reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $109.4 billion for the quarter ended June 27, up 16% year over year, with net income of $29.8 billion and diluted EPS of $2.02, up 29%. iPhone revenue was $54.25 billion, up 22% and ahead of the $53.86 billion consensus. Services came in at $30.74 billion against expectations near $31.22 billion. Tim Cook called it the strongest June quarter in company history, with double-digit growth across iPhone, Mac and Services and in every geography. The stock fell after hours on the Services line.

So what for TSD: A 16% revenue beat that trades down on one soft segment is the market telling you what it is paying for: recurring revenue, at a premium to hardware sales. That is the same argument for restructuring TSD toward managed AI retainers instead of one-off site builds. Same work, different multiple — and it is an easy line to use with a client who runs their business on projects and wonders why cash is lumpy.

◆ Space and infrastructure

Contract July 29–30

The Space Force gave SpaceX all 18 launches of its orbital early-warning constellation

Two task orders worth about $1.6 billion cover 18 Falcon 9 launches from Vandenberg through the end of 2027, carrying Space-Based Sensing and Targeting satellites — the Pentagon's orbital early-warning layer for detecting airborne threats and passing targeting data to forces in near-real time. It is the first task order since the Space Force raised its National Security Space Launch ceiling to $17 billion, and it puts SpaceX above $7 billion in Pentagon contracts this year. An acquisition official described the timeline from requirement to signed contract as roughly two months, which they called unprecedented.

So what for TSD: Not directly actionable, but the procurement speed is the signal — a two-month federal acquisition cycle means the buying side is willing to move fast when the capability already exists and the vendor is the obvious one. That is the same dynamic that closes SMB deals: show a working thing, be the only person who can wire it into their systems, and the decision timeline collapses.
The Defense Post Tech Times
