◆ On your stack

Clear August 2

No new advisory landed, and the Vercel changelog has not moved since July 31

Checked this morning: nothing new on Next.js, React, Vercel or Supabase since the last briefing. The most recent Vercel changelog entries are still the July 31 batch — AI Gateway team and project spend budgets, updated DeepSeek V4 Flash weights served by default, a dedicated AI Gateway logs page, Vercel MCP on the 2026-07-28 spec, and Passport reaching general availability.

The standing patch floor has not changed. The May 2026 coordinated release fixed 13 advisories across denial of service, middleware and proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning and XSS, and everything is patched in Next.js 15.5.18 and 16.2.6. Earlier minors of 15.x and 16.x are not getting backports.

So what for TSD: No action required this weekend. If any client project is still pinned below 15.5.18 or 16.2.6, that is the one outstanding item, and it will not be fixed by waiting — those minors are never getting a patch. Run npx next --version across the portfolio once and close it out.

Exploited Ongoing

The vulnerability an AI agent actually used this week was Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-3055

The agent-driven campaign detailed in today's AI briefing landed its confirmed compromises through CVE-2026-3055, an out-of-bounds read in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway configured as a SAML identity provider, scored 9.3 on CVSS v4.0. Citrix patched it on March 23 and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on March 30. Fixed versions are 14.1-66.59, 13.1-62.23, and 13.1-37.262 for the FIPS and NDcPP builds.

The interesting part is the age. This is a four-month-old patched bug that an autonomous agent found, selected, and exploited without a human picking the target.

So what for TSD: Not your stack, so no direct action. It matters for any client running an on-premise Citrix gateway, which in Charlotte means the larger dental, medical and legal offices that still have a VPN appliance in a closet. That is a real, specific opening line for a security conversation with an SMB that has never bought anything from you.

◆ Hardware and the buildout

New August 2

Mexico became the second-largest supplier of servers to the United States

Mexican server manufacturing hit $46.9 billion in year-to-date sales to the US, putting it behind only Taiwan. The AI datacenter buildout needs assembled machines faster than the traditional supply chain moves them, and final assembly has migrated to the closest large manufacturing base with the right labor and trade posture.

So what for TSD: Background, but useful background. The compute you rent through Vercel and Anthropic is now physically assembled a few days' truck ride from Charlotte, which is one reason inference prices keep falling instead of rising. Cheap compute is the substrate every TSD product margin sits on.
Techmeme — Financial Times, August 2
Cool August 1

Defcon 34 badges ship an open-source chip you can verify

This year's Defcon badges include Baochip-1x, an open-source chip whose security properties are independently verifiable, and which doubles as a hardware security token. The pitch is that you can inspect the silicon design rather than take a vendor's word for what the secure element does.

So what for TSD: Nothing to buy today. Worth watching as the "prove it, do not trust it" pattern spreads from chips into AI supply chains, which is the same argument that will eventually sell verified-agent work to clients.
Techmeme — Wired, August 1

◆ Security, and what AI is doing to work

Breaking August 1

A Coldcard exploit drained 1,196 Bitcoin wallets in a 41-minute window

Roughly $70 million was stolen from 1,196 Coldcard wallets during a 41-minute window on July 30, disclosed August 1. Coldcard is a hardware wallet, the product category people buy specifically to avoid this outcome. The speed is the story: a single window, over a thousand victims, no opportunity for anyone to react in time.

So what for TSD: No crypto exposure here, so this is a lesson rather than a threat. Anything that concentrates a single point of failure across a thousand customers fails a thousand times at once. That applies directly to a shared API key or a single service-role Supabase credential reused across client projects. Separate keys per client, and rotate on offboarding.
Techmeme — CoinDesk, August 1
Breaking August 2

Apple capped bug report submissions after a flood of AI-assisted reports

Apple introduced a submission cap and a 30-day cool-off period on Feedback Assistant bug reports, citing the volume of AI-assisted submissions. Researchers can request higher quotas. It follows similar throttles at curl and the Internet Bug Bounty, and it is the first time a vendor of Apple's size has formally rate-limited AI-assisted disclosure.

So what for TSD: The generalizable version of this is showing up everywhere. When AI makes producing a thing nearly free, the receiving side starts charging for attention. Expect the same pattern in inbound sales: cold email that reads as agent-generated is going to get filtered harder every quarter, which raises the value of Grant and Bishop showing up in person.
Techmeme — Financial Times
Business July 31

Chime cut about 10% of staff and put AI in the memo

Chime Financial cut roughly 150 of its 1,500 employees. CEO Chris Britt told staff that AI is changing what is possible but requires new skills, and that smaller teams with fewer layers are moving faster and getting more done. The company framed it as a flattening rather than a retrenchment. Shares moved 0.4% on the day.

So what for TSD: A public, named company just said out loud that a smaller team plus AI beats a larger team. That is the exact argument TSD makes about itself, and it now has a citation attached. Use it in the pitch: a one-person studio that compounds itself with agents is the same structural bet a $1B fintech just made on stage.
