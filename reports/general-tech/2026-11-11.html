◆ Security: patch today, rotate this week

Advisory Nov 11

Node.js HIGH-severity security releases for 22.x, 24.x and 26.x are scheduled for today; Next.js 16.4.6 and the Better Auth update round out the patch list

The Node.js project's pre-announcement (posted Nov 3) says new 26.x, 24.x and 22.x versions will ship "on or shortly after" Wednesday, Nov 11, with the highest severity fixed in each line rated HIGH. Details are withheld until the builds are out; end-of-life lines (20 and older) are always affected and get no fix. As of this filing, the newest releases on GitHub are 26.13.0 and 24.22.2 from Oct 28 and the 27.0.0 alpha from Oct 29, so the patched versions were not yet published. Separately, Next.js 16.4.6 (Nov 10) is an LTS backport that pins Tailwind to 4.3.3 in create-next-app, auto-compresses route handler responses when not already compressed, and fixes Turbopack dev-server garbage collection; it carries no new advisory. Vercel's security bulletins still list the Nov 3 Better Auth release that resolves 12 advisories (upgrade to 1.7.16 or 1.8.0-beta.5; WAF rules reduce exposure but do not replace patching) and a Linux kernel advisory (CVE-2026-98432) Vercel has finished applying on its side.

So what for TSD: Patch instruction: when the Node builds land, bump the Node version in every Vercel project to the patched 24.x or 26.x line and redeploy; Vercel's build image picks up the minor automatically but pinned engines and local .nvmrc files do not. Run npx next@16.4.6 upgrades across client repos in one pass. If any client app uses Better Auth, 1.7.16 is non-negotiable.

Breach Nov 10

Railway's incident report: AI-driven attackers escaped the build sandbox, decrypted customer env vars, and ran $740,000 of AI usage on stolen keys

Railway, a cloud host popular with indie developers, published a full report on the Oct 20–21 intrusion it first disclosed Oct 22. Attackers exploited a previously unknown vulnerability in build infrastructure to escape filesystem isolation and get an internal credential, used it to read build histories and copy Railway's source code, studied that code for weaknesses, then abused a misconfigured trust relationship to reach credentials that decrypt customer environment variables. Railway calls it a roughly 26-hour contest in which attackers using AI agents probed many routes in parallel while the team hardened them. Maximum exposure is 1.85% of workspaces (about 100,000); 1,852 users' linked-account tokens, including GitHub OAuth tokens and AI agent keys, were exposed and reset. No evidence that customer repos or databases were accessed. The stolen secrets generated about $740,000 of unauthorized AI usage; Railway is working with providers on refunds and will cover what they decline. Wiz ties it to a "Black October" campaign that also hit Northflank, OpenCode, Warp and Lightning AI. Related this week: Fireworks AI's second breach (datasets, partial weights, billing data), Vanderbilt University told staff and students to leave campus Wi-Fi during an active ransomware incident, and Wiz reported an attacker looting AWS secrets from multiple Qubole customers Oct 14–26.

So what for TSD: Every client secret you hold lives in Vercel or Supabase env stores, which is the same shape as Railway's. This week: rotate the Anthropic, ElevenLabs, Gemini, Supabase service-role and Stripe keys across all client projects, cap monthly spend on each AI provider, enable Anthropic's IP allowlist on server keys, and keep agent keys off any machine that also holds a GitHub token. Then write it up as the "November key rotation" line item on the Monthly Security retainer.

◆ The stack: Vercel, Supabase, Next.js

Platform Nov 9–10

Vercel: Sandbox snapshots in under 0.4s, 64 GB build disks, 2 GB container layers, WebSocket writes for Workflow SDK, and two new models on AI Gateway

Vercel's changelog for Nov 9: Sandbox snapshot creation is up to 10x faster, median down from 4.4 seconds to under 0.4 using a new indexing format; build disk space rose to 64 GB with no config change or extra charge; the Container Registry now accepts 2 GB compressed image layers; Workflow SDK event and stream writes now use WebSockets by default; and GLM 5.3 Flash joined Token Pass. Nov 10: Gemini 4 Carbon and MiniMax H3 Image and Highspeed are available on AI Gateway under one API key with fallbacks, spend tracking and request traces, and Speed Insights and Web Analytics now fully support custom environments. On the Supabase side, the Nov 10 changelog adds Correlated Logs (logs connected by request, trace or user in Studio, with a terminal log explorer, JSON export and SQL queries in CLI v3), supabase-flutter v3 (Nov 6) is a breaking release that adds type safety, the TLS 1.2 cipher cut on project HTTP APIs started Nov 10, and framework adapters for Hono, H3, Elysia and NestJS in @supabase/server are removed Dec 1.

So what for TSD: Sub-second sandbox snapshots make "an agent spins up a preview of your site change in a few seconds" a feature you can actually ship inside TSD Concierge. Check any client integration that calls Supabase from an old runtime (legacy PHP, an embedded device, an old Zapier step) against the TLS change, and drop Hono/H3 adapters before Dec 1.

◆ Chips and big tech

Governance Nov 8–10

Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into Jensen Huang; the stock fell 4.27% Monday and more Tuesday, with Q3 earnings due Nov 17

On Sunday, Nov 8, Nvidia's board announced an investigation by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel after reports that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort; reports say he acknowledged an affair with a woman US officials believe is a Chinese spy. The board said it has no reason to believe national security or trade secrets were compromised. Huang told employees he had "fallen short" of his responsibility and would cooperate. CNBC had the stock closing down 4.27% on Monday; 24/7 Wall St. reported a further 3% drop Tuesday to about $173 while AMD and Broadcom rose about 3%. Nvidia reports fiscal Q3 on Tuesday, Nov 17 after the close, with guidance of about $108 billion in revenue plus or minus 2%. The same week Google added TPU 8tx (confidential computing on AMD EPYC hosts) and TPU 8i Max (two 8i chips per unit, doubling compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth), the third and fourth systems in its eighth-generation line, with availability pointing to 2027 and no pricing, and Tom's Hardware reported Nvidia now requires overseas cloud partners to reserve 51% of capacity for non-Chinese customers.

So what for TSD: Not a trade signal and not advice. The practical angle is that SMB owners read these headlines as "AI is shaky," and your pitch should stay about their missed calls and their website, with the vendor names in the appendix.

Apple launched one-time $49.99 Usage Packs that add Apple Intelligence capacity after a monthly limit is hit, starting with Logic Pro; unused usage expires after 12 months. ◆ Space and transport

Launch Nov 13

Starship Flight 15 targets Friday with the first attempt to catch both the booster and the Ship; NASA names eight CLPS 2 lunar-delivery companies and books Starship for 2028 Moon comms

SpaceX is targeting Friday, Nov 13 at 5:45 p.m. ET from Starbase, Texas, with a 75-minute window, pending regulatory approval, for Starship Flight 15. The plan is to catch the Super Heavy booster and then the upper-stage Ship with the launch tower's arms, which would be a first. A delay or a waved-off catch is possible. On Nov 7 NASA selected eight companies (Blue Origin, Firefly, Intuitive Machines, ispace-U.S., Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, SpaceX and Voyager Technologies per GovConWire) as eligible to compete under CLPS 2, a 10-year lunar delivery contract with a combined $10 billion ceiling, and separately tasked SpaceX under an existing contract to use Starship to deploy communications satellites into cislunar space in 2028. On the ground, Elon Musk said Nov 9 that Tesla will refund deposits to many Cybertruck customers because orders exceed what the plant can build before the line closes in about eight months to make room for the Cybercab; he gave no counts and did not address the non-refundable order fee.

So what for TSD: A Friday evening launch with a double catch is the kind of thing clients bring up on Monday; it costs you nothing to know the date. The Cybertruck line closing is the better business lesson: a product with demand can still be the wrong use of a factory.
