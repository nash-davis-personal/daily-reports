◆ The outage

Breaking Aug 31 – Sept 2, 2026

Two days of Microsoft 365 degradation, traced to shared authentication config

Microsoft 365 customers began losing access to Outlook mail on Monday, August 31, with delivery delays, send failures and sign-in errors on Exchange Online. Because the fault sat in a core authentication configuration used by multiple Microsoft 365 services, the impact widened well past email: SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, Copilot, Purview, Defender XDR, the Microsoft 365 Admin Center and Universal Print all showed degradation over the following two days.

Microsoft characterised the root cause as a misconfiguration rather than an attack. Service was still degraded through Tuesday, and by September 2 Microsoft said availability remained stable above 99% and that most users should no longer be seeing impact.

So what for TSD: Two of your product lines exist precisely because a business cannot afford to be unreachable, and the last 48 hours gave every Microsoft-shop prospect in the Charlotte metro a firsthand demonstration. This is a warm-outreach window with a real event attached: a short note asking how their week went, and what happened to inbound leads while email was down, is the honest version of the receptionist pitch. Also check that no client site of yours routes contact-form delivery through a single Microsoft 365 mailbox with no fallback.

◆ Platforms and regulators

Breaking Aug 31, 2026

The FTC and 22 states sued Amazon over a hidden reserve price in ad auctions

The Federal Trade Commission and 22 state attorneys general filed suit in the US District Court for the Western District of Washington, alleging that for more than seven years Amazon secretly inflated prices in its search advertising auctions. According to the complaint, Amazon told advertisers they would pay one cent more than the second-highest bidder, then inserted an undisclosed internal figure it called a "soft reserve price" that functioned as a phantom bid from Amazon itself, with the result that winners paid their own bid roughly 80% of the time.

The complaint covers Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, and puts the cost to more than a million brands and sellers at $20 billion or more. Amazon said the suit rests on a misunderstanding of how its auctions work and that it presents no evidence of harm to shoppers.

So what for TSD: If any client sells on Amazon, their ad spend history is now the subject of a federal complaint, and their reported blended acquisition cost may be wrong in a direction that favors owned channels. That is the honest opening for a website-and-SEO conversation: a channel you rent can change its pricing behind a curtain, a site you own cannot.

◆ Hardware and silicon

New Sept 1–2, 2026

Wi-Fi 8 launches at IFA while Taiwan says the bottleneck is wiring

TP-Link used IFA 2026 in Berlin to launch what it calls the world's first complete consumer Wi-Fi 8 lineup, built on the IEEE 802.11bn standard. The Archer 8 Ultra is a tri-band router rated at 19Gbps aggregate with 18 internal antennas, 10Gbps wired ports and claimed coverage of 3,600 square feet. The Deco 8 Ultra mesh covers up to 9,800 square feet in a three-pack and supports more than 200 devices. Archer 8 Ultra pre-orders open September 30 in select regions; the Deco mesh is scheduled for Q1 2027, with extenders, a travel router and client adapters by Q2 2027.

SEMICON Taiwan opened September 2 in Taipei with more than 1,300 companies and 4,300 booths, and a stated theme that data movement in AI systems can now consume more energy than the computation itself, making interconnect and packaging the constraint rather than raw chip performance. TSMC's Compact Universal Photonic Engine, its co-packaged optics platform, entered mass production this year.

So what for TSD: Wi-Fi 8 is a 2027 problem for client sites, so do not let anyone talk you into re-testing for it yet. The useful read is the Taiwan one: if interconnect is the binding constraint on AI capacity, per-token prices fall more slowly than the model-release cadence suggests, so keep AI pricing on annual terms.
