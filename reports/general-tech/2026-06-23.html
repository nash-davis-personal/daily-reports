◆ Your stack — action items

Migrate Deadline: end of 2026

Supabase is deprecating legacy API keys — start rotating now

Supabase is retiring its legacy API keys by the end of 2026. The replacement is a two-key scheme: publishable keys (sb_publishable_…) for client-side use and secret keys (sb_secret_…) for server-side. The old anon and service_role JWTs keep working until the cutoff, but new projects and any hardening pass should use the new keys.

So what for TSD: This touches every Supabase project you run — Cohors, GrantWell, the portal, the renters funnel, the RAG demos. Don't scramble in December: open each project, generate the new publishable/secret keys, update the Vercel env vars, and confirm client vs. server usage is split correctly. Do it in one focused pass while it's a non-event, before it's an outage.

Ecosystem June 2026

Supabase joins Tailwind as a partner and ships one-click Stripe Sync

Two smaller but useful Supabase moves: it's now an official Tailwind partner, and the dashboard added a one-click integration for the Stripe Sync Engine — letting you query customers, subscriptions, invoices, and payments as standard SQL right alongside your app data.

So what for TSD: The Stripe Sync piece is the one to file away. Any client build with subscriptions or invoices (a Managed-AI retainer dashboard, a booking product) gets a built-in revenue table without you wiring a webhook pipeline by hand. That's an afternoon saved per billing-aware project.

Space Launch: today, 6:53 a.m. EDT

SpaceX flies its first Starfall reentry capsule

SpaceX launched its first uncrewed Starfall capsule this morning on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral. The cylindrical vehicle — about 2.5 ft tall, 10.2 ft across, ~4,600 lb, carrying up to 1,000 kg of cargo — is a demo of point-to-point cargo delivery through space and an in-space manufacturing play, with recovery in the Pacific under parachutes like Dragon. Forecasters gave it 95% favorable conditions. SpaceX hasn't disclosed payload contents or how many capsules are aboard.

So what for TSD: No direct line to a client build, but it's the kind of "what's now physically possible" shift worth tracking — logistics and manufacturing markets are where the next wave of operational software demand shows up. File it; don't act on it.

Semiconductors June 2026

TSMC ramps 2nm/3nm and accelerates the 28nm phaseout

As TSMC ramps 2nm and 3nm production, it's pulling back on legacy nodes: monthly wafer starts at Fab 15A, its main 28nm site, have fallen more than 25% since the start of the year. China continues to strengthen its domestic toolchain even while it stays far from ASML's most advanced lithography.

So what for TSD: Leading-edge capacity going to AI silicon while legacy nodes shrink keeps the cost curve for inference moving the right way over time — your AI products get cheaper to run as the buildout matures. Nothing to do today; it's a tailwind under your margins.
