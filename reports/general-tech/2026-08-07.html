◆ Your stack

Patch 15.5.23 ◆ August 7

Next.js shipped a 15.x patch this morning porting traversal guards into FlightClient

One commit, contributed by eps1lon: "[15.x] Port ReplyServer traversal guards to FlightClient." It brings a protection that already existed on the server side of the Flight protocol over to the client side on the maintenance branch. Vercel did not publish a separate advisory alongside it, so treat it as hardening rather than an emergency.

The bigger version story is four days old. Next.js 16.3.0 landed August 3 with contributions from more than 88 developers, and it bumped vendored lodash to 4.17.23 to resolve CVE-2025-13465. It also fixed styled-jsx losing styles during concurrent rendering, improved Turbopack, and cleaned up the macOS Rosetta 2 warning. Canary builds on 16.3.1 have been coming out daily since.

So what for TSD: Run npm outdated next across the client sites tonight. Anything on 15.x goes to 15.5.23; anything on 16.x should already be at 16.3.0 for the lodash CVE. Neither is a fire drill, and both are ten-minute jobs that keep you off the version where the next real advisory lands. Sites on 13.x or 14.x get no security patches at all any more.

Standard August 6

Vercel's Agent Plugins proposal went 1.0 with AWS, Microsoft, OpenAI and Cursor on the committee

Covered in full in today's General AI briefing. The short version for the platform side: Vercel initiated the spec, the technical steering committee draws core maintainers from AWS, Cursor, Microsoft, OpenAI and Vercel, and Google has said it will adopt the format in Agents CLI and Data Agent Kit.

A plugin is a directory with a plugin.json, a skills/ folder and an optional mcp.json. ChatGPT, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Kiro and VS Code support it at launch.

So what for TSD: Vercel is now writing the packaging standard for agent extensions on top of already hosting your deployments. That is a reason to keep the deploy story where it is, and a reason to publish a TSD plugin under your own name while the registry is nearly empty.

◆ Silicon

M&A August 6

AMD is acquiring Taalas, which burns model weights directly into the chip

Taalas, founded in Toronto in 2023 and funded to $219 million, builds inference silicon where the model is not loaded from memory at run time — it is etched into the hardware. Its first test chip served Meta's Llama 3.1 8B at close to 17,000 tokens per second. AMD plans to fold the technology across Helios systems, Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs and ROCm. The deal is expected to close in Q4 2026.

It lands seven months after Nvidia's $20 billion Groq acquisition, and both deals point the same direction: the money in AI is moving from training to serving, and serving rewards specialised parts over general-purpose GPUs.

So what for TSD: Inference-per-dollar keeps falling, and it falls fastest for small fixed models. The economics of running an 8B-class model yourself for narrow jobs — classification, routing, extraction inside Concierge — get better every quarter, while the frontier calls stay expensive. Worth designing the next AI product so the cheap tier can be swapped without a rewrite.

Earnings August 6

Atlassian's cloud business reaccelerated to 31% and the stock added more than 30%

Q4 FY26 revenue of $1.8 billion, up 28% year over year, with cloud revenue at $1.2 billion growing 31%. Adjusted EPS came in at $1.87 against a $1.50 consensus. Subscription ARR is $6.6 billion, up 23%, and remaining performance obligations grew 44% to $4.8 billion. Guidance calls for 18% subscription ARR growth in FY27.

The AI line is the part worth noting: Atlassian says Rovo is in use at more than 80% of the Fortune 500, Rovo-assisted actions grew 50% quarter over quarter, and customers who adopted it grew ARR at more than twice the rate of customers who did not.

So what for TSD: "Customers who turned on the AI features expanded twice as fast" is the cleanest public evidence yet that AI attach drives retention, from a company that reports real numbers. Put that stat in the Managed AI retainer pitch. It answers the "is this a gimmick" objection with someone else's audited revenue.

Ruling August 6

A New Mexico court ordered Meta to cap teen usage at 90 hours a month

Chief District Court Judge Bryan Biedscheid ordered Meta to pay $567 million into a child mental health fund, on top of the $375 million in civil penalties a jury assessed in phase one after finding 75,000 violations of the New Mexico Unfair Practices Act. Of the new money, $420 million goes to treatment services for young people over five years.

The product orders are the unusual part. Under-18 accounts on Facebook and Instagram face a hard cap of 90 hours a month. Push notifications to those accounts pause from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. daily, and from 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. on academic weekdays. The order also tightens adult-to-minor contact controls and adds safeguards around AI chatbots.

So what for TSD: A state court just wrote engagement-limiting product requirements into a remedy. If any client site collects data from minors — youth sports, tutoring, a salon booking flow with under-18 clients — the design questions a plaintiff's lawyer would ask are now on the record. Cheap to get right at build time, expensive later.
