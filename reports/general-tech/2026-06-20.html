◆ Security — do this first

Patch Standing advisory

Confirm your Next.js apps carry the RSC remote-code-execution fix

With multiple active campaigns running this weekend, treat it as a prompt to verify the one advisory that actually touches your stack. The critical React Server Components / Next.js RCE — CVE-2025-55182 (React) and CVE-2025-66478 (Next.js) — comes from insecure deserialization in the RSC "Flight" protocol and allows unauthenticated remote code execution. It affects Next.js App Router from 14.3.0-canary.77 through 16.0.6 and React 19.0–19.2. It cannot be reliably blocked at the WAF layer; upgrading is the only complete fix.

Patched lines: Next.js 15.5.7 and 16.0.7 (plus 15.0.5 / 15.1.9 / 15.2.6 / 15.3.6 / 15.4.8 on older branches); React 19.0.1 / 19.1.2 / 19.2.1.

So what for TSD: Action item, today — run npm ls next react across the live apps (tsd-modernization-app, the funnels, the demos). Anything on Next ≤ 16.0.6 with App Router needs to move to ≥ 16.0.7 (or the matching 15.x patch) and redeploy. Then check indirect deps for a vulnerable React. This is unauthenticated RCE — it's the highest-priority chore on the list.

Active campaign June 19, 2026

FortiBleed and a PeopleSoft zero-day are live this weekend

CISA urged Fortinet customers to secure FortiGate appliances against a campaign codenamed FortiBleed, attributed to Russian-speaking actors; the compromised count stood at 86,644 devices as of June 19. Separately, ShinyHunters is exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day to break into enterprise systems, steal data, and extort, with universities hit particularly hard.

So what for TSD: Neither product is in your stack, so no direct action — but if any client runs a FortiGate or PeopleSoft, a one-line heads-up is the kind of touch that turns a website vendor into a trusted advisor. Cheap goodwill.

◆ The AI-silicon race

Semiconductors June 2026

Qualcomm goes after data-center silicon; Intel 18A-P enters risk production

Qualcomm is pushing beyond mobile — launching custom AI data-center silicon and in talks to acquire AI-chip startup Tenstorrent for as much as $10 billion. Intel's 18A-P node entered risk production with reported interest from Apple and Google and a UMC partnership aimed at TSMC's foundry lead, while Samsung and SK Hynix began shipping seventh-generation HBM4E memory samples to major customers.

So what for TSD: No direct action, but this is why your inference bill keeps falling — more competition in chips and memory feeds straight through to cheaper model serving, which is the cost base under every AI product you sell. Tailwind for the Managed-AI model.

Infrastructure June 2026

SpaceX names water as an AI-infrastructure constraint

In an amendment to its IPO filing, SpaceX warned investors that access to water — for cooling large-scale compute — is becoming a critical factor in operating and expanding AI infrastructure. It's an early sign the data-center buildout is bumping into physical limits beyond power and chips.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do, but worth filing — the "AI is constrained by physical resources" narrative will shape what clients believe about cost and reliability over the next year. Useful context for the honest-about-AI pitch.
