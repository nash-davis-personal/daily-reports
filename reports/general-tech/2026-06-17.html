◆ Patch watch — do this first

Exploited June 16, 2026

CISA gives a June 18 deadline for an actively-exploited cPanel flaw

CISA added CVE-2026-54420 — a flaw in the LiteSpeed cPanel plugin (CVSS 8.5) — to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, ordering federal agencies to patch by June 18. Separately, Cisco shipped a fix for a Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaw (CVE-2026-20262) that's also under active exploitation. June was Microsoft's heaviest Patch Tuesday ever (200 vulnerabilities, six zero-days), so the broader exploit climate is hot.

So what for TSD: If any client (or your own) site runs on cPanel/LiteSpeed hosting, confirm the plugin is patched today — actively-exploited + a public deadline is the worst combination. Most of your stack is Vercel-hosted and not exposed here, but client legacy hosting is exactly where this bites.

Standing fix since May 2026

The Next.js / React security set is still the only complete fix — confirm you're upgraded

Vercel's May coordinated security release addressed 13 Next.js advisories spanning denial-of-service, middleware/proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning, and XSS — including auth bypass via App Router segment-prefetch and DoS in React Server Components. Fixed upstream React versions are 19.0.5, 19.1.6, and 19.2.5, with Next.js backports across affected lines. Vercel's guidance is unchanged: patching is the only complete mitigation.

So what for TSD: Nothing new dropped this window, but this is a standing to-do across your portfolio. Spend 20 minutes confirming every live Next.js app (TSD app, Bisque/Clay, the funnels, Praxis) is on a fixed React + Next.js line. Auth-bypass and SSRF on a client site is a reputation event you can't afford.

◆ Hardware & the post-phone bet

Launch June 16, 2026

Snap unveils standalone "Specs" AR glasses at $2,195

At AWE 2026, Snap CEO Evan Spiegel introduced Specs — standalone augmented-reality glasses priced at $2,195 (with a refundable $200 deposit), pre-orderable now and shipping this fall in the US, UK, and France. The dual-Qualcomm-chip glasses use Snap's LCoS display with a 51° field of view and ~4 hours of battery, and lean on Lens Studio developer experiences. It's a direct shot at Meta in consumer AR, betting people are tired enough of phone screens to pay $2k+.

So what for TSD: Not a near-term build target — battery and price keep AR a hobbyist/enterprise device for now. Watch it as a future surface for the kind of guided, location-aware experiences you already prototype (Praxis, the 3D master plans). Don't pivot; just note the platform is forming.

Chips June 16, 2026

Intel starts producing its most-advanced chip, reviving Apple-deal chatter

Intel began production of its most-advanced chip, inching toward a possible Apple manufacturing deal, per the June 16 tech roundup. The same day surfaced more platform signals: Qualcomm argued AI agents will replace traditional apps, and Mobileye targeted a 2027 US robotaxi launch. The chip-supply story matters because Apple sourcing US-made silicon would reshape the foundry market that ultimately sets compute prices.

So what for TSD: Indirect but real — cheaper, more competitive silicon flows downstream into cheaper inference, which is what makes your AI products profitable at SMB price points. Nothing to act on; a tailwind to track.
