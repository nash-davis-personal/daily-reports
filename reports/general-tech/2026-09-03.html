◆ Power is the constraint now

Infrastructure Sep 1, 2026

ERCOT and Texas open an audit of a 474 GW interconnection queue

Data-center power requests in Texas have gone from about 48 gigawatts in 2023 to more than 474 gigawatts. The state's all-time peak demand, set July 22, was 91,089 megawatts. Governor Greg Abbott ordered the Public Utility Commission and ERCOT to stop approving new data-center connections on August 3, and on September 1 the two launched an audit of what is actually in the queue.

The industry term is "ghost demand": developers filing multiple speculative requests to hold queue positions cheaply, without named customers or committed funding. Many are likely duplicates or from firms that cannot finish the projects. Texas holds roughly 20% of the national data-center pipeline. Pennsylvania signed a comparable executive order on August 18, adding permitting requirements and disclosure for projects over 25 megawatts.

So what for TSD: Inference prices have been falling because compute kept arriving. If interconnection queues stall in the states doing most of the building, that curve flattens. Do not price a multi-year Managed AI retainer on the assumption that per-token costs keep halving. Build a model-swap layer into Front Desk and Concierge so you can move providers when one gets expensive, and hold a margin buffer in the retainer number.

Chips Sep 2–4, 2026

Semicon Taiwan opens with 1,300 companies and a supply chain running behind demand

Semicon Taiwan runs September 2 to 4 in Taipei with more than 1,300 companies and 4,300 booths, both records, and an agenda that has widened past chipmaking into advanced packaging, smart fab, quantum and system integration. Reporting from the show has TSMC increasing equipment purchases as AI demand outpaces supply-chain planning, SK Hynix weighing a Kioxia production tie-up in Japan, and Korea's KEPCO seeking about $18 billion from Samsung and SK Hynix to fund grid expansion.

Separately, Nvidia led a $125 million round into iPronics, a Spanish startup building programmable photonic chips that move data with light inside AI data centers, and Tencent-backed Enflame's Shanghai IPO was oversubscribed more than 6,000 times on a roughly 6.1 billion yuan raise.

So what for TSD: Same conclusion as the Texas item, arrived at from the supply side. Even the power utilities are now asking the chipmakers to pay for the grid. Treat cheap inference as a current condition rather than a trend you can extrapolate into a 24-month contract.

◆ Big-tech moves

Leadership Sep 1, 2026

John Ternus replaced Tim Cook as Apple CEO

The transition announced in April took effect September 1. Ternus, senior vice president of hardware engineering since 2021 and at Apple since 2001, becomes CEO and joins the board. Cook, CEO since 2011, becomes executive chairman. Arthur Levinson, non-executive chairman for the past 15 years, becomes lead independent director.

Coverage frames the handoff around Apple's position in AI, where it has been the slowest of the large platform companies to ship.

So what for TSD: A hardware engineer running Apple most likely means device-side AI gets pushed harder. Perplexity's move this week to split its Computer agent between cloud and local Apple silicon is a sign of that direction. Nothing to change today, and worth watching if on-device inference makes a privacy story cheap enough to sell to a medical or legal SMB client.

Layoffs Sep 2, 2026

Uber cut 3,300 roles and pointed the money at robotaxis

Roughly 10% of global headcount. Uber says organizational complexity was slowing decisions, and is flattening management layers, consolidating teams, concentrating staff in major hubs and cutting remote positions. Resources move toward autonomous vehicle development.

So what for TSD: "We cut remote roles and consolidated into hubs" is the second half of every AI-efficiency story this year, and mid-market companies copy it. That is a live wedge for outbound: the SMB that just lost two coordinators still needs the work done. Have Grant and Bishop open with the workflow, not the technology.

◆ Your stack

Vercel Sep 1–2, 2026

AWS PrivateLink lands on Pro, and the free-domain offer covers .app and .dev

Vercel shipped AWS PrivateLink on Pro and Enterprise on September 1, connecting deployments to AWS-hosted databases and services over a private network path instead of the public internet. On September 2 the free-domain-with-Pro offer expanded to .app and .dev, bringing eligible TLDs to eight. The AI Gateway also picked up Claude Fable 5.1, Qwen 3.8 Max 0902, Gemini 3.8 Flash and Meta's Muse Spark 1.3, with GLM-5.3 discounted 50% through DigitalOcean until September 7.

No new Next.js or React security advisory landed in this window. The last coordinated Next.js security release was August 2026, covering unauthenticated RCE on Windows-hosted servers and the Image Optimization API with AVIF files.

So what for TSD: PrivateLink is a concrete answer for any client whose data sits in AWS and whose compliance person asks how it travels. The .app and .dev addition is worth a line in your next proposal, since it removes a small recurring cost from the client's side. On patching, confirm every client project is on the August 2026 Next.js release or later this week; run the check now rather than when the next advisory drops.
