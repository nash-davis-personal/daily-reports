◆ Security — do these first

Advisory Sept 3–4

Google patched an actively exploited V8 zero-day in Chrome

CVE-2026-85046 is a type-confusion flaw in V8, the JavaScript and WebAssembly engine, rated CVSS 8.8. A malicious page can get arbitrary code execution inside the renderer sandbox, and Google confirmed an exploit exists in the wild without naming the actors or targets. The fix landed in the September 3 Stable Channel update: Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83 on Windows and macOS, 152.0.7977.82 on Linux, rolling out progressively. Salvatore Gulizia reported it on August 4. This is the sixth Chrome zero-day Google has fixed in 2026, and Chromium-based browsers — Edge, Brave, Arc, Opera — inherit it.

So what for TSD: Open chrome://settings/help on every machine you touch, confirm you are on 152.0.7977.82 or higher, and relaunch. Do the same on any client machine you have access to and send a two-line note to the rest — this is the kind of thing an SMB owner never does on their own, and it costs you nothing to be the person who told them. Staged rollout means "checked yesterday" is not the same as "patched."

Threat Sept 3–6

ASCII smuggling crossed over from AI prompt injection to plain phishing

Microsoft's security team went looking for prompt-injection attempts against Defender for Office 365 and found something else: attackers using the Unicode Tags block (U+E0000–U+E007F), which holds invisible shadow copies of printable ASCII, to break up keywords that trigger signature-based mail filters. A word like "funding" gets split by characters no human sees but every parser reads. Volume of that block in email went from roughly 5,000–20,000 a day to more than 1.3 million on February 9, 2026, in the campaign Microsoft traced. Microsoft's recommendation is to strip or normalize invisible Unicode before running spam signatures, and to do the same normalization before any email content reaches an AI assistant.

So what for TSD: This one lands directly on your products. Anything that reads untrusted text and hands it to a model — TSD Concierge over a client's site content, an inbox summarizer, a lead-intake form — needs a Unicode normalization pass on ingest. Strip the Tags block and other zero-width characters before the text reaches Claude. That is a ten-line utility in your shared package and it closes a hole that will otherwise be somebody's incident report.

Breach Sept 5–6

Rhysida published all 5.79 TB it took from Berlin's state network

Berlin's Senate refused a 30 BTC demand, worth around €2 million, and the Rhysida crew released the full set: roughly 1.44 million files spanning more than 5,000 personnel records, health and severe-disability data, plaintext credentials, passports and ID documents, and Bundestag committee protocols. It is the largest claim the group has made against a European public body. The interesting detail for anyone running infrastructure is the timeline — suspicious data outflow was first detected around August 7 and the affected departments were not disconnected from the Landesnetz until August 14. Seven days between detection and isolation is where the terabytes went.

So what for TSD: Write down, for each client, who can pull the plug and how long it takes. For your deployments that is: revoke the Supabase service key, rotate the Vercel deploy token, kill the API keys. If you cannot do all three in under ten minutes from your phone, fix that before you sell another retainer. "We detected it fast" is worth nothing without an isolation step attached to it.

◆ Hardware and the buildout

New Sept 4

Microsoft announced Project Zenith, a Windows 11 build that arrives ready to code

Zenith is a preconfigured developer image: VS Code, GitHub Copilot, PowerToys, Git and GitHub CLI, PowerShell 7, Python 3.14+, uv, NVM, Node 24+, WSL 2 with Ubuntu, .NET 10, plus MCP-based agent security, all installed with developer settings already applied. The hardware bar is the story — at least 64 GB of unified memory and at least 250 GB/s of memory bandwidth, which Microsoft says is what it takes to run 30B-plus-parameter coding models locally without metered cloud usage. It ships first on AMD's Ryzen AI Halo, with more OEM and silicon partners promised.

So what for TSD: Not a purchase for you — a MacBook and the Claude API is a better dollar-per-output trade than a 64 GB Windows box. What it signals is that "the model runs on the machine" is becoming a normal enterprise expectation, which will eventually show up as an SMB asking whether their data leaves the building. Have an answer ready: what you send to the API, what you store in Supabase, and what never leaves their site. That conversation sells retainers.

Analysis Sept 6

Swiss Re says the AI buildout is becoming an insurance concentration problem

Global data-center insurance premiums are projected to reach $24.2 billion by 2030, up from $10.6 billion, which makes it the biggest new growth line reinsurers have. The risk picture behind that number: more than 40% of US data-center capacity sits in significant-to-very-high tornado zones — places with at least three days a year of EF1-or-stronger tornadoes — and more than a quarter is in areas seeing three or more large-hail days a year. Single sites can cost north of $20 billion to build, lenders want limits covering full construction cost, and developers keep clustering campuses within about 20 miles of each other in places like Abilene, Texas and Northern Virginia. That clustering is what turns weather into a correlated loss.

So what for TSD: Read this as a second-order cost signal. Insurance, power and land are all pushing the same direction, and none of them get cheaper on the schedule token prices have been falling. Anything you sell with a fixed monthly price and unbounded model usage is a bet that inference stays cheap. Cap the usage in the contract or meter it.

◆ Deals and moves

Business Sept 6

Kalanick's Atoms bought Pronto, rehired Levandowski, and took $100 million from Uber

Atoms, Travis Kalanick's industrial-AI holding company, acquired Anthony Levandowski's Pronto and brought Levandowski back to lead autonomy work that the FT reports is aimed at robotaxis. Atoms publicly denies it is building robotaxis. Uber confirmed a $100 million investment as part of the $1.7 billion a16z-led round from July — the same Uber whose trade-secrets fight over Levandowski cost it roughly $350 million and helped push Kalanick out as CEO. Pronto's pitch is autonomy that ports across OEMs rather than a single vehicle platform.

So what for TSD: No direct read for your stack. Worth tracking as a marker for how much capital is flowing into physical automation versus software — the same buyer psychology reaches SMBs eventually, and Charlotte-area logistics and trades clients will start asking about it. File it, do not chase it.
