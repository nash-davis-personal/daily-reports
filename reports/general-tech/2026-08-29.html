◆ Patch and rotate first

Security Aug 26–28, 2026

Two alleged TeamPCP principals charged over the Shai-Hulud supply-chain worm

The Australian Federal Police, working with the FBI and the Western Australia Police Force, executed search warrants at properties in Cottesloe, Hamilton Hill and Mandurah on August 26 and charged two men — Louis Michael Gaebler, 23, and Ruben Ian Thomson, 21 — with multiple offences including data intrusion and unauthorised modification of data. They appeared in Perth Magistrates Court on August 27. Authorities describe the pair as principal members of a cybercrime syndicate that built malicious open-source packages to steal data and extort businesses, and estimate more than 1,000 organizations compromised, over 500,000 credentials taken and at least 300GB of data stolen.

TeamPCP is the group behind Shai-Hulud, a self-propagating worm that spread through open-source registries, hitting both PyPI and npm across several campaigns; the recent "Mini Shai-Hulud" round caught two OpenAI employees. The confirmed list of modified tools is the part to act on: Trivy, the container vulnerability scanner; KICS, a static analysis tool for infrastructure-as-code; LiteLLM, a widely used library for routing requests across model APIs; and the Telnyx Python SDK. Two arrests do not remove poisoned versions from a lockfile that already resolved them.

So what for TSD: Do this before you write any code today. Run npm audit and check every project lockfile against the affected versions, with LiteLLM the one to look hardest for, since it is exactly the kind of dependency a model-routing layer picks up quietly. Then rotate: any token that has ever sat in an environment where a poisoned package ran is burned — Supabase service keys, Vercel tokens, Anthropic and OpenAI keys, GitHub PATs, Telnyx and Vapi credentials. The delivery angle for Grant and Bishop is sharper than usual this week: the poisoned packages were security tools, which makes "your vulnerability scanner was the vulnerability" a cold-email opener that lands with any prospect running a dev team. Sell a fixed-fee dependency and credential audit, and quote the 500,000-credential number.

Privacy Aug 28, 2026

Meta patched a smart-glasses flaw that let a wearer defeat the recording light

Meta fixed a privacy vulnerability in its smart glasses where the camera recording indicator could be bypassed. The mechanism was as simple as it sounds: a user could cover the LED after recording had already started, which disabled the protection rather than stopping the recording. Meta paired the fix with a public awareness campaign about what the recording indicator means.

So what for TSD: Small item, useful precedent. If you ever ship a client product with a "we are recording" or "you are speaking to an AI" disclosure — and Front Desk is exactly that product — the indicator has to be enforced server-side, not by something the caller or the operator can suppress. Check that Front Desk's AI disclosure plays before the first substantive exchange and cannot be skipped by config, because that is the detail a regulator or a plaintiff's lawyer looks at first.

◆ The open-weight layer is being bought

M&A Aug 27, 2026

Nvidia has reportedly agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

The Information reported that Nvidia reached an agreement to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, with Bloomberg reporting serious conversations valuing the company around $13 billion. Neither company has publicly confirmed. If it closes it would be Nvidia's largest acquisition ever, dwarfing the $6.9 billion Mellanox deal in 2020, and it would put the most widely used platform for sharing and running open-weight models under the company that sells the hardware those models run on. The stated logic is reducing dependence on hyperscalers and frontier labs while gaining the largest U.S. developer community for open models.

It is not an isolated deal. Nvidia has a separate $6 billion agreement for Poolside with most of that company's employees moving over, and Stripe bought OpenRouter for more than $7 billion two weeks ago, with Patrick Collison framing tokens as the central currency for companies building with AI. TechCrunch puts the demand context bluntly: only about 6% of companies currently use open-weight models, and adoption is driven mostly by cost-sensitive, high-volume inference like customer service. Analysts are split on whether a chip vendor owning the neutral model hub is good for the ecosystem.

So what for TSD: You route through OpenRouter and you pull from Hugging Face, so both of your open-weight dependencies now have new owners inside a month. Nothing breaks today, and the thing that changes over the next year is pricing and defaults, not availability. Two practical responses. Keep your model calls behind your own thin adapter rather than calling a provider SDK directly from application code, so a routing change is one file. And when you quote a client on self-hosted or on-prem open weights, download and store the weights you depend on rather than assuming the hub will serve them on the same terms in 2027 — that is a ten-minute job now and a bad surprise later.

◆ Custom silicon, priced in warrants

Semis Aug 27–28, 2026

Marvell's Google agreement tops out at $120 billion, and the stock fell about 8% anyway

Marvell disclosed an expanded agreement with Google covering custom chips for AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers and other parts of the TPU ecosystem. The structure is the story: a warrant with 240 tranches tied to cumulative revenue, one block of shares unlocking for every $500 million of custom-chip revenue Marvell books from Google, running from fiscal Q3 2027 through fiscal 2033 and reaching roughly $120 billion if fully realized. That ceiling is a maximum, not a booking.

Fiscal Q1 revenue was a record $2.42 billion with data center up 27%, and management guided fiscal Q2 to about $2.7 billion. The stock still dropped roughly 8% on Friday. The two reasons given are that low-margin custom AI silicon is pressuring gross margin in the near term, and that the Google revenue becomes substantially meaningful only around fiscal 2029 — a long time to wait after a 220% run in the stock.

So what for TSD: No direct action, and one transferable lesson about how to write a contract. Marvell got paid in a structure that only converts as the customer actually spends, which is exactly the shape of the performance-linked pricing you keep considering and never write down. For a client where you cannot defend a flat retainer on hours, propose the Marvell shape: a small fixed base plus a step that unlocks on a measured business outcome — booked appointments through Booking Bridge, calls answered by Front Desk. It reads as confidence rather than discounting, and it survives the "what am I paying for" conversation in month four.

◆ Hardware you can actually buy

Robotics Aug 28, 2026

Hugging Face launched Microduck, a $399 open-source bipedal robot

Microduck is 25cm tall, carries 15 motors, a camera, lidar and an articulated beak rated to lift 800g, and costs $399. It comes in Cream, Graphite, Lavender and Sky, and first deliveries are targeted before Christmas in North America, Europe and the UK. The design is open source, which is the whole point — it is a cheap, hackable body for the robotics models that have been shipping all year without anywhere affordable to run.

So what for TSD: Not a revenue line, and possibly the cheapest marketing asset on this page. A $399 walking robot that you programmed, filmed doing something specific in a Charlotte shop, and posted, does more for inbound than another landing page iteration. If you buy one, buy it with a shoot in mind and not as a toy — and note the ownership angle it hands you, since "open source, you own it" is the same argument you already make about client source code.
