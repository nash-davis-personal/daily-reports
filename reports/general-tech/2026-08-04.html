◆ Your stack this week

Security August 4

No new advisory on Next.js, React, Vercel or Supabase

Nothing new to patch. The last coordinated Next.js security release was the July 21 batch of eight advisories, and nothing has shipped since. The May 6 release remains the big one of the year — thirteen advisories spanning denial of service, middleware and proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning and XSS, patched in 15.5.18 and 16.2.6. If any client site is still below those versions, that is the outstanding item.

Yesterday's Rails Active Storage flaw, an arbitrary file read that chains to RCE at CVSS 9.5 with public exploits circulating, still stands for anyone touching a Rails codebase. It does not affect Nash's stack directly.

So what for TSD: No emergency deploys today. Spend the reclaimed time on the check you keep deferring: run npm audit plus a version sweep across every client repo and confirm every one is at Next.js 15.5.18 or 16.2.6 and above. Do it now while there is no fire, because the next advisory batch will not wait for a convenient week.

New August 4

Vercel gave eve agents a browser

Today's changelog entry is short — "your eve agent can now navigate the web like a human" — and it lands on top of a real open-source project. The vercel-labs agent-browser repository ships a namespaced browser toolset as an eve extension, with sandbox bootstrap helpers, docs and examples. That follows July's extension system, which lets you package tools, connections, skills, instructions and hooks and install them like normal dependencies.

eve itself is Vercel's open-source agent framework, introduced in June. The trajectory over two months is clear: the framework got an extension mechanism, then a browser. Vercel is building the thing that turns a Next.js developer into an agent developer without leaving the platform.

So what for TSD: This is the cheapest path to a new billable service. Every SMB client has a "go look at this site and pull the data into our system" job — competitor pricing, supplier stock, permit filings, review monitoring. Build one eve agent with the browser extension against a real client need this week, deploy it on infrastructure you already pay for, and you have a monthly retainer line that costs almost nothing to run.

Platform August 3

WAF custom rules for Blob hit general availability, and Container Registry repos can be shared

Two entries yesterday. Vercel WAF for Blob is generally available, so custom WAF rules now apply to Blob storage in production across all plans, with beta settings carried forward automatically. Separately, Container Registry repositories can now be shared with up to 100 Vercel teams.

The Blob change is the useful one for a small studio. Blob URLs are public by default, which means a client's uploaded gallery, PDF menu or product photos are bandwidth anyone can consume from anywhere.

So what for TSD: Add a referrer-based WAF rule on Blob for every client site that stores images or documents, and cap it to the client's own domain. It takes minutes and removes the scenario where someone hotlinks a client's asset library and the overage lands on your bill. Pair it with the AI Gateway spend budgets from August 1 and you have hard ceilings on the two costs that can run away.

◆ The memory crunch reached the supply chain's politics

Hardware August 4

HP, Asus and Acer started shipping laptops with Chinese CXMT memory

Nikkei Asia reports that several major PC makers completed validation of ChangXin Memory Technologies DRAM in mid-2026 and have since begun using it at small scale. The chips go into a limited number of notebook models sold outside the United States. CXMT prioritizes domestic customers like Huawei, and the PC makers are moving carefully to avoid antagonizing Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix, who supply the rest of their volume.

The driver is the AI-induced memory shortage that has been building all year and that already pushed MacBook Air pricing up $200 with waits into September. When three of the largest PC vendors accept geopolitical risk to secure DRAM, the shortage is structural rather than a quarter of bad planning.

So what for TSD: If a machine purchase is anywhere on your plan for this fall, move it forward. Laptop and RAM prices are going up rather than down through the AI buildout, and the configuration you want in November will cost more and ship later than the one you can order today. This is also a live objection-handler for clients delaying hardware spend — the wait is not going to get cheaper.

◆ Business moves

M&A August 4

Bending Spoons is buying Airtable for $1.285 billion in cash

Bending Spoons entered a definitive agreement to acquire Airtable at an enterprise value of $1.285 billion, which with Airtable's net cash implies roughly $2.25 billion in equity value. It is an all-cash deal expected to close by year end subject to regulatory approval, and it is the company's first acquisition since listing on Nasdaq on July 1. Bending Spoons bought AOL in January and Eventbrite in March.

Airtable, founded in 2013, is the spreadsheet-database hybrid a lot of small businesses use to run operations without writing code. Bending Spoons has a well-known playbook for acquired software: cut costs, raise prices, run it for cash.

So what for TSD: This is a lead list. Any client running operations on Airtable is looking at price increases and feature changes under a new owner within a year. A Supabase-backed custom app that does exactly what their base does, that they own outright, is an easy pitch against a bill that is about to go up. Search your client notes for Airtable and give Grant and Bishop the names.
Bloomberg Reuters Il Sole 24 Ore
Legal August 3–4

Apple asked a judge to enjoin OpenAI and two former employees, and OpenAI published the receipts

Apple filed Monday for a preliminary injunction barring OpenAI and two former Apple staff from accessing, acquiring, using or disclosing alleged confidential information, plus a concurrent motion for expedited discovery. The named former employees are Chang Liu, a senior systems electrical engineer, and Tang Yew Tan, a vice president of product design for iPhone and Apple Watch. Apple alleges the trade secrets benefit OpenAI's move into consumer hardware.

OpenAI responded in a blog post saying the request is based on false information and unnecessary because it does not have and does not want Apple's trade secrets, and pointed to messages showing Apple employees continued texting a former colleague after the departure.

So what for TSD: Relevant to you as a contract term. You sell client-owned source code as a differentiator, which makes IP ownership language load-bearing in every agreement you sign. Make sure each contract states plainly what the client owns, what TSD retains as reusable components (Front Desk, Concierge, Booking Bridge), and that reusing your own framework across clients is explicitly permitted.
