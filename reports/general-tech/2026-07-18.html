Security Jul 20, 2026 (scheduled)

Next.js's first scheduled security release covers nine vulnerabilities, patching the 16.2 and 15.5 lines

The advance notice now carries numbers. Monday's release fixes nine vulnerabilities — four high-severity and five medium, with no criticals flagged — and Vercel says the patch updates will land on Next.js 16.2 and 15.5. CVE identifiers, proof-of-concept details and affected code paths stay withheld until the patches are public, which is the point of the program: give defenders a window without handing attackers a target list.

This is the first release under the new monthly cadence, which pairs a roughly monthly rhythm with advance public notice and keeps an emergency lane open for anything that can't wait until the next slot.

So what for TSD: Do the inventory today so Monday is mechanical. Every client site needs to be on 16.2 or 15.5 before you can take the patch — anything sitting on an older minor (16.0, 16.1, 15.3, 15.4) needs the minor bump first, and that's the part that can break a build, so find it now rather than at 9am Monday. Sites already on 16.2 or 15.5 are a one-line version bump plus a redeploy. Since the CVE list is embargoed, assume the worst case for the high-severity four and treat this as a same-day patch across the portfolio. It does not belong in next sprint.

Breaking change Jul 1 & Aug 9, 2026

Supabase's Postgres 14 deprecation already fired — and self-hosted gateways change in August

Supabase ended support for Postgres 14 on July 1, 2026. Projects still on a deprecated version were automatically upgraded to the latest available Postgres. The exception matters more than the rule: projects using extensions that don't survive on Postgres 17 — timescaledb, plv8, pls, plcoffee, or pgjwt — were paused instead and stopped serving traffic.

Looking ahead, the week of Aug 9, 2026 the default self-hosted Supabase API gateway switches from Kong to Envoy in docker-compose.yml, with Kong demoted to an optional override.

So what for TSD: Check every client project's Postgres version in the dashboard right now, and specifically check whether any of them ever used pgjwt — it was common in older auth setups and it is on the pause list. A paused project is a client whose site is down and who may not have told you. If any project got force-upgraded to 17, re-run the app's queries against it before you assume nothing broke. The Envoy swap only bites if you self-host, so pin your compose file now if you do.

◆ Security desk

Critical Jun–Jul 2026

"HollowByte": 11 bytes of TLS permanently inflates an OpenSSL server's memory, and the fix shipped with no CVE

A TLS handshake message declares its own size in a 4-byte header. Vulnerable OpenSSL builds allocate that declared size before the payload arrives and before checking whether the claim is honest. An attacker sends waves of connections claiming randomized sizes, then drops them; OpenSSL frees the buffers, but glibc holds small-to-medium allocations rather than returning them to the OS, and the randomized sizes stop the allocator from reusing them. Resident memory climbs and stays climbing after the attacker disconnects. Okta's tests against NGINX drained low-capacity servers entirely and cost higher-spec boxes up to 25% of memory, at attack bandwidth low enough to stay under alerting thresholds.

The unusual part is the disclosure. OpenSSL shipped the fix in June with no CVE, no advisory, and no changelog entry pointing at it. Patched versions are 4.0.1, with backports to 3.6.3, 3.5.7, 3.4.6 and 3.0.21, which now grow the buffer only as real data arrives.

So what for TSD: Vercel-hosted sites are covered by the platform, so this is not a Next.js problem. It's a problem for anything you or a client runs on a VPS or in a container behind NGINX — voice-AI endpoints, self-hosted Supabase, a webhook box, a client's legacy server. Run openssl version on those hosts and confirm you're at or above 3.0.21 / 3.4.6 / 3.5.7 / 3.6.3 / 4.0.1. Because there's no CVE, your dependency scanners and the usual "we're patched" dashboards will not flag this — you have to look manually. That's also a clean reason to send a managed-services client a note this week.

◆ Chips & hard tech

Milestone Jul 15, 2026

Intel is first to ship high-volume logic chips patterned with High-NA EUV

ASML confirmed that Intel Foundry is patterning selected Intel 18A layers on a portion of shipping Core Ultra Series 3 "Panther Lake" processors using an EXE High-NA EUV system — the industry's first customer shipment of a mass-produced logic product built this way. The qualification is narrow by design: specific layers, dual-qualified in Oregon against the existing 0.55 NA platform, at yields matched to the older NXE scanners. That's how every lithography generation gets introduced before it spreads across a node.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do today. The read is that Intel has a credible manufacturing story again, which matters to you only as the second supplier keeping AI-inference compute cheap over the next few years. Every product you sell — TSD Front Desk, TSD Concierge — has a token cost floor set by how many people can make advanced chips. One is a monopoly; two is a market.

Funding Jul 16, 2026

microagi raises $55M — Germany's largest seed ever — to sell footage of humans doing chores

Munich-based microagi closed a $55M seed led by Hummingbird, with Northzone, LocalGlobe, Village Global and Redalpine joining. The company is roughly ten months old, founded by an ex-Red Bull Racing aerodynamicist, an ex-Mercedes-AMG F1 engineer, a former Alan Turing Institute researcher, an RWTH Aachen engineer, and a WhatsApp-commerce founder. It pays more than 20,000 people across 15 countries to record themselves performing physical tasks, then sells that footage to the labs training humanoid robot brains, while putting engineers on-site with industrial customers so each deployment feeds the next.

So what for TSD: Ignore the robots and look at the business model. Investors paid a record price for proprietary training data plus a services motion that generates more of it — a company that sells the picks and does the deployment work. That's structurally what you already do: every client install of TSD Concierge or Front Desk produces domain-specific data and workflow knowledge. Right now you throw that away at the end of each engagement. Start writing down what each vertical's install teaches you, because the reusable artifact is what turns a one-off build into something with compounding value.
