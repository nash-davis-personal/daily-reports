◆ Patch now

Security Nov 11 · 22.23.5 / 24.22.3 / 26.13.1

Node.js fixes 16 CVEs; the worst leaks TLS keys from HTTPS servers

The Node.js project shipped v22.23.5, v24.22.3 and v26.13.1 on Wednesday. The advisory counts 16 CVEs: seven High, six Medium, three Low, plus dependency updates to llhttp on every line, undici on 24 and 26, and sqlite on 22 and 24.

The one to read twice is CVE-2026-123864. A zero-length TLS write can re-enter the HTTP parser, producing a use-after-free and out-of-bounds read that can put TLS keys and other users' data into response bodies. It affects all three lines; plain node:http is not affected. The other High fixes: an unauthenticated HTTP/2 remote abort (CVE-2026-123863, lines 24 and 26), an HTTP/2 memory and file-descriptor leak after a protocol error during shutdown (CVE-2026-123865), an HTTP/2 stream-teardown use-after-free that can send freed heap memory to the peer (CVE-2026-123866), a TLS server crash for servers using SNICallback or session callbacks (CVE-2026-126051, 24 and 26), a TLS 1.2 renegotiation trick that bypasses client-certificate auth (CVE-2026-123872), and a use-after-free in HTTP/2 respondWithFile() (CVE-2026-123874, 24 and 26). The Medium set includes proxy credentials leaking into ERR_PROXY_INVALID_CONFIG error messages (CVE-2026-123871).

So what for TSD: Do this today, in this order. (1) Vercel-hosted sites: Vercel terminates TLS at its edge and patches its own Node runtime, so your exposure there is low; confirm each project's Node version is 22.x or 24.x in Project Settings and redeploy once so the build picks up the newest patch. Any project still pinned to Node 20 is out of support and gets no fix, so move it. (2) Anything you run yourself that serves HTTPS or HTTP/2 straight from Node (a Vapi webhook box, the Mac running this daily-reports server, any client VPS): upgrade Node now and restart. (3) Update your local machine with nvm install 24 or your installer of choice. If a client asks, the plain answer is "a server bug that could expose encrypted traffic was fixed yesterday and your site was updated within a day."

Security Nov 11 · httpd 2.4.71

Apache HTTP Server: critical remote bug in mod_http2

Wiz published details of CVE-2026-119618, a race condition in assign_headers() in Apache's HTTP/2 module. A remote attacker with no credentials can corrupt heap memory, and code execution is possible. Affected versions are httpd 2.4.55 through 2.4.70 with HTTP/2 enabled; 2.4.71 fixes it. The stopgap is to set Protocols http/1.1. Wiz reported no public exploit and no attacks in the wild at publication, and says 64% of cloud environments it sees contain a vulnerable resource. No CVSS score was published on the page.

So what for TSD: Nothing you host on Vercel runs Apache. This one is for prospects and for the old sites you replace: most cPanel and shared WordPress hosting is Apache with HTTP/2 on, and those hosts patch on their own schedule. When you audit a prospect's current site, check the Server response header; an unpatched Apache is a concrete, non-scary line for the "why move" slide.

Windows Nov 10–12

Record Patch Tuesday, and the patches themselves fail on some Windows 11 machines

Tuesday's Microsoft release fixed 1,235 flaws, 66 of them Critical, the largest batch on record per BleepingComputer. One was already being exploited: CVE-2026-124194, an Outlook spoofing bug that leaks NTLMv2 password hashes when a user opens a crafted message file. On Thursday Microsoft confirmed the November security updates fail to install on Windows 11 systems that had taken the October preview update; The Register cites KB5132061 and error codes 0x800F0993 and 0x800F0989.

So what for TSD: Your own machines are Macs. Your clients' front desks are not. If a client mentions a Windows update that keeps failing this week, this is why, and the fix is Microsoft's to ship. The Outlook bug is a good reason to remind any office client not to open emailed .msg or .eml attachments from strangers.

◆ Chips and companies

Developing Nov 11–12

Nvidia's board met on whether to remove its CEO; no decision announced

The Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove founder and CEO Jensen Huang. CNBC confirmed the board met Wednesday on the question, and as of its Thursday morning story there was no decision and no company statement. The New York Times reported a second relationship, with Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports to Huang; the board's investigation, opened Nov 8, began with reporting that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. Bloomberg says the board is working with an outside firm on candidates and may name a temporary chief, and Semafor says some directors favor CFO Colette Kress for that role. Both of those are single-outlet reports.

Shares were down 6.9% before the open Thursday and more than 20% over the past month; after-hours figures Wednesday ranged from about 5% to 8.6% depending on the outlet. Bernstein cut its price target from $400 to $200. AMD rose 3.9% and Broadcom 3% premarket. Two senators asked the administration to preserve documents on Nvidia's China exports. Q3 results are due Nov 17.

So what for TSD: No effect on anything you ship. It is the largest company in the AI supply chain going into earnings with its leadership in question, so expect a noisy week in every AI headline your clients read. If one asks whether "the AI thing is falling apart," the honest answer is that this is a governance story at one supplier and the models you build on run on four different chip families.
