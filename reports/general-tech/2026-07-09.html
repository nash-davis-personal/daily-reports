◆ The AI buildout meets a chip wobble

Shift Jul 8, 2026

China may clear H200 purchases — Nvidia pops, but the sector is cooling

Nvidia rose 3.47% on July 8 after reports that Chinese officials told Alibaba, ByteDance and DeepSeek they may soon be permitted to buy some of Nvidia's H200 processors. The oddity: Nvidia is up just ~3.2% year-to-date while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index has surged ~47% and AI chip stocks have added roughly $2 trillion in value — making the company at the center of the buildout a laggard. The index has now fallen 16% from its June 22 peak and slipped below its 50-day moving average for the first time since April.

So what for TSD: Indirect, but worth watching. Compute supply and pricing sit upstream of every inference bill Nash pays. More chips reaching big buyers plus a cooling chip rally points toward continued downward pressure on model-serving costs — the same trend making Terra and Grok cheap. Good for Managed AI margins; nothing to act on today.

◆ Nash's stack: Vercel + Next.js

Platform Jul 2026

Vercel expands from host to "operating layer" — Services, Agent, Connect, and voice

Vercel is pushing well past static/Next hosting. Vercel Services (launched July 1) makes microservices first-class: deploy Go, Rails, FastAPI and other Docker backends inside one project with secure service-to-service bindings. Vercel Agent (private beta) watches production deployments, investigates alerts and anomalies on its own, and opens fixes as PRs for review. Vercel Connect gives agents secure access to tools and data, and AI Gateway now handles realtime voice, STT and TTS with routing and observability — including Grok audio and Claude Sonnet 5.

So what for TSD: Two concrete openings. Vercel Services means Nash can take on client jobs that need a Python or Go backend without leaving the stack he already knows. And AI Gateway voice is a real candidate to consolidate the Front Desk receptionist's voice pipeline — one vendor, built-in observability — worth a head-to-head against the current Vapi setup on latency and cost.

Framework Jun 29, 2026

Next.js 16.3 lands a persistent build cache and 90% less dev memory

Next.js 16.3 turns on a persistent Turbopack build cache by default (via turbopackFileSystemCacheForBuild), so next build reuses previously computed work — up to 5.5x faster production builds after hardening on Vercel's own sites. Dev-server memory is down as much as 90% on large apps, and the release adds an experimental Rust port of the React Compiler plus Vite-style import.meta.glob. Both memory eviction and filesystem caching are on by default.

So what for TSD: This shortens Nash's delivery loop directly. Faster builds and lighter dev memory mean quicker iteration on client sites and cheaper CI. Turn the persistent build cache on across the Next repos this week and pocket the time.
