◆ Your stack: ship faster, then patch

Feature Jun 25, 2026

Next.js 16.3 preview brings SPA-speed navigation to the App Router

Vercel announced the Next.js 16.3 preview with two features aimed squarely at perceived speed. Instant Navigations lets you mark a route Stream, Cache, or Block to make a link click feel immediate. Partial Prefetching caches a reusable shell per route on the client, so a click renders that shell instantly while the rest of the page streams in. It's the responsiveness of a client-side SPA without leaving the App Router.

So what for TSD: This is a free conversion lever. Test 16.3 on the portfolio first, then roll Instant Navigations into the highest-traffic routes on client sites (Bisque, Studio C, the demos). "Your site now feels instant" is a real upgrade you can show a client, and it costs you a config change, not a rebuild.

Patch Active

Confirm every TSD app is on the patched Next.js / React versions

Vercel's coordinated security release fixed 13 advisories across denial of service, SSRF in the self-hosted WebSocket upgrade handler, App Router XSS, Image Optimization DoS, and cache poisoning in RSC responses and middleware redirects. Patched releases: Next.js 16.2.5 and 15.5.16; React server packages react-server-dom-webpack / -parcel / -turbopack at 19.0.6, 19.1.7, and 19.2.6. The DoS issues (CVE-2026-23869/23870) hit App Router server functions on Next 13.x–16.x.

So what for TSD: Spend 20 minutes today. Run npm ls next react-server-dom-webpack across the fleet and bump anything below 16.2.5 / 19.2.6. Vercel-hosted apps inherit some WAF mitigations, but self-hosted or older client sites are the exposure. This is also a sellable security-patch sprint (see today's Money Moves).

◆ Hardware: the AI buildout keeps printing

New Jun 25, 2026

IBM unveils the first manufacturable sub-1nm chip

IBM announced "NanoStack," a 0.7nm (7-angstrom) architecture it calls the world's first manufacturable sub-1nm chip technology, claiming up to a 50% performance gain or a 70% energy reduction versus the 2nm node. Shares rose more than 6%. Practically, it's a credibility marker that the transistor-scaling roadmap still has road left — production silicon is years out, but the direction matters for everyone building on compute.

So what for TSD: No near-term effect on what you ship, but it's the long-run tailwind under the whole business: cheaper, more efficient compute keeps pushing inference costs down, which is what makes Front Desk and Concierge affordable for a small clinic. Cheaper silicon = fatter margins on managed-AI retainers.

Markets Jun 24, 2026

Micron posts a record quarter as AI memory demand explodes

Micron reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $41.5B with gross margin near 85% — a company record — and guided the August quarter to $49–51B. The stock jumped roughly 16% and dragged the rest of the memory complex up with it (Sandisk +22%, Applied Materials +13%). The read-through: high-bandwidth memory, not just GPUs, is now a hard bottleneck in the AI buildout, and pricing power has shifted to the suppliers.

So what for TSD: No direct action — this is backdrop. But it confirms the AI infrastructure cycle is still accelerating, which keeps demand high for the applied layer Nash actually sells. When the picks-and-shovels guys are printing records, the "help my SMB use this stuff" market is healthy.
