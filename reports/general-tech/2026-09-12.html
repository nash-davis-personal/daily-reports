◆ Patch this weekend

Critical September 10–11

GitLab's CVE-2026-85706 is a 10.0, and attackers moved in a day

The flaw sits in GitLab's repository commits API. Improper path confinement plus missing authentication enforcement lets an unauthenticated attacker read arbitrary files off the server in a single HTTP request — source, secrets, tokens, whatever the process can reach. GitLab scored it CVSS 3.1 10.0 and it affects Community and Enterprise editions from 18.7 before 19.1.8, 19.2 before 19.2.6, and 19.3 before 19.3.2.

Fixes are in 19.1.8, 19.2.6 and 19.3.2. Internet-wide probing started the day after disclosure, and CISA has added the bug to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. GitLab.com itself is run by GitLab; the exposure is self-managed instances.

So what for TSD: Your own repos are on GitHub, so nothing to patch on your side. The exposure is client-side: any SMB or agency partner running a self-hosted GitLab behind their firewall is one unauthenticated request from leaking every credential in it. If you have touched a client's self-managed GitLab, send the version numbers above today — that is a free, high-trust touch that costs you one text message.

Breaking September 8–11

Microsoft's September server updates are killing Remote Desktop Services

KB5122876 for Windows Server 2019, KB5122882 for 2022 and KB5122871 for 2025, all released September 8, are cutting off Remote Desktop connections. The failure pattern is nasty: servers run normally for a few hours after install, then new connections hang and time out while existing sessions cannot disconnect or log off. Debugging points at a deadlock between Remote Desktop and the Local Session Manager with no timeout set, and the worst cases need a hard reboot.

Microsoft told BleepingComputer it is investigating. As of September 10 there was no out-of-band fix and no timeline. Admins report that removing the September updates restores RDS — and removes this month's security fixes along with it.

So what for TSD: Not your stack, but it is the stack under a lot of Charlotte-area shops with an on-prem server their bookkeeper logs into. This is a concrete reason to move a client's scheduling or intake off a Remote Desktop session and onto something you host — and it is a real, checkable event to open that conversation with rather than a pitch.

Security September 11

An AI-assisted operator hit 395 organizations through print servers

Researchers documented a campaign that compromised 440 exposed PaperCut instances across 395 organizations in 48 countries, stealing credentials from 280 victims. Education was the heaviest-hit sector at 204 victims. The operator, described as likely Russian-speaking, used OpenAI Codex and DeepSeek to assist the work, and in one school went from initial access to domain admin in seven minutes.

The interesting part is the speed, not the vulnerability. Print management software is exactly the kind of forgotten internet-facing box a small organization never patches, and agent assistance collapses the time between finding one and owning the domain.

So what for TSD: The attacker economics changed in the direction of small targets. Any client site you host should have its admin surface off the public internet and its dependency alerts watched — sell that as a line item in the Managed AI retainer rather than absorbing it, because "we patch and monitor" is now a thing a shop owner understands the reason for.
BleepingComputer / GreyNoise reporting · TechStartups summary, September 11

◆ Compute became a balance-sheet problem

Earnings September 11

Oracle booked $7.4B of AI infrastructure revenue and spent $28.5B to get it

Oracle's infrastructure revenue came in at $7.4 billion with cloud growth reported in the 64–70% range year over year. Capex for the period ran $28.5 billion against $8.5 billion a year earlier, which pushed free cash flow to roughly negative $5 billion. Backlog was reported at $664 billion, and the company raised $20 billion in equity.

That is the whole shape of the AI infrastructure trade in one quarter. Revenue is real and growing fast; the spend to serve it is growing faster, and the gap is being financed rather than earned.

So what for TSD: Inference prices are being subsidized by balance sheets that are currently cash-flow negative. Budget your client AI products at today's token prices with a margin that survives a 2x increase, and avoid signing a fixed-price retainer whose economics only work at promotional rates.
TechStartups, citing Financial Times reporting
Infrastructure September 11

Microsoft maps 38 gigawatts while the Pentagon lends $5B to a compute provider

Microsoft laid out a path to 38 gigawatts of datacenter capacity by 2032, up from roughly 12 GW today, a figure that exceeds New York State's peak electricity demand, with AI workloads around a third of the 2032 total. It brought 88 sites online in FY2026, 31 in one quarter, on $145 billion of capex this year.

On the same day, the Department of Defense's Office of Strategic Capital moved on a loan of about $5 billion to Fluidstack, a London-founded, New York-based compute provider previously backed by $1.5 billion from Jane Street. It would be the office's largest loan to date.

So what for TSD: Power and siting are now the binding constraint on AI capacity, not chips. That is the real reason a provider can pause signups overnight, and it argues for keeping a second model provider wired into every product you ship.
TechStartups, citing Bloomberg, WSJ and Reuters reporting

◆ Silicon money keeps arriving

Markets September 11

Enflame closed up 188% on its Shanghai debut

The Tencent-backed AI chip designer jumped 188% on its first day of trading, peaking at 475 yuan, after raising about 6.12 billion yuan — roughly $911 million. Retail demand oversubscribed the offer about 4,000 times and the post-IPO market capitalization landed near $26.3 billion.

Enflame is still unprofitable and projects breakeven somewhere in 2026–2027, and Tencent holds roughly 18% of it while accounting for the large majority of its 2025 revenue. Domestic financing for Chinese accelerators is not scarce, which is the context for this week's 20–50% price rises on Chinese AI chips under a memory shortage.

So what for TSD: No direct action, but it tells you the cheap open-weight models you rely on will keep having hardware to train and serve on. That is what makes a multi-provider routing strategy durable rather than a temporary arbitrage.
TechStartups, citing South China Morning Post
