◆ Patch first — your stack and the enterprise apps around it

Action item Effective Oct 30, 2026

Supabase will enforce new access-security defaults on all existing projects

Supabase has announced that new security behavior around table access will be enforced on every existing project starting October 30. Between now and then, the built-in Security Advisor flags affected tables, and Supabase is emailing project owners so they can review access, add the right grants, and verify their app still works under the stricter defaults.

So what for TSD: Every client site you've shipped on Supabase needs a once-over before October. Open the Security Advisor on each project this week, fix anything it flags, and confirm the app still reads/writes correctly. Do it now while it's a 20-minute check — not in late October when a missed grant silently breaks a client's booking form.

Heads-up 16.2.9 LTS ◆ June 9

Next.js holds at 16.2.9 — confirm every client site is actually on it

Next.js 16.2.9 (LTS) remains the current stable release after this month's security-and-stability work, which backported fixes across Server Components, the App Router, Middleware/Proxy, route handlers, and cache handling. No newer stable has shipped since last week's briefing — the job is making sure your deployed sites are pinned to it, not still on an earlier 16.2.x.

So what for TSD: Run a quick audit: for each Next.js client project, check the locked version and bump anything below 16.2.9, then redeploy. The recent advisories touch DoS, middleware/proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning, and XSS — exactly the surface a client-facing site exposes.

Security June 13

Active PeopleSoft extortion campaign and a fresh Splunk Enterprise flaw

Google's security units identified an active compromise-and-extortion campaign targeting Oracle PeopleSoft, attributed to the group ShinyHunters. Separately, a vulnerability in Splunk Enterprise (versions 10.0.0–10.0.6 and 10.2.0–10.2.3) was disclosed June 13. In parallel, an INTERPOL-led operation took down the Sniper Dz phishing-as-a-service platform, with 201 arrests across 13 countries.

So what for TSD: You don't run PeopleSoft or Splunk, but your SMB clients' larger vendors might — and phishing-as-a-service is the threat that reaches them directly. It's a clean reason to add a basic "security hygiene" line to a managed retainer: dependency patching, 2FA, and a phishing-aware contact form are cheap to deliver and easy to sell after a week of headlines like these.

◆ The buildout behind the models

Hardware April data

Global chip sales hit $110.5B, up 11%, on AI-datacenter demand

Worldwide semiconductor sales reached $110.5B in April, up 11% from March, with full-year 2026 sales projected to top $1.5 trillion, per the Semiconductor Industry Association. A separate SIA-Deloitte study estimates annual revenue from chips deployed in AI data centers could exceed $1.2 trillion by 2028. Meanwhile Microsoft kept widening its in-house model family (MAI-Code-1-Flash, MAI-Thinking-1) to reduce its dependence on OpenAI.

So what for TSD: The supply side of the AI boom is still accelerating, which means the API prices you build on aren't going to fall to zero — capacity is being bought, not given away. Keep designing client AI features to be cost-aware (cache aggressively, route cheap models for easy turns) so your margins don't depend on prices that the buildout is keeping firm.
