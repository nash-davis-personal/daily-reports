◆ The layer everyone forgot to own

New Aug 16, 2026

Stripe finalizes a deal to acquire OpenRouter for more than $7 billion

Bloomberg reported Sunday that Stripe has agreed to buy OpenRouter for over $7 billion, confirming talks the Wall Street Journal surfaced last month. OpenRouter is the aggregation layer that lets a developer hit one endpoint and route to any of more than 400 models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and DeepSeek, choosing on price, latency or capability without rewriting anything. It serves roughly eight million developers. In May it raised a $113 million Series B at a reported $1.3 billion valuation, so this is about five times that in three months. Bloomberg notes the final price could still move.

The logic is that Stripe already runs the metering and billing rail for a large share of internet businesses, and per-token AI spend is turning into a line item on those same invoices. Owning the router means owning usage data across every major lab and being the default place a startup's AI costs get measured and charged.

So what for TSD: Two things. First, if you are not already using an aggregation layer for your product traffic, this is the signal that model-portability is becoming standard plumbing rather than an optimization, and it is the cheapest hedge against the kind of provider outage Claude had Sunday night. Second, and more useful commercially: Stripe buying the metering layer tells you where the money is in AI services. It is in the recurring measured usage, not the build. Price TSD Front Desk and Concierge per-conversation with a floor rather than as flat projects, because the whole industry is repricing toward metered consumption and your clients will find that structure familiar within a year.

◆ Your stack

Check this Aug 5 & Aug 9, 2026

Two Supabase changes took effect this month; Next.js and React security is quiet

Nothing new to patch on the framework side. The most recent Next.js security advisories are still the batch from July 21, covering SSRF in rewrites, SSRF in Server Actions, a Turbopack middleware bypass, App Router denial of service, cache confusion and an unbounded Server Action payload on the Edge runtime. If you patched those, you are current. Vercel's changelog has nothing since Encrypted Client Hello on August 14.

Supabase is where the live changes are, and both were announced in July with August effective dates, which is the kind of thing that slips past. As of August 5, an explicit version clause in CREATE EXTENSION or ALTER EXTENSION is deprecated and the version is ignored, so a migration that pins pgvector or pg_cron to a specific version no longer does what it says. And the week of August 9, self-hosted Supabase switched its default API gateway from Kong to Envoy. If you rely on Kong's HTTPS listener, a custom kong.yml, or the kong service name in a compose file, you have to opt back in. Also worth noting from July 14: the realtime schema is now fully locked, and any create, alter or drop against it fails with a permission error.

So what for TSD: Run one grep across your migration folders this week for "CREATE EXTENSION" and "ALTER EXTENSION" with a version clause. Those lines are now lies, and a client project that assumed a pinned extension version is quietly running whatever Supabase ships. Fix the assumption rather than the syntax: assert the extension's behavior in a smoke test instead of pinning it. The Kong-to-Envoy switch only matters if you self-host anything, which today you do not, but note it before you quote a client on an on-prem deployment.

Infrastructure Aug 17, 2026

Agent workloads made the CPU the bottleneck, and AWS is rationing cycles

IEEE Spectrum published a piece today on a shift that has been building quietly. The AI buildout has been a GPU story, then a memory story, and CPUs were treated as a solved commodity because they are poor at inference. Agentic systems changed the ratio. Researchers from Georgia Tech and Intel profiled five agent workloads and found that in the tool-dominated ones, up to 88 percent of runtime went to CPU-side processing: retrieval, summarization, file I/O, preprocessing, and the plumbing between tool calls.

The practical consequence is showing up inside Amazon. AWS leaders told engineers to conserve CPU cycles, and engineers report waiting days for EC2 capacity they used to get in hours. Amazon disputes that there is a formal crackdown, but the underlying pressure is not in dispute: spare general-purpose compute stopped being spare.

So what for TSD: This is why your agent runs feel slow in ways that a faster model does not fix. Before you reach for a bigger model on Concierge or the briefing pipeline, profile where the wall-clock actually goes. If most of it is retrieval and file handling, the wins are caching, batching tool calls, and cutting round-trips, and those are free. It is also a pricing argument: when you quote a Managed AI retainer, the cost driver is tool-call volume as much as tokens, so meter on conversations rather than promising unlimited agent runs.

◆ Who is actually paying for the buildout

New Aug 17, 2026

The Meta and BlackRock El Paso campus is reported to be only partially insured

The venture behind the roughly $14 billion El Paso data center campus is structured with BlackRock-managed funds holding 80 percent and Meta the remaining 20. Meta contributed land and construction-in-progress worth about $2.3 billion; BlackRock put in roughly $4.9 billion in cash, funded in part by a $12.5 billion debt package. A special-purpose vehicle called Sopaipilla Investor issued bonds maturing in 2048 against a campus designed to deliver up to a gigawatt for AI workloads. Marsh handled project risk analysis, working for both sides of the transaction.

The Financial Times reported today that the project lacks insurance against total loss, leaving lenders exposed. Bloomberg has separately described the coverage as partial. Neither Meta nor BlackRock has publicly detailed the policy structure, so treat the specifics as reporting rather than confirmed terms, but the direction is consistent across outlets: 22-year paper against a single-purpose asset whose economics depend on AI demand holding.

So what for TSD: No direct action, but this is the shape of the risk sitting under your input costs. Model prices have been falling because capacity is being financed aggressively. If that financing tightens, the discount windows you are planning around, including Gemini 3.7 Flash's price through December 31, are the first things to close. Do not architect a client's unit economics on a promotional token price. Quote with the post-promo rate as your floor and treat the discount as margin.
Reuters via Yahoo Finance · Insurance Business · Construction Review
Analysis Aug 16, 2026

PJM's capacity modeling overstated its shortfall, and ratepayers covered about $12 billion of it

SemiAnalysis published a teardown of how PJM, the largest US grid operator, models the capacity its existing power plants can actually deliver. The finding is that the methodology dramatically overstated the supply shortfall, and the auctions priced accordingly. Their estimate is that better modeling would have saved about $6.7 billion in the 2025/26 auction while procuring only 14 megawatts less power, and another $4.9 billion in 2026/27 for 0.8 gigawatts less.

The compounding factor is speculative data-center load. When PJM includes announced data centers in its forecast, it must procure capacity to serve them, and ratepayers pay for that reserved capacity now whether or not the facilities are ever built. PJM's most recent capacity auction cleared at a record $16.1 billion in total cost, up 9.5 percent from the prior record.

So what for TSD: Indirect, but real for your client conversations. PJM does not cover the Carolinas, so Charlotte-area SMBs are on Duke's rate structure rather than this one. What travels is the pattern: commercial electricity is becoming a visible line item for small businesses, and the shops you serve are already feeling it. If you are selling a modernization scope to a trades or retail client this fall, "your overhead is going up and your marketing spend has to work harder" is a truer opener than anything about AI.
