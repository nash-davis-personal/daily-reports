Security Release due Jul 20 · advisories from May 2026

Confirm your Next.js apps are patched before the next drop

Vercel's next coordinated Next.js security release is due July 20. The last coordinated round shipped 13 advisories spanning denial of service, middleware/proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning and XSS. The one most likely to touch TSD: CVE-2026-23870, a high-severity denial of service in React Server Components that hits App Router deployments across Next 13.x, 14.x, 15.x and 16.x — a crafted request to a Server Function endpoint can spike CPU and take the app down. Two others to know: CVE-2026-44578 (SSRF via crafted WebSocket upgrade on self-hosted Node) and CVE-2026-44573 (middleware-auth bypass on the Pages Router with i18n).

TSD's Next 16 apps run App Router, so the RSC DoS is the relevant one. The middleware-bypass CVE targets middleware-based auth on the Pages Router — TSD's proxy.ts pattern isn't that setup, so that one is lower concern.

So what for TSD: Today: run npm ls next across your client repos and bump any App Router app to the latest patched Next release, then redeploy on Vercel. Put a reminder for July 20 to pull the next security release the day it lands. This is a five-minute check that prevents a client site going down to a single malicious request.

Earnings Q2 2026

TSMC posts record revenue on AI demand

Taiwan Semiconductor reported Q2 revenue of NT$1.27 trillion (about $39.6B), up 36% year over year, driven by AI accelerator orders from Nvidia and Apple-tied clients. TSMC is the single clearest read on whether the AI infrastructure boom is still accelerating — and this quarter says it is.

So what for TSD: Nothing to build here, but it's the macro backdrop for your pitch: AI capability is compounding and getting cheaper at the model layer while demand at the chip layer keeps climbing. That's the "this isn't a fad" evidence for a hesitant SMB owner deciding whether to invest in an AI receptionist now.

Meta pushes its custom "Iris" chip toward production

Meta plans a September manufacturing start for Iris, its custom TSMC-built data-center chip (with Broadcom), aiming to scale compute to 14 gigawatts by 2027 as part of up to $145B in AI infrastructure spend this year. Every hyperscaler is now trying to own its silicon — the same move Anthropic is making with Samsung.

So what for TSD: Custom silicon at the giants pushes inference cost down over the next few years, which quietly improves the margin on every AI product you resell. No action — just keep pricing AI retainers on today's API cost, knowing the trend is in your favor.

◆ Breaches to know

Incident Jul 13

Progress ShareFile disables accounts; a US lab loses 540K records

Progress disabled ShareFile customer accounts tied to on-premises Storage Zone Controllers after a credible external security threat, citing risk to confidential documents and credentials. Separately, Centers Laboratory disclosed a breach affecting 540,000 people, with the WorldLeaks extortion group claiming 720GB including patient identities, insurance and diagnostic data.

So what for TSD: If any client uses ShareFile, forward the advisory and tell them to verify account status today. Broader point: this is the recurring proof for a security-hygiene add-on — dependency patching, credential rotation, backup checks — that Grant or Bishop can pitch to your existing SMB base as a low-lift monthly line item.
