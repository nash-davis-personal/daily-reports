Ownership September 9

Tailwind Labs is joining Shopify

Tailwind Labs announced it is joining Shopify, describing the move as a stable long-term home for a framework installed more than 110 million times a week and used to style products including ChatGPT, X, Cloudflare, Reddit and Shopify itself. Tailwind says its open-source projects stay MIT-licensed and nothing changes for existing open-source users. The company is closing new sign-ups for its commercial products, Tailwind Plus and ui.sh; existing customers retain access.

Shopify's side of the story is storefronts. PYMNTS frames the deal as fuel for Shopify's custom storefront push, which is where you would expect Tailwind's roadmap attention to drift over the next year.

So what for TSD: No client build needs to change. MIT code cannot be taken back, and a maintained framework under a profitable owner is a better bet than a small company selling templates to pay for it. Two practical notes. If any build depends on Tailwind Plus components, confirm your license still covers it and do not plan a template or kit product around Tailwind Plus licensing. And for any client that sells products, a Shopify storefront with Tailwind is now the most first-party pairing on the market, so lead with it when an ecommerce prospect asks what you build on.

Breaking change Removal September 23

Supabase removes the logs.all endpoint in twelve days

Supabase's Management API logs.all analytics endpoint is removed on September 23, 2026. Scripts calling it must move to the new ClickHouse-backed logs endpoint, which accepts ClickHouse SQL only, so existing queries need a rewrite on top of the URL change. The notice has been on the changelog since July 23. Supabase's other recent entries are fixes worth knowing about: daily backups could occasionally be skipped during a 10-minute scheduling window (fixed August 12), and physical restores could leave stale database credentials behind (fixed July 30).

So what for TSD: Patch instruction: search every repo you own for logs.all today, including any monitoring or status scripts for client projects, and rewrite those queries in ClickHouse SQL before the 23rd. If nothing turns up, the backup fix is still a prompt to confirm each client project's most recent backup exists and that a restore actually connects.

Platform September 10–11

Vercel doubled Sandbox storage and wired in OpenAI's Agents API

Vercel Sandbox now provides 64 GB of storage per sandbox by default, up from 32 GB, on the latest SDK and CLI and when created from an image. A day earlier Vercel published a guide to building OpenAI Agents API apps on Next.js, Functions, Queues and Sandbox, and Vercel is one of the nine launch sandbox partners for that API. GitHub Copilot joined the AI SDK harness layer through @ai-sdk/harness-github-copilot, using the same interface as the other supported harnesses. FastAPI frontends and static files are now promoted to the CDN at build time, so they no longer invoke Functions. And Tako Search, live web search usable by any AI Gateway model, is free through September 30.

So what for TSD: The free Tako Search window is the cheapest experiment on this list. A Concierge that can answer "are you open on the holiday" from a live search costs you nothing to prototype this month, so wire it into one demo site before the 30th and decide whether it earns a line item. The Agents API guide plus 64 GB sandboxes means an agent that clones a client repo, runs a build and reports back now fits comfortably on infrastructure you already pay for.

Database September 10

PlanetScale's Neki brings sharding to real Postgres

PlanetScale released Neki in platform preview: horizontally sharded Postgres from the team behind Vitess, built from scratch. Every shard is a full Postgres cluster with one primary and at least two replicas across three availability zones. Applications connect to a Neki router over the standard Postgres wire protocol, so existing drivers, ORMs and connection strings keep working, and Neki handles routing, resharding, online schema changes and version upgrades.

So what for TSD: No SMB client of yours needs sharding, and Supabase stays the right default. What Neki gives you is an honest answer when a prospect asks whether a Postgres app will scale: there is now a managed path to scale real Postgres horizontally without rewriting the application. Keep your schemas plain Postgres and that path stays open.

Antitrust September 9–10

The DOJ opened a formal probe of Nvidia's Groq deal

The Justice Department is investigating whether Nvidia structured its licensing agreement with AI chip startup Groq, valued at roughly $17B to $20B across reports, to avoid antitrust review. Nvidia took a non-exclusive license to Groq's chip technology and hired several executives including founder Jonathan Ross. No merger notice was filed. The DOJ has sent Nvidia a formal demand for information. Reporting says the agency may fine the company but would probably not require the deal to be undone.

The legal question is broader than Nvidia: whether a technology license paired with a mass talent transfer counts as an acquisition that should have been reported before it closed. That structure has been used repeatedly across AI.

So what for TSD: The pattern regulators are now testing is the one where a startup's product survives on paper after its leadership and core team leave. If a small AI vendor you depend on gets licensed-and-hired, expect the product to stall. Keep model and voice providers behind an interface you control, such as AI Gateway or your own adapter, so a vendor event costs you a config change.

Silicon September 10

Memory shortages pushed Chinese AI chip prices up 20% to 50%

Reuters reported that Huawei lifted the indicated price of its Ascend 950DT accelerator card above 250,000 yuan, about $37,255, a 20% to 50% jump from quotes two months ago, and Cambricon repriced its next-generation chip 20% to 30% higher. The cause is high-bandwidth memory. Since U.S. export controls on advanced HBM tightened in December 2024, Chinese chipmakers have leaned on grey-market supply that costs several times what buyers outside China pay, and memory is a large share of an accelerator's cost.

So what for TSD: No direct cost to you today. Memory is the choke point across the whole industry, and rising memory prices are already squeezing PC and phone configurations, so client hardware refreshes will cost more this year. The number to watch is whether memory scarcity slows the token price declines your AI product margins now depend on.
