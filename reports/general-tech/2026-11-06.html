◆ Your stack: Vercel, Next.js, Supabase, Node

Vercel Nov 5–6

Vercel Schedules (public beta) runs cron and one-time jobs without a redeploy; Token Pass credit now applies automatically on AI Gateway

Vercel Schedules is now in public beta on all plans. A schedule runs a task on a recurring cadence or at a single point in time, in production, preview or custom environments. Static schedules in vercel.json can target a private Vercel Function, an HTTP path, or a Vercel Queue topic; schedules created at runtime target a Queue topic. Any schedule can be enabled, disabled or run on demand without redeploying, and Eve can create dynamic schedules. On Nov 6 Vercel also made Token Pass credit (the $30/month Pro add-on with $80 of credit for featured open-weight models) apply automatically to included model usage through AI Gateway whether a request authenticates with an API key, an OIDC token or an fx login. And Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash video generation went live on AI Gateway on Nov 5 with one key, spend tracking and request traces.

So what for TSD: Schedules replaces the pile of cron routes and third-party pingers across client projects. Move Booking Bridge reminders and the daily report jobs to a schedule that dispatches to a Queue topic so a retry is free; the one-time variant is exactly what "send the follow-up 24 hours after the call" needs in TSD Front Desk. Public beta means test it on one client before you migrate all of them.
Vercel changelog: Schedules · Vercel changelog: Token Pass on AI Gateway, Omni 1.2 Flash · Token Pass docs
Status Nov 6

Next.js: no new advisory since the Nov 3 set; Supabase's TLS cipher change lands Tuesday; Node.js security releases follow around Nov 11

GitHub's advisory list for vercel/next.js still shows the seven entries published Nov 3 and nothing newer; the fixed versions remain 16.4.5 (Active LTS) and 15.5.29 (Maintenance LTS). The highest-impact item in that set is the cross-tenant cache disclosure when unstable_cache and use cache are combined. On Supabase, the breaking change announced Oct 12 takes effect Nov 10: an outdated subset of TLS 1.2 ciphers is disabled on project HTTP APIs (Auth, Data APIs, Storage, Realtime, Edge Functions). Supabase says under 0.0001% of traffic uses them, but a client that does will fail to connect rather than degrade. Node.js has security releases for 22.x, 24.x and 26.x scheduled on or about Nov 11 with a highest severity of HIGH, as noted in the Nov 4 edition.

So what for TSD: Three lines for the weekend checklist: grep '"next"' */package.json across client repos and confirm every one is on 16.4.5 or 15.5.29; check any client with an old on-prem integration (a POS, a Windows scheduled task) that calls Supabase directly, since that is the only kind of client the cipher cut will bite; and plan the Node bump for the Vercel runtime next week once the Node advisories publish.

◆ Chips: the rout, the handset miss, and the Taalas price

Markets Nov 5

The semiconductor index fell 12.77% in one session; Qualcomm's handset chips dropped 26% on a 14-year low in phone shipments

Thursday was the worst day for the iShares Semiconductor ETF since March 2020, down 12.77% on the Nasdaq close, with Nvidia off roughly 9% and AMD about 17%. Two causes were named across coverage. First, investors read Tuesday's election results and local ballot measures as a signal that data-center construction and AI rules could tighten; one estimate put candidates critical of the buildout in control of states with about 40% of planned US data-center capacity. Second, phone-chip results were weak: Qualcomm's fiscal Q4 handset revenue came in at $5.14 billion, down 26%, after Counterpoint measured global smartphone shipments down 20% in the September quarter to a 14-year low, with memory costs pushed up by AI data-center demand. Qualcomm's chip-segment guidance of $9.0–9.6 billion beat the $8.88 billion estimate, which did not help; shares fell about 9% premarket and more during the day, and at least eight brokerages cut targets. Arm's outlook also disappointed. JPMorgan's take: the AI boom stays on track but gets costlier and slower. Futures were mixed Friday morning with Nasdaq futures bouncing; the index numbers and the a-political read are in today's America brief.

So what for TSD: Nothing in your delivery stack changes, but two client-facing things do. Cheap phones get scarcer and pricier into 2027 as memory goes to data centers, so mobile performance budgets on client sites matter more; keep the Lighthouse mobile score a line item. And when a client asks whether "the AI bubble popped," the answer is that model prices kept falling the same day the stocks did (see Omni 1.2 Flash in General AI).

Deal Nov 5

AMD's 10-Q puts Taalas at about $2.9B in cash and stock, $1.4B of it contingent; World Labs at about $8.2B in stock

AMD's quarterly filing for the period ended September 26 discloses what the August press release did not: the agreement to buy Toronto inference-silicon startup Taalas is valued at approximately $2.9 billion, including up to about $1.4 billion of contingent consideration, in cash and stock, with closing expected by the end of 2026 subject to regulatory approval. Taalas, founded in 2023, etches model weights directly into silicon for low-cost inference; AMD plans to fold it into the Instinct roadmap. The same note lists the September agreement to acquire World Labs Technologies in an all-stock deal valued at approximately $8.2 billion. Both are pending.

So what for TSD: Inference keeps getting its own silicon, which is why per-token prices fall every quarter even when chip stocks do not. Budget the AI line in client retainers on the assumption that your model cost halves roughly yearly, and keep the margin.

◆ Security: the Cursor marketplace incident, and what else slipped through last week

Security Oct 29, in THN's Nov 2 recap

A modified Claude Code extension on Cursor's marketplace tried to add an SSH key, enable sshd and open a reverse tunnel; up to 5,000 users installed it

This one predates the window but was missing from earlier editions and is directly relevant. On Oct 29, a tampered copy of the Anthropic.claude-code extension (version 2.1.313; CSO also names 2.1.312) was published on Cursor's VS Code marketplace and stayed up for about 11 hours. It attempted to append an attacker-controlled key to authorized_keys, enable the local SSH service, open a reverse tunnel, and send host details home. Enabling sshd needs admin rights the extension usually does not have, which limited real access, but the key and persistence still landed where it ran. Cursor put the impact at no more than 5,000 users, removed the versions, took the marketplace down, audited access, rotated keys and rebuilt it; Anthropic said the attacker used access to Cursor's marketplace, and the early blame on Open VSX was wrong. In the same recap: the Gheist campaign planted a secret-dumping GitHub Actions workflow named "sync-check" in every repo reachable with stolen tokens (13,500+ branches across 850+ accounts, per Wiz), and 319 Chrome extensions with 13.4 million installs were hijacking searches.

So what for TSD: If Claude Code was ever installed through Cursor's marketplace on your Mac, check ~/.ssh/authorized_keys for a key with the comment svc-telemetry, verify by fingerprint rather than comment, remove any tunnel process, and rotate the GitHub and Vercel tokens that machine holds. Then look at GitHub Actions in every client repo for a workflow you did not write. Ten minutes, once.
