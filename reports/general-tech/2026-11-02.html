◆ Your stack

Security Release Tue, Nov 3

Next.js has a scheduled security release tomorrow covering seven medium vulnerabilities

In an Oct 27 post, the Next.js team (Jamiboy Mohammad and Sebastian Silbermann) said a scheduled release on Tuesday, Nov 3 "will address seven medium vulnerabilities in Next.js." Full advisories, including impact, affected versions and upgrade instructions, will be published with the release. This is the monthly program Next.js started in July; critical bugs still get emergency patches, like the two Oct 14 RCEs.

Nothing else changed on the stack over the weekend. The GitHub advisory list still ends with the Oct 14 critical pair, Next.js 16.5 is in canary (canary.36 on Oct 31), React's last releases are still the Oct 14 security patches, and the Supabase CLI 3.0 beta reached beta.17 on Oct 30.

So what for TSD: Block an hour Tuesday afternoon. When the advisories post, read the affected-versions table first, then bump next on every client repo to the patched release for its line (16.x or 15.x), run the build, and let Vercel ship a preview before you promote. Do the client apps that use middleware/proxy, use cache and image optimization first, since those areas have produced most of this year's advisories. Medium severity buys you a day or two, so finish the pass by Thursday.

Dev tools Nov 1

Linux 7.4-rc1: a merge window about a third bigger than usual, much of it AI-triggered

Linus Torvalds released the first 7.4 release candidate with almost 20,000 commits, against the roughly 15,000 a cycle usually brings. He credits much of the increase to AI: fixes for issues AI tools found, and more patches that AI suggests outright, though machine-written patches are often rewritten before they're accepted. He called it a "much healthier model" because the flood arrived during the merge window, where big changes belong. About a third of the changes are AMD GPU register definitions.

So what for TSD: The biggest open-source project now treats AI-found bugs and AI-drafted patches as normal, with human review as the gate. That's the workflow to describe to clients who ask whether AI-written code is safe: AI drafts, a person reviews and merges, tests decide.
The Register (Nov 1)

Week ahead Nov 2 – Nov 5

Apple and Palantir report tonight, then AMD, Arm, Qualcomm and the SMB software names

Confirmed after-close reports: Apple and Palantir on Monday; AMD, Arista, Astera Labs, Block and Toast on Tuesday (Shopify reports before the open); Qualcomm, Arm, AppLovin, HubSpot and Figma on Wednesday (Datadog before the open); Cloudflare, Atlassian and Dropbox on Thursday. Previews focus on AMD's data-center demand and Helios rack shipments, and on whether Palantir can steady AI-software sentiment after last week's selloff. Premarket Monday, AMD was up 2.2%, Nvidia 1.5% and Micron 2.7% as futures rose.

So what for TSD: Shopify, Toast, HubSpot and Block are the tools your SMB clients pay for. Listen for what they say about small-business spending and AI add-on pricing; if they're raising prices for AI features, your flat-fee AI products look cheaper by comparison. Cloudflare's call is worth a skim for edge and bot-traffic trends.

Chips Nov 1 – Nov 2

South Korea's chip exports rose 259% to a record, while Kioxia says NAND price gains are slowing

South Korea's October exports hit a record $115.3B, up 93.7% from a year earlier. Semiconductors made up $56.4B of that, up 259%, on AI memory demand. The Kospi rose 2.4% Monday. In Japan, Kioxia shares fell about 14% after the flash-memory maker said average selling prices grew in the mid-20% range last quarter, against 70% the quarter before, and guided operating profit of 2 trillion yen versus the 2.35 trillion analysts expected.

So what for TSD: Memory is the cost line behind cloud GPUs and laptops alike. Slowing NAND price growth is an early hint that storage and hardware prices may ease next year. Hold off on any hardware upgrade you don't need right now, and don't promise clients lower AI hosting costs on the strength of one quarter.
Korea Times (Nov 1) · CNBC live (Kioxia, Nov 2)
Funding Nov 2

Fractile raises about $700M at $6.5B, and Salience Labs raises $125M for optical data-center chips

UK chip startup Fractile raised about $700M (Companies House filings show roughly $774M) at a $6.5B valuation, with the UK's £500M Sovereign AI Fund joining; Dealroom reports a $250M supply agreement with Anthropic. Separately, Salience Labs raised a $125M Series B led by Bullhound Capital, with AMD Ventures and Intel Capital participating, to scale its photonic switching for AI data centers.

So what for TSD: Nothing to act on. More inference-chip suppliers competing for Anthropic's business is one more reason per-token prices keep falling, which widens margins on retainers priced per month.
