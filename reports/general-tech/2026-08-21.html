◆ Your stack — patch this one

Critical Aug 20, 2026

Next.js pre-announced a critical security release for August 26

Under the security release process Vercel announced in July, the Next.js team published advance notice on Thursday that the August 26 release will fix one critical severity vulnerability. Patched versions will be 16.3.3 and 15.5.24, published together with the full advisory covering impact, affected versions and upgrade instructions. Nothing about the bug itself has been disclosed, which is the point — the notice exists so teams can clear a window before the details go public.

Assume that once the advisory drops, working exploit attempts follow within days. That has been the pattern on every recent Next.js middleware and cache advisory.

So what for TSD: Block out Wednesday, August 26. Today, run npx next --version across every client repo and write down which are on the 16.x line and which are still on 15.x, because you will need 16.3.3 or 15.5.24 respectively. Anything pinned below 15.5 needs a minor upgrade first, and that is the work you do this weekend rather than Wednesday under pressure. Bisque and every Vercel-hosted client site is in scope.

Feature Aug 19–21, 2026

Vercel shipped tracing, custom metrics, Bun 1.4 and an Agent in Slack

The changelog ran hot this week. Friday brought always-on tracing for production and preview traffic, so you can sample traces and debug an incident without first reproducing it locally. Thursday added custom metrics in Vercel Observability emitted straight from Functions, Bun 1.4 in Functions with better Node compatibility, Vercel Agent in Slack code channels for coordinated agent sessions, plus CLI support for the Container Registry and for listing, inspecting and resolving Toolbar comments from the terminal. Wednesday put Algolia in the Marketplace and moved SOC 2 and other compliance attestations into Team settings.

So what for TSD: Two of these are directly billable. Always-on tracing means you can answer "the site was slow yesterday afternoon" with data instead of a guess, which is exactly the kind of thing a Managed AI retainer is supposed to cover. And compliance docs in Team settings means when a client's insurer or franchisor asks for a SOC 2 attestation on the hosting, you pull it yourself in a minute instead of opening a support ticket.

◆ Autonomy hit fleet scale in one city

New Aug 20, 2026

Nevada approved up to 8,000 robotaxis in Clark County

The Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved three commercial permits on Thursday: Tesla up to 5,000 vehicles, Waymo up to 1,000, and Uber up to 1,000 through its Motional and Zoox partnerships. The window is twelve months.

The ceilings are not forecasts. Tesla's Cybercab chief engineer said 5,000 "has always been a ceiling for us" and that the company would be "extremely happy" with 2,500 in a year, citing non-technological constraints. Even at half the authorized number, Las Vegas becomes the largest head-to-head robotaxi market in the country. The Livery Operators Association opposed the permits at the hearing.

So what for TSD: Watch the second-order businesses, because that is where the SMB work is. Fleet charging, cleaning, depot maintenance and remote-ops staffing all scale with vehicle count and all need websites, booking and phone handling. Charlotte is not Las Vegas, but the vendor categories that spin up around a robotaxi depot are the same trades you already sell to.

◆ Security and the cost of showing a competitor your product

Security Aug 20, 2026

A fake crypto conference was used to phish security researchers

Huntress published the campaign on Thursday after one of its own researchers was targeted and played along to collect detail. The lure was an invitation over X to a cryptocurrency conference supposedly run by a well-known crypto news site, timed around Black Hat and DEF CON earlier this month. Victims were sent a Google Doc with a fake encryption sidebar built in Google App Script, prompting them to enter a "decryption key."

The payloads were platform-specific: a macOS infostealer, a legitimate remote desktop tool repurposed as Windows malware, and a counterfeit Ledger wallet installer. The operator ran the account @HartmansDoeke and has not been identified.

So what for TSD: The technique is a shared Google Doc that asks you to type a secret into a sidebar. That works on anyone, and your clients are softer targets than DEF CON attendees. Add one line to your onboarding docs: no legitimate document ever asks you to enter a password or key to view it. It costs nothing and it is the single highest-yield security thing you can tell an SMB.

Business Aug 20, 2026

Runlayer and Rippling dropped their lawsuits, and Rippling shipped the product

Runlayer, an AI security startup founded by Andrew Berman that launched in November 2025 and has raised $42 million from Khosla and Felicis, sued Rippling over its MCP gateway. Rippling had tested Runlayer's gateway for more than a year with close engineering collaboration, then built its own and planned to sell it. Runlayer's suit alleged Rippling violated the contractual agreements covering those tests. Rippling countersued on patent grounds, which observers read as a cost-inflation move.

Both sides dropped everything on Thursday. No settlement, no payment, no fees exchanged. Rippling released its MCP gateway the same day.

So what for TSD: The relevant lesson is about long unpaid pilots. A prospect who wants months of "close engineering collaboration" before paying is buying your roadmap, and a contract clause did not stop it here. Keep pilots short and paid, and do not walk a prospect through your architecture before money moves.
