◆ Patch watch — your stack

Security Jul 20, 2026 (scheduled)

Next.js is moving to scheduled security releases — the first drops July 20 and fixes multiple issues

Vercel is formalizing Next.js security updates with advance notice, and the first scheduled release is July 20, 2026, which it says will address multiple security issues. It follows a heavy run of recent advisories: CVE-2026-23870 (a DoS in React Server Components / App Router Server Functions affecting versions 13.x–16.x), CVE-2026-44578 (SSRF via crafted WebSocket upgrade requests on self-hosted Node deployments), and CVE-2026-44573 (an authorization bypass on the Pages Router when i18n is configured alongside middleware auth).

Separately, Supabase changed a default: new projects created after July 9 now ship with log_connections=off, reducing log noise. Minor, but worth knowing if your logging assumptions rely on the old default.

So what for TSD: This is a patch instruction. Put July 20 on the calendar and plan to bump every client site on Next.js the day the release notes land — you run Next 16 with the proxy.ts export-proxy pattern, so App Router Server Functions and any self-hosted Node deployment are exactly the surface these CVEs target. For Vercel-hosted sites, redeploy after upgrading; for anything self-hosted, the SSRF fix (CVE-2026-44578) is the priority. Don't wait for a client to ask.

◆ Security desk

Critical Jul 2026

Zoom patches a critical (9.8) account-takeover flaw in Zoom Workplace for Windows

Zoom released security updates for CVE-2026-53412, a CVSS 9.8 vulnerability in Zoom Workplace for Windows that could enable account takeover with network access. Separately, CISA warned that Russia-linked state actors are increasingly compromising home and small-office routers to build residential proxy networks for reconnaissance and potential attacks on critical infrastructure.

So what for TSD: Two quick client wins. Any SMB client running Zoom Workplace on Windows should update immediately — a one-line heads-up email is the kind of low-effort touch that keeps a retainer relationship warm. And the CISA router advisory is a nudge to check that clients' office routers are on current firmware with default creds changed; it's exactly the "we watch your whole surface" positioning that justifies a managed-services fee.

Business Jul 16, 2026

TSMC posts a 77% profit jump and adds $100B to its Arizona expansion

TSMC reported Q2 net profit up roughly 77% year over year to about $22B, with revenue up 34% to $40.2B on continued demand for advanced AI-data-center chips and packaging. It plans another $100B for its Arizona operations — bringing total planned US investment to $265B — with the expansion potentially adding up to four more fabs, including 2-nanometer manufacturing.

So what for TSD: Indirect, but it sets the cost floor you build on. TSMC's capacity and pricing shape what AI inference costs 12–24 months out; a bigger US buildout is a bet that compute demand keeps climbing, which is the tailwind under every AI product you sell. Nothing to act on today — just the macro reason the AI-services market you're in isn't cooling.
