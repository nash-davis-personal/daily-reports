◆ Secure your dev pipeline first

Security Jul 2026

GitHub is fielding a run of serious flaws — including one in its own AI agent

Three items landed close together. Wiz disclosed a critical remote-code-execution bug in GitHub's internal Git infrastructure (CVE-2026-3854). Separately, Noma Labs found "GitLost," a prompt-injection flaw in GitHub's new Agentic Workflows that abuses how the agent blends system instructions with untrusted user data in one context window — attackers can trick it into leaking private repos. And the "Cordyceps" CI/CD weakness left 300+ exploitable repositories across npm, PyPI, crates.io, and Go, touching projects from Microsoft, Google, Apache, and Cloudflare.

So what for TSD: Direct action. Nash lives in GitHub across every client repo and runs agentic automation. This week: audit GitHub Actions workflows for untrusted-input paths, pin third-party actions to commit SHAs (not tags), rotate any broad-scope tokens, and be conservative about what any AI agent may read in a private repo. "Prompt injection leaks private code" is now a live threat, not a thought experiment.
GBHackers — GitLost agentic-workflow flaw
Stack watch Jul 1, 2026

No new Next.js security release in July — but verify you're patched from May

Worth clearing up: July's Next.js releases (16.2.10 and 15.5.20, both July 1) were maintenance-only, republishing a WASM package with no security content. The real coordinated security release was in May, patching 13 advisories (DoS, middleware/proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning, XSS) with fixed versions at Next.js 16.2.6 / 15.5.18 and the matching React RSC packages. Separately, the Next.js 16.3 preview (persistent Turbopack cache, first-party agent tooling) is still preview, not stable.

So what for TSD: Run npm ls next across client projects; anything below 16.2.6 (or 15.5.18 on the 15.x line) still carries May's advisories. A five-minute check that prevents a real DoS/cache-poisoning exposure on a client's live site. Don't push 16.3 into production yet.

◆ The AI-infrastructure money machine

Business Jul 7, 2026

Amazon launches a $25B+ bond sale to fund AI infrastructure

Amazon returned to the bond market for at least $25B across up to eight tranches (3–40 year maturities) to fund its AI buildout, and told underwriters it won't issue more debt this year. Demand peaked near $62B before settling around $41B. The context: a $200B 2026 capex budget, up sharply from $131B in 2025, with data centers, chips, and hardware taking most of it. Inference cost is becoming the AI industry's next big line item.

So what for TSD: The hyperscalers are borrowing to drive per-token costs down; that deflation flows to Nash as a buyer of compute. What he rents to run Concierge, Front Desk, and his agents should keep getting cheaper — margin quietly improves without changing his prices. It also reinforces that the durable money is in applying AI to a business, not owning the infrastructure.
CNBC — Amazon $25B bond sale for AI
Chips Jul 2026

Chip-startup funding stays hot; India commissions its third fab

Investors have put ~$10.7B into semiconductor startups (seed through pre-IPO) in 2026 so far. Cerebras Systems went public last month after a $1B February pre-IPO round, and MatX — building chips tuned for AI labs — raised a $500M Series B led by Jane Street. On the geography side, India commissioned its third semiconductor plant, adding real OSAT/packaging capacity and another node in the global supply chain.

So what for TSD: Indirect but real — more chip supply and competition is the upstream reason inference keeps getting cheaper. Nothing to act on today, but it's the backdrop that makes AI-heavy products viable to sell to price-sensitive SMBs.
