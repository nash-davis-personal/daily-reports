◆ Security is now an operations line item

Breaking Aug 25–30, 2026

Boston Scientific's cyberattack is still stopping orders from shipping

Boston Scientific became aware of an incident affecting its IT systems on August 25 and disclosed it on August 26. The company said the incident has caused, and is expected to keep causing, problems accessing the information systems and business applications that support its operations, including its ability to process and ship customer orders. The disruption is global; employees at its manufacturing site in Cork, Ireland were sent home because they could not work.

The scale is what makes this instructive. Boston Scientific reported $20.07 billion in 2025 revenue and roughly $21 billion over the trailing twelve months, and its portfolio is cardiac and neurological implants. Five days into the incident, order processing is the thing that is still broken. A company that size has a security team, a SOC and an incident-response retainer, and it is still down.

So what for TSD: Use this as the concrete story in every conversation about backups and access hygiene with a client who runs on one admin account and a shared password. The pitch line is the specific one: a $20 billion company with a real security team could not take orders for a week. Then price a $600–$900 one-time hardening pass — enforce MFA on the CMS, Supabase, Vercel and domain registrar, rotate service keys, document who has access, verify a restore actually works. Grant and Bishop can sell that with a single sentence and no technical vocabulary.

Analysis Aug 26–28, 2026

Half the Carhartt "breach" was synthetic, and the real data came from Databricks

ShinyHunters claimed the Carhartt attack on August 13, said it had taken more than 50GB of customer, employee and corporate data, and published the archive after a $3.3 million ransom demand went unpaid. Troy Hunt of Have I Been Pwned worked through the dump and found it had been injected with millions of lines of fabricated records. Stripping the synthetic entries dropped the count from 24.8 million to 13.6 million; removing duplicate Microsoft 365 addresses and accounts flagged for deactivation brought the final authentic figure to 12,933,413. Exposed fields include email addresses, names, phone numbers and physical addresses. Over 15,000 @carhartt.com employee addresses were in there too.

Hunt linked the compromise to Carhartt's Databricks analytics platform. That is the detail worth keeping: the data did not leave through the storefront, it left through the copy of the storefront's data that somebody made for reporting.

So what for TSD: Two things. First, when a client panics about a breach headline, the claimed number is frequently inflated — check Have I Been Pwned before you let them send a customer email. Second, audit where client data gets copied to. Every analytics export, every Google Sheet a marketing contractor made, every Supabase table a no-code tool reads from is a second front door with worse locks than the first one. Add "list every place this data is duplicated" to your onboarding checklist this week.

◆ Chips, courts and corporate control

New Aug 29, 2026

CXMT sued the Pentagon to get off the Section 1260H list

ChangXin Memory Technologies, China's largest maker of DRAM, filed suit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia seeking removal from the Defense Department's list of Chinese companies with military ties. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is among the named defendants. CXMT was added to the list in January 2025 and kept on it after a review in June, and argues the designation was arbitrary, unsupported by sufficient evidence, and a violation of its due-process rights. The company says its chips are designed for civilian and commercial use.

It joins a growing queue. Alibaba filed its own challenge to a similar designation shortly before. Litigating the 1260H list has become a standard corporate response rather than an exceptional one, and each case adds to the record of how much evidence the designation actually requires.

So what for TSD: Indirect and worth tracking rather than acting on. CXMT is a meaningful share of world DRAM supply; anything that changes its access to US customers moves memory prices, and memory prices eventually move the hosting and hardware bills underneath everything you deploy. File it next to the semiconductor tariff proposal on finished electronics.

Breaking Aug 28–29, 2026

OpenAI put a November 12 shutoff date on Cursor after the SpaceX takeover

SpaceX completed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, Cursor's parent, earlier in August. On Friday OpenAI said it will stop supplying models to Cursor, proposing November 12 as the date, invoking a change-of-control cancellation window in the contract. OpenAI's stated reason is that it cannot be confident SpaceX will operate within its terms of service, pointing to X's conduct after Musk's acquisition. Musk said on X that he "couldn't care less." Cursor co-founder Michael Truell said the company is engaged with OpenAI. Anthropic co-founder Tom Brown said Anthropic will add compute to support Claude models inside Cursor.

So what for TSD: A live example for the client conversation about tool risk. When a business builds on a vendor's AI feature, the model behind it can be pulled by a third party's M&A, with about ten weeks of notice. Any client-facing AI you ship should route through your own key on a provider you contract with directly, and you should be able to say which model each product uses and what the fallback is. Write that down for TSD Front Desk and TSD Concierge if it is not already documented.

◆ Consumer hardware

New Aug 26, 2026

Apple's iPhone event is September 9, and the foldable is expected

Apple sent invitations on August 26 for an event at Apple Park on Wednesday, September 9 at 10 a.m. Pacific, tagged "Surprise and shine." Expected: the iPhone 18 Pro, the iPhone 18 Pro Max, and a foldable widely reported as the iPhone Ultra. There is no standard iPhone 18 this cycle — Apple is reported to be holding the more affordable models for spring 2027, which splits its phone launches across two dates for the first time.

So what for TSD: Put September 9 on the calendar as a client-site QA date. A foldable shipping in volume means a new viewport class and a new set of layout breaks on sites you built for phone and desktop. Pick your two most layout-fragile client sites now and check how they behave at unusual aspect ratios, so you are ahead of the first "it looks broken on my new phone" call instead of behind it.
