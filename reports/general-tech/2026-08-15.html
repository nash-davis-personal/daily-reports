◆ Security

Critical Aug 13, 2026

CISA publishes 14 ICS advisories, including a 10.0 in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway

ICSA-26-225-02 covers CVE-2026-19188, an OS command injection flaw scored CVSS 10.0 in the Haiwell IoT Cloud HMI Gateway. The affected sectors named are energy, critical manufacturing, and water and wastewater. Two of the fourteen advisories carried federal remediation deadlines that fell on August 14.

Maximum severity plus network-reachable plus water utilities is the combination that gets acted on. This does not touch a web stack, and it is the highest-consequence disclosure in the window.

So what for TSD: No patch action for you. It is a live sales fact for the trades side of your network: any Charlotte-area client running connected industrial or building-automation gear has a vendor question to ask this week. "Do you know what's on your network and who can reach it" opens more doors than a website pitch does.

A cyberattack on the French tax agency exposed data belonging to approximately 700,000 taxpayers. Scope and attribution were not detailed in the initial reporting.

◆ Your stack

Release Next.js 16.3.1

Next.js ships a maintenance release; the instant() test helper landed in 16.3

16.3.1 is maintenance: Turbopack fixes, next/image improvements, cache and HMR reliability work, and backported fixes for routing, SSR and dev workflows. The 16.3 minor before it added an instant() test helper for verifying instant navigation and finding slow routes.

On security, the last release that mattered is still the May 2026 coordinated one — 13 advisories across DoS, middleware and proxy bypass, SSRF, cache poisoning and XSS, all patched in 15.5.18 and 16.2.6. Nothing new has landed since.

So what for TSD: Take 16.3.1 on client projects; a patch release with HMR and cache fixes pays for itself in dev time. Then use instant() on the two or three routes clients actually complain about — a measured navigation number is a better renewal argument than "the site feels faster."

API change Aug 2026

Vercel Blob adds putImage; Supabase deprecates pinning extension versions

Vercel Blob's new putImage(pathname, bodyOrUrl, options) runs an image through Vercel Image Optimization and stores only the optimized output, accepting either the image content or a public URL that gets fetched server-side. The optimizeImage option on put and the putFromUrl function are both deprecated in its favor and still work.

On the Supabase side, specifying an explicit version when creating or updating a Postgres extension was deprecated as of 2026-08-05, and a fix landed for physical restore credential handling so current passwords get reapplied after a restore.

So what for TSD: Straight cost cut on the image-heavy client sites. Storing only the optimized output instead of the original shrinks Blob storage and kills a transform step you are currently paying for on every request. Grep your projects for putFromUrl and optimizeImage and migrate them while they still work.

◆ Silicon and the cost of hardware

Earnings Aug 14, 2026

SMIC ran at 93.7% utilization, cleared $3B in a quarter, and is raising prices

China's largest foundry shipped roughly 2.9 million 8-inch-equivalent wafers in Q2, passed $3 billion of quarterly revenue for the first time, and more than tripled quarterly profit to $479.2 million. It is raising prices on sought-after capacity and plans to start reporting AI chip revenue separately.

Applied Materials landed in the same window with fiscal Q3 revenue of $9.12 billion, up 25% year over year, adjusted earnings of $3.50 per share, and a stated plan to double semiconductor-system output by 2028. Memory makers, meanwhile, are prioritizing data center demand, which tightens supply and raises component costs in PCs and phones.

So what for TSD: Hardware quotes your clients receive are going up for reasons that have nothing to do with them. That is the argument for software and process work over equipment refreshes this year, and it is a genuine reason a booking-automation build beats buying another terminal.

Autonomy Aug 14, 2026

Uber and Pony.ai are putting 2,000-plus robotaxis on European roads

The partnership expands from Zagreb, Croatia into four additional European markets and the Middle East, with more than 2,000 vehicles planned across five European cities. Uber is positioning its ride-hailing network as the distribution layer for multiple autonomous-driving providers rather than betting on one, and is targeting roughly 120,000 driverless vehicles across at least 15 cities during 2026.

So what for TSD: The pattern is worth stealing at your scale. Uber owns the demand and stays neutral on the supplier underneath. That is exactly the position to hold with model vendors: own the client relationship and the integration, keep the model swappable.
