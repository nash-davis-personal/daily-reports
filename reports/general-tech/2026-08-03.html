Critical July 29 – Aug 1

Rails patched a critical Active Storage flaw that turns an upload into a file read

The Rails security team published CVE-2026-66066, rated CVSS 9.5. Active Storage handed untrusted uploads to libvips without disabling libvips' unfuzzed image loaders, so an unauthenticated attacker who uploads a crafted file and triggers variant generation can read arbitrary files from the server — including the process environment holding secret_key_base and service credentials. From there, escalation to remote code execution is on the table.

Fixed in 7.2.3.2, 8.0.5.1 and 8.1.3.1; libvips should be 8.13 or newer. Rails 6.x is affected only where Active Storage was configured outside its defaults. The Ethiack researchers who found it estimate 500,000+ sites in scope, and maintainers published full details plus forensic tooling because public proof-of-concept exploits appeared almost immediately.

So what for TSD: Not your stack, and that is the point worth selling. Any prospect running a Rails site with user uploads — salons, shops, anyone with a legacy build from an agency that has moved on — is exposed right now and probably does not know. That is a real, checkable, dated reason for Grant or Bishop to call a lapsed lead today, and "your image uploads can leak your server keys" opens a modernization conversation better than a redesign pitch does.

◆ The AI buildout is now visible in what hardware costs

Supply August 2

The memory shortage reached the MacBook Air, at $200 more and several weeks late

Apple's cheapest laptop is short across the retail network. US delivery estimates run two to six weeks, and higher-RAM configurations slip furthest, with some pushed to September. The 13-inch M5 launched at $1,099 in March and went to $1,299 in June, so buyers are paying $200 more and still waiting.

The cause is upstream. Memory makers have been reallocating capacity toward the high-bandwidth RAM that AI server buildouts consume, and contract DRAM prices rose 90–95% quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026 and another 58–63% in Q2. Apple has trimmed RAM configuration options and started sourcing Chinese memory for its China market. TrendForce expects the pressure to persist into 2027–2028.

So what for TSD: If a machine upgrade is anywhere on the plan for this school year, the price only goes one direction for the next several quarters — decide now or accept 2027 pricing. The broader read matters more: the AI datacenter buildout has started taxing ordinary consumer goods, which is a concrete, non-hype answer for any client who asks whether the AI boom is real.

TSMC's quarter shows where all that memory and silicon is going

TSMC's second-quarter revenue rose 33% to more than $40 billion with earnings per share up 77% to $4.31, and the company guided Q3 to $44.6–45.8 billion. First-half revenue was up 35%. The stock is up more than 50% year to date, well ahead of the broader market.

On the export side, Nvidia is permitted to ship H200 chips to China under a revenue-sharing arrangement that carries a 25% export levy.

So what for TSD: Background only. It is the number to keep in your head when a client asks whether AI spending is a bubble about to pop: the company that physically manufactures the chips is guiding revenue up again next quarter. That is not proof the boom lasts, and it is the most concrete evidence available that demand has not turned yet.
