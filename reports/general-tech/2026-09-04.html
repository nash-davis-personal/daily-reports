◆ Patch and platform items on your stack

Deprecation Removal Sept 23

Supabase removes the logs.all Management API endpoint on September 23

Supabase is migrating Management API log querying to a new ClickHouse-backed logs endpoint and removing logs.all on September 23, 2026. Anything calling that endpoint stops working on that date. No Next.js, React or Tailwind security advisory landed in this window; the most recent Next.js coordinated security release remains the August 2026 one, where Vercel-hosted apps needed no customer action and the second issue only affected Windows filesystems.

So what for TSD: Grep every project for logs.all today — it takes a minute and the failure mode is a monitoring script that silently stops reporting on a client project. If nothing hits, you are clear. Nothing to patch on the Next.js side this week.

Feature Sept 3

Vercel added cheaper build machines and put Cursor's cloud agents in Sandbox

Two changelog entries on September 3. Pro and Enterprise teams can now select Basic build machines at 2 vCPU and 8 GB, a lower-cost tier than the default. Separately, Cursor Cloud Agents can run inside Vercel Sandbox on scale-to-zero workers, which puts agent execution on infrastructure you control rather than a vendor's.

On September 2 the free-domain-with-Pro offer expanded to include .app and .dev, bringing the eligible list to eight top-level domains, one free domain per Pro team per year.

So what for TSD: Switch the small client sites to Basic build machines. Marketing sites and Vite builds do not need the default machine, and build minutes are a line item you are paying on every push across a portfolio. The free .app or .dev is worth claiming for whichever product you are demoing next.

◆ The AI buildout keeps buying things

Deal Sept 3

Nvidia confirmed a $12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face

Nvidia will pay roughly $11.9 billion to Hugging Face investors and up to $1 billion in employee retention, its largest acquisition to date. The platform hosts about three million models, half a million datasets and a million applications, used by more than 18 million developers. Jensen Huang said Hugging Face stays open to the whole ecosystem and that Nvidia chips will not be required to build or deploy through it. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, pending regulatory approval.

The strategic read is straightforward. Nvidia has spent years supplying the open-model ecosystem with hardware and now owns the distribution layer where models are discovered, fine-tuned, evaluated and pulled. The hardware-neutrality promise is the part regulators and competitors will be watching, and it is worth noting Nvidia committed $12.9 billion of stock the same week its shares rose on the news while Broadcom's fell on better cash numbers.

So what for TSD: No immediate action, but do not build a product whose only moat is "we host our fine-tune on Hugging Face." Anything you pull from HF in a client deployment — an embedding model, a small classifier — should be vendored into your own storage so a platform change during a two-year regulatory review cannot break a client's site.

Earnings Sept 3

Broadcom's AI chip revenue hit $16.7 billion and the stock fell anyway

Broadcom reported $16.7 billion of AI semiconductor revenue for its fiscal third quarter, up 221% year over year and 54% sequentially, on total revenue of $29.6 billion, up 86%. It guided fiscal Q4 AI revenue to $21.7 billion, which would be up 236% year over year. Shares closed down about 2.7%.

That split is the story. Nvidia rose on the day it committed $12.9 billion of stock to an acquisition; Broadcom fell on a better cash statement. The complex is being narrated as one trade and priced as two.

So what for TSD: Read it as a demand signal, not a stock tip. Custom-accelerator revenue tripling means inference capacity keeps expanding, which is the mechanism behind the per-token price cuts you have been quoting clients on. Your cost basis for AI features gets cheaper as long as that curve holds.

◆ Someone built an agent for your job

New Sept 2

Webflow launched Source, an agentic workspace that hands agents the codebase

At Webflow Conf on September 2, Webflow unveiled Source, a limited research preview of a platform where marketing teams and AI agents build and optimize sites together. The design decision that matters: Source connects to the codebase and the martech stack, giving agents direct access to a site's code rather than forcing them through a visual abstraction layer, while marketers still build visually on top of that same code. Webflow also announced Campaigns, which takes performance marketers from brief to live landing pages with personalized variants and matched ads, and Assets, an agentic creative tool built on technology from Vidoso, which Webflow acquired earlier this year.

So what for TSD: This is aimed squarely at the "custom marketing site" half of your business, and pretending otherwise is a mistake. The honest response is to stop selling pages and sell the things Source cannot: an owned Next.js codebase with no platform rent, integrations into the client's actual booking and phone systems, and a human who answers when it breaks. Worth an hour on the waitlist to see the product firsthand before a prospect brings it up in a sales call.
