◆ Chips are the whole market right now

Breaking Jul 10, 2026

SK Hynix raises $26.5B in the largest-ever US listing by a foreign company

SK Hynix made its Nasdaq debut Friday, raising $26.5 billion — the biggest US share offering ever by a foreign company — and climbing roughly 13% on the day. Chairman comments to CNBC framed memory demand as "enormous," and the debut helped lift the broader indices into a weekly gain. It's the clearest sign yet that high-bandwidth memory, the stuff that feeds AI accelerators, is now a first-order investment story in its own right, not a footnote to Nvidia.

So what for TSD: No direct product impact, but it's the market telling you AI demand is still accelerating at the hardware layer — which underwrites the AI-services demand you sell on top. When a client asks "is this AI thing a fad," you have a concrete, current answer: the biggest foreign listing in US history this week was a memory-chip maker riding AI demand.

Deal Jul 6, 2026

Broadcom extends its Apple chip partnership through 2031

Broadcom said on July 6 it will develop and supply custom ASIC silicon found in "multiple generations" of Apple products through 2031, extending a relationship that already covers RF, Wi-Fi/Bluetooth and networking chips. Apple accounts for about 20% of Broadcom's annual revenue. The extension signals Apple is doubling down on tight, in-house-adjacent hardware control as its AI strategy leans harder on on-device inference.

So what for TSD: Reinforces the theme from last week's Apple–OpenAI lawsuit: Apple wants to own its AI hardware stack rather than rent capability from a lab. If you ever plan a client feature around "the iPhone's built-in AI," assume Apple is steering that toward its own silicon and models — keep your builds provider-neutral and don't bet a roadmap on any one platform's assistant.

◆ Your stack: one patch worth closing

Fix Disclosed Apr 8, 2026

The Next.js App Router DoS CVE is still worth a patch pass

There was no fresh Next.js, Vercel, React, Tailwind or Supabase advisory this week — but CVE-2026-23869 is still live for anyone who never patched. A crafted HTTP request to any App Router Server Function endpoint can trigger excessive CPU and take a site down (CVSS 7.5). It affects Next.js 13.x–16.x on the App Router. Fix is Next 15.5.15+ or 16.2.3+. Vercel deployed WAF rules that cover hosted projects automatically, but Vercel's own advice is not to rely on the WAF alone.

So what for TSD: A slow news week is the time to do hygiene. Run npm ls next across every client repo; any App Router site below 16.2.3 (or 15.5.15 on v15) should be bumped this week. Your Vercel-hosted clients have WAF cover, but if any client self-hosts on a Node server, patching is not optional. Ten minutes now beats a "the site's down" text later.
