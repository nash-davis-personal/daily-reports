◆ Patch this first

Security August 4 ◆ deadline August 7

CISA flagged Langflow, Tomcat and N-central as actively exploited

The Langflow entry is the ugly one. CVE-2026-9198 carries a CVSS of 9.8 and works by chaining two separate mistakes in IBM's open-source Langflow: an unauthenticated auto-login endpoint that issues a superuser bearer token to any caller who can reach it, and a code-validation endpoint that executes arbitrary Python. Grab the token from the first, post code to the second, and you have remote code execution with no credentials. IBM shipped the fix in Langflow OSS 1.10.1 on July 17; a public proof of concept appeared about a week after disclosure, and CISA added the CVE to the KEV catalog on August 4 alongside flaws in Apache Tomcat and N-able N-central. Federal civilian agencies have until August 7 under BOD 26-04.

The pattern behind it is the thing to internalise. Langflow is a visual builder for LLM pipelines — the kind of tool people stand up quickly on a VPS to prototype an agent, then forget. A default-permissive auth endpoint plus an endpoint whose whole job is running code is a combination that shows up across this entire generation of AI tooling, because these frameworks were designed for a laptop and get deployed on the open internet.

So what for TSD: Two concrete jobs. First, go inventory anything you or a client ever spun up to try an agent idea — Langflow, Flowise, n8n, any self-hosted LLM builder sitting on a droplet — and either patch it to 1.10.1 or take it offline today. A forgotten prototype on a client's VPS is now an actively exploited entry point with your name attached to it. Second, on the good-news side, nothing new dropped on Next.js, React, Vercel or Supabase this week, so if your projects are already on 15.5.18 or 16.2.6 you have no emergency there.

◆ The memory shortage stopped being a datacenter story

Hardware Early August

A $1,999 graphics card costs $4,300, and consoles are following it up

The mechanism is simple: memory makers pointed capacity at HBM and server parts to serve AI buildouts, GDDR allocation shrank, and graphics-memory prices climbed through the second quarter. VRAM now accounts for more than 80% of a high-end card's build cost. RTX 5090 street pricing is running $4,300 to $5,000 against a $1,999 MSRP, Korean retail has hit roughly $5,100, and the RTX 50 Super refresh has been delayed indefinitely because the math no longer works. The 5090 and 5080 are the worst affected, sitting 75% or more above list.

What is new in the last few weeks is the spread beyond enthusiast hardware. Nvidia and AMD have both pushed phased increases, Nintendo raised the Switch 2, Sony raised the PS5 for a second time, and Valve raised the Steam Deck by nearly 50%. Memory is an input to almost everything with a chip in it, so this eventually shows up in laptops, phones and any client hardware refresh.

So what for TSD: Forget local inference. Any plan that involved buying a GPU to self-host a model for a client is dead on cost for at least the next few quarters, and renting API tokens is now the cheap option by a wide margin — which is a point worth making out loud in a proposal when a client asks why you are not "keeping it in-house." Second, if your own dev machine is due for replacement, the price curve is going up rather than down, so buy now or plan to wait a year. Third, this is a live objection-handler for Grant and Bishop: the reason an AI feature is a monthly subscription instead of a hardware purchase is that the hardware doubled in price and the subscription did not.

◆ The market started charging for capex

Earnings August 4–5

AMD and SpaceX both beat, and both got sold

AMD reported Q2 revenue of $11.5 billion and adjusted EPS of $1.66 — records on both lines, growth of 50% and 246%, a double beat against consensus — and the stock fell about 6% to $486.60 on Wednesday. Part of that was competitive: Elon Musk called Nvidia's Vera Rubin the best architecture available and committed SpaceX to Nvidia chips exclusively for its Starmind orbital compute program.

SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company beat on revenue and adjusted EBITDA, and the stock closed down 13.6% at $108.27 because capital expenditure on AI computing infrastructure hit $15.8 billion in the quarter, with total capex above $18 billion and no sign of slowing. The broader indices still set records on Middle East de-escalation hopes. The signal underneath is that investors have stopped rewarding AI spending on announcement and started asking when it converts.

So what for TSD: The same question is walking into your sales calls about six months behind. "When does this convert" is exactly what a Charlotte SMB owner means when they hesitate on an AI receptionist, and the answer that closes is arithmetic rather than capability: this many missed calls a month, this much average job value, therefore this payback period. Build that calculator into the pitch deck and let the prospect enter their own numbers.

Courts August 4–5

Apple asked a judge to freeze OpenAI's hardware work, and OpenAI moved to dismiss the same week

Apple filed for a preliminary injunction and expedited discovery against OpenAI and two former Apple employees — Chang Liu, a senior system electrical engineer, and Tang Yew Tan, formerly VP of product design for iPhone and Apple Watch — seeking to bar them from accessing, using or disclosing what Apple says are its trade secrets. The underlying suit, filed last month, claims that misappropriated material is what enabled OpenAI's move into consumer hardware.

OpenAI filed a motion to dismiss on August 5 and published a blog post calling the allegations baseless and the suit careless and oddly personal. The hearing on Apple's injunction and discovery motions is set for October 1.

So what for TSD: No direct exposure, but it is a clean illustration of why your contracts should be boring and explicit. You hire nobody and you hand over source code on day one, which means the one clause that actually protects you is a clear statement of what the client owns and what remains your reusable IP — the TSD Front Desk and Concierge cores are yours, the client-specific configuration and site are theirs. If that line is not written down in your current agreement, write it before the next signature.

◆ Space

Space August 5

A four-tonne Falcon 9 upper stage crashed into the Moon by accident

The stage had delivered Blue Ghost 1 and Hakuto-R Mission 2 in 2024 and was left adrift; solar activity and gravitational nudges eventually put it back on a lunar trajectory. It struck near Einstein crater at about 2:35 a.m. EDT on August 5 at 8,700 km/h, and is expected to have left a crater roughly 60 feet across and 12 feet deep. The European Southern Observatory detected sodium and lithium in a debris plume that persisted for five to ten minutes.

NASA noted there was never any risk to Earth, and that a meteoroid with equivalent energy strikes the Moon roughly every six days. The number that stuck was the running total: human-made debris on the lunar surface has now passed 209 tons, at a moment when several countries and companies are planning permanent installations there.

So what for TSD: Nothing operational. It is the best cold-open you will get this week, and if you ever want a client-facing newsletter with a personality, an item like this at the bottom is what makes people finish reading the ones above it.
