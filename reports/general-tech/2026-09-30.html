◆ Patch now: your stack

Security Sept 29–30

Next.js's nine security fixes will ship as 16.3.8 and 15.5.27, and 16.3.7 doesn't include them

Vercel updated its advance notice Sept. 29: 16.3.7 was published with a bug fix and does not contain the security fixes planned for Sept. 30. Those now ship as 16.3.8 (16.x line) and 15.5.27 (15.x line) and cover nine vulnerabilities: one critical, two high, five medium and one low. At about 6:40 a.m. ET, npm latest was still 16.3.7 and backport was 15.5.26, with no new advisories on GitHub yet, so CVE IDs and affected ranges are not public.

This follows the Sept. 22 out-of-band fix for CVE-2026-94545, a critical remote-code-execution bug in next/og ImageResponse on the Node runtime (CVSS 9.5 per The Hacker News), fixed in 16.3.6.

So what for TSD: Patch instruction: when 16.3.8 / 15.5.27 hit npm today, bump every client site on Next 15 or 16 and redeploy. Don't treat 16.3.7 as patched. Confirm every site is at least on 16.3.6 right now for the ImageResponse RCE.

Security Sept 28

JadePuffer: an AI-driven attacker tried to wipe an Azure tenant minutes after a secret leaked on GitHub

Microsoft (tracking it as Storm-3168) and Sysdig describe two compromised service principals from one tenant. One ran 300+ read operations over about 15.5 hours to map the environment. The other ran 150+ destructive or credential-harvesting operations in about 35 minutes, including 100+ storage-account deletion attempts in roughly seven minutes, and removed Azure Site Recovery locks to block restoration. Credentials for one principal had appeared in a public GitHub issue. Sysdig calls it the first ransomware operation run end to end by an LLM.

So what for TSD: The Supabase service_role key and Vercel tokens are the equivalent in TSD's world. Run a secret scan on every repo and issue tracker, rotate anything ever exposed, and turn on Supabase PITR or backups for any client with data worth ransoming.

◆ Space & security

Space Sept 28

Starship reached orbit on Flight 14 and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites

The booster landed offshore as planned, the ship reached orbit, deployed its 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and completed its deorbit burn and reentry. One Raptor Vacuum engine on Ship 41 shut down early during ascent, so controllers shortened the mission from nearly 10 hours to 3 hours 9 minutes, ending with a splashdown in the North Pacific.

So what for TSD: No direct stack impact. More Starlink capacity does widen rural broadband, which over time reaches the small-town clients whose sites and voice agents currently run on weak connections.

Breach Sept 29

The Pentagon confirmed a personnel-records breach covering about 3.05 million people

Attackers were inside a Defense Manpower Data Center system from October 2025 to July 2026 through a file-sharing vulnerability patched July 16. Exposed records, stored unencrypted, cover about 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased people and include names, SSNs, birth dates, contact details and service data. Notices went out Sept. 18 with a year of credit monitoring.

So what for TSD: It's a clean example for client conversations: data you don't collect can't leak. Use it to argue for trimming intake forms and encrypting sensitive fields in Supabase.

◆ Chips & big tech

Business Sept 28

Nvidia added a record $150B to its buyback, and AMD is buying World Labs for about $8.2B

Nvidia's $150 billion increase brings its authorization to $235 billion, which it calls the largest increase in history, and it expects to use the rest through fiscal 2028. It bought back $39 billion in the first half of this fiscal year. The same day, AMD agreed to buy Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, which builds models that generate and simulate 3D environments, in an all-stock deal expected to close by year-end. It is AMD's second-largest acquisition after Xilinx.

So what for TSD: AI-infrastructure cash flow is still enormous, which keeps inference prices falling. That's good for margins on TSD's AI retainers.
