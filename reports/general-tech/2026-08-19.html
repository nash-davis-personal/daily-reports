◆ Shipped on Nash's stack

Feature Aug 18, 2026

Vercel KMS signs JWTs inside Functions, so the private key never enters your codebase

Vercel shipped managed key signing for JSON Web Tokens in Functions. The signing key lives in Vercel KMS and the Function calls out to sign, which removes the usual pattern of stuffing a private key or shared secret into an environment variable and hoping nobody prints the env in a debug route.

Three other things landed the same day. Vercel for Platforms can now deploy from an end user's own GitHub repository using a temporary token that is encrypted and discarded after the deploy. Cline joined the AI SDK harness layer through @ai-sdk/harness-cline, so it runs behind the same HarnessAgent interface as the other agents. And Z.ai's GLM-5.3 went live on the AI Gateway with unified billing and request tracing. On Wednesday the Chat SDK picked up an Instagram Direct Messages adapter covering media, quick replies, reactions and story replies.

So what for TSD: Two concrete jobs. First, grep every client repo for a JWT secret in an env var and move those to KMS — it is a short change that turns into a real line on a security-hardening invoice. Second, the Instagram DM adapter is the cheapest new product surface you have: most Charlotte salons, detailers and trades take more inbound through Instagram DMs than through their contact form, and TSD Front Desk answering DMs after hours is the same pitch you already win with on phones.

Improve Aug 2026

Supabase tightened realtime filters, and pinning extension versions is now deprecated

Postgres Changes now supports AND filters, additional filter operators, and column selection on the subscription payload, which means a realtime subscription can stop shipping whole rows over the socket for a client that only needs two fields. Separately, as of August 5 specifying an explicit version when creating or updating a Postgres extension on Supabase is deprecated.

So what for TSD: If any dashboard you built subscribes to a wide table and filters client-side, move the filter and the column list into the subscription — it is a few lines and it cuts socket traffic on the exact pages clients complain are slow on mobile. Also scan migrations for pinned extension versions before that deprecation bites.

◆ The one you patch

Breaking CVE-2025-62593

CISA gave federal agencies three days to fix an actively exploited Ray RCE

CISA added CVE-2025-62593 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 17 after confirming exploitation in the wild, with a remediation deadline of August 20 for federal civilian agencies. The bug carries a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.4 and stems from insufficient protection on Ray's HTTP API endpoints, including job endpoints that accept requests capable of launching code. Anything before Ray 2.52.0 is affected.

Ray is the distributed compute framework underneath a lot of AI training and inference infrastructure at large companies, and it has a long history of being deployed on an open port because it was assumed to be sitting on a trusted internal network. That assumption is what the exploitation is monetizing.

So what for TSD: You almost certainly do not run Ray. The transferable lesson is the pattern — an internal-only service exposed to the internet because someone assumed the network boundary. Do a five-minute pass over every client deployment for admin endpoints, webhook receivers and internal APIs that answer unauthenticated requests. That audit is a sellable fixed-fee deliverable.

◆ Silicon, and where it is allowed to go

New Aug 19, 2026

Cerebras overclocked its wafer and put three of them in a rack

The CS-4 is built on the WSE-3 Turbo, a TSMC 5nm wafer-scale part with four trillion transistors, 900,000 AI cores and 44GB of SRAM on the wafer itself. Three of them go into a rack on the Nexus architecture, for a claimed 750 PFLOPs, 7.2 Tbps of I/O and 129.6 PB/s of memory bandwidth. Cerebras claims up to 30x faster inference than GPU systems and has demonstrated around 2,000 tokens per second on the K2 Think reasoning model. It is in early access with general availability expected later this quarter.

So what for TSD: Watch this as a price signal rather than a purchase. Wafer-scale inference competing on tokens per second is what eventually drags per-token pricing down across the board, which is the input cost on every AI product you sell on a monthly retainer. Margin on TSD Front Desk gets better without you doing anything.

New Aug 19, 2026

The first real H200 shipments reached ByteDance and Tencent, and Beijing told them to leave the chips in Hong Kong

The Financial Times reports ByteDance and Tencent each took delivery of roughly 10,000 Nvidia H200 processors in recent weeks, the first meaningful volume since Washington cleared purchases of up to 100,000 units per buyer. The twist is on the Chinese side: regulators have told the companies to keep the hardware in Hong Kong, which sits outside the mainland customs border, in order to protect demand for domestic chipmakers.

Neither company currently operates data centers in Hong Kong, and Hong Kong's data-center capacity and power supply are both constrained. Ten thousand accelerators each is a rounding error against what either company would deploy given a free hand.

So what for TSD: No direct action. The read is that Chinese labs are compute-constrained by their own government as much as by export controls, which is part of why models like GLM-5.3 optimize hard for tokens burned per task. Cheap, efficient open weights keep arriving, and Vercel's AI Gateway now bills GLM-5.3 like any other model — worth a price comparison on your lower-margin automation work.

◆ The robot listing that broke price discovery

New Aug 19, 2026

Unitree opened 629% above its IPO price on its Shanghai debut

Unitree Robotics priced at 150.80 yuan and opened at 1,100, a 629% pop that briefly put its market capitalization near 445 billion yuan, or about $66 billion. It settled to 883.9 yuan by midday and closed at 845, valuing the company around $50 billion. The Hangzhou company sold 40.45 million shares for roughly 6.1 billion yuan, about 10% of enlarged share capital. Retail investors subscribed more than 8,000 times the shares available to them.

It is the first general-purpose humanoid robotics company to list on a mainland Chinese exchange, and the debut landed on the opening day of the World Robot Conference in Beijing.

So what for TSD: Nothing to buy or build here. Note it as a sentiment marker: capital is paying six times fair value for physical-AI stories while the same week's semiconductor tape is selling AI infrastructure hard. That split is worth naming out loud when a client asks whether the AI thing is a bubble — the honest answer is that some of it plainly is, and none of it changes whether answering their phone at 9pm makes them money.
