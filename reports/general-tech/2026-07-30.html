◆ Patch status

Clear July 30

No new advisory on your stack — but two actively exploited flaws landed on the gear around it

Nothing new from Vercel, Next.js, React, Tailwind or Supabase since the July 20 security release. If client projects are on Next.js v16.2.11 (Active LTS) or v15.5.21 (Maintenance LTS), you are current; those fixes also land in v16.3.0 when it goes stable.

The exploited bugs are elsewhere. CISA added CVE-2026-20316 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on July 29: a hard-coded password in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center. Two days earlier it added CVE-2026-16812, an unauthenticated OS command injection in on-premises Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator scored at the maximum CVSS 10.0 — no credentials needed, and Arista has said the product is exposed by default, which means every unpatched instance is reachable as designed. Federal civilian agencies have until today to remediate it, and scanning for exposed orchestrator endpoints is already happening.

So what for TSD: Nothing to ship for your own deploys. If any client runs their own firewall management or SD-WAN — the kind of thing a mid-size shop's MSP installed and forgot — send a one-line email naming both CVEs and asking whether their vendor has patched. It costs you five minutes, it is exactly the sort of thing an owner remembers, and it is a clean reason to be in their inbox this week.

◆ Big tech pays for the buildout

Earnings July 29

Microsoft beat on everything and then told the market it will spend a quarter of a trillion dollars

Revenue came in at $90.01 billion, ahead of the $87.63 billion consensus and up about 18% year over year, with adjusted EPS of $4.74 against $3.65 a year ago. Azure growth accelerated to 43% from 40% the prior quarter, beating the ~40% analysts expected, and Azure revenue passed $100 billion for the full fiscal year for the first time, up 41%. Management guided Q1 Azure growth to roughly 45% and quarterly capex above $50 billion.

The headline number is the spending. FY27 capex guidance is $255–260 billion, against roughly $190 billion committed in 2026. Microsoft also extended the assumed useful life of data centers and office buildings from 15 to 25 years starting in fiscal 2027 and shifted new data-center leases from finance to operating treatment — accounting changes that reduce reported future depreciation and bring its calendar-2026 capital spending outlook to about $175 billion.

So what for TSD: Two things you can use. Azure at 43% and accelerating means enterprise AI budgets are still expanding, which is the tailwind under every AI proposal you write. And the depreciation change is worth understanding before a client's CFO asks you about it: the industry is now stretching the accounting life of AI hardware to make the spending work. That is a reason to keep your own client commitments short and cancelable rather than betting on multi-year infrastructure pricing holding.

Silicon July 29

Amazon's own chips are now a $20 billion business, and it is going direct-to-device from orbit

Amazon's custom-silicon line — Graviton CPUs, Trainium training accelerators and Nitro security chips — passed a $20 billion annual run rate, growing more than 100% year over year, with multi-year commitments from OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta and Uber. Separately, Amazon entered direct-to-device satellite connectivity, letting ordinary smartphones talk to its low-Earth-orbit constellation without a dedicated dish.

So what for TSD: Inference is going to keep getting cheaper as the hyperscalers substitute their own silicon for Nvidia's margin. Price AI retainers on the value delivered rather than a markup over token cost, because the token cost is on a downward slope you don't control.

◆ Policy reshapes hardware

Policy July 28–29

The FCC closed the US market to foreign-made humanoid and quadruped robots

The FCC added foreign-made two-legged humanoids, four-legged quadrupeds and connected power inverters to its Covered List, blocking equipment authorization for new devices. The stated reasoning is that connected robots carry cameras, microphones, sensors, navigation and remote-control software capable of mapping homes, factories, warehouses and critical infrastructure, and that offshore production leaves supply chains exposed. The order lands on a category China dominates — Unitree, Agibot and UBTech are the market leaders, and Unitree had just gone commercial in Europe on July 22 with a North American push expected. Firms that can demonstrate secure, auditable supply chains and locked-down firmware may have a route back in.

So what for TSD: A direct read-through to client conversations: connected hardware is now regulated on data-collection risk, not just radio compliance. Anything you install for a client that has a camera, a microphone or a cloud callback is going to face that question eventually. Knowing where a device's firmware and data go is becoming part of the spec.

Funding July 29

Commerce signed seven CHIPS letters of intent worth $874 million, aimed at interconnect

The Commerce Department announced seven letters of intent totaling $874 million in federal incentives under the CHIPS and Science Act, targeting technologies meant to raise the performance of the fastest computers and strengthen the domestic compute supply chain, with named applications in materials, industrial optimization, robotics, defense, drug discovery and finance. GlobalFoundries took $300 million of it to work on faster AI chip interconnects — the bandwidth between chips, which is where a growing share of the bottleneck now sits.

So what for TSD: Background, not action. The constraint on AI capacity has moved from raw compute to memory, power and the links between chips. It is worth knowing when a client asks why AI pricing isn't falling faster than it is.
