Security Jul 20, 2026

Next.js July 2026 security release: eight CVEs, four high

Next.js shipped fixes in v16.2.11 (Active LTS) and v15.5.21 (Maintenance LTS). The four high-severity issues: CVE-2026-64641 — DoS via crafted requests to App Router Server Actions (excessive CPU blocks the process); CVE-2026-64642 — middleware/proxy bypass in apps built with Turbopack and a single i18n.locales entry, which lets auth checks be skipped; CVE-2026-64645 — SSRF/open redirect when a rewrites()/redirects() destination hostname is built from request input; and CVE-2026-64649 — SSRF in Server Actions on custom servers. Four medium CVEs cover an SVG image-optimization DoS, an unbounded Edge Server Action payload, disclosure of internal Server Function endpoint IDs, and two fetch cache-confusion bugs.

So what for TSD: Do this today. On every TSD site running Next 16, run npm install next@16.2.11 (or next@15.5.21 on 15.5), redeploy, and confirm the build. The middleware/proxy-bypass CVE-2026-64642 is the one to eyeball given TSD's proxy.ts pattern — verify no client relies on single-locale middleware for auth. This is also the exact wedge for a paid Managed Care retainer (see Money Moves).

Security Jul 22, 2026

Researchers find 434 exploitable flaws in AI-generated apps

A security analysis of AI-generated applications surfaced 434 exploitable flaws, with denial-of-service, broken authorization, and exposed secrets among the most common. It lands alongside Google shipping Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber, a model tuned to find, validate and patch critical vulnerabilities.

So what for TSD: If you ship AI-assisted code, this is your pre-flight checklist: scan for hardcoded secrets, confirm every mutating route checks authorization, and rate-limit anything that can be hammered. It also opens a service line — a fixed-fee "AI-app security audit" for other builders and SMBs shipping vibe-coded software.

TSMC adds $100B for Arizona; 2026 capex now $60–64B

TSMC said the AI build-out has not slowed, adding a $100B investment to grow its Arizona facilities and lifting 2026 capex to $60–64B (at least $4B above prior guidance), with revenue growth projected above 40%. So what for TSD: The compute supply that powers every model Nash resells keeps expanding, which is why inference prices keep falling. Cheap, abundant capacity is the tailwind under productized AI offers — plan pricing assuming model COGS keeps dropping, not rising.

Breach Jul 2026

AMD investigating a claimed IntelBroker breach

AMD is investigating a potential breach after the group IntelBroker claimed to have stolen sensitive data including upcoming product details, customer databases and internal documents. AMD says it is working with law enforcement to determine scope and mitigate damage.

So what for TSD: Vendor and supply-chain breaches are the norm, not the exception. Reinforces basic hygiene on client projects — rotate any credentials that touch third-party APIs, and don't store client secrets anywhere a single leak exposes them.
