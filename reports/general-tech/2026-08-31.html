◆ Your stack

Security Aug 25, 2026 — still open

The two critical Next.js RCEs are patched upstream, not on your servers

Next.js published 16.3.3 and 15.5.24 on August 25, a day earlier than planned because a second critical issue turned up during the embargo. GHSA-2xp9-vwfh-vxw4 comes from the upstream libheif dependency and can produce unauthenticated remote code execution when Image Optimization processes a crafted AVIF file. CVE-2026-75604 can produce unauthenticated remote code execution on Windows-hosted Next.js servers using the Pages Router, or the App Router without Cache Components; Linux and macOS are unaffected.

Applications hosted on Vercel are protected at the platform layer and need no customer action. Anything self-hosted, on a client's own box, or behind a container image you built yourself is on you.

So what for TSD: Run npm install next@16.3.3 on the 16.x sites and npm install next@15.5.24 on anything still on 15.5, then redeploy. Confirm which client sites are Vercel-hosted and which are not, because only the second list needs your time — and any site doing image optimization on user-supplied uploads is the one to do first.

◆ The company that made the phone

Leadership Effective Sept 1, 2026

Ternus inherits Apple with the AI problem still unsolved

Apple announced the succession in April: Cook to executive chairman, Ternus to chief executive effective September 1, approved unanimously by the board after a long-running succession process. Cook stayed through the summer to hand over. Ternus joined Apple in 2001, has run hardware engineering since 2021, and is the first Apple CEO to come from a pure hardware background since Jobs.

He starts nine days before the September 9 iPhone event, at which a foldable is expected. The inherited problem is software: Apple Intelligence and the delayed Siri rebuild are the gap between Apple and every other platform vendor right now, and it is not the part of the company Ternus built his career in.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do today, but the platform risk is worth tracking. If Apple keeps ceding assistant surface area, the phone stays a browser for your clients' customers and your web-first delivery keeps working. A serious on-device Siri that books appointments would change how SMB booking flows get discovered.

◆ Hardware, borders and breaches

Trade Aug 30, 2026

China took 86% of humanoid robot shipments while Washington built the wall

Washington tightened restrictions on foreign-made advanced robotic systems across July and August and imposed steep tariffs on imported drones and their components, with the drone tariffs taking effect in September and further component tariffs in 2027. Over the same period, TechCrunch reports, the five largest humanoid robot makers by shipments — AgiBot, Unitree, Galbot, UBTECH and Leju Robotics — were all Chinese and together accounted for 86% of global shipments in the first half of 2026.

The mechanism is a loop rather than a subsidy: lower prices put more units in the field, fielded units generate real-world data, the data improves the systems, and volume drives cost down again. Chinese makers are also pulling more of the stack in-house on top of an existing manufacturing base. The likely outcome is a fragmented market rather than a clean split, with US and allied builders competing where security requirements dominate.

So what for TSD: Watch it for client cost, not for a play. Any Charlotte-area client quoting drone work — roofing inspections, site surveys, real-estate media — is looking at higher hardware prices from September. If one of them mentions it, that is an opening to talk about what the software layer around their existing fleet could do instead.

Breach Aug 28–30, 2026

Hasbro is notifying employees five months after the March attack

Hasbro filed breach notification letters with state regulators including the Massachusetts Attorney General on August 28, tying the exposure back to the cyberattack that hit its systems on March 28 and forced parts of the business offline. Exposed data varies by person and includes names, addresses, phone numbers, national ID numbers, Social Security numbers, financial account details, payment card numbers and driver's licence data. The Massachusetts filing lists 436 state residents; the total has not been disclosed. Hasbro says attackers entered through a compromised employee account, has no indication of misuse, and is offering identity protection.

The earlier disclosure put direct cleanup at $11 million with a further $25 million of product sales delayed by the shutdown.

So what for TSD: One compromised employee account, $36 million of damage, and a five-month gap before anyone was told. That is the story to tell a client who thinks MFA on their booking admin and Supabase dashboard is optional. It costs you nothing to enforce and it is a concrete line item in a Managed AI retainer.
