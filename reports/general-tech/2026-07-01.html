◆ Your stack: Next.js and Vercel

Patch Next.js 16.3

Next.js 16.3 ships security fixes alongside Instant Navigations

The 16.3 line ships security fixes spanning Server Components, the App Router, and Middleware/Proxy — the reported classes include denial of service, auth bypass, XSS, SSRF, and cache poisoning, with a specific DoS advisory tied to connection exhaustion in apps using Cache Components. It also previews Instant Navigations: per-route control to stream with Suspense, cache with use cache, or block, making first-click navigation feel like a single-page app while keeping the server-centric model.

So what for TSD: Do this today — check the Next.js version pinned in every client repo and TSD app, bump to the latest 16.x patch, and if any project uses Cache Components, prioritize it. Then, on high-traffic client sites, trial Instant Navigations on the money pages (booking, contact) for a snappier feel at no infra cost.

Feature Vercel

Vercel Functions can now handle WebSocket connections

Vercel added WebSocket support to Functions, so realtime features — live chat, streaming AI responses, collaborative or live-status UI — can run natively on the platform instead of requiring a separate always-on server or a third-party realtime vendor.

So what for TSD: This is a sellable add-on. Build a live-chat or live-availability widget once, then resell it to existing clients as a paid upgrade. It also tightens the AI Concierge — streaming responses over a WebSocket feel faster than request/response polling.

◆ Security: a bad week worth learning from

Breach Jun 2026

ShinyHunters hit 100+ orgs via Oracle PeopleSoft; a Defender zero-day was exploited

The ShinyHunters group claimed to have compromised Oracle PeopleSoft servers at more than 100 organizations, including colleges, allegedly stealing hundreds of thousands of student records. Separately, a Microsoft Defender vulnerability tracked as CVE-2026-33825 was reported exploited in the wild as a zero-day before a patch shipped, and Tata Electronics confirmed an incident after the World Leaks group posted 200,000+ files. Attackers are also targeting AI support systems directly — one report describes manipulating an AI support flow to hijack Instagram accounts.

So what for TSD: Two moves. Patch anything Microsoft Defender related on your machines now. And use the headlines as a soft sales hook — offer existing clients a free 15-minute "is your site current and safe" check, then upsell a managed-updates retainer. Recurring revenue that also keeps your builds patched.

◆ Bigger picture

Business Build 2026

Microsoft swaps GPT-4 for its own coding model on its own chips

Microsoft is making Project Polaris, an in-house coding model running on its Maia chips, the default for every GitHub Copilot subscriber in August, displacing GPT-4 Turbo (with a three-month fallback). It's a business/hardware story more than a capability one: Microsoft cutting its dependence on OpenAI for its highest-volume developer product, and proving out its own silicon.

So what for TSD: If you or a client leans on Copilot, expect the default engine to change in August — test your workflows before it flips. The broader signal: platforms are verticalizing onto their own models and chips, which over time should push developer-tool pricing down.
