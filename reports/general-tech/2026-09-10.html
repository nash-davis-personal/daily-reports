◆ Patch this week

Security September 8

Microsoft closed a record ~974 vulnerabilities, two already exploited

Counts differ by tracker — Microsoft's own notes tally 974, BleepingComputer counted 966, ZDI 972, Tenable 964 — but every count makes this the largest Patch Tuesday ever shipped, with more than 110 rated critical. The two that matter immediately are the ones attackers already have. CVE-2026-85880 is a heap buffer overflow in Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call that takes a low-privilege foothold to SYSTEM. CVE-2026-81963 is an improper link resolution flaw in the Windows Update Stack that does the same job by a different route.

Both are in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a September 22 remediation deadline for federal agencies, which is the practical signal for everyone else. Two more unexploited flaws rated 9.8 sit in Remote Desktop Services and Windows DNS. Google's Android bulletin the same week patched 180 issues including critical remote code execution across Framework, System and Kernel.

So what for TSD: Neither zero-day touches your Vercel or Supabase surface, and both touch the Windows machines your clients run your sites on. That is a fifteen-minute email that makes you look like the technical adult in the room: "Microsoft shipped an unusually large update this week and two of the flaws are being used right now — reboot into it before the 22nd." Send it to every retainer client. It costs nothing and it is the cheapest reminder that you exist between invoices.

◆ Nash's stack

Platform September 10

Vercel Sandbox went multi-region, with failover

Vercel Sandbox now runs in every Vercel compute region. You pick a region per sandbox, set a project default, and configure failover regions for when one is unavailable. This lands a day after Vercel reported an 18x routing speedup from moving Sandbox onto regional replicas, so the two changes are the same project finishing.

Sandbox is where untrusted or agent-generated code executes — the thing you reach for when a user, or an agent, hands you code you did not write and you still have to run it.

So what for TSD: This is the piece that makes an agent feature safe to sell. If a Concierge or Front Desk build ever needs to execute something it generated — a computed quote, a data transform, a client-uploaded script — Sandbox with a pinned region and a failover is the boundary, and it now runs close enough to your users that latency stops being the excuse. Worth an hour to wire into one build so you know the shape before a client asks.

Models September 9

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is on AI Gateway, and production auth went free

Three Vercel changes on September 9 matter to a solo shop. DeepSeek's brand-new V4.1 Flash is available through AI Gateway with one API key, native image support, spend tracking and request tracing — so the cheapest capable long-context model of the week is one string change away, with the billing already instrumented. Vercel Authentication can now protect production domains at no additional cost on every plan, requiring a Vercel sign-in with project access before a visitor sees the deployment. And v0 added one-click Marketplace integrations for Resend, MongoDB Atlas, Auth0, Clerk and Amazon OpenSearch.

The free production authentication is worth reading against yesterday's news that Password Protection moved to $20 per project per month on Pro. If the people who need to see a staging build have Vercel accounts, the free path now covers it.

So what for TSD: Two concrete jobs. First, audit which client projects are actually paying $20 for password protection and move any internal-only ones to Vercel Authentication — that is recurring cost you can delete today. Client stakeholders without Vercel accounts still need the paid door, so bill it through as a line item rather than absorbing it. Second, the AI Gateway listing is the fastest possible version of the DeepSeek cost test in today's AI briefing: swap the model string, run last month's Concierge transcripts, read the spend tracking.

◆ Agents reach the checkout, silicon reaches the datacenter

Launch September 8

Meta's Muse acts inside your inbox, calendar and payments

Meta launched Muse, a personal agent that connects to email, calendar, payments and a long list of consumer services, and carries out multi-step errands: booking travel, sending mail, filling forms, buying things. Checkout runs through Link by Stripe, which mints a one-time card number so the merchant never sees the real card. It runs in its own isolated VM. Distribution is the point — muse.ai, iOS, Android and inside WhatsApp, with Meta's glasses to follow. Free tier is metered; Power is $20 a month and Maximum is $100.

This is the first consumer-scale product from Meta's "personal superintelligence" push and the first serious attempt to convert its planned 2026 AI infrastructure spend into revenue that is not advertising. Coverage is skeptical on exactly one axis, and it is the right one: Meta's engineers retain paths into the data the agent handles.

So what for TSD: Agents that complete a purchase are about to hit your clients' websites as traffic. Two immediate consequences for every site you ship: the booking and quote flows need to work without a human reading a layout — real form labels, real semantic HTML, a machine-readable path to availability — and the analytics need to tell you when a bot converted. Being the shop in Charlotte whose client sites are already agent-legible is a differentiator with a shelf life, so use it now.

Silicon September 8–9

Qualcomm paid Amazon $4B in warrants to get into AI inference

Qualcomm issued Amazon warrants for 25 million shares at $161.26 — roughly $4B — as part of a data-center partnership under which AWS is reported to buy up to $60B of Qualcomm AI chips. The work spans multiple generations of custom silicon aimed specifically at inference, plus high-speed optical interconnect reaching 1.6 terabits per second. Qualcomm stock rose about 3% on the announcement.

A day later Apple's "Surprise and Shine" event shipped the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max plus a book-style foldable, all on the A20 Pro built at 2nm, alongside Apple's own C2 modem — another step away from Qualcomm's modem revenue. The two stories are the same story from both ends: Qualcomm is buying its way into inference because its phone business is being designed out from underneath it.

So what for TSD: Inference silicon is getting a serious second supplier, which is the structural reason the token prices in today's AI briefing keep falling. Plan your AI product margins on costs that keep dropping and price on outcomes instead of usage, so the savings land in your pocket rather than in a per-token discount you already gave away.
