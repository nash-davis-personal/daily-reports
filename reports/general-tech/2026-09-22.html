◆ Patch this first

Security Notice Sept 21 · ships Sept 22

Next.js 16.3.6 and 15.5.26 ship out-of-band for a critical upstream issue

Josh Story, Karim Rahal and Sebastian Silbermann posted a one-paragraph heads-up on September 21: a critical security issue was identified in an upstream dependency, and Next.js 16.3.6 and 15.5.26 would be published out-of-band on September 22. The full advisory, GHSA-vcvr-r3jv-pc5j, is being withheld until the patch lands, so impact, affected versions and upgrade instructions are unknown as of this writing. The advisory URL returned 404 when checked for this briefing, and the npm registry still listed 16.3.5 as latest with 15.5.25 on the backport tag.

The pattern is the one to read. "Critical," "upstream dependency," and an advisory held back until release usually means the maintainers think the fix is easier to reverse-engineer than to defend against. Next.js has now shipped critical security releases in May, August and September.

So what for TSD: Do this today. Run npm view next version; the moment it reads 16.3.6, upgrade every client project on the 16.x line and every project still on 15.x to 15.5.26, then redeploy. Projects on Vercel still need the upgrade — hosting does not patch your lockfile. Inventory first: grep -h '"next"' ~/Documents/**/package.json across the client repos so nothing gets missed, and read GHSA-vcvr-r3jv-pc5j the moment it publishes to see whether self-hosted or App Router deployments carry extra exposure.

◆ The money behind the compute

Markets September 21

AMD crossed $1 trillion, and SoftBank borrowed $11 billion to keep paying OpenAI

AMD climbed more than 8% on September 21 to an all-time high of $613.92, taking its market capitalization above $1 trillion for the first time and making it the fourth US semiconductor company to get there, after Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron. The stock has run roughly 24% in five sessions and more than 180% this year, on expectations that AI agent inference will pull hard on CPU capacity as well as GPU.

On the financing side, SoftBank launched a bond deal of more than $11 billion — $10 billion in dollar notes across 3.5-, 5.5- and 7.5-year maturities plus €1 billion in euro paper — to fund the $10 billion third tranche of its OpenAI investment, expected to close October 1, and to retire a bridge loan raised for the same purpose. At intended size it would be the largest non-financial corporate bond deal ever out of Asia Pacific and Japan. SoftBank's cumulative OpenAI investment would reach about $64.6 billion for roughly 13%. Separately, Huawei's rotating chairman said Chinese demand for its AI compute already exceeds what it can build, so overseas Ascend shipments are limited to small test batches.

So what for TSD: Frontier compute is increasingly financed with debt rather than cash flow, which is what makes today's cheap inference prices structurally temporary. Do not build a client offer whose margin depends on a specific model's current price. Quote outcomes, keep the model behind an adapter, and write a one-line "model and pricing may change" clause into the retainer so a repricing is a config change rather than a renegotiation.

Regulation September 21

Ireland's DPC fined Google €403 million over location data

Ireland's Data Protection Commission found Google did not lawfully or fairly process location data in Web & App Activity — the setting that records browsing and search history — or in Location History, which maps where a phone has been. It also found the company fell short on lawfulness, fairness and transparency in Android's Location Accuracy feature. The fine is €403 million, about $463 million, and closes an inquiry opened in February 2020. It is the fourth largest privacy penalty the Irish regulator has issued, behind fines against TikTok and Meta.

So what for TSD: The finding is about consent quality and transparency, not about exotic tracking — which is exactly where a small business site gets caught. Spend an hour this week auditing what every client site actually loads: analytics, session replay, pixels, chat widgets. If a client site sets tracking before consent, or its privacy page does not name each processor, that is a fixable problem and a legitimate reason to open a conversation about a paid compliance pass.
