◆ Security, one layer out from your stack

Exploited KEV Nov 10 · reported Nov 12

CISA confirms attacks on an Outlook flaw that leaks authentication data

CISA confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-124194 in Microsoft Outlook and added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Nov 10, which gives federal agencies until Nov 24 to patch. The flaw can expose a user's authentication data after a user interaction. Microsoft classifies it as spoofing; it does not give an attacker code execution on its own.

Separately, attackers are exploiting zero-days in N-able N-central, the remote monitoring tool many managed IT providers use to run client machines, and N-able has patched the ones that needed no login. One open flag from the same day's reporting: a researcher who has published several Windows exploits this year is said to have released another that grants SYSTEM privileges. Only one outlet carried it in this pass.

So what for TSD: Your clients are small offices that live in Outlook and often pay an outside IT shop. Send the two or three you know best a three-line note: install November's Microsoft updates this week, and ask your IT provider whether they run N-central and have patched it. It costs you five minutes and it is the kind of note that makes an owner think of you as the person who watches this.

Breach Disclosed Nov 11–12

Notion: a breach at its inference vendor exposed AI chat transcripts

Notion said attackers who breached Fireworks AI reached a dataset of Notion AI chat transcripts from fewer than 1% of workspaces, tied to the Insights feature. Notion says the vendor contract promised zero data retention; Fireworks says its zero-retention services show no impact and traces the intrusion to a breach at a third-party cloud provider in late October. The Cloud Security Alliance's briefing makes the plain point: the contract said nothing would be kept, and the data was sitting at rest in a dataset.

So what for TSD: The engineering lesson is about side features. The inference call was covered; the analytics pipeline that stored requests and responses was the leak. List every table, bucket and vendor log where your products keep conversation text, and set a retention period on each. Details and sources are in today's AI brief.

◆ Chips, companies and space

Leadership Nov 12

Nvidia: Huang resigns, Kress named interim CEO, a board chair for the first time

Nvidia's press release and 8-K on Thursday say Jensen Huang resigned as president, CEO and director effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim chief executive, director Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first board chair, and a search for a permanent CEO will consider inside and outside candidates. Coverage ties the departure to the board investigation opened Nov 8.

The stock opened down more than 13% and recovered to close down 4.34%. AMD and Broadcom each gained more than 5%, and CoreWeave, which rents Nvidia hardware, fell about 7% according to TSD's Macroscope board notes. Quarterly results are due Nov 17.

So what for TSD: No action. For a builder the relevant question is supply and price of compute over the next year, and one day of trading does not answer it. The Nov 17 call is where the interim chief has to give guidance.

Earnings Nov 12

Cisco grows 24% on AI networking; Applied Materials grows 53%

Cisco reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $18.5 billion, up 24%, which coverage calls its strongest growth in fifteen years. Networking revenue rose 43% to $11.1 billion, product orders grew 28%, and non-GAAP earnings per share were $1.40. It raised its forecast for hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue this fiscal year to $9 billion from $7.5 billion and its full-year revenue outlook to $74.8 to $75.8 billion. Gross margin slipped to 63.7% from 65.5%, and management flagged tight supply of optical components.

Applied Materials, which makes the machines chip factories run on, reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.41 billion, up 53%, against a consensus of about $10.3 billion, with non-GAAP earnings of $4.16 per share and record full-year revenue of $34.45 billion.

So what for TSD: The spending on data centers is still accelerating two steps upstream of Nvidia, in the switches and the factory tools. For you that reads as continued downward pressure on the price of a model call through 2027, which is the cost side of every AI retainer you quote.

Space Today, 5:45 p.m. EST target

Starship Flight 15: first attempt to catch the ship

SpaceX is targeting no earlier than 22:45 UTC today (5:45 p.m. EST) for Starship's fifteenth flight from Pad 2 at Starbase, Texas. The plan is to put Ship 42 into low Earth orbit at about 275 km, deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and then catch the upper stage with the launch tower's arms, which has never been tried. The booster catch has been done before.

The date still depends on the FAA. Flight 14's license covered only that flight, so this one needs a modification, and as of Nov 12 reports said the FAA's page did not yet list it. Treat the time as a target.

So what for TSD: Nothing to do with client work. It is the one event today worth having on a second screen: a caught upper stage is the step that makes the whole vehicle reusable, and that is what the price of putting data centers and satellites in orbit hangs on.
