◆ The memory squeeze reaches consumers

Breaking July 27–28

AI data centers bid away the world's DRAM, and phone prices are the receipt

Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron control nearly all global DRAM output and have been shifting capacity toward high-bandwidth memory for AI accelerators. The consumer side is paying for it. Mobile DRAM contract prices are tracking a roughly 93–98% quarter-over-quarter jump in Q2 on top of a 58–63% rise in Q1, taking LPDDR5 from around $10 per gigabyte to near $20. Sub-$200 phones have seen bill-of-materials inflation of 20–30% since January, and TrendForce expects some budget models to revert to 4GB of RAM.

It is now visible in named products. Google is expected to price the base Pixel 11 around $899, roughly $100 higher, with RAM cost cited as the reason. Qualcomm has signalled double-digit percentage chip price increases beginning in September. Analysts do not expect meaningful relief before late 2027.

So what for TSD: Two practical effects. Client device refresh cycles stretch, so more of your traffic is old mid-range Android on constrained memory — keep testing on a throttled device and keep bundles lean, because JS parse cost hurts more on 4GB phones. Second, if you spec hardware for a client build, quote it fresh; anything you priced in the spring is stale.
Android Authority TechPowerUp, citing TrendForce IDC
Markets July 28

The memory trade reversed hard: SK Hynix -14%, Samsung -13%, Kospi off more than 10%

Korean memory names were hammered overnight and dragged the Kospi down more than 10%. The proximate cause is not demand — it is the structure of the demand. Nvidia's reported $250 billion financing backstop for OpenAI's Ohio data center has investors using the phrase "circular financing," where a chip supplier underwrites its own customers' ability to buy. Layer on the worry that Chinese models are closing the capability gap and the return on all that capex looks less certain than it did a week ago.

SK Hynix reports results around 8 p.m. ET tonight, which is the single best read available on whether the memory cycle is actually turning or the market is just repricing risk.

So what for TSD: Watch it, do not trade it. The operational read is that the AI buildout narrative now has a visible bear case, which means the "everyone is adopting AI, get on board" pitch is weaker than the boring one that works anyway: this specific automation saves this business this many hours a week.

◆ Platforms and products

Delay July 27

Apple pushes smart glasses to WWDC 2027 on privacy concerns

Bloomberg reports Apple has moved its smart glasses launch to WWDC in June 2027, with consumer availability at the end of that year. Privacy is the stated cause. Meta spent the same week adding Threads support, a Muse Spark AI upgrade and neural handwriting input to its Ray-Ban Display line, which leaves it effectively unopposed in the category for another eighteen months.

So what for TSD: Nothing to build for. Useful as a reminder that face-worn cameras are a live privacy issue — relevant if any client in a salon, clinic or retail space asks you about in-store video or AI camera analytics. That conversation needs a consent answer before a technical one.
Tech Startups, citing Bloomberg
Layoffs July 27

Monday.com cuts about 20% of staff while restructuring around AI

Monday.com is cutting roughly 630 roles, about a fifth of its workforce, as it reorganises around its AI platform. This is a profitable, growing SaaS company shedding a fifth of its people, which makes it a cleaner signal than the usual post-overhire correction — the work-management category is being rebuilt around agents and the headcount that supported the old shape is going.

So what for TSD: Direct prospecting signal. SMBs paying per-seat for work-management tools are about to get less human support from the vendor. "You are paying for seats you barely use and the support is thinning" is a real opener for a booking or intake automation that replaces a tool subscription rather than adding to it.
