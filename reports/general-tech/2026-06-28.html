◆ Security — patch first, read later

Exploited Jun 25 · deadline Jun 28

CISA flags actively-exploited Cisco and PTC flaws with a Sunday federal deadline

CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on June 25 based on evidence of active exploitation: CVE-2026-20230, a server-side request forgery flaw in Cisco Unified Communications Manager, and CVE-2026-12569, an improper-input-validation issue in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM. Federal civilian agencies have until Sunday, June 28 to remediate both. Neither is in your stack, but "actively exploited, patch within days" is the bar that should trigger a quick dependency check.

So what for TSD: Two-minute hygiene pass — confirm your own dependencies are current (you're on Next.js 16.x; the big May Next.js/React security release should already be applied) and flag any client running Cisco UC phone systems or PTC PLM so they get patched. This is also the seed of a paid "patch & secure" sprint — see today's Money Moves.

Hygiene June 2026

A 24-billion-record credential dump resurfaces the boring basics

Researchers flagged a compilation exposing roughly 24 billion records — usernames, emails, plaintext passwords, and login URLs — largely assembled from infostealer-malware logs, prior breaches, and Telegram dumps. Nothing exotic; the danger is password reuse and the long tail of credentials sitting in old logins.

So what for TSD: Your client portals already run Supabase passkeys + magic-link, so you're ahead of the curve. Use this as the prompt to enforce 2FA on any client admin dashboard that still allows passwords, and rotate any shared service credentials older than a few months.

◆ The platforms you build on

Policy June 2026

AI models are becoming regulated supply, not just a product you buy

A June executive order on AI and cybersecurity directs the federal government to build a framework for evaluating model capabilities and designating the most powerful as "covered frontier models." In practice that already means OpenAI shipping GPT-5.6 to vetted partners only and Anthropic's Fable 5/Mythos 5 sitting offline. For a developer, the platform layer just got a new failure mode: the model behind your app can be reclassified and pulled.

So what for TSD: Treat your AI provider like any other dependency that can have an outage — abstract it. A thin routing layer that can swap Claude → Grok 4.3 → an OpenAI tier without a rewrite is cheap insurance, and it doubles as a feature you can sell.

GA June 2026

Grok 4.3 hits general availability on Amazon Bedrock

xAI made Grok 4.3 generally available on Amazon Bedrock — lowest reported hallucination rate among frontier models, a 1M-token context window, and configurable reasoning effort. The business angle that matters here is distribution: a strong model now sits inside the AWS console with no waitlist, billed through an account most agencies already have.

So what for TSD: This is the swap target for the routing layer above. A GA, low-hallucination model on Bedrock is a credible fallback for TSD Concierge — and "fewer wrong answers" is something an SMB owner actually understands.

Stack June 2026

Supabase turns off connection logging by default for new projects

Supabase changed the default Postgres logging setup for new projects, switching log_connections off to cut log noise and volume, and is migrating existing Free and Pro projects to the new default. Small change, but it touches every project you spin up.

So what for TSD: Expect quieter connection logs on new and migrated projects — that's intended, not a bug. If you ever relied on connection-log lines for debugging a client's auth issue, flip the setting back on for that project explicitly.
