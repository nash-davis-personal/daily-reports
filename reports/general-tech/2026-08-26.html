◆ Patch this before you do anything else today

Security Aug 25, 2026

Next.js 16.3.3 and 15.5.24 fix two critical unauthenticated RCEs

Vercel had announced this release for Wednesday. It found an additional critical vulnerability in an upstream dependency and pulled the release forward to Tuesday, so the patched versions have been available since August 25.

The first issue, GHSA-2xp9-vwfh-vxw4, originates in libheif inside the sharp library. When Next.js optimizes an attacker-controlled AVIF image, it can lead to unauthenticated remote code execution. The patched releases disable AVIF optimization entirely until an upstream fix propagates. The second, CVE-2026-75604 (GHSA-p293-qw3h-jr36), affects applications that use both the Pages Router and the App Router without Cache Components when the server runs on a Windows filesystem. Linux and macOS are not affected, and Vercel says there is no known workaround for affected Windows-hosted applications.

Applications hosted on Vercel are protected and require no customer action or redeploy: the platform runs on Linux, and Vercel disabled AVIF optimization across its managed Image Optimization service, so AVIF files are now delivered unprocessed.

So what for TSD: Do this today. For every Next.js project you maintain — Bisque, Studio C, Sonderwerks, the demo sites, anything running locally or on a client's own box — run npm install next@16.3.3 (or next@15.5.24 on the 15 line), commit the lockfile, and push. Your Vercel-hosted production is already safe, so this is about self-hosted and local surfaces plus keeping the lockfile honest for the next audit. One real consequence to check for: if any client site serves AVIF through next/image, those images now pass through unoptimized, so watch the payload size on image-heavy pages and consider forcing WebP in images.formats until AVIF comes back.

◆ Vercel shipped a lot on the same day

Platform Aug 25, 2026

Vercel Connect is generally available, and it kills long-lived tokens in agent code

Connect issues short-lived, scoped credentials at runtime instead of storing long-lived tokens for the services an agent or app talks to. It covers 100-plus services, with dedicated connectors for Slack, GitHub, Linear, Discord, Notion, Salesforce, Figma and Snowflake in the dashboard, plus generic OAuth and API-key connectors for everything else. Adapters ship for Better Auth, Auth.js, eve, the AI SDK and MCP clients. Each token is scoped to the task, refreshed automatically, and expires on its own.

Pricing is usage-based: Hobby includes 500 token requests and 1,000 triggers a month at no cost, Pro and Enterprise bill $3 per 10,000 token requests. Vercel also added a Linq connector on the same day that pushes messages over iMessage, RCS and SMS.

So what for TSD: The Linq connector is the one to look at first — a managed way to send iMessage, RCS and SMS from a Vercel app is directly useful for Booking Bridge confirmations and Front Desk callbacks, and it removes a Twilio-shaped line item from your build. On Connect itself: the free Hobby allowance is enough to prototype, and it lets you tell a client's IT person that no standing credential for their Google Workspace or their CRM sits in your environment variables. That is a real objection you will eventually hit on a larger deal. See today's General AI briefing for Okta shipping the enterprise version of the same idea this week.

Pricing Aug 25, 2026

Speed Insights got a free tier on every plan

Real-user performance monitoring is now free on all plans. The paid product is renamed Speed Insights Plus and keeps slow-route diagnosis, detailed breakdowns of where pages get slow, and historical data. The old arrangement gave Hobby users one free project and then took the free access away on upgrade to Pro unless you bought the add-on; the free tier now carries over. Anyone already paying keeps the same capabilities and the same price under the new name. Pro pricing for Plus is $10 per project per month.

So what for TSD: Turn it on for every client project today. Real Core Web Vitals from actual visitors is a retainer deliverable you can now provide at zero cost — a monthly "here is what your site's speed did" line in the Managed AI report costs you nothing and is the kind of thing that makes a retainer feel worth paying. Reserve Plus for the one or two sites where you are actively chasing a slow route.

AI Gateway Aug 25, 2026

Free MiniMax models, Wan 3.0 video, and video generation as a background job

MiniMax M3 and M2.7 are free on AI Gateway through September 6 when GMI Cloud serves them, via the minimax/minimax-m3-free and minimax/minimax-m2.7-free model IDs or a provider pin. M3 is MiniMax's first model with a 1M-token context window and native multimodal input, aimed at software engineering, terminal tool use and agentic browsing.

Alongside it, AI Gateway can now run video generation as a background job with three ways to wait for the result: a webhook, a start-and-status pair, or short polling.

Separately, asynchronous video generation plus a 30-second Wan 3.0 clip is enough to build a "monthly social video for your shop" add-on for the detailing and salon clients without standing up your own queue infrastructure.

◆ The end of a twenty-one-year experiment

Shutdown Aug 25, 2026

Amazon is closing Mechanical Turk on September 30

AWS posted the notice on the Mechanical Turk site on Tuesday. The platform launched in 2005 and matched businesses with people willing to do Human Intelligence Tasks — data labelling, transcription, surveys — for small amounts of money. It stopped accepting new customers on July 30 and closes fully on September 30.

The stated context is decline. Models now do most of what HITs were used for, and the work that still needs humans went to a newer generation of data vendors — Scale AI, Mercor, Prolific — built specifically to recruit and manage people who train models. Bezos originally described MTurk as "artificial artificial intelligence," which reads differently now that the artificial version arrived.

So what for TSD: No direct dependency. It is a clean data point for a sales conversation, though: the largest marketplace for paying humans to do small repetitive digital tasks is closing because software got good enough, and that is exactly the argument you make when a shop owner asks whether an AI receptionist can really handle their calls. Use it as one line, not a lecture.
