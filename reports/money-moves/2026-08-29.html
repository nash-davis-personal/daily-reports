2. The Assistant Report Card — a scored monthly eval that makes a retainer defensible

The shift: Anthropic published "Automated Researchers Can Reliably Mitigate Alignment Failures" on August 28: an automated system given ten benchmarks searched the literature, proposed a method, trained for 30 minutes and iterated, improving all ten without degrading general performance, at roughly $4 an hour in inference against $150 an hour for a human researcher. source ↗ today's AI brief

Why you: Every Concierge and Front Desk client eventually asks what they are paying for in month four, and "it is still running" is a weak answer. The loop in that paper is a product: a graded test set, a cheap model that proposes changes, a short eval, repeat. You have already built a harder version of this machinery for the briefing and podcast engine, so the build is assembly rather than invention, and the running cost is small enough that it disappears inside an existing retainer. The deliverable is the real asset — a one-page score with last month's number next to this month's is the artifact that renews a contract without a conversation.
First move: Pick your weakest-performing deployed assistant. Write 30 real questions a customer would actually ask it, with a graded ideal answer for each, and check them into the repo next to the prompt. Run the current version against them and write down the score. That number, before any tuning at all, is the first page of the report card, and you can send it to that client this week with "here is where your assistant stands and here is what next month's number should be."

3. An AI dependency sweep aimed at businesses whose AI feature somebody else built

The shift: Two men were charged in Western Australia on August 26 as principals of TeamPCP, the group behind the self-propagating Shai-Hulud worm; authorities estimate 1,000+ organizations compromised, 500,000+ credentials and 300GB+ of data stolen, and the confirmed poisoned packages include LiteLLM, the model-routing library, alongside the Trivy and KICS security scanners and the Telnyx Python SDK. source ↗ today's tech brief

Why you: You have run patch-sweep offers at your own clients already. The new wedge here points somewhere else: at the businesses that hired a freelancer or a small agency to bolt on a chatbot in the last 18 months, walked away with a repo they cannot read, and have never rotated a key since. LiteLLM being on the poisoned list is what makes that a specific, provable worry rather than a vague one. You can read those repos in an afternoon, and the finding — a stale API key with no spend cap, sitting in an environment where an unpinned dependency ran — is concrete enough that the fix sells itself. It is also a clean way to inherit a competitor's client without ever criticizing the competitor.
First move: Before anything else, run the sweep on yourself: check every lockfile you own for affected versions and rotate Supabase service keys, Vercel tokens, model API keys, GitHub PATs and Telnyx or Vapi credentials that could have been exposed. Then write the finding up as a two-page anonymized sample report. That document is the sales asset — Grant and Bishop send it with one line about the arrests, and the ask is a small fixed fee for the same review on the prospect's system.

5. A performance-linked retainer tier, built like Marvell's warrant ladder

The shift: Marvell disclosed a custom-silicon agreement with Google structured as a warrant with 240 tranches, one block unlocking per $500 million of custom-chip revenue booked, running through fiscal 2033 to a $120 billion ceiling — payment that converts only as the customer actually spends. source ↗ today's tech brief

Why you: You lose deals to price with owners who cannot picture the return, and you are the only vendor in that conversation who can actually measure the outcome, because Booking Bridge counts appointments and Front Desk counts answered calls. A small fixed base plus a step that unlocks on a measured number reads as confidence rather than discounting, and it survives month four. Be honest with yourself about the tradeoff: this trades some near-term cash for a higher close rate, so cap how many clients you carry on it at once and keep the base high enough to cover your delivery time on its own.
First move: Take the one proposal currently stalled on price. Rewrite it as a base that is roughly half your usual monthly, plus a per-unit step on the one number that client cares about — booked appointments, calls answered after hours, forms completed. Put a written cap on the total so it cannot spook them, and send it Monday with the base and the step on separate lines. Close it before the September 15–16 FOMC week, when owners' attention goes to rates.
