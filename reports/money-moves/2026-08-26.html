6. A pricing-disclosure technical review, timed to the FTC comment window

The shift: The FTC opened a 30-day comment period on August 19 on a proposed enforcement policy statement covering personalized pricing — using a customer's own data to set an individual price. The Commission signals it will apply Section 5 of the FTC Act to businesses that do this without adequate disclosure, on the reasoning that consumers expect a displayed price to be the same one everyone else sees. Comments close September 18. source ↗ · today's America brief

Why you: A lot of ordinary e-commerce and booking software varies price by visitor without the owner ever deciding to do that — it arrives inside a plugin or a "smart pricing" toggle. Finding it is a technical audit, which is squarely your job. Judging whether it breaks the law is a legal opinion, which is squarely not. That line is the whole offer and you should state it out loud: you tell them whether their site shows different prices to different visitors and where that behavior comes from, and they take that finding to their attorney. Your retail clients are the natural first call.
First move: Audit one retail client's checkout this week — hit it from a clean browser, a logged-in session and a different geography, and diff the prices. Write the finding as a one-page memo naming what varies and which system causes it. If anything varies, that memo is worth $500 to them and it is the template for the next ten. Be explicit in writing that you are not giving legal advice, and that this is a proposed policy statement rather than a rule in force.
