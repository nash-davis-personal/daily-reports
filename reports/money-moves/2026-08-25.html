On the other side, Ramp's July spend index has Claude Opus 5 at 3.5% of AI spend in its launch week against 28% for Opus 4.8, and the FT reports average prices paid for Anthropic models are down roughly 25% since mid-July because enterprises route cheap work to cheap models. source ↗ FT summary ↗

Why you: Every retainer you quoted this year was priced against higher token costs, so this is margin that arrives on contracts already signed. The routing build is the reusable part: on a receptionist product most turns are trivial, so classifying the request and escalating only the hard ones is a week of work you do once and reuse across every client forever. The Macroscope board has the AI and tech capex theme accelerating at high conviction, which means the price floor is more likely to keep falling than to snap back, so pricing your offers as if costs stay flat leaves money on the table either way.
First move: Instrument per-conversation cost in Front Desk this week so you have a real number instead of an estimate. Then add the classifier: intent detection, FAQ lookup and appointment slotting go to a cheap model, and only genuinely hard turns escalate. Once you can see the new cost per conversation, make one deliberate decision rather than drifting: either the margin stays with you, or the next three pitches lead with a lower monthly price and a longer guarantee. Do not quote annual retainers off the promotional rate, since the guarantee expires November 21.

5. Run a security hardening pass across the portfolio and bill it

The shift: Taiwanese research firm TeamT5 told Bloomberg that Chinese state-affiliated hacking groups more than doubled attack volume after handing routine work to AI, with DeepSeek the preferred tool because it is powerful and lightly guarded. Documented cases include generating exploit code and mapping a target's domains from 1,000 collected IP addresses. Volume rose while the success rate did not, since every confirmed intrusion still came from a human. source ↗

Why you: More probes at the same hit rate means low-effort targets get hit more often, and small business sites are the definition of a low-effort target. You know exactly where the holes are in your own portfolio because you built it: Supabase projects without row-level security, service-role keys that have passed through a shared env file, form and login routes with no rate limiting. Vercel's new Config and Secret environment variable types landed yesterday and make the key half of this concrete, since a Secret value is write-only once set and cannot be read back by a client with dashboard access or an agent with CLI access.
First move: Do it unpaid on your three highest-traffic client sites this week: confirm row-level security is on, rotate any service-role key that has ever been in a repo or a shared env file, mark every provider key as Secret, and turn on firewall rules for login and form routes. Write the findings up as a two-page report. That report is now the sample you sell the same pass with, at a flat fee, to every other client and to anyone Grant and Bishop are already talking to.
