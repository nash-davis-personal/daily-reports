This week the regulators handed you an offer: the pixel-and-cancel audit

The FTC sued a telehealth company for two things thousands of ordinary local businesses do without thinking — letting an ad pixel fire on a sensitive page, and running a recurring charge nobody can find the cancel button for. ◆ Best bet right now

Sell a fixed-fee "Pixel & Cancel Audit" to every local business with a membership plan

On July 29 the FTC, joined by Utah and Los Angeles County, sued Hims & Hers for sharing customer health information with Meta and Snap through customer-list uploads and automatic pixel events, and for billing refills without clearly disclosing when the next charge would hit or how to stop it. The federal click-to-cancel rule was vacated in 2025, but the FTC keeps bringing these cases under Section 5, and roughly 30 states have their own automatic-renewal laws — several stricter than the vacated federal rule. Meanwhile pixel wiretap litigation is live and expensive: a judge recently pushed Google and Meta into discovery over prescription data collected on a telehealth site.

Your version: a two-hour sweep of a client's site. What pixels fire, on which pages, before consent. Whether any of them fire on something a customer would consider private — an appointment form, a service selection, a patient or client portal. Whether the renewal date and the cancel path are stated where a normal person would see them. Deliver a one-page findings sheet with a fixed-fee fix quote attached. Price the audit at $500–900 and the remediation separately.
