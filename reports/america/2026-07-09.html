◆ Extreme weather

National Jul 8, 2026

Historic heat dome tied to at least 44 deaths as a second one forms out West

A record-breaking heat dome has pushed life-threatening conditions across major metro corridors, affecting more than 200 million Americans and now tied to at least 44 deaths as of July 8. Since late June the East has broken at least three dozen daily high-temperature records; New York City's Central Park reached 100°F for the first time since 2012. A second heat dome began forming over the western and central US on July 6, and the first-ever heat advisory was issued for Alaska.

Why it matters: Extreme heat strains the power grid, drives up cooling costs, and poses a direct public-safety risk — especially for outdoor workers and older residents. Utilities across affected regions are managing near-peak demand.

Infrastructure Jul 4–6, 2026

Storms breaking the dome knocked out power to more than 1.3 million

Several rounds of severe thunderstorms moved across the country from July 4–6, temporarily breaking the eastern heat dome but causing more than 1.3 million power outages and four fatalities from flooding and wind. The whiplash between extreme heat and severe storms has kept emergency crews and utilities stretched.

Why it matters: Back-to-back heat and storm damage compounds the strain on grids and first responders, and outages during extreme heat raise the health risk for anyone without backup cooling.

◆ Markets & the economy

Markets Jul 8, 2026

Stocks slip and oil spikes as Middle East tensions rattle commodities

Wall Street closed mixed on July 8: the Dow fell 1.1% (−576.76 points to 52,348.39) after being down more than 855 points intraday, the S&P 500 slipped 0.3% to 7,482.71, and the Nasdaq rose 0.2% to 25,870.65. The move followed remarks at a NATO summit that a ceasefire with Iran had ended, which sent oil higher — WTI crude up 4.4% to $73.52 and Brent up 5.4% to $78.19. Energy shares gained, with Marathon Petroleum up 5.4%.

Why it matters: Higher crude feeds through to gasoline and shipping costs and complicates the inflation picture the Fed is weighing. For anyone managing their own money, an oil-driven inflation scare tends to push back the timeline for rate cuts.
Yahoo Finance — Stock market news, July 9
The Fed Jul 8, 2026

June Fed minutes show a divided committee; jobs and inflation still crosscutting

Minutes from the June FOMC meeting, released July 8, showed officials split between arguments for a rate hike and a cut, with the target range held at 3.50–3.75%. Futures price roughly a 75% chance the Fed stays put next meeting. The backdrop is mixed: June payrolls rose just 57,000 while unemployment ticked down to 4.2%, and CPI is running 4.2% year-over-year through May — the largest 12-month increase since April 2023.

Why it matters: A divided Fed and sticky 4%+ inflation mean borrowing costs — for mortgages, small-business loans, and credit lines — are likely to stay elevated near-term, keeping pressure on households and small firms.
