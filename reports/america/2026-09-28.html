◆ Weather

Storm Sept 27–28

The nor'easter killed one, cut power to more than 130,000, and is finally pushing out Monday

The storm battered southeastern New England for a second day Sunday. More than 130,000 customers were without power Sunday morning, worst on Long Island, with New Jersey at about 50,000 and Connecticut at 25,000. A New York City Housing Authority employee was killed by a falling tree in Brooklyn. Gusts reached 81 mph at Montauk Point, and tidal surges flooded low-lying New Jersey neighborhoods. Boston Logan logged 338 cancellations and 381 delays by Sunday evening.

By early Monday about 18,000 customers were still dark. Forecasters expect the storm to stay nearly stationary through midday, with 35–40 mph gusts and minor high-tide flooding, before moving out this afternoon.

Why it matters: Monday's Northeast commute and flights are still disrupted. Expect shipments routed through the corridor to run a day or two behind this week.

North Carolina Sept 27

Hatteras Island is cut off by road; an emergency ferry started Sunday

The nor'easter breached the dune at Pea Island and undermined about 500 feet of NC 12, closing the highway between the Marc Basnight Bridge and Rodanthe. Dare County declared a state of emergency for Hatteras Island. NCDOT began an emergency ferry between Stumpy Point and Rodanthe at 11 a.m. Sunday, the first use of that route in more than ten years, limited to permanent residents, essential personnel and critical business vendors. Visitors are barred. NC 12 on north Ocracoke reopened Sunday afternoon, restoring limited Hatteras–Ocracoke ferry service. NCDOT plans a temporary road but hasn't given a timeline, and local outlets report the closure could last weeks.

Why it matters: This hits the Outer Banks shoulder-season economy directly. Anyone with a Hatteras rental or trip planned should assume no access for visitors until NCDOT announces a temporary road.

◆ The economy

Week ahead Sept 28 – Oct 2

Inflation Wednesday and jobs Friday test the first rate hike in nearly three years

August PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, comes out Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET; core PCE last printed 3.3% year over year, and analysts expect the headline to rise 0.4% for the month after 0.2% in July. Friday's September jobs report follows August's 162,000 payrolls and 4.1% unemployment. JOLTS and consumer confidence are Tuesday, ADP Wednesday, and ISM manufacturing and jobless claims Thursday. Micron reports Wednesday and Nike Thursday.

Stocks come in strong: the S&P 500 gained 1.21% last week and the Nasdaq 2.06%, led by chips, while the Russell 2000 fell 0.8% and utilities dropped about 3% as the 10-year settled near 5.17%. WTI oil ended the week near $92.70.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as sideways, medium conviction, holding through a rate shock. The 10-year at 5.17% stays above the board's 4.75% wire, and October rate-hike odds on Kalshi were about 63%. The next dated test is Wednesday's PCE, followed by September core PPI on Oct. 15. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-09-27)

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money or pricing a loan, this is the week the rate path gets repriced. Small caps and rate-sensitive sectors lagged while yields sat above 5%, so a hot PCE or jobs number would most likely hit those first.

Federal funding Deadline Sept 30

The fiscal year ends Wednesday, and the government stays funded through Dec. 11

The Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027 passed the Senate 90–6 and the House 370–48 and was signed Sept. 2. It extends current spending levels through Dec. 11, so there is no Oct. 1 lapse. The next funding deadline falls after the November elections.

Why it matters: Federal services small businesses rely on (SBA loan processing, IRS, permits that touch federal agencies) keep running through at least mid-December.

◆ Science & space

Launch September 28

SpaceX attempts Starship's first orbital flight from Texas this morning

The FAA approved Flight 14 late Saturday. The window opens at 8:15 a.m. ET, with backup dates Sept. 29–30. The ship is set to deploy 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites and complete about six orbits before splashing down. The result wasn't known when this briefing was filed.

Why it matters: A successful orbital flight is the gate for Starship's larger payload and crewed-lunar plans, and for a big jump in Starlink capacity.
