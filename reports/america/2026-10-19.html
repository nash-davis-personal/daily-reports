◆ Economy and markets

Markets Mon Oct 19

Futures rise after a week of losses

In early Monday trading Dow futures were up 0.16% near 52,300, S&P 500 futures up 0.59% near 7,750 and Nasdaq 100 futures up 1.15% near 29,730 (FXStreet, about 08:58 GMT). Tech stocks had fallen the prior week on AI-financing worries. Per the Macroscope board, the S&P 500 fell 1.95% last week while the equal-weight version rose 0.49%, so the typical large company held up better than the index headline.

A separate headline cites lower oil prices as helping sentiment; I could not find an oil price for Monday.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the index decline was concentrated in a few chip and AI names. A portfolio heavy in those holdings had a worse week than the S&P 500 number suggests.

Fed Meeting Oct 27–28

Traders price an 85% chance the Fed holds

CME FedWatch puts the chance of unchanged rates at 85.1%, up from 42.4% a month ago. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said at an IMF event Friday that he does not prejudge policy decisions, and the Fed is in its pre-meeting quiet period.

Why it matters: Rate-hike fears have faded, but the 10-year Treasury yield was 5.09% on the Oct 16 reading (Treasury, via the board), so borrowing costs for small-business loans and variable-rate debt stay high even without a hike.

Credit Oct 14–19

AI-linked borrowers face a bond-market test

CoreWeave fell about 25% over the week, bond investors are avoiding its debt, and its credit-default swaps reached distressed levels (FXStreet). MUFG warned the dollar could reverse if AI sentiment weakens, given heavy foreign inflows into AI-linked US assets. CoreWeave's CEO cites a contracted backlog above $104 billion.

Why it matters: AI spending has been a big contributor to market gains and to investment in data centers and power. Trouble financing it would show up in chip and equipment companies' earnings this week first.
