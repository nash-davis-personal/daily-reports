◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 24

The Dow closes above 50,000 for the first time while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq fall

The Dow rose about 423 points, or 0.85%, to 50,018.70, led by health care and other value sectors. The S&P 500 fell 0.50% to 7,201.09, its eighth decline in nine sessions, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.48% to 23,733.54. Nvidia dropped 5.86% after Monday's 31.48% gain. Markets are closed Thursday and close at 1 p.m. ET Friday.

Why it matters: The headline record and the broad index point in different directions. For a personal retirement or brokerage account, the S&P 500 is closer to what most index funds hold, and it is still below where it started the month.

Consumers Nov 24

Consumer confidence comes in at 103.3, well above the 92.2 forecast

The Conference Board's November reading beat expectations by about 11 points, going into the Thanksgiving-to-Cyber-Monday shopping stretch. The full component breakdown was not in the coverage reviewed.

Why it matters: Households say they feel better than economists expected, which supports holiday spending at stores and local service businesses this week.

Data today Nov 25, 8:30 ET

October PCE inflation, revised Q3 GDP and jobless claims all come out this morning

Economists expect headline PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, to rise to 3.8% from 3.6% a year earlier, with core at 3.2%. The second estimate of third-quarter GDP is forecast near 3.5% annualized against 3.4% before. Jobless claims come a day early, forecast near 200,000. October new-home sales follow at 10 a.m. Markets are pricing a high chance of a quarter-point rate increase at the December Fed meeting; one source puts it near 98%.

Why it matters: A hotter inflation number would add weight to the expected December rate increase, which feeds into credit card, auto and small-business loan rates.

Corporate Nov 24

Dell's quarterly revenue doubles to a record $54.9 billion on AI servers

AI server revenue was $22.0 billion, up 291%, and the order backlog reached $132.3 billion. Dell raised its full-year revenue forecast to $202 billion and authorized a $25 billion share buyback, per reports of the release.

Why it matters: Spending on data centers is still growing fast, a major support for tech-heavy index funds even on days when chip stocks fall.

◆ National

Travel Nov 24 to 25

Near-record Thanksgiving travel meets storms on both coasts

TSA expects about 17.6 million air travelers over Thanksgiving week, slightly below last year, and more than 3 million on Sunday, Nov 29. AAA projects about 81.5 million people traveling 50 miles or more. The FAA forecasts the busiest Thanksgiving airspace in 16 years. A strong storm is forecast for New York City and the Tri-State area on Thursday with 1 to 3 inches of rain, worst from about 1 to 8 p.m.; California issued evacuation warnings ahead of near-record high tides, and storms are possible across Florida and the Gulf Coast through Wednesday.

Why it matters: Delays at Northeast and West Coast hubs ripple to connecting airports like Charlotte. Check flight status before leaving and plan the return trip around Sunday's crowds.

Charlotte Nov 24

Trilon Group plans an East Coast hub in Mecklenburg County with 262 jobs

The Denver-based engineering and consulting firm plans a $3.3 million investment to set up its regional hub in Charlotte, announced by the governor's office. Business North Carolina grouped it with announcements totaling about 700 jobs and $144 million across three North Carolina cities.

Why it matters: Steady professional-services hiring keeps Charlotte's office and housing demand up, and each new firm is another buyer of local services.
