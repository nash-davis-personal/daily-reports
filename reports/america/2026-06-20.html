◆ The economy

Inflation June 17, 2026

Energy prices push the inflation outlook higher

Alongside its June 17 decision to hold the fed funds rate at 3.50%–3.75%, the Fed sharply revised its PCE inflation projection up to 3.6% for the year, from 2.7%, attributing the bulk of the increase to higher energy prices; the 2027 forecast was also raised to 3.3%. New projections show several officials now expect at least one rate hike this year, with the median path implying a year-end rate near 3.8% — a reversal from the cuts the median implied in March. GDP growth was nudged down for 2026 (to 2.2%) while the labor market held roughly steady, with May payrolls up 172,000 and unemployment at 4.3%.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, the message is "higher for longer." Energy is doing the work, so gasoline and utility costs are the squeeze to watch on household budgets, and borrowing costs — mortgages, card rates, business loans — aren't easing soon. Short-term cash and money-market yields stay attractive while the path is up.

◆ Charlotte & North Carolina

Local economy June 2026

Charlotte posts its best business-recruitment year in a decade

Charlotte-metro nonfarm employment rose 2.9% over the past 12 months — nearly 40,000 net new jobs — placing it among the fastest-growing large U.S. metros, ahead of Raleigh, Nashville, Tampa, and Austin. The city reported its strongest recruitment year in a decade, anchored by financial-sector wins: SMBC Group chose Charlotte for its second U.S. headquarters (~2,000 jobs, $50.5M), SoFi is building a regional hub (225 jobs), and AssetMark is establishing an East Coast hub ($10M, 252 jobs). Officials cite a pipeline of more than 7,000 additional jobs and $1.4B in potential investment.

Why it matters: The Charlotte metro's growth keeps outrunning the national slowdown, and the incoming employers are concentrated in finance — more well-paid workers and more vendors relocating to the region. For local small businesses, that's a widening customer base.

◆ Weather & science

Weather Early June 2026

The Southwest heat wave kept strengthening into June

A major heat wave that gripped the southwest half of the country since late March continued to strengthen in early June, according to NASA Earth Observatory satellite observations — already ranked among the most extreme weather events of the century. Sustained extreme heat raises grid demand and energy costs, which feeds back into the same energy-driven inflation picture above.

Why it matters: Prolonged heat strains the power grid and lifts energy prices in affected regions — a direct line to higher cooling bills and the elevated energy costs driving the national inflation revision.
