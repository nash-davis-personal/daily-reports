◆ Markets & the economy

Markets Jun 30, 2026

Stocks close out their best quarter in six years

Equities finished little-changed on June 30 to cap a quarter in which the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posted their strongest three-month run in six years and the Dow its best since 2022. The S&P sits near record highs even against a backdrop of firmer inflation.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, a strong quarter into a data-heavy week means the next few sessions carry outsized weight — a soft jobs number or hot inflation read could set the tone for the second half of the year.

Data Jul 2, 2026

June jobs report pulled forward to Thursday ahead of the holiday

Because Independence Day falls on Friday, the June nonfarm payrolls report is released Thursday, July 2. Estimates cluster around a slowdown from May — roughly 115,000 to 170,000 jobs added depending on the survey — with the unemployment rate expected near 4.3%. The May JOLTS reading is expected to show job openings easing toward ~7.3 million.

Why it matters: The jobs number is the week's main economic signal. A clear slowdown or a surprise strength changes the odds on the Fed's next move, which flows directly into small-business borrowing costs.
Kiplinger — June jobs report preview
Rates Jun 2026

Sticky inflation shifts the rate debate from cuts to holds

Core PCE inflation rose to 3.4% year-over-year in May, the highest since October 2023. The Fed raised its 2026 core inflation projection to about 3.3% and now does not expect a return to its 2% target before 2028 at the earliest. Under Chair Kevin Warsh, markets have moved away from pricing rate cuts, with some now weighing whether the next move could be a hike later in the year.

Why it matters: Higher-for-longer rates keep the cost of loans, credit lines, and financing elevated for households and small businesses. It's an argument for favoring cash-now work over debt-financed expansion.

◆ Weather & public safety

Heat Jun 30, 2026

Dangerous heat wave blankets the Midwest and East

A strong ridge is driving a significant, dangerous heat wave across the Midwest and eastern US, with hazardous heat forecast to persist through at least the weekend, especially in the East, before easing early next week. Extreme heat is, on average, the deadliest weather hazard in the country, tied to roughly 273 deaths a year over the past decade per CDC and NOAA figures. Forecasters are also watching a low in the eastern Pacific that could become a tropical depression midweek.

Why it matters: Heat this widespread is a real health risk — the guidance is to limit midday exertion, hydrate, and check on older neighbors and anyone without reliable air conditioning.

◆ Charlotte & North Carolina

Local Jun 2026

A building boom keeps outrunning the available workforce

Charlotte has roughly $20 billion in construction projects on the horizon but not enough qualified workers to build them, with about 40% of NC counties reporting the same shortage and some areas importing out-of-state crews for basic road and school work. On the jobs-created side, Siemens Energy is expanding in Mecklenburg County as part of a $421 million statewide expansion adding 500 jobs, and Maersk picked Mecklenburg for its North American headquarters, adding about 520 jobs.

Why it matters: The region keeps drawing employers and investment, but the labor bottleneck is real — a constraint that ripples into project timelines, wages, and demand for anyone who serves the trades and the businesses following the growth here.
