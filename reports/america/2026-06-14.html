◆ Markets & the Fed

Markets June 12–13

SpaceX's record debut held a 25% gain; index inclusion adds forced buyers

SpaceX closed its first day of Nasdaq trading up roughly 25% from its $135 IPO price, at $168.70 — about a $1.77 trillion value, the largest IPO in history. More than 25 exchange-traded funds had registered around the listing, over half of them leveraged, and MSCI index inclusion noted June 13 begins a wave of structural buying as passive funds add the stock.

Why it matters: A debut this size that holds its gains validates the AI-and-space infrastructure valuation thesis that other giants — including Anthropic and OpenAI — are leaning on for their own coming listings. For a personal investor, the lesson is the mechanics: index inclusion can move a stock for reasons that have nothing to do with the business, and leveraged ETFs amplify both directions.

The Fed FOMC ◆ June 16–17

The Fed is expected to hold as the Iran war and oil drive the outlook

The FOMC meets June 16–17 and is widely expected to leave rates unchanged. Analysts say day-to-day rate expectations are being driven more by the Middle East conflict and the resulting move in oil than by the underlying data; the 10-year Treasury yield is expected to stay in the 4–4.5% range near term. Where the Fed once penciled in one or two cuts this year, markets now price just one — or none — by December. A Dallas Fed analysis estimates the war could add about 0.6 percentage points to Q4-over-Q4 headline PCE inflation.

Why it matters: Higher-for-longer rates keep borrowing expensive — business loans, equipment financing, mortgages, and credit lines all stay pricey through the summer. For small businesses weighing an investment, the cost of capital isn't easing on schedule, and an oil-driven inflation bump would push any relief further out.

◆ Charlotte & North Carolina

Local 2026

SMBC picks Charlotte for its second US headquarters; the regional pipeline keeps filling

Japanese banking group SMBC chose Charlotte for its second US headquarters, bringing about 2,000 jobs and a $50.5 million investment. AssetMark Financial established an East Coast hub in Charlotte's central business district — a $10 million investment and 252 new jobs — and regional leaders say the area has over 7,000 jobs and $1.4 billion in potential investment in the pipeline. The counterweight: housing and other essentials in NC have been rising faster than wages.

Why it matters: Large financial-services HQs landing in Charlotte pull a long tail of local demand with them — vendors, contractors, professional services, and the small businesses that serve a growing white-collar workforce. It's concrete evidence the local economy keeps expanding even as national rates stay tight.
