◆ Trade and the economy

Breaking Aug 22, 2026

US–Canada trade talks collapsed at the deadline and 50% tariffs took effect

Negotiations ran out of time just before a midnight deadline and the United States imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods. The affected list includes hockey sticks, building materials, liquors and certain categories of clothing. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada will retaliate dollar for dollar, with counter-tariffs taking effect September 8 on steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp, paper and electronics.

Each side attributes the breakdown to the other. Carney described the US demands as uneconomic and unfair and called the tariffs a miscalculation; US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Canada declined to finalize a deal on terms agreed earlier in the week. Building materials and appliances are the categories with the most direct pass-through to US construction and renovation costs, and the September 8 date gives importers on both sides about two weeks of visibility.

Why it matters: Building materials on the US list and appliances on the Canadian list both feed into home construction and renovation pricing. Any small business that imports from or sells into Canada has a two-week window before the second round lands to reprice, reroute, or pull orders forward.

Week ahead Aug 24–28, 2026

PCE Wednesday, Nvidia Wednesday afternoon, Warsh Friday morning

Wednesday carries the July personal consumption expenditures price index, with consensus looking for core PCE at +0.2% month over month and the annual rate holding at 3.3%. Personal income and spending, July durable goods, and the annual revisions to nonfarm payrolls also land during the week. A hotter core print lifts the odds of a September hike; a softer one supports the case that the committee can stay patient. The rates market finished last week pricing roughly 9 basis points of tightening for September and about 23 basis points across the rest of 2026.

Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 after Wednesday's close, with management already guided to about $91 billion in revenue and the call at 5 p.m. Eastern. Then Jackson Hole runs August 27–29 at Jackson Lake Lodge under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy," with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — confirmed in May by a 54–45 Senate vote, the narrowest ever for the job — delivering his first keynote as chair on Friday morning. Warsh has trimmed forward guidance since taking office and has said he wants the speech to frame the big questions rather than move near-term policy.

The nearest alert-grade tripwire is the 30-year Treasury sitting exactly on its 5.25% line without clearing it, with the 10-year one basis point under 4.75%; the yield curve read is steepening at high conviction. ◆ National news

Breaking Aug 20, 2026

All eight aboard a charter flight to a remote Alaska radar site were killed

A twin-engine Cessna 441 Conquest operated by Security Aviation, chartered by the US Army Corps of Engineers, went down around 11:45 a.m. at the Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site airport in western Alaska, roughly 460 miles southwest of Anchorage. Two pilots, two Army Corps of Engineers employees and four contractors were aboard. None survived.

The crash followed a missed approach to the runway in foggy conditions. The NTSB and FAA are investigating, with five NTSB investigators traveling to the site; the wreckage will be documented and moved to a secure facility for examination.

Why it matters: Remote federal facilities in Alaska depend almost entirely on charter aviation into short strips with marginal weather and no alternates nearby. The investigation will look at that dependency as much as at the aircraft.

Trend Aug 21, 2026

Flock license-plate cameras are being destroyed in at least 36 states

NPR reported a surge in physical attacks on Flock Safety automated license plate readers across at least 36 states: cameras stolen, sawed off their poles, driven into, covered in paint and stickers, and shot. Instagram and TikTok carry how-to videos framed as jokes, including instructions for cutting through a metal pole quietly.

The response through official channels is larger than the vandalism. More than 100 cities have deactivated their cameras or canceled contracts outright. Flock's readers log vehicle movements for police departments; critics object to the volume of location data that accumulates on people who are not suspected of anything.

Why it matters: A surveillance technology adopted city by city is now being unwound city by city, and the contract cancellations matter more than the sawed-off poles. Municipalities budgeting for automated enforcement should expect the procurement to become a public argument.

◆ Science & space

Upcoming Launches Aug 30, 2026

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is a week from launch

Roman is scheduled to lift off at 7:26 a.m. Eastern on August 30 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center. It carries a mirror the size of Hubble's and a 300.8-megapixel visible and near-infrared wide-field instrument that images a patch of sky roughly 100 times larger than Hubble's cameras at comparable sharpness. Its destination is the Sun–Earth L2 point, about 930,000 miles out.

The science goals are measuring dark energy's effect on cosmic expansion and structure growth, testing the consistency of general relativity, and finding exoplanets by gravitational microlensing. The telescope is named for NASA's first chief of astronomy, called the "Mother of Hubble" for her role in getting that observatory built.

Why it matters: This is the largest NASA astrophysics launch since JWST, and it flies commercial. A survey instrument this wide changes what the field can ask, because it stops having to choose which small piece of sky to point at.
