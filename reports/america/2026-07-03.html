◆ Economy & markets

Data Jul 2, 2026

June hiring slowed sharply, with earlier months revised lower

The economy added 57,000 jobs in June — roughly half what forecasters expected — and the unemployment rate held at 4.2%. The Bureau of Labor Statistics also revised April and May down by a combined 74,000. Gains continued in health care, social assistance, and professional/business services, while leisure and hospitality shed jobs. Stock futures rose after the release and short-term Treasury yields fell, with the 2-year easing to about 4.14%.

Why it matters: A cooling labor market makes near-term Fed rate hikes less likely — traders took a September move off the table and now look to October at the earliest. For anyone managing their own money or a small business, that points to borrowing costs holding rather than climbing in the immediate term, though hiring softness is worth watching.

Policy Jul 2, 2026

Fed leadership signals inflation is still "too high"

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh told CNBC that prices are "too high" and pushed back on any idea the central bank would tolerate an inflation objective above 2%. At the same time, the softer pace of job growth suggests further rate hikes are unlikely to be necessary this year, per market commentary following the jobs data.

Why it matters: The signal is "higher for longer" on the inflation goal without an imminent hike — rates likely sit near current levels rather than rising soon. That's the backdrop for mortgage, auto, and small-business borrowing costs heading into the second half of the year.

◆ National events & weather

Weather Jul 3–4, 2026

A historic heat dome threatens the hottest Fourth of July on record

More than half the country is trapped under a heat dome through the holiday weekend, with over 300 records expected to fall by Saturday and overnight lows too warm to offer relief. Philadelphia could approach its all-time high near 106°F, and New York City already tied a 100°F record. Cities across the East and Midwest — including parts of North Carolina — activated emergency plans and altered America 250 celebrations. Pop-up severe thunderstorms threaten the Mid-Atlantic on July 4 with damaging winds and flash flooding, while dry, windy conditions fuel wildfires in Utah and southern Colorado.

Why it matters: Extreme heat is a genuine safety issue for holiday travel and outdoor plans, especially in the Carolinas. Expect power-outage risk from storms, and take heat precautions seriously — the danger this weekend is the heat itself more than the storms.

Security Jul 2, 2026

DHS confirms a breach of a federal information-sharing network

The Department of Homeland Security confirmed that hackers breached the Homeland Security Information Network, a platform used to share sensitive information among federal, state, local, and private-sector partners. The confirmation came amid a broader wave of cybersecurity activity and consolidation across the industry.

Why it matters: A breach of a network trusted by state and local agencies underscores that even hardened public systems are targets. For small businesses and individuals, the practical takeaway is the ordinary one — keep software patched, use strong unique logins, and treat unexpected messages with suspicion.
