◆ The economy

Effective today September 8

Canadian counter-tariffs on more than 700 US products came into force

Canada applied counter-tariffs of 15%, 25% and 50% to over 700 US-made products beginning today, covering roughly $27.6 billion in imports from the United States. The targeted categories are steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics. Duties on US steel and aluminum were doubled to 50%, matching the rate the US had already applied to Canadian steel and aluminum.

The measures follow the collapse of negotiations in August, with each government stating the other had negotiated in bad faith. Canada is the largest export market for a long list of US manufacturing states.

Why it matters: American manufacturers and farm-equipment sellers with Canadian customers face an immediate price disadvantage of 15% to 50% at the border. For small businesses on either side, the practical effect shows up in landed cost, contract repricing and cross-border shipping terms rather than in headline trade statistics.

Rates FOMC September 15–16

Futures are pricing a rate hike, not a cut, ahead of next week's meeting

August nonfarm payrolls came in at 162,000 against a consensus near 55,000, and rate expectations moved sharply. Fed funds futures have been pricing roughly even-to-better-than-even odds of a hike at the September 16 decision, with readings across the week ranging from about 50% to 58% depending on the instrument and the hour. Governor Waller has separately signaled willingness to hold if inflation keeps progressing toward 2%.

The Treasury market is positioned the same way: the 10-year sits at 4.78%, above the 4.75% line, and the 3-month bill is at 3.91%. The August CPI report — the last inflation print before the decision — is released Friday, September 11 at 8:30am ET.

The macro backdrop: Regime reads "Sovereign-supply regime, policy book re-armed," direction sideways with medium conviction. The nearest alert-grade wire is the September any-hike probability holding above 50% (52% at last adjudication), alongside the 10-year above 4.75%. The next dated catalysts are CPI on September 11 and the FOMC decision on September 16. — TSD Macroscope board (as of 2026-09-07)
Why it matters: Anyone with a variable-rate business line, a construction draw, or a mortgage decision pending is looking at a policy path that has inverted from what was priced a few months ago. For a personal balance sheet, the concrete items are the reset schedule on any floating-rate debt and the reinvestment rate on short-duration cash, which is currently near 3.9% on 3-month bills.

Markets September 8 close

Stocks slipped as megacap tech led the decline

The S&P 500 closed near 7,707, down 0.15% from the prior session, in a holiday-shortened week. The declines were concentrated in the largest names: Apple fell about 2.55%, Alphabet about 2.10% and Microsoft about 2.05%. Futures had opened mixed with rising crude, Middle East hostilities and the Canadian tariffs all in the frame.

Why it matters: A flat index with sharp megacap losses means the rest of the market carried the tape. For an individual investor holding index funds, the concentration cuts both ways — the same five names that drove the run higher now set the daily direction on the way down.

◆ Energy and the Gulf

Escalation September 5–8

US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers; crude is holding near multi-month highs

US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday, September 5, after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired ballistic missiles toward a US aircraft carrier and a guided-missile destroyer. Both ships evaded the attacks and no American personnel were harmed. The US permanently disabled two crude carriers, the M/T Downy off Kharg Island and the M/T Stark 1 near Jask, and destroyed the M/T Kylo near the Gulf of Oman after directing its crew to abandon the vessel; the Kylo was not carrying oil.

Iran has threatened to declare a restricted zone outside the Strait of Hormuz. Prices reflect the risk without pricing a closure: Brent for November traded near $97.20 and WTI for October near $92.56, after crude rose close to 10% the previous week. Roughly 7 million barrels a day of crude and refined products still transit the strait.

Why it matters: Crude near $97 flows into diesel and gasoline within weeks, and diesel sets the freight cost embedded in nearly every physical good. For a small business, the pass-through shows up in delivery surcharges and supplier price letters before it shows up in the CPI print.

◆ Weather and public safety

Storm September 7–8

Hurricane Lowell passed west of Kauai as a Category 2 and shut the island down

Lowell made its closest approach late Monday, passing just west of Niihau and Kauai as a Category 2 hurricane with 110 mph winds, its center about 90 miles west of Lihue. The center stayed offshore and the island still took the worst of it: wind gusts up to 80 mph, rainfall rates of 1 to 3 inches per hour, and nearly 14 inches of rain in 24 hours at Kilohana. Forecasters warned of flash flooding, landslides, extreme surf and possible tornadoes in Kauai County overnight.

Public schools on Kauai, County of Kauai offices and State of Hawaii offices on the island were closed Tuesday, September 8.

Why it matters: A storm whose eye never made landfall still closed schools and government offices across an entire county. Rainfall totals near 14 inches in a day are the flooding and landslide driver on Hawaiian terrain, independent of the wind category.
