◆ Markets

Breaking July 28

A memory-chip selloff in Asia becomes a question about how the AI buildout is funded

South Korea's Kospi fell more than 10% overnight as SK Hynix dropped over 14% and Samsung Electronics over 13%. The trigger was not weak demand for memory. It was the structure behind the demand: Nvidia's reported roughly $250 billion financing guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio data center has investors describing the arrangement as circular financing, where the chip supplier underwrites its customers' ability to buy the chips. A second worry runs alongside it, that Chinese models are closing the capability gap and weakening the return on record AI capital spending.

US futures split the difference. Nasdaq-100 futures were down 0.8–0.9%, S&P 500 futures were about flat to down 0.1%, and Dow futures were up roughly 0.2–0.3% on lower oil and falling bond yields. The VIX sat near 19.0. SK Hynix reports around 8 p.m. ET, which will be the first hard data point on whether the memory cycle itself is turning.

Why it matters: Semiconductors have carried a large share of index gains this year, so a sustained repricing there moves ordinary retirement balances even for people who own no chip stocks directly. The distinction that matters is between an earnings problem and a financing-structure problem — so far this is the second.

Fed July 28–29

The Fed opens a two-day meeting with an unusually uncertain outcome

The FOMC began its meeting Tuesday with the decision due Wednesday. Traders lean toward a hold at 3.50–3.75%, but conviction has weakened: CME FedWatch showed a 64.2% probability of no change on July 24, down from 87.2% a week earlier on July 17. A hike is not off the table, which is why commentary is describing this as one of the harder calls to handicap in years.

Consumer confidence data was scheduled for 10 a.m. ET today, and the week carries several more releases into Friday.

Why it matters: The path of short-term rates sets the cost of credit-card balances, auto loans, HELOCs and small-business lines. A shift in expectations of this size within ten days moves mortgage and business-loan pricing before the Fed does anything.

Earnings July 28

A broad slate of consumer and industrial names reports today

Visa, Coca-Cola, Boeing, Ford and PayPal all report Tuesday. That spread covers card spending, consumer staples pricing, aerospace manufacturing, autos and digital payments, which together give a wider read on the household than another software quarter would. Big Tech results land later in the week under explicit pressure to show that record AI spending is producing returns.

Why it matters: Visa and PayPal volumes are among the cleanest real-time measures of consumer spending, and Ford's commentary speaks to how much of the tariff and input-cost picture is reaching sticker prices.

◆ Energy and prices

Energy July 27–28

Crude keeps retreating as US–Iran talks continue

Oil extended its decline on continued diplomatic movement, following the pause in strikes reported over the weekend and Oman-mediated talks on Hormuz transit. Falling crude was one of the two things holding Dow futures positive today, the other being lower bond yields.

Why it matters: Retail gasoline follows crude with a lag of roughly one to two weeks, so the relief from last week's spike should reach pumps in early August if the current level holds. Diesel matters more for anyone running trucks or trades routes.
