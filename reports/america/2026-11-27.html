◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 27, pre-open

Stocks reopen for a shortened session after a volatile holiday week, with futures higher

US markets were closed Thursday and close early today at 1 p.m. ET, with bond trading ending at 2 p.m. Early Friday, futures on the Nasdaq-100, S&P 500 and Dow were up 2.38%, 1.74% and 1.30% as of 5:41 a.m. ET. On Wednesday the Dow closed up 0.33% at 50,182.95 after erasing an intraday drop of more than 1,100 points, and the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78. Coverage names rising oil prices, inflation and proposed new tariffs as the weights on the market. Holiday sessions are thin; last year's Black Friday volume was about 47% below the daily average.

Why it matters: Thin volume makes big percentage moves easier and less meaningful. For anyone managing their own money, today's close says little about the trend.

Consumer Nov 25–27

Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion in Black Friday online sales after November ran 9.1% ahead of last year

Adobe reported $82.5 billion in US online spending from Nov 1 to Nov 22, up 9.1% from a year earlier, with every day above $3 billion. It forecasts $12.9 billion for Black Friday (up 9.2%), $15.1 billion for Cyber Monday and $275.1 billion for the full Nov 1 to Dec 31 season (up 6.7%). Buy now, pay later is forecast at a record $21.3 billion for the season. The National Retail Federation's count of in-store and online shoppers comes after the weekend; last year it recorded 202.9 million.

Why it matters: Spending is running ahead of forecast even with inflation above 3%. The heavy use of installment plans is worth watching as a sign of how stretched household budgets are.

Rates this week

Markets still price a December Fed rate hike after October core PCE came in at 3.3%

October PCE inflation was 3.8% and core PCE 3.3% year over year, per the Bureau of Economic Analysis release on Nov 25. The Fed raised its policy range to 3.75% to 4% earlier this month, and prediction-market and futures pricing still puts a quarter-point December hike well above 80%. Oxford Economics said in a Nov 16 note that it expects a 25 basis point hike and thinks markets overprice the odds of a half point.

Why it matters: Another hike would push loan, card and mortgage rates higher into the new year. Small businesses carrying variable-rate debt or planning to borrow in early 2027 face higher costs.

◆ Travel

Travel Nov 27–30

The return trip is the next test for a near-record Thanksgiving travel week

Travel experts usually expect the Sunday after Thanksgiving to be the heaviest air-travel day of the season. The record to beat is 3,133,924 people screened by TSA on Nov 30, 2025. No current national storm warning for the return weekend turned up in today's search; check the National Weather Service and airline alerts before driving or flying.

Why it matters: Anyone flying Sunday should plan for long lines and full flights regardless of weather.
