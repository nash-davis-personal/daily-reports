◆ The economy & the Fed

Rates June 17

Fed holds at 3.50–3.75% and removes its prior outlook for a 2026 rate cut

At its June meeting — the first under new Chair Kevin Warsh — the Federal Reserve kept its policy rate in the 3.50–3.75% range, citing elevated inflation, solid economic activity, and stable unemployment. The closely watched "dot plot" no longer projects a cut this year; instead, 9 of the officials see at least one hike in 2026 and 6 see at least two. Officials tied the firmer stance largely to higher energy prices.

Why it matters: Borrowing stays expensive and may get more so. For a personal-money or small-business lens: variable-rate debt, business lines of credit, and any financing-dependent purchase get pricier, while cash and short-term Treasuries keep paying well. Anyone budgeting around a "rates will fall this year" assumption should re-plan.
CNBC: June Fed decision · U.S. Bank market note
Inflation May data

Consumer prices up 4.2% year over year as core PCE climbs to 3.3%

The May Consumer Price Index showed a 4.2% annual inflation rate, a multiyear high, and the Fed's preferred core PCE gauge rose to 3.3% in April from 3.0% in December 2025. Energy prices were a primary driver of the re-acceleration, which is what shifted the Fed from a cutting bias to a holding-and-watching one.

Why it matters: Higher prices erode purchasing power for households and squeeze small-business margins — input costs, energy, and wages all press upward at once. Businesses that haven't revisited pricing in a year are likely behind; consumers feel it most in energy and everyday goods.

Markets June 17 close

Stocks sit near a record even as the rate outlook turns hawkish

The S&P 500 closed around 7,420 in mid-June, near an all-time high, even as the Fed signaled higher-for-longer rates. The resilience reflects continued corporate strength — particularly the AI and infrastructure buildout — outweighing rate worries for now, though higher rates raise the bar for valuations if earnings cool.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, it's a split-screen market: equities near records but with a stiffer rate backdrop. The neutral takeaway is that diversification and a clear time horizon matter more when "cash is competitive" and stocks are richly priced at the same time. (Not advice — just the lay of the land.)

◆ Weather & the nation

Weather June 21–22

Severe storms and flooding across the Plains and Ohio Valley; summer heat builds South and West

The Weather Prediction Center flagged a moderate risk of excessive rainfall across the Central Plains, Middle Mississippi Valley, and Ohio Valley, with storms capable of large hail, damaging winds, and tornadoes developing along a front from the mid-Mississippi Valley to the central High Plains. At the same time, much-above-average temperatures are forecast for the West Coast, Great Basin, Southwest, Rockies, and the Gulf and Southeast as summer begins — a setup with heavy rain risk in the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic early in the week.

Why it matters: Flash-flood and severe-storm risk through midweek means travel disruption and property risk across the nation's midsection, while early-season heat across the South and West raises power demand and heat-safety concerns. For the Charlotte/NC area, the relevant watch is heat and any heavy-rain bands tracking through the Mid-Atlantic.
