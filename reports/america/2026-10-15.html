◆ Economy and markets

Inflation Oct 14

September CPI runs hot and stocks sell off

Consumer prices rose 0.6% in September and 3.7% from a year earlier, slightly above the 3.6% economists expected. Higher gas and energy prices drove most of it. The S&P 500 fell 1.11% to 7,718.94, the Nasdaq 1.88% to 26,692.87, and the Dow slipped 0.09% to 51,621.48. The Russell 2000 of smaller companies entered a correction (10% below its recent high), and a chip-stock index fell 4.3%.

The 10-year Treasury yield was around 5.27% midday. CME FedWatch showed about 78% odds of the Fed holding at its next meeting, and New York Fed President John Williams said one more hike this year looks appropriate.

Why it matters: Borrowing costs stay high for longer. Anyone carrying a variable-rate loan, a credit line or a credit card balance is paying more, and small-company stocks are the ones taking the hit.

Today Oct 15, 8:30 ET

Producer prices, retail sales and jobless claims print this morning

The Census Bureau releases September retail sales at 8:30 a.m. ET; consensus is around +0.3% after August's 1.2%. BLS releases September PPI at the same time, with headline forecasts of +0.5% to +0.6% month over month. Weekly initial jobless claims are expected around 199,000–200,000 after 197,000. These figures were not out when this brief was filed.

Why it matters: A hot PPI with soft retail sales would mean rising costs and weakening demand at the same time. That's the combination that squeezes small-business margins hardest.

Banking Oct 14

Bank of America misses, absorbing a $1.2B Treasury penalty

Bank of America reported Q3 earnings of $1.08 a share, two cents below estimates. The Treasury Department's $1.2 billion penalty for anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations, announced October 13, cut earnings by $0.17 a share. Investment-banking fees fell 11% and markets revenue 6%, while net interest income rose 8%. Shares closed at $51.39, down 4.76%, after falling nearly 10% intraday. JPMorgan rose about 1.2% the same day.

Why it matters: Bank of America is headquartered uptown and is one of Charlotte's largest employers. A penalty this size usually means more compliance spending and closer scrutiny of costs locally.
Yahoo Finance CNBC
Housing Oct 15

Mortgage rates stay near 7.4–7.5%

The average 30-year fixed conforming rate was 7.449% on October 15, up from 7.388% a day earlier, per Mortgage Research Center data reported by Fortune. Zillow-based figures from U.S. News put it at 7.52% on October 14. Rates track the 10-year Treasury, which rose after the CPI report.

Why it matters: A $350,000 loan at 7.45% costs about $2,435 a month in principal and interest. Buyers and anyone hoping to refinance are still locked out, which keeps pressure on real-estate, lending and home-services businesses.

◆ Industry and national

Manufacturing Oct 14

Japan to invest up to $21B in two New York chip factories

Japan will invest up to $21 billion in two new semiconductor fabs in New York to be run by GlobalFoundries, under the $550 billion US–Japan investment arrangement. The plants will be jointly owned by Japanese interests and the US government, whose stake in GlobalFoundries would rise to about 7%. Renesas and Apple are expected to be early customers, though allocations aren't final.

Why it matters: It's one of the largest single foreign commitments to US chipmaking, and it extends the government's direct equity role in domestic manufacturing. Expect construction and supplier jobs in upstate New York over several years.
Reuters
Charlotte Oct 14

Wegmans opens its first Charlotte-area store

Shoppers lined up as Wegmans opened in Ballantyne, a store of more than 100,000 square feet that adds another major chain to Charlotte's crowded grocery market. Separately, WFAE reports Mecklenburg County expects $414.3 million in sales-tax revenue for the fiscal year that ended in June, below what was budgeted.

Why it matters: Grocery competition in south Charlotte just got tougher for independent and specialty food sellers, and softer county sales-tax revenue is an early sign that local consumer spending is cooling.
