◆ A major fire event in eastern Washington

Breaking August 1–3

Fires around Spokane destroyed hundreds of structures and emptied a large part of the county

The Old Trails fire broke out Saturday and was joined by the Fairview and Autumn Lane fires, together designated the Spokane Complex. As of Sunday the three had burned 7,708 acres at zero containment. The Washington State Emergency Operations Center put structure losses around 700 on Sunday morning; Spokane County Fire District 3 Chief Cody Rohrbach counted 640 destroyed in the Old Trails fire alone. Reporting has settled on at least 600 homes, businesses and other buildings lost.

Roughly 60,000 people were under evacuation orders, with some counts above 65,000, and large parts of the area placed at Level 3 — leave now. A wildfire emergency was declared for the state. No injuries have been reported, which given the speed of the fires and the size of the evacuation is the notable fact. Spokane Mayor Lisa Brown called it the worst natural disaster the region has faced.

Why it matters: A loss of 600-plus structures puts this among the more destructive US wildfire events of the year and will move insurance and rebuilding costs across the inland Northwest. Zero containment on Sunday means the acreage and structure numbers were still preliminary as this was filed.

◆ Markets, oil and the Fed

Markets August 2–3

Brent fell as much as 7.3% after a planned strike was called off and talks were announced

Brent crude for October delivery dropped as much as 7.3% to $81.55 a barrel after the White House said it had agreed to call off a planned attack on Iran at the request of Middle East allies including Saudi Arabia, and that fresh US–Iran talks would begin Monday. Treasuries and gold rose. A further small increase to OPEC+ production quotas added to the decline.

The economic stake is the Strait of Hormuz, which Iranian forces declared closed on March 4 and which normally carries about 27% of the world's seaborne crude and petroleum products. That closure drove US retail gasoline to a monthly average near $4.30 a gallon in April. Analysts have consistently cautioned that pump prices lag crude by months, so a good headline today does not translate into cheaper fuel this week.

Why it matters: Fuel is a direct input cost for the trades and service businesses across the Charlotte metro, and a 7% move in crude on a diplomatic headline shows how much of the current price is risk premium rather than supply. If talks hold, the relief shows up at the pump over months. If they fail, the same premium comes back quickly.

Economy Week of August 3

The Fed is on hold at 3.50–3.75% with July payrolls due Friday

The FOMC left the federal funds target range at 3.50–3.75% at its July 29 meeting, with the statement noting significant progress toward the 2% inflation objective. A cooling PCE price index on July 31 reinforced the read that the tightening cycle is finished, though the market's expected path from here is contested — some of it now prices one to two hikes by year end rather than the cuts expected earlier in 2026.

Unemployment stands at 4.2%, employers are still adding jobs monthly, and weekly initial claims remain low. July non-farm payrolls and the unemployment rate arrive Friday and are the week's main input for Treasury yields.

Why it matters: Borrowing costs for small businesses are effectively frozen at current levels until the labor data moves. For anyone managing their own money, Friday's payroll print is the single number that decides whether the yield curve moves this month.

◆ Public safety & health

Public safety August 1–2

A shooting at a Twin Falls In-N-Out killed three and injured seven

Police responded to multiple 911 calls reporting gunfire at an In-N-Out in Twin Falls, Idaho at about 2:29 p.m. Saturday. Three people were killed, including a restaurant employee, and seven were injured; five remained hospitalized Sunday afternoon, two in critical condition.

Twin Falls police identified the gunman Sunday as Chad Williams, 24, who died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound. Police say they are confident he acted alone and that his family is cooperating. A motive remains under investigation, with the FBI's Salt Lake City field office committing resources to the local department.

Why it matters: The investigation is active and the motive is unestablished, so early accounts of cause should be treated as provisional.

Correction August 3

The Cyclospora outbreak is 1,947 confirmed cases in nine states, not the larger figure in circulation

CDC's investigation page puts the multistate Cyclospora outbreak linked to iceberg lettuce at 1,947 confirmed cases across nine states: Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania and West Virginia. Michigan is the epicenter and is showing signs that new illnesses are slowing after the recall.

The much larger numbers circulating conflate two things. Cyclosporiasis has been reported in 45 states in 2026 overall; the lettuce-linked outbreak is the smaller subset. Taylor Farms de Mexico recalled all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico on July 17, including Marketside-brand products sold at Walmart with best-by dates from July 18 through today, August 3. Yesterday's briefing carried the broader figure and this is the narrower, sourced one.

Why it matters: Recalled product with best-by dates running through today may still be in home refrigerators. Check the bag before the salad.
