◆ Weather & national events

Weather July 7, 2026

A rare moderate flood risk targets the Texas–Louisiana border

The National Weather Service issued flash-flood warnings for northeastern Texas and northwestern Louisiana as a cluster of slow-moving thunderstorms dumped torrential rain across the Ark-La-Tex, with Shreveport, Bossier City and Marshall in the path. The Weather Prediction Center placed the region under a "moderate risk" — level 3 of 4 — for excessive rain, forecasting 3 to 8 inches with rates of 2 to 4 inches per hour and locally up to 5 inches near the coast.

Why it matters: A level-3 excessive-rain risk is uncommon and signals genuine flash-flood danger in populated areas, not routine summer storms. Rainfall rates that high overwhelm urban drainage quickly, so travel and property disruption across the Ark-La-Tex is likely through the afternoon.

Anniversary Week of July 4, 2026

One year on from the deadly Central Texas floods

The current flood threat lands just after the one-year mark of the July 2025 Central Texas floods, which killed more than 130 people — most in Kerr County — when stalled thunderstorms dropped up to 20 inches of rain on drought-hardened ground. Two hard-hit communities are taking different rebuilding paths, and the NWS has released a new report on the event as families continue to seek answers.

Why it matters: The anniversary and the fresh NWS after-action report are shaping how flood-prone regions plan warnings and evacuations. For anyone in flash-flood country, it's a reminder that the deadliest events come from rain rate and terrain, not just storm size.

◆ Markets & the economy

Markets July 6, 2026

The Dow tops 53,000 for a record close as chips lead

U.S. stocks climbed in the first session after the July 4 holiday weekend. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.29% to clear 53,000 and set a new closing record, the S&P 500 gained 0.72% to 7,537, and the Nasdaq added 1.12% to 26,121. Technology, financials and industrials led, with the VanEck Semiconductor ETF up 2.7% at the open. Alphabet — recently added to the Dow — gained 2.45%.

Why it matters: The rally extends a strong first half into Q3 and keeps retirement and brokerage balances near highs. For a self-directed investor, note how concentrated the leadership is in chips and mega-cap tech — index gains are leaning heavily on a narrow set of names.

Data July 7, 2026

Consumers get surveyed on inflation as hiring cools but investment holds

The New York Fed's Survey of Consumer Expectations — a forward-looking read on how households see inflation and their own finances — is due July 7, in an otherwise light data week ahead of the next CPI print. The backdrop: June payrolls rose just 57,000 (below the ~115,000 forecast) with unemployment at 4.2%, yet first-quarter real GDP grew 2.1% annualized and business investment rose more than 10%, driven by new equipment and intellectual property.

Why it matters: The split picture — soft hiring, firm business investment, sticky ~4% inflation — is exactly what the Fed is weighing on rates. Household inflation expectations feed directly into that call, so the survey is a quiet but real input to whether borrowing costs ease later this year.
