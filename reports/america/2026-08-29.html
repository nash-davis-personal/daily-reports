◆ The economy

Fed Aug 28, 2026

Warsh used his first Jackson Hole keynote to say the Fed may need to raise rates

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who succeeded Jerome Powell in May, delivered his first Jackson Hole keynote Friday morning. He said inflation remains higher than the central bank's longstanding 2% goal and indicated the Fed may need to raise interest rates in the coming months. He acknowledged that recent U.S. reports show inflation has cooled somewhat, and said they do not tell him that underlying trends have meaningfully improved. The standard he set: the committee must be confident underlying inflation is moving to the objective clearly and at sufficient speed, and otherwise it has work to do. He also said he was impressed with the overall strength of the economy.

The relevant data behind that: core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose 0.2% in July and 3.3% from a year earlier, the fourth consecutive reading at or near 3.3% after 3.3% in April, 3.4% in May and 3.3% in June. Headline PCE held at 3.7% annually. Markets took the speech hawkishly — the two-year Treasury yield moved from 4.22% to 4.30%, and the odds of a hike at the September meeting increased.

Why it matters: A rate increase would be a reversal of direction rather than a pause, and it feeds straight through to variable-rate small-business credit, equipment financing and anything tied to prime. For anyone managing their own money, the move to watch is the front end of the curve rather than the headline index level.

Markets Aug 28, 2026

Indexes eased on the keynote and still closed the week higher

The S&P 500 fell 0.25% Friday to 7,711.76. The Nasdaq composite fell 0.52% to 26,402.42. The Dow Jones Industrial Average was essentially flat, down 9.45 points or 0.02% to 53,559.99. All three finished the week with gains despite the Friday drift, which is a mild reaction to a speech that moved the front end of the Treasury curve eight basis points.

Why it matters: Equities and rates disagreed on Friday. Rate markets priced a more hawkish Fed while stocks barely flinched, and one of those two readings will be wrong by the September meeting.

Labor Aug 28, 2026

The BLS benchmark revision cut estimated employment through March by 79,000

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released its annual preliminary benchmark revision Friday, estimating that total nonfarm employment for the year through March 2026 was overstated by 79,000, or 0.1%. The revision to total private employment was larger at 178,000, also 0.1%. The revision does not mean jobs were lost in March; it means the level of employment in March was roughly 79,000 lower than the monthly payroll survey had estimated, after BLS compared that survey against more comprehensive employment records. The final benchmark revision gets folded into official estimates with the January 2027 Employment Situation report, published in February 2027.

Why it matters: The revision pushes the other way from Warsh's inflation message: a slightly weaker labor market is an argument against tightening. Both numbers landed on the same morning, and both go into the September decision.

◆ Policy with a concrete effect

Trade Aug 27–28, 2026

Semiconductor tariffs may be extended to laptops, servers and consoles

The administration is preparing a further round of tariffs on imported semiconductors that would extend past raw chips to finished hardware, including data-center servers, laptops and gaming consoles. The mechanism under discussion, championed by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, would tie tariff-free chip import allowances proportionally to each company's committed investment in U.S. semiconductor manufacturing, so a company building or financing domestic production would receive duty-free import rights scaled to its domestic output. Reporting indicates the data-center exemptions granted in January could be scrapped, and that a phase-in period is under discussion.

Technology industry groups have objected, arguing the duties would make it harder for U.S. companies to procure the volume of semiconductors needed for data-center expansion. The proposal remains in development and could change materially before anything is issued.

Why it matters: If finished goods are covered, the cost lands on ordinary business hardware — laptops, workstations and servers — not just on chip importers. Any small business planning a hardware refresh has a reason to check quoted pricing and lead times before a phase-in date is set.

◆ Science and space

Launch Aug 28, 2026

The Roman Space Telescope is cleared for a Sunday morning launch

NASA and SpaceX completed the launch readiness review Friday at Kennedy Space Center, clearing the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope for launch. Liftoff is scheduled for Sunday, August 30, at 7:26 a.m. EDT from Launch Complex 39A aboard a Falcon Heavy. The Space Force's Space Launch Delta 45 Weather Squadron forecasts a 60% chance of favorable conditions.

Roman pairs Hubble-class resolution with a field of view about 100 times larger, which is what makes it a survey instrument rather than a pointing one — it is built to map large areas of sky at high detail, for dark energy, exoplanet and galaxy-formation work. Separately, NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory has resumed operating two of its three science instruments after a commercial mission that would have raised its orbit was scaled back and refocused on demonstrating rendezvous technology for in-space servicing.

Why it matters: Roman is the largest NASA astrophysics launch since Webb, and it is visible from a wide stretch of the Florida coast on Sunday morning. The Swift note is the practical one: on-orbit servicing is still being demonstrated rather than delivered, and aging observatories are managing around that.
