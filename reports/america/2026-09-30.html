◆ The economy

Markets Sept 28–29

Long-term Treasury yields reached their highest levels in nearly two decades as stocks slipped

On Monday the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 9.5 basis points to roughly 5.25%, its highest since 2007. The Dow fell about 347 points to 51,481.51, the S&P 500 fell about 0.8% to 7,683.69, and the Nasdaq dropped more than 0.9% to 26,820.38. Tuesday was calmer: the S&P 500 slipped 0.16% to 7,670.84, while the 10-year edged to about 5.28% and the 30-year reached 5.62%, its highest since 2002.

The bond move is already reaching private markets. Health-tech company Oura postponed its planned IPO, citing bond-market uncertainty.

Why it matters: Anything priced off long rates (mortgages, SBA and equipment loans, commercial real estate) is getting more expensive. Anyone managing their own money should note that the pressure is coming from the bond market while earnings season is still weeks away.

Labor Sept 29

Job openings fell to 7.08 million and consumer confidence dropped to 81.9

The Labor Department's JOLTS report showed openings fell 3.5% to 7.079 million in August, about 146,000 below estimates. Hires rose 0.9%, layoffs and discharges fell 3.6%, and the quit rate held at 1.9%.

The Conference Board's consumer confidence index fell 7.6 points to 81.9 in September, well below the 89.2 forecast. The present-situation reading fell 6.7% and expectations fell 8.6%.

Why it matters: Fewer openings with steady layoffs points to a labor market that is cooling without breaking. Falling confidence tends to show up in discretionary spending, which is what local service businesses sell.

Today Sept 30 – Oct 2

August PCE inflation is out at 8:30 a.m. ET, and the September jobs report follows Friday

Forecasts call for core PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, to rise 0.3% in August after 0.2% in July, lifting the yearly rate to 3.4% from 3.3%. The release also includes the Commerce Department's annual revision of the national accounts. The Fed raised rates a quarter point in September to 3.75%–4.00%, and futures priced about a 72.5% chance of another hike in October. The actual numbers were not out when this briefing was filed.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as sideways, medium conviction: a sovereign-supply regime holding through a rate shock. The alert-grade wire in play is the 10-year above 4.75%, which the yield is now about 50 basis points past, and the board's rates theme reads up, high conviction, in line with this week's tape. The next dated test on the board after this week's data is September core PPI on Oct. 15. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-09-28)

Why it matters: A hot core number would firm up odds of an October hike and keep pressure on long rates. A soft one would give the bond market its first relief in weeks.

Energy Sept 29

Gas is holding near $4.46 a gallon with WTI around $91

AAA's national average for regular was about $4.456, down from $4.47 a week earlier and up from $4.08 a month ago. California is highest at $6.38 and Indiana lowest at $3.86. WTI crude traded near $91 a barrel; Brent quotes varied widely by source.

Why it matters: Fuel is about 38 cents a gallon higher than a month ago, which feeds straight into delivery, trades and field-service costs heading into Q4.

◆ North Carolina

Outer Banks Sept 29–30

NC 12 reopened with one-way alternating traffic; Hatteras stays under a state of emergency

The nor'easter wrecked about 1,000 feet of NC 12 near the Pea Island Visitor Center, and NCDOT puts emergency repairs at about $2.5 million. The road opened northbound Tuesday at 3 p.m., and since 7 a.m. Wednesday an automated flagger has run alternating one-way traffic over a temporary gravel section. NCDOT expects both lanes back by the middle of next week.

The emergency Rodanthe–Stumpy Point ferry keeps running around the clock. Visitors are still barred, and only Priority One and Two residents may enter.

Why it matters: Residents and essential businesses have a road again. Tourism on Hatteras stays closed until Dare County lifts the entry restrictions.

◆ Science & space

Launch Sept 28

Starship reached orbit on its first attempt and deployed 26 Starlink satellites

Flight 14 launched from Starbase, Texas on Monday morning. The Super Heavy booster made its planned offshore splashdown, and the ship reached orbit and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites. After an engine on the ship shut down early during ascent, controllers cut the mission from nearly 10 hours to about 3 hours, and the ship splashed down in the North Pacific after a controlled deorbit burn.

Why it matters: Reaching orbit with a real payload is the gate for Starship's heavy-lift and lunar plans, and for a large step up in Starlink capacity.
