◆ Economy and markets

MarketsOct. 6

Yields and oil rise together; major indexes close lower

The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.041% in the morning on Oct. 6, its highest since July 2007, and was near 4.995% in the afternoon. The 2-year was about 4.66% and the 30-year about 5.36%. Brent for November delivery settled up 2.9% at $108.75 and WTI rose 4.38% to $105.83 on fears that Middle East supply disruptions will last. The Dow fell 328.09 points (0.63%) to 52,093.11, the S&P 500 fell 34.25 (0.45%) to 7,585.73, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 204.84 (0.78%) to 25,981.57. Figures are from one wire summary of the day.

Why it matters: Higher yields feed straight into borrowing costs, including mortgage and small-business loan rates. For a personal investor, cash and short-term Treasuries pay more than they have in nearly two decades, and long-duration holdings are the ones that move most on these days.

JobsOct. 2–3

September payrolls: 29,000 jobs, unemployment 4.2%

U.S. employers added 29,000 jobs in September, down from a revised 133,000 in August, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2% from 4.1%. The weak report eased worries that a hot economy would force the Fed to raise rates at its next meeting, which had been priced at roughly 90% in mid-September. Stocks finished the week mixed.

Why it matters: Slower hiring and high rates land hardest on small employers. Lenders price off the same yields, so expect borrowing to stay expensive.

HousingOct. 1–2

30-year mortgage rate climbs to 7.28%, sixth straight weekly rise

The benchmark 30-year fixed-rate mortgage rose to 7.28% from 7.03% a week earlier, the biggest weekly jump in several years and the sixth consecutive increase. Reporting calls it nearly a three-year high.

Why it matters: A buyer on a $400,000 loan pays noticeably more each month than six weeks ago. Housing activity usually slows when rates rise this quickly.

◆ Weather

WeatherSeason outlook

NOAA expects a below-normal Atlantic hurricane season

NOAA's final outlook calls for 7 to 13 named storms, 2 to 6 hurricanes and 0 to 2 major hurricanes, with El Niño suppressing activity. The northern and northeastern Gulf Coast and the Carolinas are among the areas most at risk of a direct hit. This is the seasonal outlook, not a current-storm forecast; check the National Hurricane Center for live conditions.

Why it matters: Below-normal is not zero. Late-season storms still hit the Carolinas, so confirm flood coverage and business-continuity plans before the season ends on Nov. 30.
