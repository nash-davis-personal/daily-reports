◆ The economy

Rates Sept 3

Waller signaled a September hold and the market repriced by 13 points

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said Thursday that "if this continues in the data due over the next two weeks, I would be inclined to support holding the target for the federal funds rate at its current setting." Fed funds futures odds of a hike at this month's meeting fell to 50.4% from 63.2% the previous day, per the CME FedWatch tool. Waller left a hike explicitly on the table if inflation comes in hot, which is why the market moved to a coin flip rather than to a settled hold.

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield fell to roughly 4.75% immediately after the remarks and was last quoted around 4.77%, off Wednesday's 4.804% close, the highest since November 2023.

Why it matters: The policy question this month is whether the Fed raises, not whether it cuts. A coin-flip meeting means borrowing costs for anyone financing a house, a truck, or a small-business line of credit stay where they are for now, and the two inflation prints Waller named are what decides it.

Markets Sept 3

Stocks took the best session in a month on the rate-hold read

The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 624.16 points, or 1.18%, to 53,686.11, its best day since August 4. The S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71 and the Nasdaq Composite added 1.4% to 26,584.06. The move tracked the yield retreat rather than any single earnings print — Broadcom fell about 2.7% despite reporting AI semiconductor revenue up 221% year over year.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the day is a reminder of what is actually driving this tape. Equity direction is being set by rate expectations, not by results; a strong quarter did not save Broadcom, and a two-clause sentence from one governor lifted the whole index.

Data Sept 3

Services activity beat, service-sector employment kept contracting, input prices hit a four-year high

The August ISM Services PMI came in at 55.4 against a 54.3 forecast, up from 54.1 in July. Business activity rose to 61.7 and new orders to 60.9. The employment index was 47.8, below the 50 line for a second consecutive month, and the prices index climbed to 72.6, its highest in four years. Initial jobless claims were 206,000 against a 205,000 forecast, with continuing claims at 1.779 million.

Those three components point in different directions at once: demand for services is expanding briskly, the firms meeting that demand are still not adding staff, and what they pay for inputs is rising fastest in four years.

Why it matters: This is the combination that makes the Fed's September call hard, and it is the one small-business owners feel directly — orders holding up while costs rise and hiring stays frozen. A prices index at 72.6 means margin pressure well before it shows up in a consumer inflation print.

Ahead Sept 4, 8:30 a.m. ET

The August jobs report lands this morning

Consensus is for nonfarm payrolls to rise about 53,000 to 56,000 in August after a decline of 23,000 in July, with individual estimates spanning a further loss of 25,000 to a gain of 121,000. The unemployment rate is expected to hold at 4.1%, near a one-year low. Average hourly earnings are expected up 0.3% on the month and 3.0% year over year, which would be the weakest annual wage growth since May 2021.

The wide forecast range is the tell. Forecasters do not agree on whether the labor market is stalling or merely slow, and this print is the last major labor read before the Fed meets.

Why it matters: Two weeks of inflation data and this jobs number are the inputs Waller named. A weak print pushes the hike odds down and takes pressure off borrowing costs; a hot wage number does the opposite. The macro backdrop: TSD's internal board reads the regime as sideways with medium conviction — "sovereign-supply regime, Fed book coming off" — with its nearest alert-grade wire the 10-year closing above 4.75%, which it did at 4.77%, and a September hike probability sitting right on the 45% line that would falsify its high-conviction rates call; its dated catalysts are resolved through September 2, leaving this morning's payrolls as the next scheduled test. ◆ Energy and shipping

Energy Sept 3

Crude held near five-week highs with Hormuz traffic sharply reduced

Brent traded around $95.26 a barrel on September 3 and West Texas Intermediate near $90.84, holding close to five-week highs. Reporting attributed the risk premium to renewed exchanges of fire between the United States and Iran and to disruption at the Strait of Hormuz, where only four ships transited following a deadly attack on a Saudi tanker that was blamed on Iran.

Why it matters: Fuel is a direct input for every trades business, delivery fleet and mobile-service operator, and a $90-plus barrel that is held up by shipping risk rather than by demand can unwind fast in either direction. It is also the clearest live link between an overseas conflict and a US small-business cost line.

◆ Weather and public safety

Storm Sept 3

Hurricane Lowell reached Category 4 south of Hawaii

As of the 11:00 a.m. HST advisory on September 3, Hurricane Lowell was about 465 miles south-southwest of Hilo with maximum sustained winds of 140 mph, a Category 4 on the Saffir-Simpson scale. It was moving west near 10 mph, with the forward speed expected to slow before a turn toward the northwest and north over the weekend. The National Hurricane Center expects Lowell to remain a major hurricane through the weekend and says it should stay well south of Hawaii through Saturday, while swells spreading north will bring life-threatening surf and rip currents to the islands for several days. Hurricanes Karina and Marie were also active in the Eastern Pacific.

Why it matters: The immediate, near-certain hazard for Hawaii is surf and rip currents rather than landfall, and the track turn this weekend decides whether that changes. Anyone with travel or property in the islands should be following NHC advisories directly rather than headlines.
