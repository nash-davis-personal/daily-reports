◆ Weather and wildfire

Breaking August 1–2

A heat dome from Arizona to Montana brought record temperatures and pushed fire danger higher

A ridge of high pressure held hot air near the ground across a band stretching from Arizona to Montana this weekend. The National Weather Service forecast 115°F in Las Vegas on Saturday and 114°F Sunday, 116°F in Phoenix, and a record 118°F in El Centro, California. The northern edge was just as unusual for its latitude: Great Falls, Montana was forecast at 104°F and Helena at 102°F, matching or passing records.

Most of Montana sat under a red flag warning with fires already burning, and the Weather Service flagged enhanced wildfire risk across most of Southern California away from the coast through Sunday. Scattered thunderstorms brought a separate flash-flood risk from the Rockies into the Great Basin and the desert Southwest, which is the pattern that turns burn scars into debris flows.

Why it matters: Heat at these levels is the deadliest weather hazard in the United States by a wide margin, and it arrives alongside the highest electricity demand of the year. The combination of record heat, dry fuel and thunderstorms without much rain is the setup that produces the large late-summer fires.
Washington Times / AP AccuWeather

◆ Public health

Breaking August 1

The Cyclospora outbreak passed 18,000 cases and 41 states, traced to shredded iceberg lettuce

Federal and state health officials have been investigating a fast-growing Cyclospora outbreak since May 1. The count now stands at more than 18,000 confirmed or suspected cases across at least 41 states, with 423 hospitalizations. CDC traced the source to shredded iceberg lettuce grown in central Mexico. Cyclospora is an intestinal parasite that causes prolonged watery diarrhea, and symptoms can take up to two weeks to appear after exposure, which is what makes it so hard to trace.

Michigan is the epicenter, with 9,680 cases spread across 70 of the state's 82 counties. Wayne County has reported 1,036, Washtenaw more than 900, Oakland 710 and Ingham 698. NPR followed the county nurses doing the actual detective work, which comes down to collecting grocery receipts, pulling food-delivery app histories, and conducting long interviews to reconstruct what people ate two weeks ago.

Why it matters: This is one of the largest foodborne parasite outbreaks on record in the US, and the delayed onset means the case count reported today reflects exposures from two weeks ago. Anyone with prolonged diarrhea after mid-June should mention the outbreak to a doctor, since Cyclospora requires a specific antibiotic and will not clear on its own.
NPR Detroit News

◆ Infrastructure and security

Breaking August 1

The water-utility intrusions now span at least seven states and may be wider

Following the federal warnings issued to water systems last week, reporting now describes intrusions at utilities in at least seven states, with officials saying the actual footprint may extend further. Some of the incidents caused operational effects at the plants involved, including pressure loss and localized flooding. Investigators have attributed the activity to actors linked to Iran; the underlying facts about which systems were reached remain under review.

Water utilities are a persistent soft target because the control systems are old, the operators are small, and many plants have a handful of staff covering an entire county.

Why it matters: Drinking water is the most locally-run piece of critical infrastructure in the country, which means there is no single agency that can patch it. If your municipal system posts a boil advisory or a pressure notice in the coming weeks, it is worth reading the notice rather than assuming it is routine maintenance.
Techmeme — New York Times, August 1

◆ Markets, through a personal-investor lens

Breaking July 31

The most visible AI hedge fund lost 67% in a single month and sold its stock book to Citadel

Leopold Aschenbrenner's Situational Awareness told investors its portfolio fell roughly 67% during July on losses in AI stocks. The firm unwound most of its public equity positions, selling the bulk of the book to Citadel, and wrote to investors that it came closer to permanent capital impairment than it considered acceptable. Its main remaining asset is a private stake in Anthropic valued around $5 billion.

The arithmetic underneath is worth sitting with. The fund had returned 439% through June and is still up roughly 80% for 2026 even after the collapse. A position can be enormously profitable on paper and still nearly kill you on the way through, which is what leverage plus concentration does.

Why it matters: The AI thesis being right and an AI portfolio surviving are two different questions. For anyone managing their own money, this is a clean case study in position sizing: the underlying bet performed, and the structure around it almost failed anyway.
Reuters via US News Seeking Alpha
Credit July 29 – August 1

CoreWeave had to pay up to place a $2.6 billion loan as AI debt investors pushed back

CoreWeave sweetened terms on a $2.6 billion leveraged loan funding GPU purchases for customers including Anthropic, Jane Street and Hudson River Trading. Pricing moved to 5.5 percentage points over the benchmark, as much as 1.25 points above initial talk, with the paper offered at a discount of 96 to 97 cents on the dollar. The cost of insuring CoreWeave's debt against default over five years rose more than 50% during July to its highest level since December. At least four AI-adjacent borrowers had to improve terms to get deals done.

Why it matters: Equity markets argue about AI valuations in public; credit markets settle it in basis points. Lenders charging more to finance datacenter buildouts is the first hard number showing the financing side of the AI boom tightening, and financing costs eventually show up in what compute costs everyone else.
Bloomberg Yahoo Finance
