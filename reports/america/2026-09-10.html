◆ The economy

Policy September 9

Treasury bought back up to $6 billion of long-dated debt, triple the expected size

Treasury conducted the first operation under an expanded liquidity-support buyback program, targeting 10-year and 20-year bonds at a maximum size of $6 billion. Investors had been guided to $2 billion. The department said future operations will be at least $4 billion. The expansion follows an August 19 announcement that operations would "at least double."

The stated purpose is keeping the government-debt market liquid. It is also read as an effort to slow the rise in borrowing costs, which have reached levels not seen since before the 2008 financial crisis. The market's answer on the day was unambiguous: long-dated yields rose as much as 5 basis points before easing, and the 10-year finished at 4.857%.

Why it matters: Buybacks are the government's tool for smoothing demand at the long end of the curve. When the tool is tripled in size and yields rise anyway, the constraint is the amount of debt being issued rather than the plumbing that absorbs it. For anyone with a mortgage, a business loan or a bond allocation, the long end is currently setting its own price.

Markets September 9

Stocks fell a third day as the 10-year hit a three-year high

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 405.41 points, or 0.77%, to 52,380.66. The S&P 500 lost 0.48% to 7,636.36 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.64% to 26,253.34 — the third consecutive decline for all three. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.857%, its highest since November 2023. Brent crude closed above $101 a barrel with WTI around $96.05, extending a run driven by supply disruption tied to the conflict in the Gulf.

The combination is the whole story: an energy shock feeding into inflation expectations, a curve repricing upward, and equities giving ground to both. Under the index averages the tape was worse than it looked — the equal-weighted S&P fell roughly twice as far as the cap-weighted version, which means the typical stock had a considerably worse day than the headline suggests.

Why it matters: A 10-year near 4.86% reprices everything anchored to it — mortgages, small-business credit lines, auto loans, and the discount rate on any long-dated asset. For someone managing their own money, this is the third session in a row where cash and short bills have been paid to wait.

Fed Ahead of September 16

A quarter-point hike is now the market's base case for next week

Market-implied odds of a 25 basis point increase at the September 16 FOMC meeting sit near 57%. The target range is 3.50%–3.75%, held there at the July 28–29 meeting on a 9–3 vote with three members dissenting in favor of a hike. Two things moved expectations since: energy costs held up by supply disruption tied to the Gulf conflict, and investor doubt about how much inflation the committee will tolerate after July's hold.

August CPI is released Friday, September 11 at 8:30 a.m. ET — the last major inflation reading before the decision.

The macro backdrop: TSD's internal read has the regime as sovereign-supply with absorption held by the plumbing, direction sideways at medium conviction. The nearest alert-grade wire is rates: the 10-year is through its 4.75% line, and the long end sits about 2bp under 5.30% on both the 20- and 30-year, the tightest unfired domestic marker on the board. Next dated catalyst is Friday's August CPI. TSD Macroscope board (as of 2026-09-09).
Why it matters: A hike into an energy-driven price shock is a different decision from a hike into demand-driven inflation, and the committee was already split in July. Whatever CPI shows Friday will be read straight into next Wednesday.

◆ Public safety

Investigation September 9

Flight data indicate the Miami cargo jet tried to abort its landing

The NTSB investigation into the September 6 crash of an Amazon-contracted Boeing 767-300 freighter at Miami International Airport has recovered both recorders, and flight data indicate the crew applied full power seconds before the aircraft overran the runway — consistent with an attempted go-around from an unsteady landing. The jet, inbound from San Juan, came to rest roughly a quarter-mile past the paved surface after striking a van carrying seven aircraft-cleaning workers on a service road and an SUV outside the airport fence.

Five people in the vehicles were killed and five were injured. The crash closed all four runways and caused a roughly three-hour ground stop. Investigators say they are examining mechanical failure, environmental conditions and crew performance without having ruled any of them out.

Why it matters: Everyone killed was on the ground, in vehicles on an airport service road. That places the safety question at the boundary between runway operations and ground traffic rather than in the cockpit alone, and it is the kind of finding that reaches every large cargo airport in the country.

◆ Science & space

Delay Late September target

Crew-13 slipped after an oxidizer leak on Dragon

NASA and SpaceX moved the Crew-13 launch date to address an oxidizer leak found in Dragon's propulsion system during standard prelaunch processing. The mission is now targeting late September from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral. Crew-13 carries commander Jessica Watkins and pilot Luke Delaney of NASA with mission specialists Joshua Kutryk of the Canadian Space Agency and Sergey Teteryatnikov of Roscosmos, the 13th crew rotation flight under the Commercial Crew Program.

Why it matters: A leak caught on the pad during routine processing is the system working. The schedule cost is real, though, and crew rotations chain into station staffing and research timelines for months afterward.
