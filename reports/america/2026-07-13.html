◆ Markets & the economy

Earnings Week of Jul 13

Big banks kick off earnings season into a rate-hike setup

Monday itself carried no major data or earnings, but it opens a consequential week. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citigroup, and Wells Fargo all report quarterly results starting midweek, giving the first broad read on lending, trading, and credit health. Semiconductor names led an early pullback: SK Hynix's US-listed shares fell about 8% after Friday's record Nasdaq debut, with Micron off roughly 5% and SanDisk about 6%. Index futures were mixed to slightly higher.

Why it matters: Bank results are a live gauge of how households and small businesses are holding up under higher rates. For anyone managing their own money, the bank commentary on loan demand and consumer credit is often more useful than the headline earnings-per-share numbers.

Fed Data + testimony Jul 14

June CPI and Warsh testimony land Tuesday with a hike on the table

June's Consumer Price Index publishes Tuesday, July 14, alongside congressional testimony from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Futures pricing implies roughly a 61% chance of a September rate hike, and Fed officials at their last meeting signaled they intend to address persistent inflation this year with one more increase. That is the reverse of the rate-cut narrative that dominated earlier cycles, so the CPI print will carry outsized weight for how firmly a hike gets priced in.

Why it matters: A higher-for-longer or higher-still rate path keeps borrowing expensive for small businesses and consumers — variable-rate credit lines, card balances, and financing costs stay elevated. Tuesday's number is the one to watch this week for anyone with floating-rate debt or a cash cushion earning yield.

◆ Weather & the nation

Severe weather Jul 12 → Jul 13

A slow storm system soaks the Carolinas with flood watches

Rounds of showers and thunderstorms moved across the Carolinas Sunday into Monday, with the National Weather Service warning of damaging winds, hail, torrential downpours, and isolated flash flooding, and an outside chance of tornadoes. The worst of Sunday's cells crossed the Charlotte metro west-to-east in the late afternoon; a severe thunderstorm warning for Lancaster County, SC flagged 60 mph gusts and quarter-size hail. Heavy rain remained possible across the Charlotte region through Monday, keeping the flooding threat elevated.

Why it matters: Localized flash flooding and wind damage are the concrete risk through Monday across the Charlotte/Gastonia area — a direct hit to Nash's home turf. Storm cleanup, roofing, and water-damage restoration demand typically spikes in the days after a system like this.
