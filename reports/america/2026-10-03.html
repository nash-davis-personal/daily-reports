◆ The economy

Markets Oct 2

Stocks closed higher after the jobs report while Treasury yields reversed and rose

The S&P 500 rose 56.27 points, or 0.73%, to 7,722.72. The Dow gained 250.40 points, or 0.49%, to 51,176.96, and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.19% to 27,190.86. The Nasdaq finished the week higher. The S&P 500 and Dow posted weekly losses. Nvidia hit an intraday record of about $237.88, putting its market value near $5.7 trillion.

The bond market moved the other way by the close. The 10-year Treasury yield, which dropped to about 5.19% after the 8:30 a.m. report of 29,000 jobs added, ended up about 4 basis points at 5.28%. The 2-year rose about 5 basis points to 4.84% and the 30-year finished near 5.63%. CME FedWatch put the odds of an October rate increase at 16%, down from 64% a week earlier. On Thursday, Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said the risks to his inflation forecast are tilted upward, citing energy prices and stronger-than-expected demand. The Fed raised its target range to 3.75%–4% last month.

Why it matters: Mortgage, car-loan and business-loan rates follow the 10-year more closely than the Fed's own rate. Friday showed that a weak jobs number lowered the odds of another Fed increase without bringing long-term borrowing costs down.

Energy Oct 2

The G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel from emergency stocks

After a G7 video conference on Friday, member countries agreed to a coordinated release through the International Energy Agency of 100 million barrels, beginning immediately and running over four months, with a substantial diesel release in the first 20 days. They also agreed to coordinate refinery maintenance, raise refinery output where possible, and refrain from energy export restrictions. The move follows eight months of conflict with Iran that has pushed fuel prices higher.

AAA put the U.S. national average for diesel at $6.37 a gallon on Friday. The record is $6.52, set Sept. 22. U.S. crude for November delivery fell 1.9% to $91.11 a barrel on the day, while Brent ended slightly higher, near $102.

Why it matters: Diesel moves freight, farm equipment and work trucks, so its price shows up in delivery fees and the cost of goods. The release is front-loaded on diesel. Whether pump prices fall depends on how much reaches the U.S. market and how the conflict develops.

Small business Effective Oct 1

New SBA rules make loans to buy a business harder to qualify for

The Small Business Administration's updated lending manual, SOP 50 10 8.1, applies to 7(a) loans assigned a loan number on or after Oct. 1. The largest changes hit change-of-ownership loans, used to buy a business or buy out a partner. For most such deals, minimum debt-service coverage rises from 1.15x to 1.25x, and lenders can no longer count projected cash flow to meet it. Lenders need three full years of financial statements, every deal gets an independent valuation, total debt is capped at the appraised business value, and purchases of $3 million or more require a quality-of-earnings report. Buyers should expect to put in at least 10% of project costs, with new limits on using seller notes toward that. A selling owner can now stay on for a transition of up to 24 months, up from 12.

Why it matters: Owners planning to sell a small business to a buyer financed with an SBA loan now need stronger historical earnings to support the price. Clean books for the last three years carry more weight, and some deals that worked under the old ratio will need a lower price or more cash down.

Corporate Oct 1 – 2

Tesla beat delivery estimates, Nike announced cost cuts, and onsemi raised its Synaptics offer to all cash

Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in the third quarter, down 2.1% from a year earlier and above the consensus of about 462,000. Its shares rose about 4% Friday. Nike reported quarterly revenue down 4% to $11.2 billion, forecast a high-single-digit sales decline for the fiscal year, and announced a restructuring targeting $2.5 billion in savings through fiscal 2031, including job cuts. Its shares fell close to 10% in after-hours trading Thursday. Chipmaker onsemi revised its agreement to buy Synaptics to an all-cash deal of about $5.7 billion, or $123 a share, after Synaptics weighed a competing bid.

◆ Around the country

Weather Sept 30 – Oct 2

Remnants of two Pacific hurricanes set rain records and flooded parts of Texas and the Plains

Moisture from the remnants of Hurricanes Polo and Rachel, pulled north by a dip in the jet stream, fed a slow-moving storm system across the middle of the country. Lincoln, Nebraska, recorded 6.15 inches in a day, breaking a record from 1910, and Omaha set a daily record of 4.74 inches. About 6 inches fell in two hours near Dodge City, Kansas, where forecasters issued a flash flood emergency Thursday. At least 15 tornadoes were reported in central Kansas on Wednesday. Dallas, Austin, San Antonio, Waco and the Texas Hill Country were in Friday's highest flash-flood risk, and rivers in Iowa, Nebraska and Kansas were running at moderate to major flood levels. Fox Weather put the population under flood threats at more than 50 million.

Why it matters: Rivers stay high for days after the rain stops. People in flood-prone parts of Texas, Kansas, Nebraska, Iowa and Missouri should watch local river forecasts and avoid driving through water on roads.

North Carolina Oct 3 – 4

Heavy rain will end Charlotte's early-October heat, with a cool week behind it

Charlotte reached the upper 80s Friday, with some spots near 90. Showers and storms develop Saturday afternoon, and steady, sometimes heavy rain continues through most of Sunday. Most of the region is forecast to get 1.5 to 2.5 inches, with more possible south and east of the city, and is under a level 2 of 4 risk of excessive rainfall. Highs drop into the 60s and 70s on Monday, and the week is expected to stay dry.

Why it matters: Sunday is a washout for outdoor work and events across the metro, and low-lying roads and creeks can flood in heavier bands. The dry, cooler stretch after it is better for outdoor jobs.
