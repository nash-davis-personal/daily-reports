Data ahead Week of Jul 13

June inflation report lands this week with the Fed on hold

The June Consumer Price Index is on this week's calendar. May's headline came in at 4.2% year over year — the largest 12-month rise since 2023 — and forecasts put June near 3.8%, which would be a step down. The Federal Reserve is holding its target range at 3.50%–3.75% after the June meeting, and under new Chair Kevin Warsh has shifted toward pure data-dependence, dropping traditional forward guidance. A softer June print keeps a later-year rate cut in the conversation; a sticky one pushes it out.

Why it matters: Borrowing costs for small businesses and consumers hinge on this data. A cooler inflation number nudges the odds of cheaper credit later in the year; a hot one keeps rates where they are. For a small operator, it's the difference between financing getting easier or staying tight into the fall.

◆ Weather & public safety

Weather Jul 11–13, 2026

A large storm system brings severe weather into the Carolinas this weekend

Forecasters are tracking one of the biggest storm systems of the year, delivering repeated rounds of heavy rain, hail, damaging wind gusts and flash flooding from the Great Plains to the mid-Atlantic coast. The severe-weather focus shifts a bit farther south on Sunday than Saturday, stretching from Louisiana through Mississippi and Alabama and eastward into much of Georgia and the Carolinas. Lightning is flagged as the most common hazard for anyone outdoors, and it does not take a severe storm to produce a dangerous strike.

Why it matters: This one is local. The Charlotte–Gastonia area sits in Sunday's severe-weather zone, so expect possible flash flooding, wind damage and power interruptions into early next week. Worth a quick check that anything outdoors — and any client site or hardware that depends on local power — can ride out a short outage.
