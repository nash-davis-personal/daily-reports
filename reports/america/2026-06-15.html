◆ The economy & the Fed

Data May CPI

Inflation ticked back up just before the rate decision

The Consumer Price Index rose 0.5% in May on a seasonally adjusted basis and 4.2% over the prior 12 months, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics — a reacceleration from the spring, with elevated oil prices a contributing factor. First-quarter GDP grew at a 2.0% annual rate and unemployment sits near 4.1%, so the picture is steady growth alongside firmer-than-hoped prices.

Why it matters: Hotter inflation makes near-term rate cuts harder to justify. For anyone carrying variable-rate debt or planning a financed purchase, "higher for longer" is the base case heading into Wednesday.

This week Jun 16–17

The FOMC is expected to hold at 3.50–3.75%

The Federal Open Market Committee meets Tuesday and Wednesday. With inflation firming and oil elevated, markets broadly expect the Fed to keep its target range at 3.50–3.75% and price in just one cut, or none, by December. The statement and the updated projections will set the tone for borrowing costs into the back half of the year.

Why it matters: The decision sets the cost of mortgages, business loans, and credit lines. A hold plus a hawkish projection would keep financing expensive for small businesses and homebuyers alike.

◆ Markets & the AI economy

Policy Jun 12

For the first time, the government pulled a live AI product offline

The Commerce Department issued an export-control restriction barring access to Anthropic's newly released Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models "by any foreign national," citing national security. Unable to separate foreign nationals from other users in real time, Anthropic disabled both models for every customer worldwide; as of June 15 they remain offline with no restore date. It is the first time an export-control order has been used to take down a live, publicly deployed AI model — reported here as a factual policy event.

Why it matters: It establishes that the government can switch off a deployed commercial AI service. Any US business that depends on a single AI vendor now has a concrete reason to keep a backup provider.

Markets Jun 15

SpaceX holds its debut premium; Nvidia returns to the bond market

SpaceX (SPCX) is trading around $168.70 — about a 25% premium to its $135 IPO price — with Nasdaq-100 inclusion expected around July 7, which would trigger forced buying from index-tracking funds. Separately, Nvidia launched its first corporate bond sale since 2021, targeting $20B and drawing roughly $85B in orders, a sign the AI infrastructure boom is now being financed with debt as well as cash.

Why it matters: Two of the market's biggest 2026 stories — a record IPO and the AI capex cycle — are both running hot, which keeps index-heavy retirement accounts concentrated in a handful of names. Worth knowing what your own portfolio actually tracks.
