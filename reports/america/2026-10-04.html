◆ The economy

Corporate Oct 2 – 3

Paramount Skydance will become "Skydance" and move to the NYSE, as global M&A activity slowed sharply in the third quarter

Paramount Skydance CEO David Ellison confirmed Friday that the media company formed by Paramount's merger with Warner Bros. Discovery will rename itself Skydance Corporation, after the production company Ellison founded in 2006. The combined company plans to move its stock from the Nasdaq to the New York Stock Exchange when the deal closes around Oct. 6, changing its ticker from PSKY to SKYD. Separately, data on global dealmaking showed mergers and acquisitions totaled $993 billion in the third quarter, down 41% from the second quarter and the first quarter below $1 trillion since mid-2025. Ten deals topped $10 billion in the quarter, the fewest megadeals since late 2024.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as a sovereign-supply regime holding through a rate shock, sideways at medium conviction, though its latest status line logs the direction as down at low conviction. High-yield credit spreads were the nearest alert line at Friday's close, 324 basis points, a fifth straight widening session, 24bp through the board's 300bp line and 39bp from the level that would reverse that read. The feed lists no dated catalyst after the Sept. 30 inflation report, and this weekend's deal and merger news doesn't contradict that read. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-10-02)

Why it matters: A 41% drop in deal volume is a sign that corporate buyers and sellers are waiting out higher borrowing costs rather than pricing deals at today's rates. For index-fund holders, widening credit spreads are a quieter warning sign than stock prices, since they show lenders demanding more to fund weaker-rated companies.

Energy Oct 3 – 4

Diesel prices have barely moved as the G7's emergency oil release gets underway

AAA's national average for diesel stood at $6.34 a gallon Sunday, regular gasoline at $4.37, little changed from Friday and still close to the Sept. 22 record of $6.52. The G7's 100-million-barrel release agreed Friday is only beginning to move, and analysts quoted over the weekend said it will take weeks for added barrels to reach U.S. refineries and pumps. Brent crude closed Friday near $102 a barrel and WTI near $91, with prices still up more than 8% for September as the U.S. and Iran continue trading military strikes around the Strait of Hormuz, now in an eighth month of conflict.

Why it matters: For small businesses that run delivery vehicles or pay freight surcharges, diesel near $6.34 means costs stay elevated for at least a few more weeks regardless of Friday's G7 announcement. Budget for current prices, not for a quick drop.

◆ Around the country

Public safety Sept 30 – Oct 3

Tennessee's prisons chief resigned and all 2026 executions were paused after a botched lethal injection

Christa Pike was given two doses of pentobarbital at Nashville's Riverbend Maximum Security Institution on the evening of Sept. 30, but remained alive, audibly breathing and snoring, well after witnesses expected the drugs to have taken effect; the curtain was closed and witnesses were eventually escorted out before she was pronounced dead or alive. She was transported to a Nashville hospital, where her attorneys say she remains critically ill, unconscious and on a ventilator. Gov. Bill Lee paused the state's remaining scheduled 2026 executions and ordered an independent review led by former U.S. Attorney Ed Stanton. On Oct. 3, Tennessee Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada announced his resignation, effective at the end of the month, saying it would let the review proceed without distraction. It was the second Tennessee execution this year to go wrong at Riverbend.

Why it matters: This is a factual update on a state execution procedure and personnel change, not a comment on capital punishment policy. Tennessee has paused further executions pending the outcome of the independent review.

Public safety Oct 3 – 4

The Coast Guard searched a second day for a medical jet that vanished off Nantucket with six aboard

A Gulfstream G-100 air ambulance operated by Canada's Latitude Air Ambulance departed Bermuda just after midnight Saturday bound for Boston, carrying four crew members and two patients/family. As it approached the New England coast, the pilot reported electrical failures and told air traffic control he was "coming in blind"; the aircraft lost thousands of feet of altitude in minutes and disappeared from radar around 1 a.m. The FAA issued a search alert at 1:20 a.m., and Coast Guard aircraft, a 47-foot motor lifeboat from Station Brant Point and the cutter William Sparling searched more than 1,600 square miles of water off Nantucket. The Coast Guard confirmed finding a debris field Saturday night but reported no survivors as of Sunday morning, with a press briefing scheduled for 11 a.m. Sunday in Boston. No cause has been determined.

Why it matters: A factual, ongoing search-and-rescue story with no cause confirmed yet; treat early speculation about what caused the electrical failure as unconfirmed until the Coast Guard or NTSB says otherwise.

Weather Oct 3 – 4

A Gulf Coast storm system pushed heavy rain and flood risk into Georgia and the Carolinas

Deep tropical moisture moving north from the Gulf combined with an approaching cold front to bring rounds of heavy rain from Louisiana and Mississippi through Alabama and Georgia over the weekend. By Sunday the heaviest rain and flood threat shifted east into Georgia and the Carolinas, with the National Weather Service's Weather Prediction Center flagging parts of the region for a slight risk of excessive rainfall. Widespread totals of 2 to 3 inches were expected across the Carolinas, with isolated higher amounts, on top of the rain and flooding that soaked Texas, Nebraska, Kansas and Iowa earlier in the week.

Why it matters: This is the same storm pattern that flooded the Plains earlier in the week, now reaching the Southeast. Anyone in Georgia or the Carolinas with outdoor work scheduled Sunday into Monday should plan around rain and localized flooding.

North Carolina Oct 3 – 4

Charlotte's weekend rain brought a sharp, cooler turn after last week's heat

After highs near 90 on Friday, Charlotte cooled into the 60s and 70s this weekend as scattered showers Saturday gave way to steadier, sometimes moderate-to-heavy rain Sunday into Sunday night. Forecasters put widespread rainfall totals at 1 to 3 inches through the end of the weekend, with the higher amounts expected south and east of the city, and said any heavier bursts of rain could cause localized, minor flooding. Skies were expected to clear Monday morning with a return to sunshine and highs in the 70s by Monday afternoon.

Why it matters: Outdoor jobs and errands around Charlotte are a wash through Sunday evening; low-lying streets and creeks can flood in the heavier bands. Monday looks clear for anything that needed to be rescheduled.
