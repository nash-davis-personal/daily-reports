◆ The consumer, according to the biggest reader of it

Earnings Aug 20, 2026

Walmart's Q2 beat on digital, and it raised the full-year outlook

Walmart reported fiscal Q2 2027 results for the 13 weeks ended July 31 before Thursday's open. Total revenue rose 5.9%, or 5.1% in constant currency. Walmart U.S. comparable sales excluding fuel rose 2.6%, held back by a 125 basis point drag from Maximum Fair Pricing legislation in pharmacy; the company said comps were steady with prior periods once that is set aside. Sam's Club comps rose 4.4% excluding fuel and Walmart International net sales rose 7.9% in constant currency.

The growth is concentrated in the non-retail businesses. Global eCommerce rose 23% and now represents about 23% of Walmart U.S. sales; Walmart U.S. eCommerce rose 24%, Sam's Club U.S. 26%, International 19%. Store-fulfilled delivery rose 40% and marketplace net sales rose more than 50%, with nearly half the marketplace business flowing through Walmart's fulfillment services. Global advertising rose 38%, with Walmart Connect in the U.S. up 43% excluding VIZIO, and global membership fee revenue rose 17%. Operating cash flow was $19.7 billion and free cash flow $5.5 billion.

Reported operating income rose 28.8%. Adjusted operating income grew about 17% in constant currency, and Walmart disclosed that figure included a 750 basis point net benefit from tariff refunds — the company received nearly $2.9 billion in IEEPA refunds during the quarter and said it put that money into price, delivering thousands of rollbacks. Excluding the refund benefit, underlying operating income growth was at the top end of its 7-10% guidance. The company raised its sales and operating income growth guidance for the full year.

Why it matters: Walmart is the broadest single read on U.S. household spending, and the read is that volume is holding while shoppers stay value-focused. The composition matters more than the headline: advertising, membership and marketplace fees are growing several times faster than store sales, which is the same margin migration playing out across retail. The tariff refund is a one-quarter item, so the durable number to carry forward is the underlying 7-10% operating income growth, not the 28.8%.

◆ Rates and the bond market

Fed Aug 19, 2026

The July minutes showed a committee where the argument was about hiking, not cutting

Minutes from the July FOMC meeting, released Wednesday at 2 p.m. ET, detailed the most divided policy vote in years. The committee held rates 9-3, with Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis' Neel Kashkari and Dallas' Lorie Logan each preferring a quarter-point increase. The hawkish view extended past the dissenters: participants said policy tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did not decline, and some judged that current financial conditions might not be tight enough to return inflation to 2%. Kansas City's Jeffrey Schmid and St. Louis' Alberto Musalem, neither of whom held a vote in July, subsequently indicated they would have supported an increase.

The minutes also recorded a discussion opened by Chair Kevin Warsh about reducing the FOMC calendar from eight meetings a year to six, on the reasoning that more information would accumulate between meetings.

Why it matters: For anyone with a variable-rate business line, a construction loan or a mortgage decision pending, the distribution of outcomes shifted. A committee where five officials are on record favoring a hike is not one about to deliver near-term relief on borrowing costs, and the "higher for longer" assumption should be the planning baseline until the inflation data changes it.

Markets Aug 19, 2026

Treasury doubled its long-bond buybacks and the 30-year backed off a 19-year high

The Treasury Department said it will at least double buybacks of long-dated government debt covering 10-year to 30-year maturities, with the program running September 9 through November 4. Bonds rallied on the news. The 10-year yield fell 5 basis points to 4.65% and the 30-year fell 9 basis points to 5.19%, after touching its highest level since 2007 earlier in the week. Equities followed: the Dow rose 0.22% to 53,463, the S&P 500 rose 0.21% to 7,708 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.16% to 26,331, ending a three-day losing streak. Bitcoin rose 11.49% to $71,862.

Why it matters: The long end of the curve has been the pressure point all week, and this is a supply-side intervention rather than a change in monetary policy. It relieves the symptom without changing the rate outlook the minutes described. For a personal portfolio, the practical read is that long-duration bond prices just got a technical bid with a fixed end date of November 4.

◆ Science and the region

Science Aug 19, 2026

Moderna's melanoma vaccine hit its Phase 3 endpoints and the stock rose 176%

Moderna announced positive Phase 3 results for its melanoma vaccine developed with Merck, meeting both primary and secondary efficacy goals. The company described it as the first positive Phase 3 readout for an mRNA-based cancer therapy. Moderna shares rose 176% to their highest level since 2023 and Merck rose roughly 11%, lifting the healthcare sector.

Why it matters: A successful Phase 3 for a personalized mRNA cancer vaccine is a genuine scientific milestone independent of the share move, and it validates a platform that has so far only delivered at scale in infectious disease. Regulatory review and manufacturing scale-up still stand between this result and patients.
