◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 20

The S&P 500 and Dow close in correction after a broad AI selloff

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, the Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73. For the week, the S&P lost 8.6%, the Dow 8% and the Nasdaq about 10%.

Reports cited doubts about AI spending, profit-taking after the Anthropic IPO, and expectations of higher interest rates. Investors moved toward defensive names such as Walmart and Costco.

Why it matters: Anyone with retirement or brokerage accounts likely saw a sharp drop last week. A correction is a 10% decline from a recent high, and the Nasdaq-100 briefly crossed the 20% bear market line during Friday's session.

The Fed Nov 18 to 20

Fed officials point toward a rate hike at the Dec 8 to 9 meeting

Minutes released Nov 18 showed the October decision to hold at 3.75% to 4.00% passed 10 to 2. Governor Waller called for "aggressive action" on inflation, and Governor Cook warned about falling behind the curve. CME FedWatch showed roughly a 10% chance of a half-point move.

Why it matters: A hike would raise costs on credit cards, business lines of credit and other floating-rate debt. The November CPI report arrives Dec 10, one day after the Fed decision.
Investing.com weekly recap
Data Nov 10 to 20

Inflation runs hot while consumers keep spending and factories cool

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October after 0.6% in September, and core prices rose 0.3%. BLS did not publish most 12-month changes because October 2025 data was never collected during the shutdown.

October retail sales rose 0.2% and core sales rose 0.6%. Industrial production fell 0.4%. Initial jobless claims were 203,000, continuing claims were 1.763 million, and Michigan consumer sentiment was 51.5.

Why it matters: Households are still spending despite very low confidence, and hiring remains slow. Small businesses should expect customers to compare prices closely this holiday season.

◆ Nation and science

Travel Nov 24 to 30

AAA expects 81.5 million Thanksgiving travelers

The forecast is down 0.1% from last year, with 72.9 million people driving. The national gas average was $3.81 on Nov 16, up from $3.07 a year earlier, and domestic round-trip airfare is averaging $800, up 17%.

Why it matters: Roads and airports will still be crowded from Tuesday through the following Monday. If gas holds near this level, it would be the most expensive Thanksgiving Day on record, above the $3.58 set in 2022.
