◆ Weather

Storm September 26

A slow-moving nor'easter flooded the coast and cut power to over 100,000 homes

A persistent nor'easter crept along the Northeast coast Saturday, killing one New York City Housing Authority employee struck by a falling tree in Brooklyn. More than 100,000 customers lost power across New Jersey, Connecticut, New York and Pennsylvania at the peak, with roughly 88,000 still out by early afternoon. About 425 flights were canceled and 3,016 delayed, with Boston Logan especially hard hit and most flights to Martha's Vineyard and Nantucket grounded. Coastal flooding and beach erosion hit Ocean City, Maryland, and forecasters warned tens of millions remained in the storm's path into Sunday.

Why it matters: Flooding and outages from Maine to Maryland are a real supply-chain and delivery headache this week for any business shipping through or flying out of the corridor — build in a buffer if you're relying on East Coast carriers or travel through Sunday.

Storm September 26–27

Hurricane Nolo is forecast to strengthen into a major hurricane as it moves away from Hawaii

Nolo sat about 165 miles south-southwest of South Point as of Saturday evening, moving west at roughly 7-9 mph and expected to strengthen to a major hurricane by Sunday. All tropical storm and hurricane watches for Hawaii have been discontinued, though high wind warnings remain for parts of the Big Island and Maui County (gusts to 75 mph) and large, damaging surf continues along Big Island coastlines.

Why it matters: The track is moving away from the islands, so this is a Hawaii travel and surf-safety story, not a US mainland weather risk.

◆ Washington

Media access September 26

The White House left CNN off Air Force One for a Saturday trip to Knoxville

CNN had been slated to travel in the press pool for President Trump's Saturday trip to a college football game at Neyland Stadium, but a schedule released Friday night omitted the network without naming a replacement; conservative outlet Real America's Voice ultimately filled the seat. It's the latest move in an ongoing access dispute between the administration and several news organizations, which last week saw a federal judge strike down an earlier ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico from White House grounds.

Why it matters: This is a press-access dispute over pool seats, not a policy change — the practical effect is limited to which outlets travel with the president, not anything that touches individuals or businesses.

◆ The week ahead

Preview September 30

A revised PCE inflation report and Starship's first orbital try headline next week

The Bureau of Economic Analysis releases August PCE inflation Wednesday at 8:30am ET, alongside a methodology revision reaching back to 2021 that could shave a few tenths off the reported 12-month rate. Markets were closed for the weekend after Friday's gains extended a weekly winning streak for the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Separately, SpaceX targets Monday morning for Starship's first attempt at reaching orbit.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as sideways, medium conviction, holding through a rate shock — the 10-year closed Friday at 5.17%, above the board's 4.75% wire, and credit spreads have widened to 280bp, within 20bp of the board's stress line. The nearest dated catalyst is Wednesday's PCE release, which the board flags as the adjudicator for whether inflation is actually cooling. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-09-25)

Why it matters: Financing costs are still elevated — the 30-year mortgage sits above 7% for the first time in 20 months — so anything you're pricing that depends on borrowed money should assume rates stay high through at least Wednesday's report.
