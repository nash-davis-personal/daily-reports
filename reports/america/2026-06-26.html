◆ Markets: a split-screen session

Markets Jun 25, 2026

Micron's record quarter lifts the chip complex as megacaps slide

Micron reported record fiscal Q3 revenue of $41.5B with gross margins near 85% and a strong August-quarter guide, sending the stock up about 16% and pulling memory and chip-equipment peers higher: Sandisk gained 22%, Applied Materials 13%, Western Digital 5%. The same session saw the megacap technology leaders fall — Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, and Meta all closed lower — after Apple and Microsoft announced price increases on iPhone and Xbox. The opposing moves left the major indexes close to flat.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, this was a textbook rotation day — capital moving out of the crowded megacap names and into the memory suppliers benefiting most directly from AI demand. A near-flat S&P can hide large moves underneath it; the index level alone told you almost nothing about what happened on June 25.

Consumer Jun 25, 2026

Apple and Microsoft raise prices on iPhone and Xbox

Apple and Microsoft each announced consumer price increases — Apple on iPhone, Microsoft on Xbox — moves investors read as a hit to demand and a sign of cost pressure, which helped drag the megacap group lower on the day. The increases land while the Fed's latest projections put headline inflation for 2026 at 3.6%, still above its 2% goal.

Why it matters: These are two of the most widely owned consumer brands in America, so the increases touch household budgets directly and feed the broader story that prices are still climbing faster than the Fed wants. For a small business, it's a reminder that input and device costs keep drifting up — worth factoring into any 2026 equipment or pricing plans.

◆ Disaster response & weather

National Jun 24–25, 2026

US deploys aid after major earthquakes strike Venezuela

Two powerful earthquakes — magnitude 7.5 and 7.2 — struck Venezuela on June 24, with a reported death toll above 188 and more than 1,500 injured. The US is deploying search-and-rescue teams, medical resources, and humanitarian assistance, and the United Nations is coordinating urban search-and-rescue. A brief tsunami advisory was issued for Puerto Rico before being lifted.

Why it matters: The direct US connection is the federal humanitarian deployment and the short-lived tsunami advisory for the US territory of Puerto Rico, which passed without major damage. Large international disasters near US waters routinely pull on American emergency-response and military logistics resources.
