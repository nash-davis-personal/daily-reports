◆ The economy

Rates September 24

The 30-year mortgage rate averaged 7.03%, its first time over 7% in 20 months

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the 30-year fixed at 7.03%, up from 6.95% the week before and 6.30% a year ago. It was the fifth straight weekly increase. The 15-year fixed rose to 6.42% from 6.26%.

Why it matters: On a $400,000 loan, the jump from 6.30% to 7.03% adds roughly $190 a month in principal and interest. Home-related businesses (movers, remodelers, stagers, mortgage brokers) typically see demand soften as rates climb, while repair-and-stay spending can hold up.
Freddie Mac via GlobeNewswire — Mortgage rates average 7.03%
Markets September 24–25

Yields hit new highs, and stocks ended Thursday roughly where they began

The S&P 500 slipped 1.90 points to 7,704.13, the Dow fell 161.61 (0.3%) to 51,349.98, and the Nasdaq rose 3.34 to 26,939.37 after a couple of intraday reversals. The S&P is up 12.5% for the year and the Nasdaq 15.9%.

CNBC reported the 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.225% late Thursday, highest since 2007, and the 30-year 5.502%, highest since June 2004. The official Treasury close for the 10-year was 5.18%. Yields eased a few basis points early Friday as oil slipped on hopes for US–Iran talks. Before Friday's open, the Dow was headed for a fourth straight weekly loss.

Fed context: the Fed raised rates to 3.75–4.00% on September 16. This week New York Fed President John Williams called another hike by year-end "reasonable," and Governor Michael Barr said further adjustments are likely.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, a 10-year above 5% means Treasuries and high-yield savings now pay more than they have in nearly two decades. The flip side is pressure on bond prices already held and on rate-sensitive stocks. A flat index on Thursday hid a weaker average stock.

Jobs September 24

Jobless claims fell to 197,000

Initial claims for the week ending September 19 fell 1,000 to 197,000, below the 204,000 forecast. The prior week was revised up to 198,000, and the four-week average fell to 202,250. Bloomberg notes claims are close to historic lows.

Why it matters: Employers still aren't cutting. A tight labor market keeps hiring expensive for small businesses and gives the Fed room to keep raising rates.
Yahoo Finance — Initial jobless claims Bloomberg
Earnings September 24

Costco beat estimates, with a lift from tariff refunds

Fiscal fourth-quarter net sales rose 11.2% to $93.9B; comparable sales rose 9.4% (6.7% excluding gas and currency). Net income was $2.998B and EPS $6.75 against a $6.52 consensus. The filing says $0.15 a share came from one-time IEEPA tariff refunds. Membership fees rose 7.3% to $1.85B, with an 89.8% worldwide renewal rate.

Why it matters: Costco's filing is a public example of IEEPA tariff refunds reaching the bottom line. Any small importer who paid those duties should confirm with a customs broker whether it has a refund claim.

◆ Weather & science

Weather September 25

Hurricane Nolo is strengthening south of Hawaii's Big Island

Nolo became a Category 1 hurricane Thursday night about 240 miles south of the Big Island, moving north at 5 mph. It's forecast to become a major hurricane this weekend with its closest pass Saturday; landfall isn't expected. The Big Island is under a hurricane watch and tropical storm warning, Maui under a tropical storm watch. Rain could reach 35 inches on Big Island slopes and 15 on Maui.

Gov. Josh Green declared an emergency, his third storm-related declaration this month, and activated more than 230 National Guard members. Schools are closed for the rest of the week. It could be the strongest storm to pass this close to the Big Island since 1959.

Why it matters: Flash flooding and landslides are the main risk. Travelers with Hawaii plans this weekend should expect disruptions.

Science September 24

NASA selected the PRIMA far-infrared telescope as its first Probe Explorer

PRIMA is capped at $1.2B, excluding launch, with a 1.8-meter mirror. It targets a 2033 launch and a five-year mission to study how planets, stars and black holes form, and where Earth's water came from.

Why it matters: It opens a new mid-priced mission class between small Explorers and flagship observatories, which means more frequent science launches at lower cost.
