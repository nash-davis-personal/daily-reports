◆ Weather & the holiday

National Jul 4

Severe storms and record heat disrupt Fourth of July events

Extreme heat and humidity fueled a severe-storm threat across the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast on July 4. Crowds around the National Mall were ordered to evacuate as a line of storms approached; the Mall reopened at 9:45 p.m. and evening remarks and fireworks went ahead after the weather passed. Philadelphia neared 100°F with a heat index toward 105°F, Amtrak ran reduced speeds through the afternoon, and a Pennsylvania man died July 2 after heat strain while doing yard work.

Why it matters: Record heat strained power grids and holiday travel across the eastern US, and severe storms briefly disrupted major public events. The heat dome's effects extend into the weekend for millions.

◆ Economy & markets

Jobs Jun data

June payrolls rose just 57,000 as the labor force shrank

Nonfarm payrolls rose 57,000 in June, below the ~115,000 consensus and down from a revised 129,000 in May — the weakest monthly gain since 2024. The unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, but largely because labor-force participation dropped 0.3 point to 61.5%, its lowest since March 2021. Average hourly earnings were solid but not accelerating.

Why it matters: Slower hiring with steady wages eases pressure on the Fed. Short-term Treasury yields fell after the report and traders took a September rate hike off the table, though futures still leave an October move on the table. The next FOMC meeting is July 28–29.

◆ Science & space

Space Jul 5

NASA's Dragonfly readies for a launch to Saturn's moon Titan

A SpaceX Falcon Heavy is set to launch NASA's Dragonfly mission — a nuclear-powered rotorcraft built by Johns Hopkins APL that will fly between sites on Saturn's moon Titan. The 20-day launch window opens July 5. Separately, SpaceX launched about 1,600 satellites in the first half of 2026 and continues near-daily Starlink flights.

Why it matters: Dragonfly is one of NASA's flagship science missions of the decade, aimed at studying prebiotic chemistry on an ocean world. A successful launch kicks off a multi-year cruise to Titan.
