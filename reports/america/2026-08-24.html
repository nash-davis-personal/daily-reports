◆ Energy, prices and the Fed's week

Today Aug 24, 2026

Treasury holds a 2 p.m. press conference on the Iran sanctions package

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is scheduled to detail the package at the Treasury Department at 2 p.m. Eastern. He has described the design publicly in advance: secondary sanctions reaching any counterparty that continues to move Iranian money or oil, with the stated aim of cutting off revenue to Iran's military and its proxies, and explicit pressure on other governments to choose a side. Tehran has publicly dismissed the threat of intensified economic pressure. The secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council has said Iran would move to halt oil traffic through the Strait of Hormuz if neighbouring countries assist the effort.

The domestic effect is at the pump. AAA put the national average for a gallon of regular at $4.09 on Sunday, about 37.5% higher than before the war began. Crude fell Monday morning ahead of the announcement, with WTI down roughly 1.62% to $85.65 and Brent down roughly 1.38% to $93.09. Retail gasoline follows crude with a lag of two to three weeks, so a one-day move in either direction does not reach the station this week.

Why it matters: Roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil moves through the Strait of Hormuz, so the enforcement question and the shipping question are the same question. For a household or a small business, the practical read is that fuel costs are set by what happens to tanker traffic over the next several weeks rather than by today's headline, and anything quoting a fixed fuel or delivery surcharge should be written to be revisited.

Calendar Aug 26–28, 2026

PCE and Nvidia on Wednesday, Warsh at Jackson Hole on Friday

Wednesday carries July PCE and the second estimate of Q2 GDP in the morning, then Nvidia's quarterly results at 4:20 p.m. Eastern. Consensus expects core PCE at +0.2% month over month, which would leave the annual rate unchanged at 3.3%. The Jackson Hole symposium runs Thursday through Saturday, with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivering his first keynote as chair on Friday morning. Friday also brings the annual benchmark revision to nonfarm payrolls through March 2026, which restates the level of employment rather than adding a new month.

Two of these are unusually load-bearing. The benchmark revision has moved the reported job count by hundreds of thousands in recent years, and Nvidia's numbers are the market's read on whether the AI capital-expenditure cycle is still accelerating.

Why it matters: A single Wednesday morning delivers the inflation reading and the growth reading, and a single Friday delivers the labour restatement and the chair's framing of all of it. For anyone managing their own money, this is a week where the information arrives in two dense blocks rather than continuously.
The macro backdrop: TSD's internal read has the regime at a late-cycle plateau with the Fed on hold — direction sideways, conviction medium. The nearest tripwire is the 30-year Treasury sitting exactly on 5.25% without clearing it, with the 10-year at 4.74% against a 4.75% line; the next dated marker is today's 4 p.m. official par close. ◆ North Carolina

Local Aug 31, 2026

Nippon Electric Glass closes its Shelby plant next Monday, ending 282 jobs

Nippon Electric Glass shuts its Shelby facility in Cleveland County on August 31, cutting 282 positions, with WARN paperwork filed with the North Carolina Department of Commerce. The company is withdrawing from glass-fibre production in North America as part of a wider restructuring. Separately, Family Dollar's Matthews distribution centre eliminated 373 positions in phased cuts that concluded in mid-August, with the company citing margin pressure and supply-chain costs. Regional job losses across the Charlotte metro total more than 1,600 so far in 2026, concentrated in manufacturing and distribution.

The other half of the picture runs the opposite direction. Nonfarm employment in the Charlotte metro rose 2.9% over the past twelve months, adding close to 40,000 net new jobs, which keeps the region among the fastest-growing large metros in the country.

Why it matters: Both numbers are true at once, and they describe different places. The growth is concentrated in the urban core and in services; the losses are in older manufacturing and distribution in the outer counties. Cleveland and Gaston County towns absorb a plant closure differently than Charlotte absorbs 40,000 new service jobs.

◆ Weather and the courts

Weather Aug 22–24, 2026

Tropical Storm Moke is dumping rain on a Big Island still draining from Lala

Moke is tracking west-northwest and staying 200 to 250 miles south of the Hawaiian islands, so no landfall is forecast. The National Hurricane Center warned late Saturday that rain bands could still drop up to 15 inches on the Big Island and 3 to 6 inches on eastern Maui, with life-threatening flooding and mudslides possible on windward and southeast slopes. As of Monday morning the storm was near 15.2N 155.0W, about 310 miles south of Hilo, carrying maximum sustained winds of 45 mph and moving west at 12 mph. It is forecast to weaken to a remnant low by Wednesday.

The compounding factor is timing. Hurricane Lala, since downgraded, dropped more than 30 inches of rain on parts of Hawaii in a short window without making landfall either. Saturated ground and cleared debris channels make the second storm's rainfall more dangerous than the same total would be in isolation.

Why it matters: Two non-landfalling systems in quick succession have produced the flooding risk that a direct hit usually delivers, which is a reminder that rainfall totals rather than wind category drive most inland damage.

Courts Aug 21–22, 2026

A federal judge vacated the 75-country immigrant visa suspension

Judge Jeannette Vargas of the Southern District of New York struck down the policy under which the United States stopped issuing immigrant visas to citizens of 75 countries on the grounds that applicants were likely to need public assistance. The ruling holds that the policy exceeded the Secretary of State's statutory authority and conflicted with the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952, which bars discrimination on the basis of nationality in the issuance of immigrant visas, because it replaced Congress's individualised system with a blanket nationality-based refusal.

The concrete effect: the policy is vacated, and visa denials issued solely on its basis are set aside. The decision was entered August 21 and reported August 22.

Why it matters: Immigrant visa processing for nationals of those 75 countries reverts to individualised adjudication under the existing statute unless the ruling is stayed or overturned on appeal.
