◆ The economy

Data July 30

Second-quarter GDP slowed to 1.5% while June inflation fell to 3.7%

Gross domestic product grew at a 1.5% annual rate in the second quarter, down from 2.1% in the first and below the 1.8% economists had forecast. Consumer spending was the bright spot in the report. Separately, the personal consumption expenditures price index fell 0.1% in June, pulling the annual rate down to 3.7% from 4.1% in May. Excluding food and energy, the annual core rate was 3.3%.

Both numbers land two days after the FOMC held the federal funds rate at 3.50–3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, with three regional bank presidents dissenting in favor of a quarter-point increase. Fed Chair Warsh said policymakers remain committed to restoring price stability and have no tolerance for persistently elevated inflation.

Why it matters: Slower growth alongside falling inflation is the combination that argues against the September rate increase the three dissenters wanted. For anyone carrying variable-rate debt or planning to borrow, the near-term path is less likely to get more expensive than it looked on Wednesday. Core at 3.3% is still well above the Fed's 2% target, so the case for cuts is not there either.
U.S. News U.S. Bank — Fed decision detail
Data July 30

Jobless claims stayed near historic lows

Initial claims for unemployment benefits rose by 9,000 to 197,000 for the week ended July 25, from a revised 188,000. Economists had expected 200,000. The prior week's original print of 187,000 was the lowest reading since September 1969 before the small upward revision. Continuing claims fell 7,000 to 1,782,000, below the 1.8 million expected and the lowest in over a month. The four-week moving average dropped 5,000 to 202,750.

Why it matters: A labor market this tight is why the Fed can hold rates without a recession scare, and it is also why wage costs stay sticky for anyone hiring. For a small business owner, hiring remains expensive and competitive; for anyone employed, job security is about as good as the data gets.
Quartz Reuters via Investing.com
Markets July 30–31

Stocks rebounded hard on Big Tech results, and the hyperscalers committed to three quarters of a trillion in capex

The Dow rose 613.92 points, or 1.2%, to 52,208.06 on July 30. The S&P 500 gained 1.7% to 7,437.64 and the Nasdaq Composite added 2.8% to 25,122.18, reversing the previous session's post-Fed selloff. Microsoft rose about 15% and Amazon about 10% on their results, while Apple fell about 7% on softer China and Services revenue. Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Alphabet together now guide to $720–745 billion of capital spending in 2026. Futures pointed higher again Friday morning, with the University of Michigan consumer sentiment reading due at 10:00 a.m. Eastern.

Why it matters: An index this concentrated moves on a handful of earnings reports, which is worth knowing if your retirement or brokerage account sits in a broad-market fund — you own more of that AI capex bet than the label suggests. The capex number itself is the thing to watch: three quarters of a trillion dollars of spending has to earn a return eventually, and that expectation is what the market is pricing.

◆ Energy and trade

Energy July 30

Gas reached $4.09 a gallon as Strait of Hormuz traffic slowly recovered

AAA's national average for regular gasoline stood at $4.09 on July 30 with crude in the $80 range. Oil actually fell that day: Brent settled down $1.42, or 1.56%, at $89.32, and WTI down $1.14, or 1.35%, at $83.32, as traders weighed talks between Oman and Iran over reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Shipping data from Kpler showed 14 commodity vessels transiting the strait in both directions on Wednesday, up from single-digit daily crossings the week before, and Qatar sent its first LNG cargo back through the waterway. Washington and Tehran continued to exchange strikes on military targets over the same period.

Why it matters: This is the largest direct cost increase most households are absorbing right now, and it flows into everything that moves on a truck. It is also the main reason headline inflation could reverse: the June PCE improvement came with energy still elevated, and a renewed disruption at Hormuz would show up at the pump within weeks. Anyone running vehicles for a living should be pricing fuel at current levels rather than last quarter's.
AAA Newsroom Reuters via Yahoo Finance
Policy Effective July 31

A 100% tariff on patented pharmaceuticals takes effect today for 17 named companies

Under a Section 232 proclamation, an additional 100% duty applies as of July 31, 2026 to patented pharmaceutical articles — drugs under a valid, unexpired U.S. patent listed in the FDA's Orange Book or Purple Book — along with the active pharmaceutical ingredients and key starting materials used to make them. Annex I of the proclamation lists the covered products by tariff schedule number. Today's start date applies to the 17 large companies named in Annex III, under an accelerated 120-day window. For every other importer, the same tariffs take effect September 29, 2026.

Why it matters: The stated goal is to move drug manufacturing onshore; the near-term mechanical effect is a cost increase on branded imported medicines and the ingredients that go into them. Generics outside the patented category are not covered by this action. Watch list prices and insurer formularies over the next two quarters, and note the September 29 date if you deal with any smaller importer in the supply chain.
Crowell & Moring client alert Arnall Golden Gregory
