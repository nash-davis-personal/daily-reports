◆ The economy

Inflation September 10

Producer prices rose 0.4% in August, 5.4% over the year

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said the Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.4% in August, seasonally adjusted. Goods prices climbed 1.1% and services 0.1%. More than three-quarters of the increase came from final demand energy, up 4.2%, and diesel fuel alone rose 24.1%, accounting for over a third of the month's gain. Unadjusted, final demand prices are up 5.4% over twelve months, and the narrower measure excluding food, energy and trade services is up 4.7%.

Weekly initial jobless claims, released the same morning, came in at 206,000 against 205,000 expected, keeping the labor picture steady while the price data ran hot.

Why it matters: Producer prices are what businesses pay before anything reaches a shelf, and diesel moves nearly everything that ships by truck. A 24% monthly jump in diesel tends to show up in delivery charges, food and construction costs over the following months, which is why today's consumer number is being read against it.

Markets September 10

Stocks fell a fourth day as oil and long yields climbed

The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 52,064.10, down 0.60%. The S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591.70 and the Nasdaq Composite 0.65% to 26,081.72, the fourth consecutive loss for all three; the S&P is down roughly 2% over those four sessions. U.S. crude topped $100 a barrel and Brent traded above $107, its highest since July, on supply disruption tied to the war with Iran. The 10-year Treasury yield ended between 4.91% and 4.95% depending on the source, its highest since 2023, and the 30-year closed at 5.36%.

Chipmakers led the declines, with Micron down about 5% and Nvidia down more than 2%. Apple rose about 3% after its foldable iPhone launch drew a strong reception.

Why it matters: Oil above $100, a 10-year near 5% and a 30-year above 5.3% raise borrowing costs for mortgages, business credit and car loans at the same time that fuel costs rise. For someone managing their own money, the week has paid short-term cash to wait while stocks and long bonds both lost ground.

Fed Today 8:30 ET · FOMC September 16

August CPI is the last inflation reading before the Fed decides

The August Consumer Price Index comes out at 8:30 a.m. ET today. FXStreet's consensus has headline prices up 0.4% on the month and 3.4% on the year, the same annual rate as July, with core prices up 0.2% on the month and 2.4% on the year, down from July's 2.5%. After Thursday's producer price data, CME FedWatch priced about a 69% chance of a quarter-point hike at the September 16 meeting and Polymarket about 63%. The target range is 3.50% to 3.75%. This brief was filed before the release, so it carries the forecasts only.

The macro backdrop: TSD's internal read has the regime as sovereign-supply with absorption held by the plumbing, direction sideways at medium conviction. The nearest alert-grade wires are all in rates: the 10-year is through its 4.75% line at 4.95, September hike odds are above the 50% line at 66%, and the 2s10s curve has flattened to +39bp, through its +40bp line. The board's inflation page still reads cooling at low conviction, and its own breakeven wire moved against that read on the PPI session, so the board and Thursday's data currently disagree. Its dated catalyst list has not rolled past September 8; the next dated events are today's CPI and the September 16 FOMC, with core PPI's direction test registered for October 15. Why it matters: A hike driven by energy prices is a harder call than one driven by strong demand, because higher rates do little to lower the price of a barrel of oil. A cooler core number today would not remove the energy pressure, and a hotter one would make next week's decision easier to predict.

Housing September 10

Existing-home sales fell back below 4 million

The National Association of Realtors said existing-home sales fell 2.0% in August to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.98 million, down 1.2% from a year earlier. Inventory rose to 1.62 million homes and months' supply to 4.9, while prices were up 1.6%. Sales held steady in the West and declined in the Northeast, Midwest and South. Sales remain up 1.6% for the first eight months of the year. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun tied the dip to high mortgage rates.

Why it matters: More homes for sale and fewer buyers is the setup that eventually moves prices, and it is showing up in the South, which includes the Charlotte region. With long yields at multi-year highs, mortgage rates have little room to ease before next week's decision.

◆ National events

Remembrance September 11

The country marks 25 years since the September 11 attacks

The commemoration at the National September 11 Memorial in New York begins at 8:30 a.m. ET, with family members reading the names of the victims. Seven moments of silence mark when each World Trade Center tower was struck and fell, the attack on the Pentagon, the crash of United Flight 93, and those who have since died of 9/11-related illness and injury. The first comes at 8:46 a.m. Vice President JD Vance and former Presidents Joe Biden, Barack Obama, Bill Clinton and George W. Bush are expected at Ground Zero, President Trump will attend the ceremony at the Pentagon, and the National Park Service holds a memorial near Shanksville, Pennsylvania. The morning ceremony is reserved for families; the public can watch online.

Why it matters: The ceremony now formally honors people who died years later of 9/11-related illness and injury alongside those killed on the day, which is how the anniversary keeps reaching responders, survivors and their families a quarter-century on.
