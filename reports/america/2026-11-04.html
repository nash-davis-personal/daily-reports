◆ The election, in facts

Results Nov 3–4, 2026

Democrats are projected to control both the House and the Senate in January

CBS News projected the House for Democrats at 10:15 PM ET and the Senate at 11:35 PM ET on Tuesday. AP's tracker early Wednesday showed Democrats with 54 Senate seats to Republicans' 44, against a 53 to 45 Republican split before the election, and said Democrats had crossed the 218-seat House threshold. CBS listed seven Republican-held Senate seats as flipped: Texas, Ohio, Kansas, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina and Maine.

Two Senate races were still open at publication. Mississippi had both candidates at 48.9% with 96% counted. Alaska stood at 48.7% to 47.5% with about two-thirds counted, and a ranked-choice tabulation would take roughly two weeks if no one reaches a majority. About 75 House races were uncalled as of 4:19 AM ET. Every number here is a media projection, and nothing is certified yet.

Why it matters: The majority decides which tax, tariff and spending bills reach the floor in 2027. For a small business, the concrete effect is on next year's legislative calendar, and the new Congress is not seated until January.

North Carolina Nov 3, 2026

NC voters approve a 3.5% income tax cap, a property tax limit and voter ID; Cooper wins the Senate seat

With 100% reporting, the income tax rate cap (SB 1080) passed with 61.92% yes, lowering the constitutional maximum state income tax rate from 7% to 3.5%. The limit on local property tax increases (HB 1089) passed with 60.42%, and the voter photo ID amendment (SB 921) passed with 59.60%. Roy Cooper won the US Senate seat with 58.72% to Michael Whatley's 39.09% at 99% reporting, called by Fox at 8:27 PM and CBS at 8:30 PM.

In the US House, AP called NC-2 (Ross), NC-4 (Foushee), NC-11 (Jamie Ager, flipping the Asheville-area seat) and NC-12 (Adams). NC-1 showed Don Davis at 50.06% with about 99% in. Final statewide turnout has not been published; pre-election data on Oct 30 showed 2.35 million early and absentee ballots, about 30% of eligible voters.

Why it matters: The two tax amendments now sit in the state constitution, which constrains how far the General Assembly and county commissions can raise income or property taxes on NC businesses in future budgets. The 3.5% figure is a ceiling on the rate, and the current rate already sits below it.

◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 3–4, 2026

S&P ends a three-day slide, the Dow falls, and futures open higher with the 10-year near 4.92%

Tuesday's close: Dow down 0.38% to 51,786.72, S&P 500 up 0.36% to 7,568.92, Nasdaq up 0.83% to 25,372.01, Russell 2000 up 0.23%. The 10-year yield rose 4 basis points to 4.95%, the 2-year to 4.61% and the 30-year to 5.32%. Gold fell 0.95% to $3,931.90. Apple dropped 1.9% on its margin forecast, Shopify fell 8.7% and Sysco fell 9.3% on a revenue miss. September factory orders rose 1.8% against a 1.3% forecast, the largest gain in five months.

After the bell, AMD, Super Micro and Arista all beat estimates and raised guidance (details in today's Technology brief). By 5:25 AM ET Wednesday, Dow futures were up 1.28%, S&P futures 0.90% and Nasdaq-100 futures 0.89%; the 10-year slipped to about 4.92% and the dollar traded near a one-month low. Uber, Qualcomm, McDonald's and Arm report today.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, the yield is the number to watch. A 10-year near 5% keeps mortgage and small-business loan rates high whatever the index does, and it makes cash and short Treasuries a real alternative to stocks for the first time in years.

Data Nov 3, 2026

September job openings rose to 7.5 million; hires and quits both climbed

The BLS JOLTS report put September openings at 7.5 million (a 4.5% rate), above every estimate in Bloomberg's survey, which centered near 7.1 million. August was revised up by 111,000 to 7.2 million. Hires were 5.4 million, led by accommodation and food services (+158,000). Quits rose to 3.4 million, up 375,000 over the year, with food service again the biggest mover. Layoffs held at 1.6 million, and the layoff rate fell for establishments with one to nine employees.

Why it matters: Openings and quits rising together means workers still have options, which keeps wage pressure on small employers, restaurants and hotels most of all. Friday's October jobs report is the next read.

Energy Nov 3, 2026

Oil spikes on the confirmed Qeshm Island strike, then settles lower

US officials told the Wall Street Journal that the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island on Nov 2 and has carried out "limited strikes" in recent weeks; three officials and the President confirmed the strike Tuesday, framed as defensive. Brent crossed $105 early Tuesday, reaching $106.61 at one point, then settled at $103.60, down $1.17. WTI dipped below $95 intraday and settled at $96.09, down 37 cents. That followed Monday's 7% drop; crude still gained more than 10% in October. Qatar said it is mediating and a delegation held talks in Tehran, while Iran's foreign ministry said it would not negotiate under coercion. Saudi Aramco's CEO said Hormuz shipments have not returned to pre-conflict levels.

Why it matters: Crude settling flat after a strike means no second shock to fuel and freight costs this week, but Brent above $100 keeps diesel and shipping surcharges elevated for any business that moves goods.

◆ Weather and public safety

Weather Nov 2–3, 2026

Election Day storms: 22 inches of rain in Florida, deadly flooding in the Four Corners, emergencies in five states

NBC News reported one storm dropped as much as 22 inches of rain in 24 hours on Florida's Atlantic coast, with Palm Bay getting nearly 20 inches in a day. Florida's governor declared a state of emergency in seven counties and at least 21 polling places were moved across eight counties. In the Four Corners, one person died in floodwaters and police suspect a second weather-related death; the San Juan River at Pagosa Springs, Colorado reached 16.81 feet on Sunday, and the governors of Colorado and New Mexico asked for a major federal disaster declaration. Arizona issued a statewide emergency and Utah declared one in San Juan County. A Sunday tornado with winds near 100 mph damaged the Keyes Community Center in Cimarron County, Oklahoma, forcing a polling change, and flash-flood risk continued Tuesday across the lower Mississippi Valley.

Why it matters: Five state emergency declarations and a pending federal disaster request in one week. Cleanup in southwest Colorado is estimated in weeks, and Florida's east coast took a year's worth of rain in a day.

Safety Nov 2–3, 2026

NTSB: the Learjet that crashed in Palm Beach County was likely breaking up in flight

A Learjet 60 flying from Austin to North Eleuthera in the Bahamas crashed in Greenacres, Florida on Sunday, killing all nine aboard and injuring one person on the ground; 77 mobile homes were evacuated. NTSB board member Tom Chapman said debris found nearly four miles away, including the left elevator, indicates the jet was likely breaking up before impact. The crew had reported icing near 35,000 feet before a rapid descent. More than 20 investigators are on scene, and recovering the main wreckage from a water-filled crater is the main obstacle. No cause has been named.

Why it matters: This is the deadliest US business-jet accident of the year. An in-flight breakup finding would carry fleet-wide implications for the Learjet 60 type, which is still common in charter service.
