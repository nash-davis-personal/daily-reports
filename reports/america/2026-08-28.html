◆ The shipping lane

Announcement Aug 28, 2026

Central Command says the Strait of Hormuz transit routes are clear of Iranian sea mines

U.S. Central Command commander Adm. Brad Cooper said the U.S. military has cleared Iranian sea mines from the internationally recognized transit routes through the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as a major milestone. Central Command said Iran has been notified that any ship or boat placing new mines will be immediately and systematically destroyed. Iran disputes the claim that the mines are cleared.

Two facts sit alongside the announcement. The first is that vessel traffic has not returned: most shipowners have continued to avoid the waterway while the reopening question stays unresolved, and traffic through the strait has run far below its normal level for months. The second is that the mine-clearing is one input among several. Iran and Oman reached an agreement over each country's share of the strait's waters and related revenues, and Tehran has said that arrangement does not by itself guarantee an immediate reopening. In normal conditions the strait carries roughly a fifth of seaborne oil.

Why it matters: The strait is the single largest swing factor in energy prices right now, and it feeds through to American households and small businesses as fuel, freight and insurance costs. Brent fell below $87 on Thursday for a fourth consecutive session on rising odds of a reopening, so the market has already priced in some of this. What has not happened yet is the tanker traffic itself. Until loaded vessels are moving at something near normal volumes, the price move reflects expectation rather than supply.

◆ The economy

Labor Aug 27, 2026

Initial jobless claims came in at 203,000, below forecast

The Labor Department reported 203,000 seasonally adjusted initial claims for the week ending August 22, a decrease of 4,000 from the previous week's revised 207,000 and below a 208,000 consensus. The four-week moving average rose 1,250 to 205,500.

Claims at that level indicate employers are not laying people off at any elevated rate. They say nothing about hiring, which is the side of the labor market that has been weaker: July payrolls came in at a decline of 23,000, and no fresh payroll print has landed since.

Why it matters: Low claims and weak payroll growth describe the same labor market from two angles: people who have jobs are keeping them, and people looking for one are having a harder time. For a small business, that means less turnover pressure on existing staff and a deeper applicant pool for open roles.

Markets Aug 27, 2026

Indexes closed higher on Nvidia, with breadth pointing the other way

The S&P 500 rose 55.29 points, or 0.7%, to 7,730.99. The Nasdaq composite added 411.16 points, or 1.6%, to 26,541.35. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 105.56 points, or 0.2%, to 53,569.44. Nvidia was the strongest single force lifting the market after beating profit expectations and guiding revenue above forecasts, and its move more than offset declines across a majority of the stocks in the S&P 500.

That last detail is the one worth holding onto. An index that rises while most of its members fall is being carried by a small number of very large companies, which is a structurally different market from one where most stocks are rising.

Why it matters: For anyone holding a broad index fund, the concentration means the portfolio's behavior is increasingly tied to a handful of AI-linked names rather than to the 500 companies on the label. Worth knowing when reading a green day.

Fed Aug 28, 2026

Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote at 10:00am ET

The Kansas City Fed's economic policy symposium runs August 27 through 29 under the theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Chair Kevin Warsh speaks this morning at 10:00am ET, his first keynote in the role. The prepared text is distributed to wire services on release and the speech is livestreamed by the Kansas City Fed.

The September FOMC meeting is three weeks out and markets are pricing roughly one-in-three odds of a hike. A new Chair's first symposium keynote is usually where the framework gets explained rather than where a specific decision gets pre-announced.

The macro backdrop: TSD's internal read has the regime as sovereign supply with the Fed on hold, direction sideways at medium conviction. The closest thing to a tripwire on the board is the 30-year Treasury sitting exactly on its 5.25% line without crossing it, alongside the 3-month bill at 3.85%, above the top of the target range. The board's own dated-catalyst list runs out at August 27; this morning's keynote is the next scheduled event on it. TSD Macroscope board (as of 2026-08-26).
Why it matters: Rates set the cost of a small-business loan, a mortgage and a line of credit. A first keynote that leans toward tolerating current inflation levels and one that leans toward acting on them point at different borrowing costs by the end of the year, which is why a twenty-minute speech gets this much attention.

◆ Science and the sky

Astronomy Aug 27–28, 2026

A 96% partial lunar eclipse was visible across most of the United States overnight

Just over 96.2% of the Moon's visible surface passed into Earth's dark inner shadow, leaving a thin bright sliver along one edge and turning the rest copper and rust-red. Maximum eclipse came at 12:12am EDT this morning, which is 11:12pm CDT, 10:12pm MDT and 9:12pm PDT on Thursday evening. It was visible from Europe, Africa, North America and South America, with the best views from the Americas; parts of the West saw the Moon rise after the earliest stages had begun.

Separately, technicians at NASA's Kennedy Space Center began installing the four RS-25 engines in the core stage of the Space Launch System rocket that will carry the Artemis III crew next year.

Why it matters: A deep partial eclipse this close to totality is uncommon, and this one was well placed for U.S. viewers. If you missed it, the next comparable event visible from here is some time off.
