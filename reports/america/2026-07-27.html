◆ Energy and the Gulf

Developing Jul 26–27

A second day without strikes, and crude gives back most of last week's spike

The United States paused attacks on Iran for a second straight day Sunday and Tehran said it had done the same, following a nearly two-week escalation that began with Iran firing at ships attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's foreign ministry said talks with Oman on safe passage through the strait had made progress and were continuing. A regional official involved in mediation described a compromise centered on Iran managing vessel transit with fewer restrictions on shipping. The White House said the President prefers a diplomatic outcome and retains other options.

Markets moved on it immediately. September Brent fell 4.66% to $92.27 a barrel Monday morning and WTI fell 5.02% to $84.83. Brent had briefly touched $102 last week, roughly $30 above where the most-traded contract sat early in the month and its highest since May.

Why it matters: Retail gasoline crossed back above $4 a gallon last week on this disruption. Pump prices lag crude by one to two weeks, so a sustained pause should start showing up at the register in early August — and it takes some pressure off the inflation print the Fed is watching.

◆ Markets and the economy

Markets Jul 27

Futures open higher into a week carrying four separate market-moving events

Dow and S&P 500 futures each gained about 0.8% Monday morning and Nasdaq 100 futures rose roughly 1.6%, with the oil selloff doing most of the work. The major averages had been tracking toward a losing month after disappointing Alphabet and Tesla results and a firmer rate backdrop.

The calendar is dense. The FOMC meets July 28–29 with the target range at 3.50–3.75%; the June CPI report showed headline inflation easing to 3.5% year over year from 4.2% and core to 2.6% from 2.9%, the first decline in five months. Futures have kept some probability on a hike rather than a cut. Q2 GDP and the June core PCE print follow. Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday after the close, Apple and Amazon Thursday, with capital expenditure and AI monetization the numbers analysts care about. The June advance durable goods report was released Monday at 8:30 a.m. ET, following a 4.5% decline in May driven by a 14% drop in transportation equipment.

Why it matters: Four inputs to the rate path land inside 72 hours, so anyone with a variable-rate business loan or a near-term financing decision gets a much clearer picture by Friday than they have today. For a personal portfolio, the megacap capex numbers are the read on whether the AI buildout is still accelerating.

New Jul 27

CME launches single-stock futures on more than 50 US names

CME Group's first batch of single-stock futures goes live today, covering more than 50 US stocks drawn from the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Russell 1000 — 55 standard contracts and 22 micro contracts, subject to regulatory approval. The list includes Nvidia, Apple, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla, Micron and SpaceX.

Why it matters: Micro-sized futures on individual names lower the capital required to take leveraged single-stock positions, and leverage cuts in both directions. Worth knowing the instrument exists; worth understanding the margin mechanics before going anywhere near it.

Carolinas Jul 2026

North Carolina ranks second nationally for business and first on the economy score

CNBC's annual ranking placed North Carolina second overall among states for doing business, with a first-place finish on the economy sub-score (up from third) and third on workforce (up from fourth). The write-up flags housing affordability as the pressure point underneath the strong sector numbers.

The Charlotte region's own tally is consistent with it: 15 announced projects since July 1 totaling more than 4,500 jobs, over 7,000 jobs and $1.4 billion in potential investment in the pipeline, SMBC Group selecting Mecklenburg County for a second US headquarters with 2,000 jobs by 2032, and Averitt building a regional campus near Charlotte Douglas.

Why it matters: Every one of those announcements is a company that will need local vendors, local hiring pages and local service contracts within 12 to 24 months. The announcement list is a public, dated lead list.
