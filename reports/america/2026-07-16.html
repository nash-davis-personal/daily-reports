◆ Economy & markets

Inflation Jul 16, 2026

June producer prices fell, and all eyes turn to the consumer

The June Producer Price Index declined 0.3% month-over-month and slowed to 5.5% year-over-year, below the roughly 6.2% expected; core PPI rose 0.2% (4.7% annual). Softer wholesale inflation pushed the US Dollar Index down about 0.5% toward 100.4. It follows the cooler June CPI reported earlier this week.

The main event today is the official June retail sales report at 8:30am ET, alongside weekly jobless claims and the Philadelphia Fed index. Analysts expected a soft reading (some estimates put the core figure slightly negative), but private card-transaction data from the NRF Retail Monitor already showed June spending up ~0.33% ex-auto/gas — a resilient consumer. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has dropped formal forward guidance in favor of pure data-dependence, so today's data carries extra weight for the autumn rate-cut debate.

Why it matters: Two months of cooling inflation plus a still-spending consumer is the "soft landing" setup markets want — supportive for stocks and for the case that the Fed can cut later this year. A weak retail print would flip the story toward hard-landing fears, so watch the 8:30am number.

◆ Extreme weather

Weather Week of Jul 13

A record-breaking heat dome baked 200M+ Americans, with the Northeast now cooling

A historic heat wave that began in the Southwest and Great Plains over the weekend swept east this week, putting more than 200 million people under dangerous heat. The National Weather Service called the event "exceptionally rare" for mid-July. Records fell across the map: Billings, Montana hit an all-time 111°F, Salt Lake City reached 109°F, Philadelphia around 107°F, and New York City neared 103°F on Wednesday, the region's peak.

The Carolinas and much of the Southeast sat under heat advisories through the stretch. Forecasts have Northeast temperatures dropping back into the 80s later this week as the dome weakens, with thunderstorms following the retreat — though some models keep elevated heat lingering into early next week.

Why it matters: Prolonged extreme heat strains the power grid, raises heat-illness risk for outdoor workers, and drives up cooling costs across the Charlotte metro and the Carolinas. Local relevance: check on outdoor-working clients and neighbors, and expect elevated utility bills this month.

Space Jul 16, 2026

SpaceX flies Starship Flight 13, and Serbia signs the Artemis Accords

SpaceX is set to launch its next Starship on the Flight 13 test mission today, carrying the first V3 Starlink satellites — a step toward the larger, higher-capacity constellation. Separately, the Republic of Serbia signs the Artemis Accords at 5 p.m. EDT during a ceremony at NASA Headquarters in Washington, adding another signatory to the US-led framework for peaceful space exploration.

Why it matters: Bigger, cheaper Starlink capacity keeps improving rural and backup internet options — relevant for businesses and homes outside dense broadband coverage in the Carolinas. The Artemis signing is a routine but steady sign the US-led space coalition keeps expanding.
