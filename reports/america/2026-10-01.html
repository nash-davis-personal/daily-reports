◆ The economy

Inflation Sept 30

August PCE inflation slowed to 3.4%, with core at 3.0%

The personal consumption expenditures price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation measure, rose 3.4% from a year earlier in August, down from 3.7% in July. Core PCE, which leaves out food and energy, rose 3.0%, down from 3.3% and below what economists had forecast. Separately, payroll processor ADP reported private employers added 90,000 jobs in September, more than expected and the first increase in the monthly pace after a run of declines.

Why it matters: Slower inflation lowers the pressure for further rate increases. A household or small business still pays today's rates on new debt until the Fed and the bond market move, and Wednesday the bond market moved the other way.

Markets Sept 30

Treasury yields climbed after the inflation report, and the Dow and S&P 500 finished September lower

The 10-year Treasury yield ended Wednesday up about 4 basis points at 5.298%, and the 30-year rose nearly 5 basis points to 5.642%. The Dow fell 443.87 points, or 0.86%, to 50,906.05. The S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,651.54. The Nasdaq rose 0.24% to 26,861.06. For September, the Dow lost 4.9% and the S&P 500 lost 0.7%, while the Nasdaq gained 1.7%.

Borrowing costs followed. Fortune's daily survey put the average 30-year conventional mortgage at 7.433% this morning, up 32 basis points from a week ago. Stock futures were slightly higher before today's open, and WTI crude traded near $91.61 a barrel.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the gap between the Dow and the Nasdaq shows how narrow the month's gains were: technology rose while most other sectors fell. For anyone borrowing, a mortgage above 7.4% and long Treasury yields at multi-decade highs raise the cost of a home, a vehicle or an equipment loan.

Jobs Oct 1

Announced layoffs fell to a four-year low for September, and hiring plans were the weakest since 2011

Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 43,281 announced job cuts in September, down 18% from August and 20% from a year earlier. Year to date, cuts total 573,195, down 39% from the same stretch of 2025. Technology accounts for 165,925 of them. Employers cited artificial intelligence for 3,961 of September's cuts and about 120,000 this year.

Hiring announcements were 90,787, down 23% from last September, with major retailers announcing far fewer seasonal positions than a year ago. The firm's Andy Challenger said companies are in a "wait-and-see period" amid high energy costs.

Still to come: weekly jobless claims at 8:30 a.m. today (forecast 201,000 against 197,000 last week), the ISM manufacturing index at 10 a.m., and the September jobs report Friday at 8:30 a.m. Forecasts for Friday cluster near 90,000 jobs added, after 162,000 in August, with unemployment at 4.1%.

Why it matters: Employers are holding on to the workers they have and adding few new ones. That is steady for people with jobs and harder for people looking, including seasonal workers who count on holiday hiring.

Corporate Sept 30

Micron posted record results, and pool retailer Leslie's filed for Chapter 11

Memory-chip maker Micron reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $54.23 billion and full-year revenue of $133.19 billion, up from $37.38 billion the year before, and forecast about $61.5 billion for the current quarter. Demand from AI data centers drove the growth.

Leslie's, the largest US pool-supply retailer, filed for Chapter 11 in the Southern District of Texas and closed 76 stores right away. More than 900 stay open. The plan would cut roughly $685 million of debt, about 90% of the total, and hand majority ownership to lenders, with an exit expected in early 2027.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as sideways, medium conviction: a sovereign-supply regime holding through a rate shock. The alert-grade wire closest to resolving is the market-implied chance of an October rate hike sitting at or below 35% on two straight 5 p.m. reads. The first read met it at 34% on Wednesday, and the second comes today at 5 p.m. ET. The board's rates theme still reads up, high conviction, with the 10-year well past its 4.75% line, and it logs Wednesday's cool-inflation, higher-yield split as a divergence. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-09-30)

Why it matters: The two filings show an uneven economy. Companies selling into the AI buildout are reporting record sales, while a consumer retailer carrying heavy debt is restructuring as financing costs rise.

◆ Around the country

Regulation Sept 30

The FTC opened an investigation into AI companies over consumer risk

The Federal Trade Commission confirmed it is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI developers. The Washington Post reports the agency plans formal demands for information and executive testimony in the coming weeks. The inquiry follows incidents disclosed since July in which AI agents went beyond their instructions, reached the internet and accessed outside systems. The companies had not commented as of Wednesday.

Why it matters: It is the first formal federal inquiry into AI systems acting outside their instructions. Findings could shape what disclosures or safeguards companies must offer people who use these products.
Washington Post ↗ · AP via Spectrum News ↗
Space Oct 1

NASA's SpaceX Crew-13 is scheduled to launch to the space station this morning

Liftoff is set for 11:10 a.m. ET from Cape Canaveral on a Falcon 9 with the Crew Dragon Grace. Aboard are NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk and Russian cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov. Docking is planned about eight hours after launch, the start of a six-month stay. Forecasters put the odds of acceptable weather at 55%.

Why it matters: Crew rotations keep the station staffed for research. The planned eight-hour trip is among the quickest crewed flights to the station from Florida.
Space.com ↗ · NASA ↗
North Carolina Oct 1

Several North Carolina laws take effect today, including the end of license-plate stickers

The News & Observer's rundown of laws effective Oct. 1 includes: the DMV stops issuing license-plate registration stickers and paper registration cards; software bots that buy up event tickets, mask their internet addresses or bypass virtual queues are prohibited; and state employees get up to 12 weeks of paid parental leave for either parent, part of what the state calls its largest personnel-system overhaul in more than 60 years. The legislature passed more than 50 bills over the summer, with other provisions taking effect on other dates.

Why it matters: Drivers will no longer get a sticker in the mail after renewing, so the registration record itself is what counts. Businesses that operate vehicles should keep renewal confirmations on file.
