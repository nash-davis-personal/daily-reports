Yields eased into a week that decides more than most

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.701%, down 0.74% on the day, easing the pressure from last week's bond selloff without undoing it. Friday's close was 4.74% and the 30-year has held near 5.3%. Oil retreated, with Brent at $88.88 and WTI at $83.11, and the dollar index rose 0.17% to 98.964. Gold gained 1.04% to $4,662.68 an ounce and the VIX rose 4.76% to 15.85. Equities finished mixed, with the Dow up 0.26% to 53,417, the S&P down 0.28% to 7,653 and the Nasdaq down 0.76% to 25,980.

The calendar explains the caution. Wednesday brings PCE, durable goods and personal spending. Thursday brings jobless claims and the advance goods trade balance, and Jackson Hole opens. Friday brings the Warsh keynote and month-end rebalancing. Markets still price further cuts, though the balance has moved away from the most aggressive easing paths.

Why it matters: Long-end yields near these levels set the price of a mortgage, a business loan and a commercial lease renewal, and three separate readings this week can move them. For anyone managing their own money, the practical point is that the information density is Wednesday morning through Friday morning, so decisions made before then are made without it.

Earnings Aug 26, 2026

Nvidia reports after Wednesday's close with about $91 billion expected

Nvidia holds its fiscal Q2 call at 2 p.m. PT, 5 p.m. ET on Wednesday. Consensus is roughly $91 billion in revenue and $2.09 in adjusted earnings per share, which would be about 95% growth year over year. The line investors will read hardest is the data centre breakdown across the hyperscalers.

The result carries more weight than a single company's quarter because chip demand is the cleanest available read on whether the AI capital expenditure cycle is still intact. Chip stocks have struggled to hold gains since July's declines on questions about returns from AI spending, though strong cloud growth at Microsoft, Amazon and Google eased some of that.

Why it matters: A large share of this year's index gains sit in semiconductors and the companies buying from them, so a miss or a soft outlook would not stay inside the sector. Anyone holding a broad US index fund holds this quarter's answer whether or not they follow the company.

Bitcoin held above $80,000 after the biggest weekly ETF inflow since October

Bitcoin reached an intraday high of $81,265 before easing back, trading near $79,000 on Tuesday and up roughly 24% from $64,146 a week earlier, its largest weekly gain in more than three years. The 13 US-listed spot funds drew a net $1.92 billion last week, the most since early October, including $606.3 million on August 20 alone, and ETF assets rose to $98.56 billion from $78.67 billion.

Reporting attributes the move to two things: US plans to increase buybacks of long-dated bonds in order to pull yields down, and a short squeeze that forced bearish positions closed. Last week's edition of this briefing had bitcoin near $78,000 after a 22% week, so this extends rather than starts the run.

Why it matters: A 24% weekly move driven partly by forced position closures is a description of volatility rather than of value. The durable fact in the story is the ETF flow, which shows the wrapper is now how most US demand arrives.

◆ Health and science

Health Aug 24, 2026

The FDA cleared Roche's Alzheimer's blood test, the second in five days

The FDA cleared the Elecsys pTau217 plasma test from Roche Diagnostics on August 24 for use in diagnosing Alzheimer's disease in patients presenting with signs or symptoms of cognitive impairment. It was cleared as a single-biomarker tool that supports both ruling in and ruling out amyloid pathology, in primary care as well as specialty settings.

It follows the August 20 clearance of C2N Diagnostics' PrecivityAD2 for adults 40 and older with signs of cognitive impairment, which detects amyloid plaque biomarkers with over 90% accuracy. Four blood-based biomarker tests are now FDA-cleared, two of them inside one week. The alternative these replace for many patients is a spinal tap or a PET scan.

Why it matters: Moving a diagnosis from a specialty procedure to a blood draw a primary care doctor can order changes who gets tested and how early. Clearance is not the same as coverage, so what determines real access is how quickly insurers decide to pay for these.

Climate Aug 22, 2026

Ocean surface temperature hit the highest level ever measured

Copernicus, the European climate change service, recorded an average surface temperature of 21.1C, about 70F, across the world's seas outside the polar regions on Saturday, August 22. That beats the previous record set in March 2024 by a hundredth of a degree.

The timing is the part scientists flagged. Ocean temperatures normally peak in the spring and decline through August, so setting the record in late August indicates warmth persisting outside the usual seasonal pattern. Researchers attribute it to a developing El Nino adding heat on top of decades of human-driven warming.

Why it matters: Warmer sea surface temperatures supply more energy to tropical systems, which connects directly to the storms that hit Hawaii this month and to the Atlantic season now underway. For the Carolinas that is a hurricane-season input rather than an abstraction.

◆ Trade, small business and policy

Trade Aug 22–24, 2026

The US-Canada tariff exchange escalated over the weekend

The United States applied 50% duties on $20 billion of Canadian goods on Saturday. On Monday the administration threatened 50% tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks and steel beginning January 1. Canada has said it will respond in two weeks with higher tariffs on US goods. These are the neutral facts of a dispute that has been escalating since talks collapsed last week.

Separately, the Treasury Department said Monday it has expanded the categories of Iran-related conduct that could draw future sanctions and will give countries defined timelines to end economic relationships with Tehran, and introduced sanctions covering digital assets, gold, aviation and shipping. It stopped short of imposing broad new penalties on third countries.

Why it matters: Vehicles, trucks and steel at 50% would reach small business input costs directly, through fleet purchases, equipment and construction materials, and the January 1 date gives buyers a window to act before it applies. Anyone with a Canadian supplier or a planned vehicle purchase has a concrete decision with a deadline attached.

Small business Aug 20, 2026

The SBA proposed the largest rewrite of small business size standards in decades

The proposed rule, published in the Federal Register on August 20, would replace nearly 1,000 industry-specific size standards with 338 standards set at the 4- and 5-digit NAICS level, eliminate all subindustry exceptions, convert construction and several other sectors from receipts-based to employee-based measurement, and remove the ceiling on size standards altogether. Thresholds rise as much as tenfold or more in professional services, IT, engineering, logistics and hospitality.

The SBA estimates roughly 114,500 firms would gain small business status and fewer than 200 would lose it. Comments are due September 21, 2026. A separate change, effective September 10, revises the 8(a) program's social disadvantage framework.

Why it matters: Size standards decide who can bid on federal set-aside contracts and who qualifies for SBA lending programs. A tenfold increase in the IT and professional services thresholds would let much larger firms compete for work currently reserved for genuinely small ones, which cuts both ways depending on where a business sits. The comment window closes September 21.
