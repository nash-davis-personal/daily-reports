◆ Election Day

National Nov 3, 2026

The 2026 general election is today

Voters are electing all 435 members of the House, about a third of the Senate (outlets count 33 to 35 seats, depending on special elections), 36 governors, and thousands of state and local officials and ballot measures. Polling hours are set by each state; North Carolina polls are open 6:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. Mail-ballot rules differ too: California, for example, counts ballots postmarked by today and received by Nov 10, so some results will take days. Georgia and Louisiana have possible runoffs Dec. 1.

Separately, Semafor reports OpenAI and Anthropic have set up teams to watch for misuse of their AI tools and misleading voting information during the election.

Why it matters: Expect slower vote counts in mail-heavy states, so final results in close races may not be known tonight. Polling-place and hours questions go to the county board of elections; in North Carolina that is the State Board's voter lookup.

◆ The economy

Energy Nov 2, 2026

Oil falls more than 7% on reports of US-Iran diplomatic talks

Brent crude settled down 7.63% at $104.77 a barrel Monday, and WTI fell about 7.4% to near $96.50, after US and Iranian officials made statements about possible talks. Even after the drop, Brent is still above $100, so fuel and shipping costs stay elevated.

Why it matters: Gasoline prices usually follow crude with a lag of a week or two. The move rests on statements about talks; no session has been held, so it can reverse just as fast.

Data Nov 2, 2026

Manufacturing survey rises to 55.5 in October, beating forecasts

The Institute for Supply Management's factory index rose 1 point to 55.5, above the 54.5 economists expected and firmly in expansion territory (above 50). Factory orders for September follow at 10 a.m. today, along with the September job openings (JOLTS) report.

Why it matters: Factories are still expanding at a solid pace, which supports hiring in manufacturing regions like the Carolinas. A hot reading also keeps price pressure in view for the Fed.

StockTitan market wrap
Markets Nov 2, 2026

Stocks split as tech sells off; big deals and earnings move single names

The Dow rose 213.67 points (0.41%) to 51,983.98 while the S&P 500 fell 0.51% to 7,541.40, its third straight loss, and the Nasdaq fell 1.07% to 25,164.36. Chipmakers led the decline: Marvell (−12.7%) and Lumentum (−18.6%) fell after agreeing to combine in an all-stock deal, and Oracle fell 8.8% after launching $10B stock and $10B debt offerings. After the close, Apple reported record fiscal-year results and Palantir revenue rose 96%; both stocks dipped after hours.

Why it matters: For someone holding broad index funds, Monday's split is a reminder of how concentrated the S&P is in big tech: the Dow, with fewer chip names, rose on the same day. Index-fund holders feel chip-sector swings more than the headline economy suggests.

Week ahead Nov 3–6, 2026

Job openings today, the Treasury refunding Wednesday, the jobs report Friday

Today brings JOLTS and factory orders, plus earnings from Pfizer and Shopify before the open and AMD, Amgen, Arista and Booking after. Wednesday is the Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement, which sets how much long-term debt it sells. Friday at 8:30 a.m. ET is the October employment report, with economists expecting about 55,000 jobs added.

Why it matters: The refunding and the jobs report are the two events most likely to move mortgage and loan rates this week.
