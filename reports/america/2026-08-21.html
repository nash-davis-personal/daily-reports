◆ The economy — a cautious shopper and a stubborn bond market

Markets Aug 20, 2026

Walmart's slowest US growth in six years knocked 700 points off the Dow

Walmart said sales at US stores open at least a year, excluding fuel, rose 2.6% in the quarter ended July 31 — short of the lowest estimate compiled by Bloomberg and the weakest reading in more than six years. Excluding the wellness category that contains its pharmacies, comps rose 3.4%; the company attributed much of the drag to federal legislation requiring pharmacies to dispense certain high-cost Medicare drugs at capped prices. US e-commerce grew 24%, down from 26% in the first quarter. The company guided third-quarter and full-year earnings and sales below analyst expectations. Walmart executives described customers making "trade-offs," citing high gas prices.

The stock fell more than 9% and pulled consumer names with it. The Dow closed down about 1.3%, roughly 700 points; the S&P 500 fell 0.8% and the Nasdaq 1%. Treasury yields rebounded — the 10-year to 4.7%, the 30-year to 5.25% — undoing most of the prior session's relief after the Treasury Department's move to tamp down yields. Oil rose after the administration said it would apply economic pressure on Iran.

Why it matters: Walmart is the broadest read on the American household there is, and 2.6% comp growth with softening online growth says the low- and middle-income shopper is trading down. Combined with a 30-year yield near two-decade highs, borrowing stays expensive at the same time consumer demand cools — the squeeze small retailers and service businesses feel first.

Data Aug 20, 2026

Jobless claims fell to 206,000, but people are staying unemployed longer

The Labor Department reported Thursday that seasonally adjusted initial claims fell 6,000 to 206,000 for the week ending August 15, below the 210,000 economists expected. The prior week was revised up from 209,000 to 212,000. The four-week moving average rose to 204,000 from a revised 199,750.

Continuing claims, which count people still collecting benefits, rose 18,000 to 1,799,000 for the week ending August 8. Layoffs remain historically sparse; re-hiring is the softer half of the picture. Separately, core CPI cooled to 2.5% year over year in July, its lowest since March 2021, still above the Federal Reserve's 2% target.

Why it matters: Low firing with slow hiring is the labor market shape that keeps the Fed on hold. For anyone managing their own money, it argues against expecting near-term rate relief; for anyone hiring, it means candidates are available but not desperate.

Trade Aug 18–21, 2026

A 50% tariff on about $20 billion of Canadian imports stays paused through today

A 50% US tariff covering roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods was scheduled to take effect early Wednesday, August 19. Hours before the deadline the two governments announced a three-day pause, running through August 21, subject to finalizing documents. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said there had been "intensive discussions" and that "substantial progress has been made, although there is important work still to be done." Details on farm protections were among the items still open. The talks sit inside a broader renegotiation of the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement.

Why it matters: The pause expires today. Any US business with Canadian inputs — building materials, packaging, machinery parts, aluminum — has a landed-cost question that resolves one way or the other within hours, and no forward pricing certainty until it does.

◆ Storm recovery and public safety

Disaster Aug 20–21, 2026

Northwest Indiana hits day nine without power

An August 11 storm with winds measured at 99 mph brought down trees and power lines across northwest Indiana, concentrated in Lake County. As of late Thursday, Northern Indiana Public Service Co. reported about 75,000 customers still without electricity, more than 20,000 of them in Gary. Residents described throwing out spoiled food, lining up for hot meals and hunting for places to charge phones.

Gary's school district, roughly 4,300 students, opened its year in early August and has been closed since August 13, with closure extended through Friday. Businesses across the affected area have been unable to reopen. Seven deaths have been reported since the storms began — a four-year-old boy, three men and three women.

Why it matters: Nine days is well past the point where an outage is an inconvenience. Wage loss for hourly workers, spoiled inventory for food businesses and closed schools compound into a local economic event, and utilities across the country are being measured against this restoration timeline.

Security Aug 20, 2026

FBI says it disrupted a planned attack on the New York State Capitol

Federal prosecutors charged Jessica Bowie, 35, of Albany, with attempting to provide material support or resources to a designated foreign terrorist organization. According to the criminal complaint, Bowie pledged allegiance to ISIS in online communications and began planning in July, conducting weeks of surveillance of the Capitol and photographing it from multiple vantage points. Undercover agents and informants posing as facilitators say she discussed concealing a device in a delivery bag and wanted to act while state senators were in session. She was arrested after purchasing items needed to build an explosive device.

Why it matters: The case is another instance of a plot developing entirely through online contact and being interdicted at the point of materials purchase, which is the pattern most recent domestic terrorism prosecutions have followed. State capitol security postures are likely to be reviewed as a result.

Aviation Aug 19–20, 2026

Midair collision at a Pennsylvania airport kills one pilot

Shortly before 7 p.m. Wednesday, a Cessna 150 collided with a Pennsylvania State Police Bell 407 helicopter at Carlisle Regional Airport in Cumberland County, about 120 miles west of Philadelphia. The helicopter's two-person crew was on a training flight performing a hover taxi just above the ground; both troopers were injured and survived. The Cessna's pilot, Dr. Paul Sokoloski, 57, of Hershey, was killed.

The NTSB said the pilots had been communicating before the collision, and described the Cessna's flight path around the airport as "unusual." A probable cause determination typically takes one to two years.

Why it matters: Non-towered general aviation airports rely on pilots self-coordinating by radio. A collision that occurred despite the pilots being in communication is exactly the scenario the NTSB studies when it considers whether that model needs changing.
