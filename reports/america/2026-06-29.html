◆ Markets & the economy

Markets Jun 29

Futures rebound at the open after a reported pause in US–Iran strikes

US stock index futures rose Monday after reports that the US and Iran agreed to halt the tit-for-tat attacks of the weekend so talks could continue. Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 1.2%, S&P 500 futures about 0.8%, and Dow futures roughly 0.4% — recovering after the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both closed the prior week sharply lower. As context and reported neutrally: US Central Command struck Iranian military targets on Saturday in response to Iranian action against commercial shipping, Iran's IRGC struck US sites in Kuwait and Bahrain early Sunday, and both sides reportedly agreed to stop by Monday.

Last confirmed close (June 26): S&P 500 at 7,354.02, Nasdaq 25,297.62, Dow 51,876.11. Monday's figures above are pre-market futures.

Why it matters: The weekend's geopolitical scare and its reported reversal set the risk tone for a thin, holiday-shortened week — a fast round trip for anyone holding equities, and a reminder of how quickly headline risk can swing oil, defense, and broad indexes.
Yahoo Finance ◆ NPR
Calendar Week of Jun 29

June jobs report pulled forward to Thursday; markets closed Friday

Because of Independence Day, the Bureau of Labor Statistics June employment report is scheduled for Thursday, July 2 instead of the usual first Friday, and US markets will be closed Friday, July 3 (the observed holiday, with July 4 falling on a Saturday).

Why it matters: The week's biggest scheduled data point lands a day early into a low-liquidity pre-holiday tape, so any surprise in the jobs number could move rates and stocks more than usual with fewer participants to absorb it.
CNBC
Fed June

Core PCE ticks up to 3.4%; Fed holds rates a fourth straight meeting

Core PCE rose to 3.4% year-over-year in May, up from 3.3% in April. The Fed held the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% for a fourth consecutive meeting in June — the first under Chair Kevin Warsh — and its June projections revised 2026 PCE inflation up to 3.6%, with officials split between holding and hiking.

Why it matters: Sticky inflation plus a divided Fed means near-term rate cuts are not the base case, which keeps financing and mortgage costs elevated for small businesses and for anyone managing rate-sensitive savings.

◆ Disasters & severe weather

Wildfire Jun 26–27

Cottonwood Fire becomes the largest active US wildfire, at 0% containment

The human-caused Cottonwood Fire near Beaver, Utah (Fishlake National Forest) ignited June 22 and grew past 92,000 acres at 0% containment by Saturday. Utah declared a state of emergency, restricted fireworks ahead of July 4, and ordered mandatory evacuations; the Eagle Point ski resort sustained severe damage. Statewide, Utah counted 12 active fires across more than 212,000 acres.

Why it matters: A major public-safety and natural-disaster event heading into the highest-risk fireworks weekend of the year, with direct evacuation and holiday-restriction effects for the region.
CBS News
Storms Jun 28

Severe-storm outbreak slams the Midwest with damaging winds and flooding

A violent line of storms swept central and eastern Iowa around June 28, with a 94 mph straight-line wind gust reported in Albion, snapped power poles, roof damage, downed trees, and rainfall over two inches an hour driving widespread flash flooding. The Storm Prediction Center flagged continued severe risk — large hail and damaging winds — shifting across the northern Plains and Dakotas.

Why it matters: A continuation of an unusually active 2026 severe-weather season, with real power-outage and property impacts across the agricultural Midwest.
