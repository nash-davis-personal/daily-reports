◆ The economy — all eyes on Friday

Data ahead Releases Fri, June 26

May PCE expected near 4.1% — the hottest inflation since 2023

The Bureau of Economic Analysis releases the May PCE Price Index on Friday. Forecasts (FactSet) call for headline PCE up about 4.1% year-over-year — which would be the highest since April 2023 — and roughly +0.5% on the month. Core PCE, the Fed's preferred gauge, is seen at about +0.4% monthly and +3.3% annual. Many economists expect May to mark this year's inflation peak, with lower oil prices and easing tariff effects cooling things afterward.

Why it matters: PCE is the inflation number the Fed watches most. A hot print this close to the Fed's hawkish turn raises the odds rates stay higher for longer — which keeps borrowing costs (business loans, card APRs, mortgages) elevated and keeps yields on cash and short-term Treasuries attractive for anyone parking money.

Markets Wed, June 24 close

Stocks drift into the inflation print; chip earnings in focus

A mixed, quiet session ahead of the data: the S&P 500 slipped 0.10% to 7,358.22, the Nasdaq fell 0.43% to 25,476.64, and the Dow added 182 points (0.35%) to 51,848.90. Micron shares dipped ahead of its after-the-bell earnings, a closely watched read on memory-chip demand tied to the AI buildout.

Why it matters: The market is in wait-and-see mode. For someone managing their own money, this is the calm before a potential move — Friday's PCE is the catalyst that decides whether the recent tech wobble extends or reverses.

Fed Decision June 17

The Fed held rates but flipped hawkish on the path

The Fed kept its target range at 3.50–3.75% on June 17, but the projections turned hawkish: the median policymaker now sees year-end rates at 3.8% (up from 3.4% in March), 17 of 18 officials judge inflation risks tilted to the upside, and 9 of 18 now project at least one hike this year. Weekly jobless claims came in at 226,000 (week ended June 13), still low.

Why it matters: This is the backdrop for Friday. The Fed has already signaled it's more worried about inflation than growth, so a hot PCE would reinforce "higher for longer" — meaningful for anyone with variable-rate debt or planning a financed purchase.

◆ Policy & events

Legislation Passed June 22–23

Landmark housing-supply bill clears Congress; signing postponed

The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act passed the Senate 85–5 on June 22 and the House 358–32 on June 23 — the most significant federal housing legislation in more than three decades, focused on increasing supply to bring down building and housing costs. The planned signing ceremony was then postponed, with the President tying it to passage of a separate elections-related bill, so the housing act has not yet been signed into law.

Why it matters: If it becomes law, a supply-focused housing package could ease one of the biggest cost pressures on households and on construction-adjacent small businesses over time. For now it's passed-but-not-enacted, so the effective date and rules are still pending.
CNBC — House passes affordable housing bill
Disaster Wed, June 24

Strong Caribbean quakes trigger a US tsunami advisory

Two powerful earthquakes struck off Venezuela's northern coast within a minute of each other on June 24 — a magnitude 7.2 followed by a 7.5 — the largest to hit the country in over a century, with dozens reported dead. The USGS issued a tsunami advisory for Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands; the advisory was the main direct US effect.

Why it matters: The human toll is in Venezuela, but the event put US Caribbean territories on tsunami alert and is a reminder that seismic risk in the region reaches US shores. No mainland impact reported.
