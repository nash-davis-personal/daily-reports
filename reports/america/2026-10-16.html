◆ The economy

Inflation Oct 15

Wholesale prices rise 1.3% in a month

The September producer price index rose 1.3% from August against a 0.5% forecast, and 6.3% from a year earlier, the largest annual gain since 2022. Core PPI rose 0.7%. It follows Wednesday's consumer price report, which showed 3.7% annual inflation with gasoline up sharply. Producer prices tend to pass through to what businesses charge over the following months.

Why it matters: Small businesses buying supplies and services will feel this before shoppers do. Expect vendor price increases this quarter, and budget for it if you sign annual contracts now.
BabyPips recap BigGo Finance
Consumers Oct 15

Retail sales beat and layoffs stay rare

Advance September retail sales rose 0.8%, well above the 0.3% expected, and 0.9% excluding autos. Part of that is higher prices, since the figure isn't adjusted for inflation. Initial jobless claims came in at 199,000 for the week, slightly under forecast, with the four-week average at 198,500. Low claims alongside last week's weak 29,000 payroll gain point to a market where employers aren't firing much but aren't hiring much either.

Why it matters: Customers are still spending, so demand for local services is holding. Hiring is the harder part, which keeps the case for automating routine work strong for small firms that can't fill roles.
BabyPips recap Investrade
Markets Close Oct 15

Stocks shake off a tech sell-off; the 10-year falls back toward 5%

Stocks opened lower as the inflation print hit AI infrastructure names, then recovered. The Dow closed at 52,177 (+1.08%), the S&P 500 at 7,744 (+0.33%), the Nasdaq at 26,713 (+0.08%) and the Russell 2000 at 2,767 (+1.38%). The semiconductor index fell for a seventh straight session; CoreWeave dropped about 8%. Healthcare hit an all-time high and homebuilders rallied. The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 13 basis points to roughly 5.05–5.09% after topping 5.32% the morning before, and the dollar index slipped 0.5%. WTI crude rose 3% to about $98, and gold gained 1.5% to $4,170.

Bank earnings were mixed: BNY beat on revenue, PNC beat on earnings and missed slightly on revenue, U.S. Bancorp raised its 2026 revenue outlook, and Schwab reported record net new assets. Traders still put about a 71% chance on the Fed holding rates at its October meeting.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the broad market held up while the AI-hardware trade kept falling, so concentration in one theme is what hurt this week. Yields near 5% also mean cash and short Treasuries pay real money.
Investrade market review CNBC on TSMC
Housing Oct 15

Mortgage rates hold at 7.40%

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the 30-year fixed at 7.40%, unchanged, ending a run of seven straight weekly increases. The 15-year averaged 6.71%, down from 6.73%. A year ago the 30-year averaged 6.27%. The survey closed before Thursday's drop in Treasury yields.

Why it matters: On a $350,000 loan, 7.40% against last year's 6.27% is roughly $260 more a month in principal and interest. Home sales, moving and renovation businesses will keep feeling that.
Freddie Mac release
The macro backdrop: The board reads a sovereign-supply regime holding through a rate shock, direction sideways, conviction medium. The nearest alert-grade wire is the yield curve: the 2-year/10-year spread sat at +33bp, inside the +35bp line, and a second official reading at or below it would flip the curve call from steepening to flattening. Next dated catalyst: China's Q3 GDP, around Oct 19–20 (date unconfirmed). Source: TSD Macroscope board (as of 2026-10-15).

◆ Weather and recovery

Disaster Oct 12–14

Florida Panhandle keeps digging out from Isaias

A week after Hurricane Isaias came ashore as a Category 2 storm, nearly 54,000 Florida homes and businesses were still without power as of Oct 14, down from about 120,000 on Oct 12. The confirmed death toll had reached four by around Oct 10, and WLRN reported more deaths on Oct 14. The state opened emergency distribution points across the region and is surveying businesses to measure losses; food, water, laundry services and business loans are available in Northwest Florida. Bay County power is essentially restored, per the governor.

Why it matters: Pensacola and the surrounding counties face weeks of rebuilding, and business disruption surveys usually come before state and federal aid decisions.
