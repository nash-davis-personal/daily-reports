◆ The economy

Data Aug 14, 2026

July retail sales fell 0.6%, the biggest monthly drop in more than a year

The Census Bureau's advance estimate put retail and food services sales at $763.6 billion in July, down 0.6% from June. Forecasters had expected a gain of roughly 0.1% to 0.2%, so the miss was large. Motor vehicle and parts dealers fell 1.8% and nonstore retailers, the category covering online purchases, fell 2.2%. Not everything moved down: clothing and accessories rose 1.9%, health and personal care rose 0.7%, and food services and drinking places rose 0.5%.

The longer view is less alarming than the headline. Sales were still 5.0% above July of last year, and the May-through-July window ran 6.3% ahead of the same period a year earlier. What changed is the direction and the categories. Big-ticket and discretionary online spending came off while restaurants and clothing held, which is a pattern consistent with households absorbing higher fixed costs rather than losing income outright.

Why it matters: This is the first monthly print in over a year that breaks the "consumer keeps spending" story, and it lands the same week that the biggest retailers report. Home Depot on Tuesday is the cleanest read on whether big-ticket home improvement and contractor demand is softening. For anyone selling to consumers or to small businesses that serve them, the July number is a reason to check whether your own pipeline slowed in the same window before assuming it is a you problem.

Markets Aug 17, 2026

Rates are at 3.50%-3.75% and the September argument is about a hike, not a cut

The federal funds target range is 3.50% to 3.75% after the July hold, and market pricing for the September 16 meeting has been unstable. Odds of a quarter-point increase ran above 80% in late July, eased toward 65% in mid-August, and CME FedWatch data as of August 14 implied roughly a 69% chance of a hold instead. July CPI came in at 0.1% on the month and 3.4% on the year, with core at 0.2% and 2.5%, so inflation has slowed without reaching the 2% target.

Energy is the swing factor. Brent traded near $88 to $91 a barrel today, down slightly on the day and about 1% over the past month, but roughly 33% higher than a year ago. Elevated crude, attributed by analysts to supply disruption tied to the Iran conflict, is what keeps a hike on the table when a slowing consumer would ordinarily argue the other way. Futures on the Dow slipped 0.1% before the open Monday, S&P 500 futures were near flat, and Nasdaq-100 contracts rose 0.4%. Friday's close had the Dow at 53,732 (−0.20%), the S&P 500 at 7,785.76 (−0.2%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,729.16 (−0.3%), after the S&P's third straight weekly gain.

Why it matters: A Fed that might raise rates while consumer spending falls is a different environment than one easing into a slowdown, and it changes the math on anything financed. Concretely: variable-rate business credit, SBA loan pricing, and mortgage rates all stop trending in the direction most people have been assuming. Wednesday's release of the July meeting minutes is the next real information, and it will show how divided the committee was on the hold.

Markets Aug 17, 2026

Nine tech firms have disclosed about $3 trillion in AI commitments that do not appear as debt

A Wall Street Journal analysis puts the combined off-balance-sheet obligations of Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, Nvidia, Broadcom, SpaceX and AMD at roughly $3 trillion, against about $600 billion in reported capital expenditure over the trailing twelve months. Most of it is purchase commitments and leases that have not yet commenced, which under current accounting sit outside reported debt. Alphabet disclosed $811 billion in purchase and contractual commitments as of June 30. Meta disclosed $347 billion in leases yet to begin. Across Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, Amazon and Alphabet, roughly $1.09 trillion is future payments on lease contracts that have not started.

The mechanical concern is cancelability. Many of these agreements are difficult to exit, so if AI revenue growth slows the payments continue against capacity that is not earning. This follows the weekend's bond-market focus on roughly $70 billion in off-balance-sheet credit backstops behind data-center financing, and is the same question asked at a larger scale.

Why it matters: For anyone holding index funds, these nine names dominate the weighting, so this is a concentration question rather than a sector question. The disclosures are public and the numbers come from filings; what is unresolved is whether the demand arrives on the schedule the contracts assume. It is worth knowing what share of your own portfolio is exposed to that single assumption before deciding whether you care.

◆ Public safety and weather

Recall Aug 14, 2026

FDA raises an egg recall to Class I after linking it to a 17-state Salmonella outbreak

Midwest Poultry Services is recalling 1,589,577 dozen white and brown cage-free shell eggs produced at Texas farms between June 6 and July 3, 2026, over potential Salmonella Enteritidis contamination found through environmental monitoring. The eggs carry sell-by or best-by dates between July 20 and August 17, 2026, and were sold under multiple brand names through Kroger and Brookshire Grocery stores across several states.

The FDA reclassified the recall as Class I, its most severe designation, reserved for products where exposure could cause serious injury or death, after linking the eggs to a multistate outbreak that has sickened 98 people across 17 states with 26 hospitalizations. Today is the last of the affected sell-by dates, which means product may still be in home refrigerators.

Why it matters: Check the carton. The affected eggs are identified by plant code and date range rather than brand, so the brand name on the box does not tell you whether it is included, and the full product list is on the FDA notice. Anyone running a restaurant, café or food service operation should verify their supply against the plant codes rather than the label.

Weather Aug 17, 2026

Extreme heat warnings across the Carolinas, with heat indices above 105

The National Weather Service in Wilmington issued an extreme heat warning covering portions of southeast North Carolina and northeast South Carolina from 11am to 8pm today, with an extreme heat watch already posted for Tuesday. Actual highs are in the mid-to-upper 90s but humidity is pushing the heat index above 105 across a wide band, and past 110 in southern Georgia, coastal South Carolina, the Florida Panhandle and the mid-South. Charlotte is under a heat advisory with a triple-digit heat index. Today is the fourth consecutive day of advisories for the Upstate, and forecasters expect dozens of daily records to fall across the South this week. A weakening front over the North Carolina mountains is expected to trigger scattered thunderstorms, some with damaging wind gusts.

Why it matters: Outdoor work in the Charlotte metro is genuinely unsafe in the afternoon window today and tomorrow. Trades crews, detailing, landscaping and construction should be shifting to early-morning schedules, and anyone scheduling on-site work should assume afternoon slots get pushed. Heat indices above 105 with multi-day duration are when heat illness cases stack up, because the overnight recovery is not happening.
