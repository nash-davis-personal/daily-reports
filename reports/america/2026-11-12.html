◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 11

A strong holiday session: S&P 500 back above 7,600

The S&P 500 closed Wednesday at 7,619.38, up 98.45 points or 1.31%. The Nasdaq Composite rose 1.60% to 25,466.19 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.49% to 52,486.49. The Treasury market was closed for Veterans Day, so the session ran without a bond-market read on Tuesday's inflation report.

Chip and AI-infrastructure stocks led. CoreWeave closed at $60.77, up 24.7% from $48.73, after reporting third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion against $3.55 billion expected, a backlog of $132.5 billion (up from $104 billion in June), and a higher 2026 revenue forecast of $13.0 to $13.3 billion. CNBC called it the stock's best day since April 2025. The company also reported a net loss of $709 million.

Why it matters: The index recovered most of last week's drop in three sessions, and one sector did most of the lifting. For someone managing their own money, a market that moves 1% to 2% a day in both directions on a single industry's news is one where a broad fund behaves very differently from a handful of chip names. That is context, and it is not advice.

Developing Nov 11–12

Nvidia's board met on its chief executive; shares fell after the close

CNBC confirmed that Nvidia's board met Wednesday to consider removing chief executive Jensen Huang, following a Wall Street Journal report that directors were preparing to do so. The board opened an independent investigation on Nov 8. No decision had been reported as of about 6:30 a.m. ET Thursday, and the company has made no announcement.

Nvidia rose almost 3% in Wednesday's regular session, then fell between 7% and 9% in extended trading, depending on the report. It was down 6.9% before Thursday's open and sits about 30% below its Oct 6 high. Bernstein cut its price target from $400 to $200. In premarket trading CoreWeave was down 7.2% while AMD and Broadcom were each up about 3%. Nvidia reports quarterly results on Nov 17.

Why it matters: Nvidia is one of the largest weights in the S&P 500, so a move of this size shows up in any index fund or retirement account that tracks it. The practical dates are Nov 17 (earnings) and whatever the board announces before then.

Prices Nov 11–12

Gasoline falls to $3.91 while retail spending slows

AAA's national average for regular gasoline is $3.908 Thursday, down from $3.943 Wednesday, $4.100 a week ago and $4.356 a month ago. It crossed below $4 on Nov 10 for the first time since July. A year ago it was $3.075, and NPR reports AAA says prices remain the highest on record for this time of year. Diesel averages $6.098, down from $6.256 a week ago and up from $3.757 a year ago. ING estimates Gulf crude exports are running at 80% to 85% of prewar levels once pipeline shipments are counted.

The CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor, released Wednesday, shows October retail spending up 0.2% from September and 3.6% from a year earlier, to $561.7 billion, the slowest monthly growth since November 2025. Core retail, which excludes restaurants, rose 0.3% on the month. An NFIB survey cited alongside it found a net 37% of small businesses plan to raise prices over the next three months.

Why it matters: Households are getting some relief at the pump while still paying about 83 cents a gallon more than last November, and anything that moves by truck is carrying diesel at more than $6. Slower retail growth heading into the holidays, with more than a third of small businesses planning price increases, means owners are choosing between margin and volume right now.

◆ Nation

Security Nov 10–12

Treasury Secretary moved to a military base; Iran says Hormuz will not return to its pre-war status

CNBC confirmed, through a person familiar with the situation, that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved from his Washington home to a military base for safety amid threats from Iran; the New York Times reported it first. A Treasury spokesperson said he "will not be deterred by the Iranian regime's threats." Separately, the Justice Department said late Tuesday that three Iranian nationals were indicted on terrorism-related charges tied to nearly 20 attacks or attempted attacks in Europe and the United States.

On the strait itself, Al Jazeera, citing AP and Reuters, reports that Iran's parliament speaker said the Strait of Hormuz will "never return" to its pre-war status and that Tehran rejected a U.S. proposal to transfer its stockpile of 60%-enriched uranium to a third country. Israeli media reported Wednesday evening that Israel's security cabinet ordered stepped-up preparations for a possible attack on Iran. U.S. Central Command says the naval blockade continues. Oil rose about 1% Wednesday.

Why it matters: The domestic effect runs through fuel. Gasoline has eased for a month, and that trend depends on Gulf exports continuing to recover; any escalation shows up first in diesel and then in shipping and grocery prices.

Weather Nov 12–14

Coastal storm for the Northeast, polar air behind it, blizzard conditions in the Sierra

The National Weather Service's Thursday morning discussion has a strengthening coastal low bringing soaking rain to the Northeast Thursday into Friday, with minor coastal flooding possible near New York City at high tide. Rain changes to accumulating snow tonight into Friday over the higher terrain of New York, northeastern Pennsylvania and northern New England; the discussion gives no totals. Polar air follows, with below-freezing temperatures reaching the Deep South by Saturday morning. Gusty winds and dry air raise fire danger across the Southeast today. In the West, a Pacific storm brings heavy snow to the Sierra Nevada Friday night into Saturday, with blizzard conditions likely.

For Charlotte, the forecast is a high of 69 today with gusts to 31 mph, 58 Friday and 54 Saturday, with lows of 36 Friday night, 32 Saturday night and 34 Sunday night and no precipitation. WBTV notes the Weather Service stopped issuing routine freeze warnings for Charlotte, the Piedmont and the foothills after Nov 11 because the growing season has ended, so this freeze will arrive without one.

Why it matters: Travel through the Northeast corridor Thursday night and Friday will be slow. In the Carolinas, the first freeze of the season is the weekend outdoor pipes, irrigation lines and heat pumps get tested, and no warning will be issued for it. Outdoor burning today is a bad idea with the wind.

Health Nov 11

Measles: more than 5,100 cases and eight deaths in 2026

CNBC, citing the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, reports more than 5,100 U.S. measles cases this year as of early November and eight deaths: six in Pennsylvania and one each in New York and Wisconsin. A Johns Hopkins tool estimates the 2026 cost at nearly $83 million, including $56 million in public health response and almost $25 million in lost productivity; those are model estimates. A Pan American Health Organization review of the country's elimination status is expected this week.

Why it matters: A change in elimination status would be a formal marker with consequences for travel advisories and public health funding. For employers and schools, the near-term effect is exclusion periods for exposed people who lack proof of immunity.

◆ Charlotte and the Carolinas

Charlotte Nov 11

A west Charlotte data center seeks a permit for 242 diesel generators

Digital Realty has applied to Mecklenburg County for an air-quality permit covering 242 diesel backup generators, totaling 726 megawatts, at its Moores Chapel Road data center, WFAE reports. The permit would allow more than 100 tons of air pollution a year. Regulators have not decided. The public comment window closes Tuesday, according to the page's summary. The project predates Charlotte's data-center moratorium.

Also from WFAE: residents in Grove Park, Eastover and Hidden Valley are suing to enforce 60-to-70-year-old single-family deed restrictions against duplex and triplex projects allowed under the city's Unified Development Ordinance.

Why it matters: 726 megawatts of backup generation is roughly the output of a mid-size power plant, sited in a residential part of the county, and residents have until Tuesday to comment. The deed-restriction suits will decide how much of the UDO's added density can actually be built in older neighborhoods.
