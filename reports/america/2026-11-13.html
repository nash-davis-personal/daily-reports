◆ Economy and markets

Housing Nov 12

Existing-home sales fall to the slowest pace since 2010

Sales of previously owned homes fell 5.5% in October to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75 million, the National Association of Realtors reported. That is 8.8% below October 2025 and the lowest reading since August 2010. NAR tied the drop to borrowing costs: Freddie Mac's average 30-year fixed rate was 7.33% in October, up from 6.86% in September and 6.25% a year earlier. Reports give the median price as $416,700 for all home types and $421,900 for single-family homes.

Freddie Mac's weekly survey, released the same day, moved the other way. The 30-year fixed rate averaged 7.11%, down from 7.18% and the fourth straight weekly decline, and the 15-year averaged 6.45%. The 10-year Treasury yield, which mortgage rates generally track, rose to about 5.10% on Thursday.

Why it matters: A sale that does not happen is also a moving truck, an inspection, a loan and a set of appliances that do not get bought. For households, the practical point is that owners with older, cheaper mortgages are staying where they are, which keeps listings thin and prices firm even as sales fall. For local trades and lenders, fewer closings means the work shifts toward people improving the home they already have.

Rates and jobs Nov 12

The 10-year yield closes at 5.10%; jobless claims rise to 200,000

In the first full Treasury session since Tuesday's consumer price report, the 10-year yield closed at about 5.10% and the 2-year at 4.86%, each up roughly an eighth of a point from Tuesday's official rates. The 30-year reached 5.43%. Market coverage attributes the move to investors pricing in the possibility of a Federal Reserve rate increase after October's inflation reading.

Initial claims for unemployment benefits rose by 9,000 to 200,000 for the week ended Nov 7, above the 195,000 economists expected and still low by historical standards. Continuing claims fell by 20,000 to 1.697 million. October producer prices are due at 8:30 a.m. today; forecasters expected a 0.4% rise.

Why it matters: A 10-year yield above 5% works through to anything financed over years: mortgages, car loans, equipment loans and business lines of credit. It also means savers are being paid again; Treasury bills and high-yield savings accounts are following the short end up. Whether rates on new loans keep rising depends on what the Fed signals in December, and nobody knows that yet.

Markets Nov 12

Stocks slip; Nvidia's founder resigns as chief executive

The S&P 500 fell 26.98 points, or 0.35%, to 7,592.40. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 189.40 points, or 0.36%, to 52,297.09, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.39% to 25,366.09. The S&P had been down nearly 1% earlier in the session.

Nvidia announced before the open that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO, director Harvey C. Jones was named the company's first board chair, and the board has started a search. The announcement followed a board investigation opened Nov 8. Nvidia shares fell more than 13% in early trading and closed down 4.34%; other chipmakers including AMD and Broadcom rose more than 5%. Nvidia reports quarterly results Nov 17.

Why it matters: The largest company in most index funds changed leaders and the index moved a third of a percent. For someone managing their own money, that is a reminder of what a broad fund does: one holding fell, competitors rose, and the total barely moved. Rising bond yields were the larger weight on the day. Treat this as context only.

Energy Nov 12–13

Oil rises more than $3 after tanker strikes in the Strait of Hormuz

Brent crude settled up more than $3 a barrel on Thursday and traded just under $96 early Friday; West Texas Intermediate was around $89. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines while crossing the Strait of Hormuz, and there were reports of Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure. The President said he would not hesitate to order new strikes on Iran if it threatens U.S. allies or ships in the strait.

Pulling the other way, the International Energy Agency said Friday that global oil supply has overtaken demand for the first time since the war began. Prices have swung between roughly $92 and $98 this week, including a 4% drop Monday on a report, later denied by a Qatari official, that Iran had eased its demands over the strait.

Why it matters: Gasoline fell below $4 this week on the earlier drop in crude. Pump prices follow crude with a lag of a week or two, so Thursday's rise is the thing to watch at the pump before Thanksgiving travel. Diesel, which sets the cost of nearly everything delivered by truck, was already above $6.

◆ Nation

Weather Nov 13–15

A nor'easter brings the first snow; polar air follows into the South

A coastal storm is producing rain along the Interstate 95 corridor and the season's first accumulating snow in the interior Northeast and the Great Lakes snowbelts. Forecasters describe it as a modest storm. The heaviest totals, 6 to 12 inches, are expected in parts of upstate New York and southern Vermont, with precipitation lasting into Saturday.

Cold air follows. The Weather Prediction Center expects highs about 5 to 15 degrees below average on Saturday across the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic and central Appalachians, with below-freezing temperatures reaching the Deep South by Saturday morning and the coldest mornings on Saturday and Sunday. Temperatures moderate early next week. Yesterday's forecast for Charlotte was a low of 32 Saturday night.

Why it matters: First-freeze weekends are when pipes, heat pumps and furnaces that sat idle since March get tested all at once. For households in the Carolinas: disconnect hoses, cover outdoor spigots and run the heat once today. For travelers, snow is confined to higher terrain inland; the coastal cities get rain.

Small business Released Nov 12

Survey: 88% of consumers say they are likely to shop small this season

American Express's annual Shop Small study, released Thursday, found 88% of consumers agree they are likely to shop at small businesses this holiday season, which the company says is the highest level since 2012. Ninety percent of the small business owners surveyed expect to use AI tools to support their business over the holidays. Small Business Saturday, the seventeenth, falls on Nov 28.

Other surveys point the same way with different numbers. Intuit QuickBooks projects a record $118 billion of holiday spending going to small businesses, up 8.3%. These are sponsor-run surveys of stated intentions; the Census Bureau's own business survey put actual AI use at 17% to 20% of firms in the first half of the year, according to Forbes.

Why it matters: Stated intentions tend to run ahead of actual sales, and Wednesday's retail monitor showed spending growth at a one-year low. Still, the two weeks before Nov 28 are when a local shop's hours, gift cards and holiday offers need to be findable, because that is when the "shop small" attention arrives.
