◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 4

The Dow rose 1,569 points as oil had its worst day of the fall and yields eased

The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 3.03% to 53,356.12, which the Motley Fool calls the strongest post-midterm session in 44 years and the best day since April. The S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite 1.44% to 25,736.51. Schwab's morning note attributed the move to the election result ending uncertainty, with the VIX falling toward 18.3 from Tuesday highs near 22. Under the surface the rally was uneven: Microsoft +6.7%, Amazon +6.1% and Apple +5.3%, while Nvidia fell 3.2% and Tesla 3.3%. After the close, Qualcomm fell about 6% and Arm about 2% on earnings, and AppLovin rose about 20%. Crude fell more than 8% on the day, with Brent near $94.69 and WTI near $88.15 in late trading per CNBC quotes recorded on TSD's Macroscope board. The 10-year yield ended at 4.90% on the official par row, down 5 basis points, with the 2-year at 4.58% and the 30-year at 5.28%.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, Wednesday was a reminder that a single day can move an index 3% in either direction around a known event, and that "the market" and "chips" moved opposite ways. Cheaper oil, if it holds, feeds into lower gasoline and freight costs within weeks and takes pressure off inflation expectations, which fell with it.

Jobs Nov 4

ADP: 86,000 private jobs in October; small business added 28,000; professional services shed jobs again

ADP's National Employment Report counted 86,000 new private-sector jobs in October, above the roughly 75,000 forecast, with September revised to 86,000 from 90,000. Service providers added 82,000 and goods producers 4,000 (construction +13,000, mining −8,000, manufacturing −1,000). Trade, transportation and utilities led with 39,000 and education and health added 28,000; professional and business services lost jobs for a third straight month. By size, mid-sized firms added 33,000, small businesses 28,000 and large employers 24,000. Base pay rose 3.2% and gross pay 4.7% year over year, and job-changers got 4.8%; ADP's chief economist said earnings growth for those workers hit its fastest pace since March 2024, helped by bonuses, overtime and more hours. Economists surveyed by Dow Jones expect Friday's BLS report to show about 59,000 jobs added; Morningstar's survey says 65,000.

Why it matters: Hiring is steady and skewed toward consumer-facing and health work, while white-collar professional services keep shrinking. Owners of small firms are still adding staff, and workers who switch jobs are getting paid to do it, which keeps wage pressure alive for anyone hiring.

Treasury Nov 4

Quarterly refunding: $125B, every coupon size held, and two words removed

Treasury will sell $125B in the refunding: $58B of 3-year notes, $42B of 10-year notes and $25B of 30-year bonds. It kept nominal coupon and floating-rate auction sizes unchanged and now says it anticipates maintaining them "for the next several quarters," dropping the "at least" that appeared in February. Analysts read "several" as roughly two quarters, putting possible changes in the first half of 2027, with the 20- and 30-year auctions seen as the likeliest to be trimmed. The statement also moves the 7-year note to a quarterly new-issue cycle starting March 2027.

Why it matters: This is the government signaling, gently, that it may reshape how much long-dated debt it sells next year. Long-end yields set mortgage and business-loan rates, so the mix matters more to households than the headline total.

◆ Science and space

NASA Nov 4

Swift re-entered over the Indian Ocean region after a 22-year run and a failed robotic rescue

NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory, launched in 2004 to catch gamma-ray bursts, re-entered the atmosphere on Nov 4; the U.S. Space Force confirmed the re-entry near Indonesia after tracking its decaying orbit. A privately built three-armed robotic craft from Katalyst Space, launched in July to boost Swift's orbit, ran into trouble and came down in September. NASA is now soliciting industry ideas on whether and how to boost, upgrade or guide Hubble to a controlled re-entry, with any mission needed by 2032. Separately, NASA leadership reaffirmed an Artemis III test flight in 2027 and an Artemis IV Moon landing in 2028, and a U.S. spacewalk at the station is scheduled for Thursday, Nov 5.

Why it matters: The first commercial attempt to rescue a NASA science satellite failed, and the agency is already shopping for the next one with Hubble. The market for servicing old spacecraft just became real, with a public scoreboard.
