◆ Economy and markets

Energy Oct 23 settle

Brent settles at $116.72, up from just over $113 on Thursday

December Brent settled Friday at $116.72 a barrel and WTI at $103.27, up $3.80 (3.8%) on the day, both the highest since May. Middle East tensions were the main driver. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.99%. Stocks fell for a second day: the S&P 500 lost 0.52% to 7,623.68 and posted its second straight weekly loss, while the Dow finished the week slightly higher despite Friday's 653-point drop.

Why it matters: Thursday's edition had Brent just over $113; Friday added roughly another $3.50. Gas and diesel prices tend to follow within a week or two, so anyone who drives for work or prices delivery should expect higher fuel costs into November.

Week ahead Oct 28–29

The Fed, GDP, PCE and big-tech earnings all land midweek

The Federal Reserve announces its rate decision Wednesday, Oct 28, at 2:00 p.m. ET, with a press conference at 2:30. Previews widely expect rates to stay put this month, with attention on whether officials signal a December hike. The advance estimate of third-quarter GDP and September PCE inflation also arrive this week; previews put GDP around 3.0–3.2% annualized and headline PCE near 3.6% a year. Sources disagree on whether PCE comes Wednesday or Thursday, so check the BEA calendar.

Earnings: Microsoft and Alphabet report Oct 28, and Amazon and Intel Oct 29. Investors will be looking at whether AI spending is showing up in revenue.

Why it matters: If inflation comes in hot alongside a hawkish Fed, the 10-year near 5% and mortgage rates above 7% stay where they are. Borrowers waiting on rate relief, and anyone with money in tech-heavy index funds, will feel this week more than most.

◆ Households and weather

Health costs through Dec 7

Medicare open enrollment: some Part D premiums jump sharply for 2027

Open enrollment began Oct 15 and runs through Dec 7, with changes effective Jan 1. Some Medicare Advantage plans are leaving specific areas or cutting benefits such as dental and raising cost-sharing. One Checkbook analysis found some standalone Part D premiums rising as much as 200%, as temporary federal premium subsidies end and the Part D out-of-pocket cap rises. More than 20 million people with Part B will get a one-time $90 payment to offset costs.

Why it matters: Seniors who auto-renew without checking could pay far more next year. Anyone helping a parent or grandparent should have them compare plans on Medicare.gov before Dec 7.

Weather Oct 21–24

Subtropical Storm Josephine stayed offshore; the West had wind, fire and storms

Josephine, the Atlantic season's 10th named storm, formed off the Carolinas on Oct 21 with winds near 65 mph and tracked northeast away from the US. Its main coastal effect was rough surf and rip currents in the Northeast. Out West, strong winds spread a Montana wildfire, Red Flag fire-weather warnings covered parts of the northern Plains, and severe storms fired over New Mexico and West Texas. Forecasters expect quieter weather into this week.

Why it matters: No major US landfall or disaster this week. Atlantic hurricane season runs through Nov 30.
