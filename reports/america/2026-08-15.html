◆ Economy and markets

Data Aug 12, 2026

July CPI rose 0.1% for the month and 3.4% from a year ago

Consumer prices rose 0.1% in July and 3.4% year over year, easing from 3.5% in June. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.2% for the month and 2.5% over the year. Every figure matched economists' estimates.

Two consecutive tame monthly readings suggest the energy-driven price burst earlier in the year is working its way out of the data. Energy remains the volatile input, tied to conditions in the Middle East. Thursday's producer price index for July came in flat month over month.

Why it matters: Inflation near 3.4% with core at 2.5% keeps the Fed on hold rather than cutting or hiking. For anyone carrying a variable rate or waiting to refinance, the practical read is that borrowing costs are unlikely to move much before year-end.
CNBC — July CPI report · BLS Consumer Price Index summary
Data Aug 14, 2026

Retail sales dropped 0.6% in July, the first decline in months

Retail and food services sales totaled $763.6 billion in July, down 0.6% from June, according to the Census Bureau's advance estimate released Friday. Forecasters had expected a gain of roughly 0.1% to 0.2%. Online sales fell 2.2%, the largest drop of any category, and car dealerships fell 2%. Core sales, which strip out autos and gasoline, also weakened.

Several mechanical explanations are in play: the lift from large tax refunds faded, gasoline and auto receipts declined, June promotions pulled some spending forward, and Amazon held its Prime Day event earlier this year than last. Sales were still 5.0% above July 2025, and the May-through-July window ran 6.3% above the same period a year earlier.

Why it matters: Consumer spending is roughly two thirds of the economy, and this is the first monthly decline in some time. It arrives after a weaker-than-expected July jobs report, so the pair together are the strongest evidence yet of a slowing demand picture. Small businesses selling discretionary goods should expect a softer late summer.
CNN Business — July retail sales · NPR
Markets Aug 13–14, 2026

The S&P cleared 7,800 and small caps set their own record to close the week

The S&P 500 closed at a record above 7,800 on Thursday after the softer inflation data eased concerns about another rate increase. Friday was mixed, with the Russell 2000 up 0.54% to close just shy of 3,070 for fresh intraday and closing records.

Small caps outperforming large caps into a record is usually read as a bet on domestic demand and lower financing costs. It sits awkwardly next to the same morning's retail sales miss.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the week produced two signals pointing opposite directions — record equity prices and a consumer pulling back. Neither one settles the question, and position sizing matters more than a call on which is right.
TheStreet — market close Aug 14 · CNBC market coverage

◆ Storms, floods and fire

Active Aug 13–15, 2026

Hawaii County under a hurricane warning as Tropical Storm Lala approaches

Lala formed off Hawaii on August 13 and strengthened through Friday, with maximum sustained winds of 65 mph and movement west-northwest at 14 mph, roughly 440 miles east-southeast of Honolulu as of late Friday. Hawaii County is under a hurricane warning; tropical storm warnings cover Maui County plus Oahu and Kauai counties. Forecasters expect it to reach the islands as a low-end Category 1.

Forecast impacts are 8 to 12 inches of rain across Maui and the Big Island with up to 25 inches at higher elevations, 4 to 6 inches elsewhere in the chain, winds near 75 mph with gusts possibly above 85 mph, storm surge up to 3 feet, and life-threatening surf. A state emergency proclamation was signed and twelve county shelters opened at 6:00 p.m. Friday.

Why it matters: A landfalling hurricane is rare for Hawaii, and the rainfall totals are the larger threat than the wind for an island with steep terrain. Anyone with travel to the islands this weekend should expect disruption.
CBS News — Lala track · Hawai'i Emergency Management Agency
Disaster Aug 13, 2026

Indiana declares a 30-day statewide disaster emergency over flooding

Governor Braun declared a statewide disaster emergency for 30 days and mobilized the National Guard as severe flooding hit the state. The White River at Anderson was cresting near 25 feet, approaching its highest level in more than a century. Local disaster declarations and evacuation orders were issued for Anderson and Yorktown, with 45 flood warnings active statewide.

Why it matters: A river approaching a hundred-year crest through populated areas means damage measured in months of recovery. Statewide declarations open the path to state resources and, if requested and granted, federal assistance.

Ongoing Aug 14, 2026

93 uncontained large fires burning, with nearly 30,000 personnel assigned

The National Interagency Fire Center counts 93 uncontained large fires with 29,612 personnel assigned. Firefighters have responded to more than 48,000 wildfires burning over 6.5 million acres nationally so far in 2026. The Northwest remains the most active area, with heavy activity across Washington and Oregon.

A firefighting helicopter crashed near the Widemouth 2 Fire in central Utah, with the NTSB leading the investigation and the FAA participating. Separately, 50 drone incursions near wildfires have been reported this year as of August 11, 24 of them in Washington; aerial suppression is grounded whenever an unknown drone is spotted.

Why it matters: 6.5 million acres is a heavy season, and personnel is the binding constraint rather than equipment. The drone figure is the avoidable part — each incursion stops air operations while a fire keeps moving.

◆ Science and space

Research Aug 14, 2026

NASA is flying a modified U-2 spy plane into wildfire thunderstorms

NASA is using a modified U-2 to study pyrocumulonimbus storms, the enormous thunderstorms that wildfire heat generates. The mission began flying in July and has already flown over fires in northern Oregon and western Canada. These fire clouds can produce lightning, strong winds and in some cases fire tornadoes.

Separately, a magnetic filament erupted from the Sun on August 14 and sent a coronal mass ejection into space. NOAA models predict a glancing blow to Earth on August 17 that could produce a G1-class geomagnetic storm with high-latitude auroras.

Why it matters: Fire-generated storms start new fires by lightning and are poorly forecast today. Direct sampling at altitude is how that gets better, which matters for a fire season already past 6.5 million acres.
