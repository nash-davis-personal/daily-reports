◆ Markets and rates

Markets Aug 17–18, 2026

Long-dated yields reached levels last seen in 2007 as oil climbed

The 30-year Treasury yield advanced more than four basis points Monday to 5.311 percent, its highest since June 2007, and continued rising Tuesday. The move tracked oil: Brent crude settled near $90 a barrel after a 60-day deadline for the United States and Iran to reach a peace agreement expired Monday, and traders priced in the possibility that higher energy costs keep inflation elevated.

Stocks fell across the board. The S&P 500 finished 0.52 percent lower at 7,745.06, the Dow lost 272.63 points or 0.51 percent to close at 53,459.78, and the Nasdaq Composite declined 0.32 percent to 26,644.91. The S&P had set a record of 7,816 the prior Thursday on cooler inflation data, so this is a pullback from a high rather than a break in trend.

Why it matters: The 30-year yield is the anchor for long-term borrowing costs, so a 19-year high shows up in mortgage rates, in what it costs a small business to finance equipment, and in the discount rate applied to anything with earnings far in the future. For anyone managing their own money, the specific thing that changed is the direction of the rate argument: two weeks ago the debate was about the timing of a cut, and it is now about whether energy prices force a hike before year-end.

Ahead Aug 18–20, 2026

Four retailers and the Fed minutes will settle whether July's spending drop was a blip

Home Depot reports before the open Tuesday. Target and Lowe's follow Wednesday morning, and the minutes of the July Federal Open Market Committee meeting are released the same day. Walmart reports Thursday.

Each covers a different slice of the household. Home Depot and Lowe's show big-ticket home spending, the category most sensitive to the rates that just hit a 19-year high. Target reads discretionary purchases. Walmart, weighted toward groceries and value, shows how lower-income shoppers are holding. The backdrop is July retail sales falling 0.6 percent month over month, the first decline in nine months and well below expectations, following a contraction in July nonfarm payrolls.

Why it matters: One weak retail sales print is noise; four retailers confirming it across income brackets is a trend. Small businesses that sell to consumers get a usable forecast out of this week, particularly home-services and home-improvement operators, whose demand tracks the same big-ticket confidence Home Depot and Lowe's are about to report on.

◆ Weather and public health

Weather Aug 17, 2026

Charlotte hit 98 degrees, breaking a record that had stood since 1911

Monday was the peak of the region's longest heat stretch of the year: 98 degrees in Charlotte, past the August 17 record of 97 set in 1911, on the fifteenth consecutive day at or above 90 degrees across the Charlotte metro and Upstate South Carolina. A heat advisory covered most of the area with heat index values above 105.

A severe thunderstorm watch was issued Monday afternoon for several western North Carolina counties including Ashe, Avery, Burke and Caldwell.

Why it matters: Fifteen consecutive days above 90 with a heat index over 105 is a load problem before it is a comfort problem. It pushes grid demand, moves outdoor work to early mornings, and drives the emergency HVAC and cooling calls that local trades absorb. The 1911 record falling is the datable marker on a stretch that has already been running for two weeks.

Public health Aug 2026

The Cyclospora outbreak tied to recalled lettuce has reached 9,481 illnesses in 17 states

The multistate Cyclospora outbreak linked to iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms de México, served at Taco Bell locations and sold at retail, has grown to 9,481 reported illnesses across 17 states, with at least 398 hospitalizations and two deaths, both in Michigan. Taylor Farms de México recalled all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico from both foodservice and retail channels on July 17.

Separately, the Salmonella Enteritidis outbreak tied to shell eggs recalled by Midwest Poultry Services stands at 98 infections across 17 states with 26 hospitalizations and no deaths. Those eggs carried sell-by dates running through August 17, so the last of them left shelves this week. The FDA is tracking 18 active outbreak investigations.

Why it matters: Nearly 9,500 illnesses makes this one of the larger foodborne outbreaks in recent years, and the produce involved moved through both restaurant and grocery channels. For any business handling food, the practical step is confirming supplier lot documentation rather than relying on a recall notice reaching you.

Public safety Aug 15–17, 2026

Police are seeking additional shooters after five were wounded at Virginia State University

Gunfire shortly after midnight Saturday near the Quad Annexes at Virginia State University wounded five people. A 21-year-old man was critically injured; a 17-year-old boy, a 23-year-old man, a 19-year-old woman and a 20-year-old VSU student sustained non-life-threatening injuries. The shooting came hours after freshman orientation events, at the start of the fall semester.

Camron Harris, 19, who is not a student, was found hiding in a dorm room Saturday afternoon and charged with multiple counts of malicious wounding and use of a firearm. As of Monday, investigators were seeking others who fired weapons during the incident and working to determine whether they were aggressors or acting in self-defense.

Why it matters: The investigation is open and the count of people who fired is not settled, which is the reason the case is still moving three days later. Campus operations resumed and students returned to class.
