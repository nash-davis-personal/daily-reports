◆ Science & space

Launch Aug 30, 2026

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope launched from Kennedy Space Center

NASA and SpaceX cleared the mission after Friday's Launch Readiness Review, and the Falcon Heavy lifted off from Launch Complex 39A at 7:26 a.m. EDT. Weather had been the open question: the Space Force's 45th Weather Squadron put favorable conditions at 50% Saturday on cumulus cloud and surface electric field concerns, improving to about 70% by Sunday morning as clouds broke. A backup opportunity had been held for 7:22 a.m. Monday. The two side boosters were set to return to landing zones at Cape Canaveral.

Roman is heading to the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point, roughly a million miles from Earth, where the James Webb telescope operates. Its instrument surveys the sky in infrared with a field of view about 100 times wider than Hubble's, which is what makes the statistical work possible: measuring dark energy's effect across large-scale structure, and detecting exoplanets by microlensing at a volume no earlier observatory could reach. Spacecraft health and cruise milestones were still being reported at press time.

Why it matters: Roman is NASA's next flagship astrophysics observatory and it launched about nine months early, which is an unusual outcome for a program of this size. Its survey data will be public and enormous, and it becomes the reference dataset for exoplanet and cosmology work for the next decade.

◆ The economy

Announced Aug 28, 2026

The administration announced an agreement covering 65 billion barrels of Venezuelan reserves

President Trump said Friday that the United States had entered an agreement giving a US-aligned arrangement majority control over more than 65 billion barrels of proven Venezuelan oil reserves, at no cost to the American taxpayer. He credited Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, working with Venezuelan interim president Delcy Rodriguez, and said the transaction more than doubles American oil reserves. He also said it should bring down prices at the pump.

The market response on the day was small. Brent settled at $88.29, down 0.26%, and WTI opened around $83.54. Reports of the Venezuela arrangement pressured prices, and analysts attributed more of the week's softness to earlier reports that Iran and Oman had agreed to allow additional traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. Proven reserves in the ground are a long-dated quantity; the timeline from an announced concession to incremental barrels at a US refinery runs in years, and Venezuelan production capacity has been constrained by decades of underinvestment.

Why it matters: For a household or a small business, the near-term effect on fuel costs is likely small. Reserves are a stock, and pump prices respond to flow — refinery runs, current production and the crack spread. The concrete thing to watch is Venezuelan export volume in the monthly data over the next several quarters, and whether any of it actually lands on the US Gulf Coast.

Calendar Sept 1–4, 2026

A short week with the August jobs report at the end of it

US markets are closed Monday, September 1 for Labor Day. ISM Manufacturing lands Wednesday, September 2, alongside the final S&P Global manufacturing PMI and construction spending. JOLTS job openings, factory orders and the Fed's Beige Book follow Thursday. The Employment Situation for August — the nonfarm payrolls report — publishes Friday, September 4 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. The FOMC then meets September 15–16.

Friday's report carries more than usual weight. On August 28 the Bureau of Labor Statistics published its preliminary annual benchmark revision, cutting estimated total nonfarm employment through March 2026 by 79,000 and private employment by 178,000, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole keynote the same morning to say the central bank may need to raise rates in the coming months. The two-year Treasury yield moved from 4.22% to 4.30% on the keynote.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, the September FOMC meeting is now a live decision rather than a formality, and Friday's payroll number is the largest single input into it between now and then. A soft print and a hawkish Chair pull in opposite directions.

◆ Public health

Health Aug 25, 2026

Two measles deaths in Pennsylvania are the first in the United States this year

The Pennsylvania Department of Health confirmed two measles-related deaths. Both people lived in Lancaster County and were unvaccinated, and one of the two was an infant. They are the first confirmed measles fatalities in the United States in 2026 and Pennsylvania's first in 35 years.

The state has confirmed 393 measles cases across 28 counties this year, with roughly half of them in Lancaster County. About 20% of Pennsylvania's cases this year have required hospitalization. The health department reported that none of the confirmed cases were in people who had received both recommended doses of the MMR vaccine; two doses are about 97% effective at preventing infection.

Why it matters: Measles is among the most transmissible diseases known and it spreads through populations with low vaccination coverage rather than uniformly. The practical takeaway for a family is a records check: confirm two documented MMR doses, particularly before travel or before an infant too young for the first dose is around unvaccinated contacts.
