◆ Markets & the economy

Markets Close: Mon, June 22

Small caps hit a milestone; the big indexes go nowhere

The Russell 2000 closed at 3,000 for the first time ever, up 2.12%. The rest of the tape was quiet: the S&P 500 added 0.12%, the Dow rose 0.44%, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.27%. Chip names led the morning bid — Micron up about 4%, with gains in Western Digital, SanDisk, Intel, and TSMC.

Why it matters: A first-ever close at a round number for the small-cap index signals breadth widening beyond mega-cap tech — historically a sign of broader risk appetite. For someone managing their own money, it's a reminder that the index that's been left behind can move fast when sentiment shifts.

Energy June 22

Oil eases below $77 as U.S.–Iran talks reportedly progress

U.S. crude futures slipped to trade below $77 a barrel after Iran said there had been major progress in recent discussions with the U.S., with both sides pointing to a peace deal within two months. Energy commodities fell on hopes of restored supply; lingering expectations of a hawkish Fed kept a lid on Treasuries.

Why it matters: Energy has been the main driver pushing CPI and PCE higher, so a sustained move down in crude would take pressure off the inflation read the Fed is watching most closely. Cheaper fuel also flows straight to household and small-business costs.
Schwab — market update
Data ahead Thursday, June 25

Quiet calendar now, May PCE and Q1 GDP on Thursday

The early-week economic calendar is light, but it ramps up: Thursday brings the May Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — plus the third estimate of first-quarter GDP. After last week's Fed meeting dropped its prior rate-cut outlook, this is the next hard read on whether inflation is still running hot.

Why it matters: PCE is the number the Fed weights most. A hot print reinforces "higher for longer" on rates (and on anything financed); a cooler one reopens the cut debate. Expect Thursday to set the tone for rates through the rest of the month.

◆ Weather, science & the regions

Weather June 22 outlook

Western heat wave and a broad severe-storm threat run through midweek

NOAA flagged above-average heat across the West — 100°+ from west and southwest Texas into the Southwest and interior California valleys. Separately, a potential severe-weather outbreak stretched from the High Plains to the Mid-Atlantic, with damaging winds, large hail, and flash flooding; central Arkansas was singled out for up to 6 inches of rain. The Atlantic basin is quiet, with no tropical formation expected over the next seven days.

Why it matters: Extreme heat strains the grid and outdoor labor; the storm corridor threatens travel and property across a wide swath of the central and eastern U.S. through the first week of summer.

Space Launch: today, June 23

SpaceX flies its first Starfall reentry cargo capsule

SpaceX launched its first uncrewed Starfall capsule this morning at 6:53 a.m. EDT on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral. The vehicle is designed to carry up to 1,000 kg of cargo and return to Earth under parachutes in the Pacific, demonstrating point-to-point delivery through space and in-space manufacturing. The launch follows SpaceX's June 12 stock-market debut on the Nasdaq.

Why it matters: It's an early step toward moving physical goods through space rather than around the planet — a long-horizon shift in logistics, and a marker of how fast commercial spaceflight is widening beyond satellites.
