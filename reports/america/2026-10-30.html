◆ The economy and markets

Fed Oct 28

The Fed holds rates at 3.75%–4%, with two officials voting for a hike

The vote was 10–2. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. The range has been unchanged since a quarter-point hike in September. The statement said activity has been expanding at a solid pace and inflation remains elevated relative to the 2% goal. Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role in the decision. Markets had priced roughly a 7% chance of a hike going in, and pricing now leans toward one more quarter-point increase in December.

Why it matters: Borrowing costs stay high through at least December. Variable-rate debt, credit lines and new loans won't get cheaper soon, and savings yields stay elevated.
CNBC Yahoo Finance live TD Economics
Data Oct 29

Q3 GDP grows 3.4%; September inflation runs 3.6% year over year

The BEA's advance estimate put real GDP growth at a 3.4% annual rate, up from 2.2% in Q2 and above the roughly 3.2% consensus. For the quarter, the PCE price index rose 2.1% annualized and core PCE 2.5%, down from 5.0% and 3.3% in Q2. The monthly numbers were hotter: PCE prices rose 3.6% in the year to September, with core up 0.3% on the month. Advance GDP readings have historically been revised by about half a point; the second estimate comes Nov 25.

Why it matters: A strong economy plus inflation running well above 2% is the mix that keeps a December hike in play. For households, that means wages and jobs look solid while prices keep rising faster than the Fed wants.

Markets Oct 29

Alphabet's record drop pulls the Nasdaq down 1.4% while the average stock rises

The S&P 500 slipped 0.30% to 7,720.39, ending a three-day win streak, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.38% to 26,184.72. Alphabet closed down 12.64%, about $500B of market value, after free cash flow went negative on AI spending. Microsoft ended roughly flat after strong Azure growth. The equal-weight S&P 500 rose about 1.2%, and small caps rallied. After the close, Amazon fell about 7% on an earnings miss despite 39% AWS growth, and Intel fell about 23%. One recap put the 10-year Treasury yield near 5.04%.

Why it matters: For anyone holding a broad index fund, a handful of mega-caps now move the headline number more than the rest of the market combined. Days like Thursday show how concentrated that exposure is. Context only; no investment advice here.

◆ Charlotte and public safety

Charlotte Oct 27

The Clearing House Payments Co. will bring 200 jobs and $11.5M to uptown Charlotte

Gov. Josh Stein announced the payments company will open an operations center and client hub in uptown Charlotte. The new jobs average $162,322 a year, against a Mecklenburg County average of $90,706, and the project qualifies for a state Job Development Investment Grant. Charlotte beat Atlanta, Dallas and Tampa. Hiring is planned for 2028–2032, per The Charlotte Ledger. The city and county count about 5,172 announced jobs and $563.7M in investment over the past 12 months.

Why it matters: Charlotte's banking and payments cluster keeps adding high-wage jobs, which supports local spending and service businesses uptown over the next several years.
NC Commerce WFAE Queen City News
Recall Oct 29

About 338,800 DREO space heaters recalled over fire risk

The CPSC announced a recall of DREO model DR-HSH015 space heaters, which can overheat and catch fire. DREO received 17 reports of ignition, including three with property damage and one burn injury. The same CPSC batch included about 4,726 Ariston-made NTI and HTP gas boilers with condensate dishes that can break.

Why it matters: Heating season is starting. Anyone with a DREO heater should check the model number and stop using affected units.
KWCH CPSC release (PR Newswire)
