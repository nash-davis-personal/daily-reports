◆ Markets

Markets August 3

A record Dow close, cheaper oil, and Amazon's third trillion

The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed Monday at 53,178.41, up 693.38 points or 1.32%, a record. The S&P 500 rose 1.48% to 7,600.50, roughly 0.1% below its own record, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 2.1% to 25,913.9. The move followed the weekend statement that planned strikes against Iran were being held off at the urging of regional allies, which sent Brent crude down 4.7% to $83.77 a barrel.

Amazon's market capitalization passed $3 trillion for the first time, making it the fifth US company to reach that level. The move extended a post-earnings run built on AWS revenue of $42.2 billion, ahead of the roughly $40.5 billion Wall Street expected. Hyperscaler results have generally been strong enough this season to keep investors comfortable with very large AI capital spending plans.

Why it matters: Falling crude reaches consumers within a few weeks at the pump and lowers input costs across freight and manufacturing, while a record index level with the S&P still short of its own high shows the gains concentrated in a handful of large names. For anyone holding a broad index fund, most of this month's move traces to the same small group of companies.

Earnings August 4

SpaceX reports as a public company for the first time, with a $116 billion lockup two days behind it

SpaceX releases second-quarter results and holds a call at 4:30 p.m. ET today, its first as a public company. It listed on June 12 at $135 a share, ran as high as $225.64 on a valuation above $2.6 trillion, and has since fallen back, closing recently around $119.85. The IPO lockup expires Thursday, August 6, releasing roughly 911.5 million pre-IPO shares worth about $116 billion at current prices.

The numbers investors have flagged are Starlink profitability, launch economics, AI-related capital spending and capital allocation. Starlink reported 10.3 million subscribers across 164 countries as of March 31, up from 5.0 million a year earlier, on $11.4 billion of 2025 revenue. AMD also reports after the bell today.

Why it matters: A first earnings report and a lockup expiration in the same week is an unusual amount of new information hitting one very large stock at once. Anyone holding index funds has exposure whether or not they hold the ticker, and heavy selling into a lockup can move a stock independently of what the results say.

Earnings August 3

Palantir's US commercial business grew 149% and the company raised its full-year outlook

Palantir reported second-quarter revenue of $1.935 billion, up 93% year over year and 19% from the prior quarter. US revenue was $1.573 billion, up 115%. The split: US commercial at $764 million, up 149%, and US government at $809 million, up 90%. Adjusted operating income was $1.194 billion on an 86% adjusted gross margin.

Full-year 2026 revenue guidance rose to a range of $8.150 billion to $8.158 billion, with US commercial guidance lifted above $3.424 billion, implying at least 134% growth. Shares rose roughly 12% after the release.

Why it matters: The commercial line growing faster than the government line is the cleanest public evidence available that ordinary US companies are spending real money on AI deployment rather than piloting it. That is a demand signal for anyone selling software services, and a valuation question for anyone holding the stock.

◆ The fire season

Disaster Week of August 3

Three fires above 130,000 acres are burning in Oregon and Washington

The Big Grass Fire in Malheur County, Oregon is the largest active wildfire in the country at 304,229 acres. The Coleman Creek Fire in Harney County, Oregon has burned 212,182 acres and remains fully uncontained, pushing heavy smoke across the eastern Oregon high desert. In Washington, the Kaiser Canyon Fire on the Colville Reservation, lightning-sparked on July 16, stands at 134,167 acres.

The Spokane Complex, a cluster of fast-moving fires covered in yesterday's briefing, destroyed roughly 640 structures and prompted evacuations for about 60,000 residents. Nationally, 38,747 fires have burned about 3.45 million acres so far this year.

Why it matters: Smoke from uncontained fires of this size degrades air quality hundreds of miles downwind, which affects outdoor work and anyone with respiratory conditions well outside the fire perimeter. Insurance availability and pricing in the affected counties is the slower consequence.

◆ The economy's next data point

Economy August 7

July payrolls arrive Friday against a weak June baseline

The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the July employment situation report Friday, August 7 at 8:30 a.m. ET. June nonfarm payrolls came in at 57,000, below the 115,000 consensus and down from a revised 129,000 in May. Barclays looks for about 100,000 in July, with the unemployment rate holding at 4.2%.

The FOMC is holding the federal funds target at 3.50% to 3.75% after a 9-3 vote, with the three dissents favoring a 25-basis-point increase on inflation grounds. Markets are currently pricing two 25-basis-point hikes before year end, and bond yields have been climbing as that expectation firms.

Why it matters: Two soft payroll prints in a row alongside pricing for rate increases is an unusual combination, and Friday's number will move both mortgage rates and small-business borrowing costs. For anyone with a variable-rate business line of credit, the direction set this week compounds over the rest of the year.

◆ Space & science

Space August 3

NASA introduced the Crew-13 astronauts, with a spacewalk Thursday and a lunar impact Wednesday

The four Crew-13 members were introduced Monday from Johnson Space Center ahead of a launch no earlier than September 12 aboard a Dragon spacecraft. NASA's Jessica Watkins commands, with pilot Luke Delaney of NASA and mission specialists Joshua Kutryk of the Canadian Space Agency and Sergey Teteryatnikov of Roscosmos, all three first-time flyers, joining Expedition 75.

On station, commander Jessica Meir and flight engineer Anil Menon are preparing for a spacewalk beginning 8:35 a.m. EDT Thursday to stage tools for a roll-out solar array platform. Separately, a spent Falcon 9 upper stage is set to hit the moon early Wednesday, August 5.

Why it matters: Routine crew rotations and station upgrades are the operating cadence of a permanently staffed orbital facility, and the September launch window sets the schedule for the research that follows. The Falcon 9 stage impact is a small but real data point in the growing debris-management problem.
