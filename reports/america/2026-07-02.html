◆ Weather & public safety

Weather Jul 2–4, 2026

A dangerous heat dome bakes the eastern two-thirds of the country through July 4

A prolonged heat dome is pushing temperatures to 100–105°F across the Midwest and East, peaking Thursday and Friday. The National Weather Service estimates more than 102 million people are under extreme-heat warnings, with another 43 million under watches. Baltimore and Washington are forecast within a few degrees of all-time records; DC's July 4 is projected at ~101°F, its hottest on record. More than 100 daily record highs and about 250 record-warm overnight lows are expected through Saturday, with heat indices topping 110°F in the mid-Atlantic. The Charlotte region is inside the affected zone.

Why it matters: Heat this extreme is a public-safety event — cooling centers, grid strain, and outdoor-work risk all rise, and the overnight lows staying warm give bodies no recovery window. Locally, expect peak demand on HVAC and home-services businesses through the holiday weekend.

◆ Economy & markets

The Fed Jun–Jul 2026

The Fed holds a fourth time; the market now leans toward hikes

The Federal Reserve kept its benchmark rate at 3.50%–3.75% for a fourth consecutive meeting — the first stretch under new Chair Kevin Warsh. Core PCE inflation rose to 3.3% in April from 3.0% in December, and elevated energy prices have pushed investor expectations toward higher policy rates. New projections show nine officials seeing at least one hike this year and six anticipating at least two; futures now point to roughly 3.8% by September and near 4% by year-end — a reversal from earlier expectations of cuts. Job gains have stayed low with unemployment little changed.

Why it matters: "Higher for longer, maybe higher still" keeps borrowing costs elevated for small businesses and anyone carrying variable-rate debt. For a personal-money lens: the easy-cut narrative for 2026 is gone, and cash/short-duration yields stay attractive while the Fed sits.

Markets Jul 1, 2026

Stocks start Q3 on a soft note after the best quarter since 2020

US indexes opened the third quarter mixed. The S&P 500 slipped 0.22% to 7,483.23 and the Nasdaq fell 0.66% to 26,040.03 as chipmakers cooled, while the Dow touched a record intraday before finishing roughly flat at 52,305.24. It follows the strongest quarter since 2020.

Why it matters: The market's strength is still concentrated in a few AI-linked names, so a chip wobble can pull the whole tape. Broad-index calm can mask how narrow the leadership has become.

◆ Science, space & the nation

Space Jun 30 – Jul 17

Spacewalk repairs the ISS arm as a busy July launch slate lines up

NASA astronauts conducted a June 30 spacewalk to replace a wrist joint on the station's Canadarm2 robotic arm. Ahead: the Soyuz MS-29 crew (NASA's Anil Menon plus two cosmonauts) launches July 14, and NASA's LOXSAT cryogenic-fluid-management demo is targeted for no earlier than July 17.

Why it matters: Routine-looking maintenance and cargo cadence is what keeps the ISS operating; the cryogenic demo is groundwork for longer-duration missions that need to store propellant in space.

National Jul 4, 2026

The country marks its 250th Independence Day

July 4 marks 250 years since the Declaration of Independence. Among the marquee events, the Navy hosts Sail250 in New York Harbor — an International Fleet Review expected to draw roughly 60 ships from 30 countries, billed as the largest maritime gathering in US history — alongside a slate of civic events and mobile "Freedom Trucks" touring the lower 48.

Why it matters: A milestone holiday concentrated on a heat-wave weekend means large crowds, heavy travel, and elevated public-safety planning — and a burst of local commerce for businesses tied to the celebrations.
