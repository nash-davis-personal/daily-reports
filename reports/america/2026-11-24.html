◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 23

The S&P 500 rises 4.73% for its best day since April 2025

The S&P 500 gained 326.78 points to close at 7,237.17. The Nasdaq Composite rose 6.5% to 24,089.56 and the Dow rose 3% to 49,595.31. The rally followed a week in which the S&P 500 fell 8.6% and closed lower seven sessions in a row, ending Friday at 6,910.39. Monday recovered just over half of that loss. Some early reports put the gain near 4.2% based on futures; the 7,237.17 close is consistent across sources.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own retirement or brokerage account, a one-day jump this size after a correction usually comes with wide swings in both directions for a while. It says little about where prices settle.

Corporate Nov 22 to 23

Nvidia's board reinstates Jensen Huang, and the stock rises 31.48%

Nvidia's 8-K says the board reinstated Huang as president and CEO effective Nov 22, eleven days after his resignation. The board said its review of a national security question included input from senior levels of the U.S. government. An independent investigation continues, and the board formed a committee of independent directors for national security and export control. The announcement came shortly before 9 a.m. ET Monday.

Why it matters: Nvidia is one of the largest weights in the major index funds, so its move accounted for a large share of Monday's gain in ordinary retirement accounts.

Data Nov 24 to 27

A short week puts inflation, GDP and jobless claims on Wednesday morning

Today brings the Conference Board's November consumer confidence index and flash PMIs. Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET brings October personal income and spending with the PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Consensus is 0.3% for the month and 3.8% over the year for headline PCE, up from 3.6%, and 3.2% for core. The same morning carries the second estimate of third-quarter GDP, with forecasts near 3.5% to 3.6% against the 3.4% first read, plus durable goods and jobless claims a day early, forecast near 200,000. The Fed's Beige Book follows that afternoon. Markets close Thursday and close early Friday.

Wednesday's PCE report is the last inflation reading before the Fed's Dec 9 decision.

Why it matters: Inflation running near 3.8% is what keeps borrowing costs high for small-business credit lines, car loans and credit cards. A hotter number Wednesday would add to that pressure before the December meeting.

◆ Business and shoppers

Earnings Nov 23

HPE reports a record year and raises its 2027 forecast; Dell reports this week

HPE's results for the quarter ended Oct 31 showed record operating profit for fiscal 2026 and free cash flow more than three times the prior year's, and the company raised its fiscal 2027 guidance. Its executives credited networking and AI infrastructure demand.

Why it matters: Dell's report later this week is the second read on whether demand for AI servers is holding up after last week's sell-off.

Retail Forecast

Adobe expects $47.5 billion in online spending from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday

Adobe's season forecast puts Cyber Week online sales up 7.4%, about 17.3% of the whole holiday season's $275.1 billion. It expects Black Friday at $12.9 billion, up 9.2%, and Cyber Monday on Nov 30 at $15.1 billion, up 6.2%, with a record 63% of Thanksgiving online spending on phones. The forecast was published in September, before last week's market drop.

Why it matters: Most of the week's online spending happens on phones, so a small retailer's mobile checkout matters more than its desktop site this weekend.
