◆ Markets & the economy

Markets H1 2026

Stocks reopen Monday off their strongest first half in years

With markets closed Friday for the holiday, Monday is the first trading day since a first half that saw the Dow climb 8.9% (its best since 2021), the S&P 500 rise 9.6%, the Nasdaq gain 12.8%, and the small-cap Russell 2000 surge nearly 22% — its best first half since 1991. Analysts now project full-year S&P 500 earnings growth near 25%, up from under 16% at the start of the year, though recent drawdowns in individual chip and AI names show how sensitive the index is to single-sector wobbles.

Why it matters: A broad rally that includes small caps, not just megacap tech, points to more widely shared gains — relevant to anyone managing their own money or a small business's cash. The concentration risk sits in the AI-infrastructure trade, so a pullback there could move the whole index.

Calendar Week of July 6

A light data week sets up CPI and a month-end Fed decision

The week is quiet on both the economic and earnings calendars: final S&P Global and ISM services PMIs for June land Monday, June FOMC minutes come Wednesday, July 8, and weekly jobless claims follow Thursday. The bigger markers are the July 14 CPI release and the Fed meeting at month-end. With little scheduled news, oil, yields, and technicals are likely to drive near-term direction. June payrolls came in soft at 57,000 with unemployment ticking to 4.2%.

Why it matters: Thin calendars leave prices more exposed to headlines and momentum than to fundamentals. For a small operator, this is a stretch where day-to-day market moves say more about sentiment than about the real economy — the signal comes with CPI next week.

The Fed June meeting

The Fed held rates but is evenly split on what comes next

The Fed voted unanimously to keep rates steady at its June meeting, but the committee is divided on the rest of 2026: roughly nine members signaled one or two rate increases would be appropriate, while about nine favored holding or a single cut. Chair Kevin Warsh has flagged bringing inflation down as the priority. Wednesday's minutes will show how that debate is framed.

Why it matters: A dead-even committee means borrowing costs could move in either direction later this year, which affects small-business loans, mortgages, and financing. The near-term path hinges on the July 14 inflation print more than on any single official.

◆ Science & space

Space weather July 3–6, 2026

A stronger-than-forecast solar storm lights auroras across 30 states

A G3 (Strong) geomagnetic storm on the night of July 3–4 drove auroras as far south as Utah, Colorado, New Mexico, and Northern California — two levels above NOAA's G1–G2 forecast. The activity, tied to repeated eruptions from an active sunspot region one physicist dubbed a "Machine-Gun Sun," continues: NOAA's July 5 outlook modeled two more coronal mass ejections from July 1–2 arriving late Saturday into Sunday, with further G1–G2 conditions possible.

Why it matters: Strong geomagnetic storms can disrupt GPS accuracy, radio, and satellite operations, and can stress power grids at the high end. This round has stayed at moderate levels, but the sun's activity is elevated enough that operators of anything grid- or GPS-dependent are watching closely.
