◆ Energy and prices

Markets Jul 22, 2026

Crude rose almost 4% Wednesday morning and the pump average is at $4.06

Brent crude futures for July delivery traded 3.5% higher at $94.20, and front-month West Texas Intermediate was up 3.8% at $87.56. The move followed an overnight round of US strikes on Iran — the eleventh consecutive round — and remarks from Secretary of State Marco Rubio that the Strait of Hormuz remains a sticking point in talks. Iran-backed Houthi forces have separately announced a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia, which puts Red Sea shipping into the same risk calculation.

The national average retail gasoline price is $4.06, up from $4.00 in yesterday's reading. Yesterday's briefing had crude near $82; the tape has moved a long way in a day.

Why it matters: Energy is an input to nearly every price in the economy, so a sustained move of this size feeds through to freight, materials and eventually the inflation prints the Fed reads. For households the effect is immediate at the pump; for small businesses running vehicles or shipping physical goods, fuel surcharges are the first line item to move.

◆ The import-duty floor changes twice this week

In effect Jul 22, 2026

A 25% Section 301 duty on most Brazilian goods began collecting this morning

USTR concluded its Section 301 investigation into Brazil on July 15 and imposed an additional 25% ad valorem duty on most imports. Beginning today, most products of Brazil entered for consumption are dutiable under HTSUS 9903.05.01. Brazil is a top-line source for US imports of coffee, orange juice, beef, steel and aircraft parts.

Why it matters: The duty is collected at entry, so it lands on importers first and shows up in wholesale costs within weeks rather than months. Any business buying Brazilian-origin inputs should confirm the HTSUS classification of what it is receiving before assuming the surcharge applies or does not.
International Trade Insights — USTR finalizes the Brazil action
Expiring Jul 24, 2026

The 10% across-the-board surcharge terminates Friday, and the replacement is not confirmed in force

The flat 10% Section 122 surcharge took effect February 24, 2026 and carries a statutory 150-day limit, which runs out July 24. Extending it requires an Act of Congress, and no extension legislation is pending. Section 122 came into use after the Supreme Court's February ruling in Learning Resources v. United States held that IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariffs, invalidating the duties in place since 2025.

USTR self-initiated two Section 301 investigations to build a replacement that does not carry a rate cap or an expiration date. On June 2 it announced affirmative determinations across all 60 forced-labor investigations and proposed an additional 10% duty on 15 economies that have taken compliance steps and 12.5% on the remaining 45. Comments closed July 6 and a public hearing was held July 7. As of this filing the final action has not been confirmed as in force.

Why it matters: If Friday passes with the surcharge gone and no replacement operative, the average effective tariff rate on US imports drops sharply for however long the gap lasts, and then likely rises again when the Section 301 action is finalized. Importers face two different duty regimes potentially days apart, which makes entry timing a real cost decision this week.

Report Jul 22, 2026

Twenty-four percent of US workers say they are staying in a job they do not want, for the health insurance

The West Health–Gallup Center on Healthcare in America published the finding today: roughly 23 million adults are in what the authors call job lock, staying in a position primarily to keep employer coverage. That is 24% of employees, up from 16% in 2021. Among workers with chronic conditions the figure is 41%.

Why it matters: Job lock suppresses the voluntary quits and job-switching that normally drive wage growth and productivity gains, so a rise of this size is a drag on labor-market dynamism that does not show up in the headline unemployment rate. It is also a direct constraint on new business formation — people who would otherwise start something stay put for the coverage.

◆ Weather and health

Weather Jul 22, 2026

Tropical Storm Bertha is forecast to make landfall in southeast Louisiana this evening

Bertha was roughly 105 miles south-southeast of Mobile, Alabama, moving northwest at about 6 mph with maximum sustained winds of 60 mph. Tropical storm watches and warnings covered the Florida Panhandle through coastal southeastern Louisiana, including Panama City, Pensacola, Mobile and Gulfport. Landfall is expected in southeast Louisiana Wednesday evening.

Forecast impacts are 1 to 4 inches of rain with isolated totals near 6 through Thursday from western Florida to southern Texas, isolated flash flooding especially in urban areas, and storm surge raising water levels up to 4 feet in spots. The system is expected to weaken to a tropical depression by Thursday.

Why it matters: A slow-moving 60 mph system produces its damage through rainfall and surge rather than wind. Gulf Coast refining and port operations sit inside the warning area, which is the channel through which a storm of this size reaches national fuel and freight costs.

Regulatory Jul 23–24, 2026

An FDA advisory committee takes up 12 peptides for compounding starting tomorrow

The FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meets July 23–24 at White Oak, with virtual public participation, to consider adding a dozen peptides to the Section 503A Bulks List — the list that determines what compounding pharmacies may legally prepare. Substances under discussion include BPC-157, TB-500 and MOTS-c, along with KPV-related substances.

Context: the FDA moved 19 widely used peptides to its Category 2 "do not compound" list in late 2023, and twelve came off that list effective April 23, 2026 after their nominations were withdrawn. A further advisory meeting on additional peptides is planned before the end of February 2027.

Why it matters: These substances are already sold widely in the wellness and fitness market, largely outside the regulated compounding channel. The committee's recommendation determines whether that demand moves into pharmacies operating under federal oversight or stays in gray-market supply, which is the practical difference in whether a buyer knows what is in the vial.
