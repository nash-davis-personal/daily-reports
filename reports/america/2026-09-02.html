◆ The economy

Breaking Sept 1, 2026

Energy repriced, and equities and bonds moved against each other

Crude rose more than 5% on Tuesday. WTI settled around $90.80, up roughly 5.9% on the day, and Brent traded near $94 to $95, both the highest since late July. The move followed US military strikes on Iranian targets around the Strait of Hormuz, which US officials described as a response to an attempt to mine the waterway and to an earlier attack on a US base in Jordan; two oil tankers had been attacked in the strait. Over the past month crude is up about 13%, and about 38% against the same point last year.

Equities fell as the energy move fed inflation expectations. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed down 419.02 points, or 0.79%, at 52,766.88; the S&P 500 fell 0.71% to 7,631.47; the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.03% to 26,099.77. The 30-year Treasury yield rose 3 basis points to 5.243%. The President met oil and gas executives at the White House on Tuesday to discuss bringing gasoline prices down.

Why it matters: Fuel is a direct input for every trade, delivery and service business, and a six-week high in crude shows up in pump prices and freight surcharges within weeks. For anyone managing their own money, an energy-led inflation impulse arriving in the same week the Fed is weighing a hike is the specific combination that moves both stock and bond prices at once, which removes the usual offset between them.

Data Sept 1, 2026

Openings edged up while hiring fell to a seven-month low

The July JOLTS release put job openings at 7.27 million, an increase of 89,000 and roughly in line with the 7.3 million consensus. The hires side moved the other way: hires fell by 278,000 and the hiring rate dropped 0.2 percentage points to 3.2%, the lowest since February. Employers are posting roles without filling them at the pace the postings imply.

Two more readings land this week. ADP's August private payrolls report, released this morning at 8:15 a.m. ET, was forecast at 47,000 against 44,000 in July. Weekly oil inventories and the Federal Reserve's Beige Book also publish today. The Bureau of Labor Statistics August jobs report follows on Friday, September 4.

Why it matters: A high opening count with a falling hiring rate describes a labor market that is advertising more than it is absorbing, which usually shows up first as longer job searches rather than as layoffs. For a small employer it means posted wages are still competitive but candidates are more available than the headline unemployment rate suggests.

Policy Aug 28 – Sept 1, 2026

A rate increase moved to the front of the September meeting

Following Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole remarks on inflation, market-implied odds shifted toward tightening at the September FOMC meeting. Fed funds futures now show roughly a 56% probability of a quarter-point increase, per the CME FedWatch tool, and CNBC characterised the decision as a coin flip with hike odds rising. Analysts quoted in the coverage said the outcome hinges on the next inflation data, and that absent a meaningful downside surprise an increase is the most likely result.

Why it matters: Small business credit, SBA loan pricing, equipment financing and commercial mortgages all reset off the short rate. A September increase would move the direction of borrowing costs for the first time in the current cycle, which matters more for a business planning capital spending than the size of any single move.

◆ Washington and the courts

Policy Sept 1, 2026

The House passed stopgap funding

The House passed a stopgap funding measure on Tuesday, September 1, removing the prospect of a federal government shutdown in the near term. The practical effect is that federal payments, agency operations, permitting and Small Business Administration loan processing continue without a lapse in appropriations.

Why it matters: A shutdown stalls SBA loan approvals, federal contract payments and permit reviews, all of which flow directly into small-business cash flow. Removing that risk from the calendar means one fewer reason for a lender or a client to defer a decision this quarter.

Legal Aug 31, 2026

The FTC and 22 states sued Amazon over its search ad auctions

The Federal Trade Commission and 22 state attorneys general filed suit in the US District Court for the Western District of Washington. The complaint alleges that for more than seven years Amazon told advertisers they would pay one cent above the second-highest bid, while inserting an undisclosed internal figure it called a "soft reserve price" that functioned as a bid on Amazon's own behalf, so winners paid their own bid roughly 80% of the time. The agency puts the cost at $20 billion or more across more than a million brands and sellers, covering Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display. Amazon said the suit misunderstands how its auctions work and shows no harm to shoppers.

Why it matters: More than a million US businesses buy these ads, and if the allegations hold, their historical customer-acquisition costs were higher than the platform reported. Any seller building a budget on Amazon ad performance is working from numbers that are now contested in federal court.

◆ Weather, space and infrastructure

Weather Sept 2, 2026

The Atlantic is empty at the climatological peak while the Pacific runs hot

The National Hurricane Center lists no active Atlantic tropical cyclones as the season reaches its early-September peak, having issued its last advisory on Tropical Depression Edouard. NOAA is maintaining its below-normal seasonal outlook of 8 to 14 named storms, 3 to 6 hurricanes and 1 to 3 major hurricanes. The Eastern and Central Pacific are active: Hurricane Karina is a Category 4 with sustained winds near 140 mph, Hurricane Lowell is a Category 4 near 130 mph, and Tropical Storm Marie is forecast to reach hurricane strength today.

Why it matters: A quiet Atlantic at peak season is the single largest variable removed from Southeast property, insurance and construction planning for the next several weeks. It is a window, not a verdict, since the season runs to November 30.
