◆ Economy and markets

Markets Nov 23, premarket

Stock futures rebound after the S&P 500's 8.6% weekly loss

At 2:29 a.m. ET, Reuters had Nasdaq-100 futures up 3.02%, S&P 500 futures up 2.24% and Dow futures up 1.58%. Later readings from other outlets ran slightly higher. Nvidia was up about 8.7% before the open after falling 14% on Friday, and Anthropic was up about 4.8% after an 8.5% drop.

Friday's closes were 6,910.39 for the S&P 500, 48,147.33 for the Dow and 22,617.73 for the Nasdaq Composite. The S&P and Dow sit more than 10% below their record closes and the Nasdaq about 18% below its peak. Overnight, South Korea prepared a market-stabilization fund, Taiwan restricted short selling and China cut margin requirements. Gold traded near $3,615 an ounce and Brent crude near $92 a barrel.

Why it matters: Futures show where traders expect the open, and they can reverse during the session. Anyone checking a retirement account today should compare against Friday's close and wait for the 4 p.m. number.

Week ahead Nov 23 to 27

A short week carries the Fed's preferred inflation gauge and a GDP revision

Monday at 9:45 a.m. ET brings S&P Global's flash November surveys. Forecasters expect the services index at 55.8, down from 56.1, and manufacturing at 54.6, down from 54.9. Wednesday brings October PCE inflation, the second estimate of third-quarter GDP and October new-home sales. US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close at 1 p.m. ET on Friday.

The Federal Reserve's last meeting of the year is Dec 8 to 9. CME FedWatch implied about a 96% probability of a quarter-point increase. HPE reports earnings today and Dell and Analog Devices report Tuesday.

Why it matters: A hike would lift rates on credit cards, business credit lines and other floating-rate debt. Wednesday's inflation figure is the last major price reading the Fed sees before it meets.

Retail Nov 26 to 30

Retailers expect 182.7 million shoppers this weekend, fewer than last year

The National Retail Federation's survey puts Thanksgiving-weekend shoppers at 182.7 million, down from 186.9 million. Black Friday leads with 126 million planning to shop, and Cyber Monday follows at 66.1 million. In the survey, 82% said they are taking steps to manage holiday costs, 59% plan to use AI while shopping, and expected gift card spending of $26.8 billion is the lowest since 2015. Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion in online Black Friday sales, up 9.2%. Small Business Saturday is Nov 28.

Why it matters: Fewer shoppers with tighter budgets means stores compete harder on price and convenience. More of the spending is moving online, and a majority of shoppers now say an AI tool will be part of how they choose.

Corporate Nov 23

CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler, and a federal probe tied to Nvidia chip exports is confirmed

Reuters reports that CrowdStrike agreed to an all-stock deal valuing Zscaler at about $43.9 billion. Separately, CNBC reports that the Director of National Intelligence confirmed a US investigation into Megaspeed, a Singapore data center operator, which the Commerce and Justice departments said began in 2024 over possible export-control violations. Megaspeed says it operates lawfully and no wrongdoing has been established. House committees have set closed-door interviews with Nvidia's former chief executive and its general counsel for Dec 1 and 2.

Why it matters: Nvidia is one of the most widely held stocks in index funds, so news about it moves ordinary retirement accounts. The security deal is one of the year's larger technology mergers and will need regulatory review.

◆ Nation, weather and infrastructure

Weather Nov 23 to 27

Storms on both coasts are forecast to cross the busiest travel days

Forecasters expect heavy rain, wind and high tides on the West Coast early this week. San Francisco's mayor asked residents to avoid nonessential travel from Monday afternoon through Wednesday afternoon, and Fox Weather expects flight delays at Seattle and San Francisco. In the East, Monday and Tuesday look clear. A storm develops over the Deep South on Wednesday, moves northeast on Thanksgiving Day and leaves New England and the Great Lakes wet on Friday. The National Weather Service in Boston called it a potentially significant coastal storm with gusts that could top 50 mph. The Midwest could see rain and some wet snow.

These are forecasts and the track can shift. AAA expects 81.5 million people to travel for the holiday.

Why it matters: In the eastern third of the country, Monday and Tuesday are the safer days to drive or fly, and Wednesday evening is the riskiest. Check the local forecast and your airline before leaving.

Infrastructure Nov 22

Officials say recent air travel, email and banking outages were technical faults

The FAA said router upgrades caused a flood of data that led to a nationwide ground stop lasting hours earlier this month, with no sign of a cyberattack. Microsoft said an Outlook outage came from an issue during a network capacity upgrade. A JPMorgan disruption slowed some bank transactions. On Sunday, the Director of National Intelligence said more disruptions are possible while systems are upgraded, and the Treasury Secretary said people should not be concerned about the security of financial institutions.

Why it matters: Officials are telling the public to expect occasional interruptions during upgrades. For holiday travelers that means leaving extra time and keeping a second way to pay.
