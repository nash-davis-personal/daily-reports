◆ The economy

Data Sept 4

August payrolls came in at 162,000, roughly triple the forecast

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 162,000 jobs added in August against a Dow Jones consensus near 53,000. The unemployment rate held at 4.1%, matching expectations. June and July were both revised upward, which removes some of the weakness that had built the case for cuts — July had originally printed a 23,000 decline.

The market treated it as a hawkish surprise. Treasury yields rose across the front end, with the 2-year reaching its highest level since January 2025, and fed funds futures moved to roughly a 58% probability of a hike at the September meeting on the CME FedWatch tool. Prediction-market pricing on Kalshi ran lower at 52–53% for the same question on the same day, so the "market expects a hike" framing depends on which venue you read.

Why it matters: The next Fed decision is September 16, and the last major input before it is the August CPI release on September 11. A hot labor number plus a rising oil price is the combination that keeps the policy rate where it is or moves it up, which flows directly into small-business credit lines, mortgage rates and any variable-rate borrowing.
CNBC — market coverage ◆ TheStreet ◆ Reuters via Detroit News
Energy Sept 4–6

Gasoline hit its highest-ever September price at $4.15 a gallon

AAA's national average for regular reached $4.15 on September 4 and sat at $4.147 on September 6, a record for the month and 24.6% above a year ago. State spreads are wide: California at $5.81, Indiana at $3.45. Brent held near $95–96 and finished the week up close to 9%.

The supply cause is the Strait of Hormuz, where throughput has fallen to about 4.9 million barrels a day from 21.6 million before the current US-Iran exchange of strikes; tanker traffic through the strait remains a fraction of normal. The EIA has raised its price outlook accordingly.

Why it matters: Fuel is a direct input for every trades business, delivery operation and commuting household, and it feeds the CPI print due September 11 that the Fed will read four days later. A record September pump price also lands in the middle of the strongest labor data of the quarter, which is the combination that makes the inflation path hard to call.

◆ Public safety and weather

Developing Sept 6

Five dead after an Amazon cargo jet overran a runway at Miami International

21 Air Flight 7598, a Boeing 767-300 freighter operating for Amazon Air and arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico, overran the runway on landing at around 2 p.m. local time Sunday, struck multiple vehicles, and caught fire. Five people were killed and others injured. More than 60 fire units responded. The airport went to a full ground stop with all runways and taxiways closed; two of four runways had reopened after 5 p.m. ET under a ground delay program. The aircraft was 32 years old, originally built for passengers and converted to freight in 2015. The NTSB is gathering information and the FAA will investigate.

Why it matters: Miami International is one of the busiest airports in the country and the largest US gateway for Latin American air cargo, so a multi-hour closure on a holiday weekend ripples through both passenger schedules and freight. Figures remain preliminary while the NTSB works.

Weather Sept 5–6

Hurricane Lowell rebuilt to Category 5 and Hawaii is under warnings

Lowell formed August 27, first reached Category 5 on September 2, weakened to Category 3 by September 4, then rapidly re-intensified on September 5 to a minimum pressure of 922 mbar. The National Hurricane Center issued a hurricane watch for the eastern portion of Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument and a tropical storm watch for Kauai County on September 6, upgrading both to warnings later in the day. Tropical moisture starts reaching the islands late Sunday, with the closest pass expected Monday evening. Kauai's forecast calls for 6 to 10 inches of rain with local maxima near 14 inches, plus storm surge and high wind.

Why it matters: A major hurricane passing close to the Hawaiian chain threatens flooding, power loss and interisland transport on Kauai and Niihau. Lowell is forecast to stay a major hurricane through Monday, and rapid re-intensification cycles like this one leave less lead time than a steady track would.

◆ Around the country

Policy Sept 2–4

Los Angeles Unified barred students from generative AI on district devices

Board leaders confirmed at a September 2 committee meeting that generative AI is off limits to all students on district-issued hardware in the nation's second-largest school system, a consequence of the new screen-time policy adopted for the 2026-27 year. The restriction covers Gemini inside Google Classroom, Docs, Sheets and Slides. Students can still see AI summaries above Google search results but cannot interact with the chatbot there. Previously, students 13 and older could access generative AI after completing a guidelines course.

Why it matters: LAUSD's choices tend to set a reference point other large districts cite, and several urban districts have adopted similar temporary bans while they write policy. For families and edtech vendors, the practical effect is that district hardware and personal hardware now have very different capabilities.
