◆ Economy and markets

Markets Oct 22–23

Two down days: tech first, then oil and yields

On Thursday the Nasdaq fell 2.6%, its steepest drop since June, and the S&P 500 lost 1.21%. Meta fell nearly 7% after raising its capex outlook, and Intel and AMD each dropped about 8%. Apple rose 1.2% to a record.

On Friday the Dow lost 652.82 points (1.23%) to 52,215.16, the S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,623.68 and the Nasdaq slipped 0.21% to 25,955.34, as oil and Treasury yields climbed and defensive sectors also fell. CNBC reports the Dow still finished the week up about 0.4% while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slipped. Coverage also says American Express fell more than 3% after announcing job cuts; that detail is single-sourced.

Why it matters: A rough week for tech-heavy index funds follows a run of record highs. For someone managing their own money, it is a reminder to check how much of a portfolio rides on a handful of AI-exposed names.

Energy Oct 22

Brent tops $113, its highest since May

Brent rose about 4% on Thursday to above $113 a barrel, with WTI near $100, after attacks on a Saudi airport and oil field. The 10-year Treasury yield traded around 5% through the week. The Oct 21 edition had Brent near $109, so this is a fast move.

Why it matters: Fuel, freight and anything delivered gets more expensive again. Businesses quoting fixed prices for jobs that need driving should build in a fuel buffer.

Data Oct 22–23

Households gloomy, layoffs still rare

The University of Michigan's final October sentiment reading was 48.0, up from a preliminary 46.3 and roughly flat with September's 48.1. It beat every estimate in Bloomberg's survey but remains the second-lowest reading on record, behind May's 44.8. Year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6%.

Weekly initial jobless claims fell near 57-year lows, per a Reuters-sourced report Oct 22. One commentary puts the figure at 194,000; that number is not yet confirmed against the Labor Department release.

Why it matters: People are unhappy about prices but mostly still employed, which tends to mean careful spending. Small businesses should expect customers to compare prices harder this holiday season.

Fed Oct 27–28

The Fed decides Wednesday with the 10-year near 5%

The FOMC meets Oct 27 and 28. The Fed last raised its target range 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00% in September. Prediction-market odds of an October hike had fallen to about 14% as of Oct 19, per the TSD Macroscope board.

Why it matters: Variable-rate borrowers and anyone shopping for a loan should know their numbers before Wednesday afternoon. Long-term rates, which drive mortgages, stay high whatever the Fed does.

◆ National

Cyber Oct 21–22

Federal and European agencies push emergency PHP patching

PHP released fixes Oct 21 for two critical flaws, nicknamed PHP2Shell, that let an attacker run code on web servers using the language, which powers a large share of websites including WordPress. The NSA issued patch direction, the EU told organizations that cannot patch to take affected sites offline, and Finland, the Netherlands and France took government sites down while they patched.

Why it matters: Any business or organization with a WordPress or PHP-hosted website should confirm with its host or web person that the site runs a patched PHP version.

Weather Oct 22

Storm brings hail, flooding and high winds to Southern California

The National Weather Service warned of gusts up to 50 mph, penny-size hail, heavy rain and frequent lightning across southeastern Ventura and central Los Angeles counties. A debris flow came down in the San Bernardino Mountains without damaging property, and an early tornado report in San Clemente was later ruled out. In the Pacific, Hurricane Vance strengthened off southern Mexico with no US landfall threat in Friday's advisories.

Why it matters: Burn-scar areas in Southern California stay at risk for debris flows with each storm this season.
