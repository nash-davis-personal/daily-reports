◆ Economy and markets

MarketsOct. 7

10-year yield hits 5.365% intraday; indexes close modestly lower

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.365% on Wednesday, its highest since April 2002. It backed off after the Treasury sold $39 billion of 10-year notes, which helped stocks trim their losses. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 341.41 points, or 0.66%, to 51,179.87. The S&P 500 lost 0.22% to 7,801.77 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.22% to 27,538.69. Both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq had closed at records on Tuesday, with the S&P at 7,818.93. Crude oil was trading near $92 a barrel Wednesday morning, up almost 5% on the day.

Why it matters: The 10-year sets the tone for mortgages, car loans and small-business credit. For a personal investor, short-term Treasuries and cash accounts are paying more than they have in two decades, and long-dated bonds are the holdings that swing most on days like this.

The FedOct. 7

September minutes: most officials see another rate increase by year end

Minutes of the Federal Reserve's Sept. 15–16 meeting, released Wednesday afternoon, say most participants judged that another increase in the federal funds rate would likely be appropriate by year end. The minutes gave no indication of when. Officials agreed unanimously that inflation was still elevated and had made little progress toward the 2% target in recent months. At that meeting the Fed raised its key rate by a quarter point to about 3.9%, its first increase in three years. Analysts quoted Wednesday morning put the chance of another quarter-point move later in October at around 20%.

Why it matters: Variable-rate debt, credit cards and business lines of credit follow the Fed's rate. A business or household carrying floating-rate debt should plan for payments staying where they are or rising through year end.

◆ Weather and public safety

HurricaneOct. 7–8

Isaias becomes the season's first hurricane; northern Gulf Coast landfall expected late Friday or early Saturday

Isaias, the ninth named storm of the 2026 Atlantic season, strengthened into a hurricane late Wednesday night in the Gulf of Mexico. The National Hurricane Center expects landfall along the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday, somewhere between Gulfport, Mississippi, and Panama City, Florida. Forecasts cited by Fox Weather call for a Category 2 storm with winds that could reach 110 mph. A hurricane warning runs from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to Florida's Bay/Gulf county line, with a tropical storm warning from Louisiana to the Aucilla River. Storm surge of up to 7 feet is possible along roughly 300 miles of coast, and the central Gulf Coast could see 3 to 7 inches of rain with up to 10 in places.

Florida declared a state of emergency for 25 counties and Alabama for 40. Evacuations were ordered for Dauphin Island, Alabama, and zones in Okaloosa County, Florida. Chevron is moving non-essential staff off Gulf platforms as a precaution; the forecast track runs east of the main offshore production area. Inland, 1 to 5 inches of rain is forecast across the Southeast through the Carolinas and Tennessee Valley, with flash-flood risk into Saturday. A first hurricane this late in the year is rare: the usual date is early August.

Why it matters: People on the warned coast have about two days to act on evacuation orders. In the Charlotte area the likely effect is heavy rain late in the week, so check drains and travel plans. Follow the National Hurricane Center and local emergency management for current guidance; forecasts change quickly.
