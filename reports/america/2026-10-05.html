◆ Economy

Jobs Oct 2

September payrolls rose by just 29,000, cutting odds of another Fed hike

The economy added 29,000 jobs in September, below expectations and about 40,000 a month over the past year (496,000 total). Markets moved toward one more hike at most, and New York Fed President John Williams said there is no urgency. The major indexes ended the week mixed: the Nasdaq Composite and S&P MidCap 400 rose, the Dow and S&P 500 fell. Ten-year Treasury yields were about 5.26% on Monday.

Why it matters: Weak hiring and a 5%+ ten-year yield together mean borrowing stays expensive while job growth slows. Savers get high yields, and anyone with variable-rate debt or a pending business loan should plan for rates staying up.

Energy Oct 5

Diesel hit a record $6.505 a gallon as the G7 prepares an emergency release

AAA put the national average for diesel at $6.505, the first time above $6.50. Crude fell to about $90 a barrel Monday as Middle East exports rose, OPEC+ held November targets and the G7 prepared to release 100 million barrels of reserves, amid the continuing US-Iran conflict. Analysts say the diesel shortage will not ease soon.

Why it matters: Diesel drives trucking, delivery and trades costs, so businesses that move goods or crews are adding fuel surcharges. Expect higher prices on freight-heavy goods before the reserve release shows up at the pump.

◆ Weather and national events

Weather Oct 3–5

Flood watch across the Southeast; Charlotte storms end the warm spell

A flood watch covered parts of the Southeast through Monday 8 p.m., with a level 2 of 4 excessive-rainfall risk from Mobile, Alabama to the Carolinas. Between 3.5 and 5.5 inches fell in parts of Greenville County, SC, with minor flooding in low-lying areas. Charlotte had heavy showers and storms over the weekend with localized flooding possible before cooler, drier weather this week.

Why it matters: Flooded roads and low-lying properties hit commutes, deliveries and local small businesses first. Check river and road status before driving through standing water.

Security Oct 2

Hackers breached the propulsion system of a US-bound oil tanker

The FBI and Coast Guard confirmed the breach, reported by Bloomberg. Manual controls were available as a backup. Separately, the Defense Manpower Data Center disclosed that data on about 3 million current and former military personnel, including Social Security numbers, was exposed and unencrypted between October 2025 and July 2026.

Why it matters: Both are infrastructure and data-protection incidents rather than local disruptions today. They matter for fuel logistics and for the identity risk to affected service members.
