◆ The economy

Jobs Oct 2

The economy added 29,000 jobs in September and unemployment rose to 4.2%

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that nonfarm payrolls rose by 29,000 in September. Economists had expected about 90,000. The unemployment rate was 4.2%, up from 4.1%, with 7.1 million people unemployed. Health care added 17,000 jobs, construction 11,000 and manufacturing 9,000. Financial activities lost 7,000. Other major industries changed little.

Earlier months were revised down. July went from a gain of 21,000 to a loss of 10,000, and August from 162,000 to 133,000. Average hourly earnings rose 5 cents, or 0.1%, to $37.81, and are up 3.0% over 12 months. The average workweek held at 34.4 hours and the participation rate was 61.8%.

Thursday's weekly data pointed the other way on layoffs: initial jobless claims slipped 1,000 to 197,000 for the week ended Sept. 26, with the four-week average at 200,000.

Why it matters: Few people are being laid off and few are being hired. Someone with a job faces little change. Someone looking for one, or a business counting on customers with rising paychecks, faces a slower market, with wage growth at 3.0% against August PCE inflation of 3.4%.

Markets Oct 1 – 2

Treasury yields fell from 24-year highs and stock futures rose after the report

On Thursday the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.344%, its highest since 2002, before turning lower. Stocks ended nearly flat: the S&P 500 rose 0.19% to 7,666.45, the Dow added 20.51 points to 50,926.56 and the Nasdaq gained 0.04% to 26,871.60.

After this morning's jobs report, the 10-year yield fell to about 5.19%. Dow futures rose about 1.0%, S&P 500 futures 0.9% and Nasdaq futures 1.3%. Yahoo Finance reports bond-market pricing now implies a 16% chance of a Federal Reserve rate increase in October, down from 64% a week ago. Oil fell 3.8% to $89.32 a barrel. Those are pre-market readings and can change by the close.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the same report that signals a weaker job market lifted stock and bond prices, because it lowers the odds of another rate increase. A single morning's move says little about where either market ends the month.

Housing Oct 1

The average 30-year mortgage rate rose to 7.28%

Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.28%, up from 7.03% a week earlier and the highest since November 2023. The survey reflects the run-up in Treasury yields through Wednesday and predates this morning's drop.

Why it matters: On a $350,000 loan, the difference between 7.03% and 7.28% is roughly $60 a month in principal and interest. Rates at this level keep owners with older, cheaper mortgages from selling, which limits the homes available to buyers.

Manufacturing Oct 1

Factory activity kept growing in September while input prices rose at the fastest pace since May

The Institute for Supply Management's manufacturing index was 54.5, against 54.6 in August and a forecast of 55.0. A reading above 50 means expansion, and this was the ninth straight month above it. New orders rose to 55.3 and employment to 52.7. The prices-paid index climbed to 77.9 from 71.1. In the survey, 58.6% of respondents reported paying higher prices, 2.8% reported lower, and no commodity was listed as down in price.

The macro backdrop: TSD's Macroscope board reads the regime as sideways, medium conviction: a sovereign-supply regime holding through a rate shock. Its nearest alert-grade wire resolved Thursday, when prediction-market odds of an October rate hike printed at or below 35% for a second straight 5 p.m. read (27%), and the board cut its rates conviction from high to medium. The 10-year yield remains above the board's 4.75% line. The board was written before this morning's jobs report and has its labor theme at sideways, so a 29,000 print is weaker than the read it carried. It also logs Thursday as a divergence, with hike odds falling on the same day factory input prices jumped. The feed lists no dated catalyst after today's report. (TSD Macroscope board, as of 2026-10-01)

Why it matters: Manufacturers are busy and paying more for materials. Those costs tend to reach shelf prices with a lag, which is the opposite signal from this morning's slower hiring and wages.

◆ Around the country

Space Oct 1

NASA's SpaceX Crew-13 launched and docked with the space station the same day

The Falcon 9 lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 11:10 a.m. ET Thursday. The Dragon docked at 7:05 p.m., 7 hours and 55 minutes later. Aboard are NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney, Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk and Russian cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov. They are starting a six-month science mission and are due back in 2027.

Why it matters: The launch went on schedule despite a forecast that gave only a 55% chance of acceptable weather, and the station has its next long-duration crew in place.

North Carolina Sept 29

Two financial firms opened Charlotte operations with 876 jobs between them

Governor Josh Stein attended openings in Charlotte this week for Capital Group's East Coast operations hub, which the state says will create 600 jobs with more than $116 million in annual payroll, and for Daimler Truck Financial Services' North American headquarters in Ballantyne, a nearly $8 million investment with 276 jobs. The state also announced that STERIS will build a $600 million manufacturing and distribution center in Sanford with 335 jobs. The governor's office says North Carolina's financial-services sector has grown 25% since 2019.

Why it matters: New office jobs in south Charlotte mean more households moving in and more demand for local services, from housing to child care to home and auto care, in the Ballantyne area in particular.

Energy Oct 1

The national average for gas slipped to $4.41

AAA put regular gasoline at $4.41 a gallon on Thursday, down nearly 7 cents from a week earlier. AAA described September as record-setting, with a monthly average of $4.33. Crude oil fell further this morning, to about $89 a barrel.

Why it matters: Fuel is a direct cost for commuters and for any business that runs vehicles. A week of declines helps after a month that averaged $4.33.
