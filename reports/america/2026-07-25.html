◆ Markets and the Fed

Markets Jul 24, 2026

Stocks stabilize Friday after a chip-driven Thursday drop

Equities looked poised to recover Friday as Brent crude fell below $100 a barrel, easing some pressure after Thursday's slide, which was driven by renewed inflation fears and nerves over the scale of AI capital spending. The move was concentrated in semiconductors: Intel fell nearly 8% on the day even after a Q2 earnings beat, with Broadcom, AMD and Micron also lower as investors rotated out of a chip sector that had run up sharply this year.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, this is the AI trade taking a breather rather than an economy-wide alarm. A single day's rotation out of chips isn't a trend, but with a Fed meeting and three of the biggest tech earnings reports landing next week, a concentrated portfolio could see outsized swings in either direction.

Ahead Week of Jul 27

A Fed decision, Q2 GDP and PCE, and Big Tech earnings all land next week

The Federal Reserve concludes a policy meeting Wednesday with its target range at 3.50%–3.75%; under Chair Kevin Warsh the Fed has dropped traditional forward guidance in favor of pure data-dependence, so the accompanying statement will get heavy scrutiny. The same week brings the first read on Q2 GDP and the PCE price index — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — plus earnings from Microsoft, Meta and Apple.

Why it matters: This is the week that sets the tone for borrowing costs into the fall. The rate path feeds directly into mortgages, small-business loans and credit lines; the GDP and PCE prints will show whether growth is holding while inflation stays elevated. Concrete effect for a small operator: watch the PCE number for where financing costs head next.

◆ Inflation and the consumer

Prices Jul 2026

Inflation is still running hot

The most recent Consumer Price Index reading came in at 4.2% over the prior 12 months — the largest annual increase since April 2023 — and it was those inflation fears, alongside AI-spending worries, that drove Thursday's market drop. Next week's PCE data will give the cleaner read the Fed actually acts on.

Why it matters: Sticky prices squeeze both sides of a small business — input costs stay high while customers get more careful about spending. For Charlotte-area SMBs, it reinforces the case for services that cut labor cost or win back time, and it keeps pressure on pricing decisions.

◆ Weather

Flooding Jul 22–24, 2026

Bertha's moisture stalls over the Carolinas and floods North Carolina

Tropical Storm Bertha made landfall on the Gulf Coast and degenerated, but its tropical moisture got pulled into a stalled front over Virginia, the Carolinas and northern Georgia — putting some of the storm's worst impacts hundreds of miles inland. Flash flood warnings hit Greensboro and Winston-Salem Wednesday night, where storms dropped up to six inches of rain — roughly a month's worth — in about 12 hours, and a short stretch of Interstate 40 northwest of Durham briefly closed Thursday morning. Looking ahead, an expansive high-pressure ridge could push central-U.S. temperatures past 110°F early next week.

Why it matters: This is a direct North Carolina impact — flash flooding across the Piedmont with road closures near the Triangle. For anyone in the Charlotte–Gastonia corridor, it's a reminder that inland flooding from spent tropical systems is the real regional risk, and heat is set to return to much of the country next week.
