◆ The economy

Energy Jul 20, 2026

The national average price of gasoline reached $4.00 a gallon

AAA's national average for regular gasoline read $4.003 on July 20, up from $3.872 a week earlier and $3.951 a month earlier — roughly 13 cents in seven days. The increase follows sharply reduced tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, a waterway that normally carries about 20% of seaborne oil, amid the ongoing US–Iran military conflict. Crude erased intraday gains later in the session on reports that Iran signaled openness to talks.

Why it matters: Fuel is an input cost for every vehicle-dependent business — delivery, trades, mobile services — and it flows into freight surcharges within weeks. It also feeds the energy component of the next CPI print, which cuts against the soft June reading heading into the July 29 Fed meeting.

Deal Jul 20, 2026

Brookfield and CPP Investments will take LXP Industrial Trust private for $5.2 billion

Brookfield Asset Management and the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board signed a definitive agreement to acquire LXP Industrial Trust at $61.20 per share in cash, valuing the transaction at approximately $5.2 billion including net debt and preferred equity. The price is a 12.3% premium to LXP's 30-day volume-weighted average and 19.8% to its 90-day average through July 17.

LXP holds roughly 53 million square feet across 108 warehouse and logistics properties in the Sunbelt and Midwest. The deal is expected to close in Q4 2026, after which LXP delists from the NYSE.

Why it matters: One of the year's largest US industrial-REIT take-privates, and a concrete read on how institutional capital is pricing warehouse space after two years of softening rents. For a self-directed investor, a premium of that size signals private buyers see public logistics REITs as underpriced relative to the underlying real estate.

Markets Jul 17–20, 2026

Stocks enter a heavy earnings week off a chip-led losing Friday

The S&P 500 fell 1.01% Friday to close at 7,457.69, the Dow dropped 406.55 points (0.77%) to 52,146.42, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.4% to 25,520.24 — its first losing week in three. The decline was concentrated in semiconductors on concern that AI hyperscalers may trim infrastructure spending.

This week is dense: roughly 135 companies report Wednesday and 168 Thursday, with Alphabet and Tesla both reporting July 22. FactSet projects S&P 500 Q2 earnings growth of 23.6%, which would be a second consecutive quarter above 20%. On rates, the federal funds target range stands at 3.50%–3.75% ahead of the July 29 FOMC meeting; June CPI, released July 14, came in at 3.5% headline and 2.6% core year over year, both below forecast, with core flat month over month.

Why it matters: A chip-led drawdown in a market whose gains have been unusually concentrated in a handful of AI names is a concentration-risk signal worth checking your own allocation against. Hyperscaler guidance this week is the direct test of whether the AI-capex doubt is real. This is context, not a recommendation.

◆ Weather & public safety

Disaster Jul 19, 2026

Flash flooding killed at least eight as 68 large wildfires burned and heat advisories spanned the middle of the country

Flash flooding that began Thursday killed at least eight people across Utah (Sunglow Campground near Bicknell), Arizona (Tucson) and Texas (Uvalde and Kerr counties). Among the dead were a Provo Fire and Rescue captain, his wife and three sons.

Separately, firefighters were working 68 large fires nationwide with more than 17,400 personnel, 140 helicopters and four military C-130 air tanker crews deployed. Heat advisories Sunday ran from the Dakotas to the Gulf Coast with heat indices near 105, and an extreme heat watch covered southeastern Kansas through Tuesday with indices near 110.

Why it matters: Three simultaneous hazards — Canadian wildfire smoke in the East, flooding in the South and Southwest, fires in the West — are driving travel disruption, outdoor-work stoppages and evacuation orders across most of the country. Any business with field crews or shipping through the affected corridors should expect delays.

Food safety Jul 17, 2026

A five-state Cyclospora outbreak reached 1,644 cases and triggered a full recall of central-Mexico iceberg lettuce

FDA and CDC report 1,644 Cyclospora infections with 94 hospitalizations and no deaths across Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio and West Virginia, linked to shredded iceberg lettuce. The most recent reported illness onset was July 13. On July 17, Taylor Farms de Mexico said it would voluntarily remove all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico from the US market and initiate a recall.

Why it matters: One of the largest US foodborne outbreaks by case count in recent years, and the supply pull affects produce sourcing for restaurants and grocers nationwide — a real cost and menu problem for any food-service business through the summer.

◆ Policy with a concrete effect on small business

Trade Expires Jul 24, 2026

The 10% across-the-board import surcharge hits its statutory expiration Friday

The 10% across-the-board import surcharge imposed under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 is set to expire July 24, 2026. The statute caps such tariffs at 15% and limits them to 150 days, so continuing it would require Congressional action. The Court of International Trade held the proclamation invalid on May 7, but a stay meant importers kept paying during the appeal.

Separately, after the IEEPA tariffs were struck down on February 20, Customs and Border Protection opened an administrative refund channel (CAPE Phase 1) on April 20. Whether the surcharge actually lapses on the 24th is not confirmed — no same-window government notice was found either way, so treat the outcome as open.

Why it matters: Any small business importing goods faces a landed-cost change within days, plus a potentially live refund claim on duties already paid. Worth a call to your customs broker this week rather than next.
