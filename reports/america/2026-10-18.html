◆ Economy and markets

Prices Oct 18

Gas prices drift down but stay far above last year

AAA's national average for regular was $4.2988 on October 18, down from $4.3085 Saturday, $4.3585 a week ago and $4.4687 a month ago. A year ago it was $3.0408. AAA's latest note says pump prices aren't budging much because crude remains high; U.S. crude settled above $100 on Friday. AAA said earlier this month that 2026 is the first year the national average has stayed above $4 in October.

Why it matters: Fuel is still the biggest drag on household and small-business budgets. At current trends, prices fall by pennies a week, so delivery, field-service and trade businesses should keep fuel surcharges in place for now.

Week ahead Oct 19–23

Meta, Tesla and chipmakers report as the Fed stays quiet

The busiest stretch of third-quarter earnings so far. Monday: Fifth Third, Cleveland-Cliffs, Steel Dynamics. Tuesday: GE Aerospace, General Motors, Northrop Grumman, RTX and Halliburton before the open, Netflix after. Wednesday: Abbott, AT&T and NextEra in the morning; Meta, Tesla, IBM, Lam Research and Texas Instruments after the close. The Fed is in its pre-meeting blackout ahead of the October 27–28 meeting.

Why it matters: For anyone managing their own money, Wednesday night is the week's swing point: Meta's spending plans and Lam and TI results are the clearest read on whether the AI build-out keeps its pace after the Nasdaq's worst week since June.

Credit Week ended Oct 16

Worries about AI debt spread from stocks to bonds

CoreWeave shares fell 25% for the week to $61.39, against a 52-week high of $141.79. About $7.75 billion of its high-yield bonds trade in the high 70s to low 80s, implying yields near 14–15%. Corporate bond spreads widened more broadly on concern about AI-related financing, while Treasuries rallied and yields fell across the curve. Jefferies projects consensus earnings growth for AI stocks will slow from nearly 100% in 2026 to about 30% in 2027 and flagged rising credit spreads at AI infrastructure borrowers.

Why it matters: When lenders start charging more to fund data centers, that can slow hiring and spending tied to the AI boom. Retirement accounts heavy in tech funds took most of last week's hit; bond funds did comparatively well.

◆ Disasters and safety

Wildfire Oct 16–18

Norte Fire near San Clemente reaches 25% containment; FEMA approves aid

The Norte Fire started at 11:33 a.m. on October 16 near San Clemente in Orange County, California, and has burned about 570 acres. Six homes were damaged or destroyed. Mandatory evacuation orders at first covered about 4,600 homes and 16,000 residents in San Clemente and San Juan Capistrano; some zones have reopened and others remain under orders. Containment rose to 25% by Saturday evening per CAL FIRE updates. FEMA approved a Fire Management Assistance Grant early October 17, which reimburses firefighting costs.

Why it matters: Late-season fires are now a fixture in Southern California. Anyone with clients or family in the area should check evacuation zones before travel.
