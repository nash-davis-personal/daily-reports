◆ The economy & the Fed

Breaking June 17, 2026

Fed holds rates but projects a hike before year-end

The Federal Open Market Committee voted unanimously to keep the federal funds rate at 3.50–3.75%, the fourth consecutive hold and the first meeting chaired by Kevin Warsh. The updated projections were the surprise: the median estimate for the rate at year-end rose to 3.8% from 3.4% in March, with nine officials now seeing at least one hike in 2026 and six anticipating two. The Fed also lowered its GDP outlook and raised its inflation forecast.

Why it matters: The market had been pricing cuts; the Fed pivoted toward a possible hike. For anyone with variable-rate debt, a mortgage in the pipeline, or financing plans, the "lower rates soon" assumption is off the table for now.

Context May 2026 data

Inflation runs hot while retail sales hold up

The Consumer Price Index rose to a 4.2% annual rate in May, the highest reading since April 2023, driven in part by higher oil and gas prices tied to an overseas conflict that began in late February. At the same time, May retail sales jumped 0.9% from April, beating the 0.5% expected — consumer spending is holding even as sentiment stays historically low.

Why it matters: Sticky 4%+ inflation alongside solid spending is what flipped the Fed hawkish. Higher input and energy costs are the squeeze small businesses feel first; budget for them rather than betting on near-term relief.

◆ North Carolina & the region

New June 2026

Charlotte keeps winning corporate headquarters

Japanese banking group SMBC chose Charlotte for its second US headquarters — about 2,000 jobs and a $50.5M investment. SoFi Technologies is opening a regional hub (225 jobs) and AssetMark Financial is building an East Coast hub ($10M, 252 jobs). Regional leaders point to a pipeline of more than 7,000 jobs and $1.4B in potential investment. North Carolina's unemployment sits at 3.9% — half a point under the national rate — and Charlotte's at 3.6%.

Why it matters: Charlotte's growth is the home-field tailwind for a local web/AI studio — more incoming firms and a tight labor market mean more SMBs that need modern sites and automation. Local relationships and chamber networks are the cheapest lead source going.

◆ Weather & science

New June 17, 2026

Tropical Storm Arthur opens the hurricane season

Tropical Storm Arthur — the season's first named storm — formed June 17 off the Texas coast, bringing flooding to the Gulf Coast. NOAA's outlook calls for a below-normal-to-near-normal 2026 Atlantic season: 8–14 named storms, 3–6 hurricanes, 1–3 major. Separately, NASA named the crew for its next Artemis mission, an Earth-orbit flight to test rendezvous and docking with the lunar landers being built by SpaceX and Blue Origin.

Why it matters: Season's open. No direct threat to the Carolinas yet, but a reminder to confirm any coastal-client sites and infrastructure have current backups and contact info before storms ramp up.
