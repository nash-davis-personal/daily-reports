◆ Economy and markets

Energy Oct 31 – Nov 1

OPEC+ is expected to hold December output steady with oil at its highest since April

Three sources told Reuters that OPEC+ agreed in principle to keep December output targets unchanged at Sunday's online meeting of seven core members (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman). Most Gulf producers are still pumping below their targets because of disruption from the war with Iran. A capacity review this quarter will set 2027 quotas.

On Friday, WTI settled up $2.12 at $104.12, Brent's active January contract rose $2.63 to $113.43, and the expiring December Brent contract closed at $117.64. That was oil's highest settle since the end of April.

Why it matters: A hold means no new supply from producers, so fuel and shipping costs stay high for households and for businesses that drive for a living. The official decision should come out later today.
CNBC / Reuters (Oct 31)
Week ahead Nov 2–6

Election Day, the Treasury refunding and the jobs report land in one week, with markets pricing a likely December rate hike

Midterm elections are Tuesday, Nov 3. The Treasury's quarterly refunding announcement follows Nov 4, and the October jobs report comes Friday, Nov 6. September's report showed 29,000 jobs added. Reuters' week-ahead column put the market-implied odds of a December Fed hike at about 80%. The TSD Macroscope board read Kalshi's December hike contract at $0.69 late Friday, so the two sources disagree on how likely a hike is, though both have it above even.

October ended with the Nasdaq down about 5.3% and the Dow up 1.7%. AMD and Qualcomm report earnings this week.

Why it matters: For anyone with a variable-rate loan or a credit line, a hike would raise borrowing costs again before year-end. The jobs report is the last major labor reading before that decision.
Tech Insider, citing Reuters (Oct 30) · Seeking Alpha week ahead

◆ Household and national

Health coverage Opens Nov 1

ACA open enrollment for 2027 starts today with higher proposed premiums and insurer exits

Marketplace open enrollment begins Nov 1 in most states. HealthCare.gov's window is reported to run through Dec 15, and some state exchanges go later. The median proposed premium increase is about 15%, ranging from under 7% in a few states to 29% in Arizona. Insurers are leaving the Marketplace in 24 states, so hundreds of thousands of people will need to pick a new plan. The out-of-pocket maximum rises to $12,000 from $10,600. HealthCare.gov states may also require an extra identity check, and Oregon moves to its own platform.

Why it matters: Self-employed people and small-business owners who buy their own coverage should compare plans actively instead of letting their plan auto-renew. Subsidies rise with benchmark premiums, but unsubsidized buyers pay the full increase.
healthinsurance.org · AARP
Clocks Nov 1

Daylight saving time ended at 2 a.m.

Clocks went back one hour at 2:00 a.m. local time on Sunday, Nov 1, in every state that observes daylight saving time. It returns in March.

Why it matters: Evenings get darker, so the evening commute is darker too, and appointment systems that don't handle time zones properly can be an hour off this week.
