◆ Inflation & the Fed

Data Released Jul 14 · 8:30 a.m. ET

June CPI is the number the week turns on

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the June Consumer Price Index this morning. Heading in, the consensus was for headline prices to fall about 0.1% for the month — pulling the annual rate down from May's 4.2% toward roughly 3.9% — driven largely by an estimated 10% drop in June gasoline prices. Core CPI, which strips out food and energy, was expected to stay near 2.9% year over year, still above the Fed's 2% target and on a gently rising trend through the spring.

The split matters: a soft headline can mask sticky core inflation. With the next Fed decision on July 29, this print shapes whether markets price the committee holding steady or leaning toward a move.

Why it matters: This is the clearest read on whether the cost of living is genuinely cooling or just getting a temporary break from cheaper gas. For anyone managing their own money, core inflation near 2.9% is the honest signal — headline relief at the pump doesn't mean rents, services and everyday goods have stopped climbing.

Policy Testimony begins Jul 14

Fed Chair Warsh's first testimony to Congress

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is scheduled to deliver his first semiannual monetary-policy testimony before Congress starting Tuesday. Markets will parse it for how the committee reads persistent core inflation against a slowing labor market, with futures pricing meaningful odds of a policy move at or after the July 29 meeting rather than a cut.

Why it matters: A new chair's first testimony sets the tone for how the Fed communicates for months. Mortgage, auto-loan and savings rates all move on the signals sent here — worth watching if you're financing anything major this summer.

◆ Markets & energy

Markets Jul 13–14

Oil climbs and tech consolidates as Hormuz tensions flare

Brent crude traded near $79 a barrel after a weekly gain tied to renewed tensions around the Strait of Hormuz, the shipping chokepoint for a large share of global oil. Earlier in the week, AI and semiconductor stocks — this year's market leaders — fell sharply as the higher oil backdrop and geopolitical risk prompted investors to take profits, with tech trailing the broader market. The stretch of calm that helped push June gasoline lower has given way to fresh volatility in July.

Why it matters: Oil is the fastest-moving input to everyday costs. A sustained climb feeds back into gas prices, shipping and eventually the next inflation reading — the opposite of the relief that softened the June number. For a self-directed investor, it's also a reminder that a rally concentrated in AI names can reverse quickly on an outside shock.

◆ Jobs & earnings

Labor June report

A soft jobs number complicates the rate picture

June nonfarm payrolls rose by only about 57,000 — well below expectations — pointing to a labor market that is stalling even as services firms cautiously resume hiring. The combination of cooling jobs and still-elevated core inflation is exactly the bind the Fed has to weigh: ease too soon and inflation reignites, hold too long and hiring keeps fading.

Why it matters: A weak jobs print usually argues for lower rates, but sticky inflation cuts the other way — which is why this week's data and testimony carry outsized weight. For small businesses, softer hiring can mean a looser labor market and easier recruiting into the fall.
