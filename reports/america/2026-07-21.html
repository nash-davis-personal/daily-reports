◆ The import-duty floor moves this week

Deadline Jul 24, 2026

The 10% across-the-board surcharge terminates Friday, and the replacement is not confirmed

Section 122 of the Trade Act authorizes a temporary import surcharge to address a fundamental international-payments problem, and caps it at 150 days unless Congress affirmatively extends it. That clock runs out July 24, 2026. No extension legislation is pending, so absent congressional action the surcharge ends on its own terms. Trade counsel estimate the average effective US tariff rate could fall from roughly 13% to roughly 7% overnight. Section 232 measures, antidumping and countervailing duties, the merchandise processing fee and product-specific duties are unaffected.

The replacement is the open question. USTR determined in June that 60 economies' handling of forced-labor import prohibitions burdens US commerce, and proposed additional duties of 10% for economies that impose such a prohibition or have committed to, and 12.5% for the rest — 46 economies including China, Vietnam, India, Thailand, Japan and South Korea fall in the 12.5% group. Comments closed July 6 and a hearing was held July 7. USTR's completion deadline on the parallel forced-labor and excess-capacity investigations was July 20. No final Notice of Action for those investigations was verifiable when this was filed. Unlike Section 122, a Section 301 duty carries no statutory sunset.

Why it matters: Any business that imports goods faces a landed-cost change within days, in either direction, and does not yet know which. For a small business, the practical consequence is that a quote issued this week may not hold next week — entry dates, not order dates, determine the rate. For consumers, a 6-point move in the average effective rate is the largest single-week change in import cost this year, though pass-through to shelf prices lags by months.

Effective tomorrow Jul 22, 2026

A 25% Section 301 duty on most Brazilian goods takes effect at 12:01 a.m. ET Wednesday

USTR announced the action July 15 and finalized it in a Notice of Action. It applies to all products of Brazil except those covered by exclusions in the final rule: goods already subject to certain Section 232 measures, civil aircraft and related parts, pharmaceutical products, humanitarian donations, informational materials, personal baggage, and goods under the applicable Chapter 99 HTS provisions. Coverage also reports carve-outs for beef, orange juice and energy products.

The duty follows a year-long investigation into Brazilian practices spanning digital trade and electronic payment services, preferential tariffs, anti-corruption enforcement, intellectual property protection, ethanol market access and illegal deforestation.

Why it matters: Brazil is a top-tier US source for coffee, orange juice, beef, steel and aircraft parts. The exclusions cover several of the most price-visible consumer categories, so the immediate grocery-shelf effect is narrower than the headline rate implies. Importers of covered goods should confirm entry timing today, because entries after 12:01 a.m. Wednesday pay the new rate.

◆ Markets and the consumer

Earnings Jul 21, 2026

GM opened a 73-company reporting day with $48.0B in revenue and a second guidance raise

General Motors reported second-quarter revenue of $48.0 billion, net income attributable to stockholders of $1.3 billion, and EBIT-adjusted of $3.9 billion at about 6:30 a.m. ET. It raised full-year EBIT-adjusted guidance for the second time this year, and now expects full-year net income of $8.4–9.8 billion, automotive operating cash flow of $15.4–19.4 billion, and diluted EPS of $8.98–10.98. Q2 unit sales were down 4.2%; pricing in trucks and SUVs held, and deferred revenue rose more than 50%.

Seventy-three companies report today in what FactSet expects to be a quarter with earnings up about 23% year over year. The Federal Reserve is in its pre-meeting quiet period; CME FedWatch put the odds of a hold at next week's meeting at 88% as of this morning.

Why it matters: A guidance raise from the largest US automaker, with unit sales down and pricing up, is a datapoint on where consumer strength actually sits: volume is softening while higher-price categories hold. For someone managing their own money, the useful read this week is the earnings breadth rather than any single print, since the economic calendar is thin and the Fed is silent until next week.

Energy Jul 21, 2026

The national average gas price printed $4.0030 as crude held near $82

AAA's national average is $4.0030 per gallon today. Crude eased to $82.43, down fractionally from the prior day. Wholesale gasoline is up about 14% over the past month and roughly 60% year over year.

The cited supply driver is disruption to Russian refining capacity, with Russian gasoline production reported around 65% of seasonal consumption levels after drone strikes on major refineries. Broader Middle East supply risk is also being priced.

Why it matters: Fuel is the fastest-moving line in a household budget and one of the slowest to show up in the official inflation print. June inflation came in soft on both core and headline, but a month of gasoline at or above $4.00 works against that in the July and August data — which is the data the Fed sees before its September decision.

◆ Weather, public health, and the states

Active Jul 21–22, 2026

The heat dome is collapsing into severe storms and flash flooding across a wide swath of the country

Dangerous heat is giving way today to scattered-to-numerous severe storms, damaging wind gusts and flash flooding, with brief spin-up tornadoes possible in places. A Flood Watch is in effect along the I-95 corridor for Tuesday. Forecasters note that saturated soil is the aggravating factor: additional rainfall runs straight off into creeks, streams and low-lying roads rather than absorbing.

Conditions improve behind the front. Wednesday runs noticeably cooler with lingering showers, and drier air arrives Thursday.

Why it matters: Flash flooding on already-saturated ground is this summer's recurring pattern and has been the deadliest weather hazard of the past two weeks. The practical window is short — a Flood Watch means conditions are favorable, not that flooding has started, and the gap between the two has been measured in minutes in several recent events.

Update Jul 19–20, 2026

The FDA walked back a lettuce test result as a false positive, and left the recall in place

The FDA re-reviewed the laboratory result that had been reported as confirming Cyclospora in recalled iceberg lettuce and declared it a false positive over July 19–20. The recall stands: traceback records and patient interviews still point to the same lettuce, supplied by Taylor Farms de México and distributed to 27 states. As of July 14 the CDC counted 1,645 confirmed cases and more than 5,100 probable.

Separately, the FDA is investigating a multistate outbreak of E. coli O145:H28 linked to frozen GreenWise-brand organic blueberries from a Chilean supplier — 12 illnesses, 4 hospitalizations and no deaths, in Florida and Georgia.

Why it matters: A withdrawn lab confirmation does not withdraw the epidemiology, and the recall guidance is unchanged for consumers and food-service operators. The distinction matters because a "false positive" headline circulating without that context has, in past outbreaks, driven recalled product back into use.

North Carolina Jul 9, 2026

North Carolina ranked second-best state for business and first in the economy category, while cost of living slid 12 places

CNBC's annual ranking put North Carolina 2nd overall behind Ohio, with Virginia third — the seventh straight year in the top three and the fifth straight in the top two. The state took 1st in Economy (up from 3rd), 3rd in Workforce, and 8th in Technology and Innovation (up from 13th). Companies promised nearly 34,000 new jobs and more than $23 billion in new investment last year; May unemployment was 3.7% against a 4.3% national rate.

The declines are all affordability and quality of life. Cost of Living fell from 23rd to 35th, Quality of Life from 29th to 34th, Education from 6th to 12th, and Business Friendliness from 4th to 8th.

Why it matters: The growth that produces the top economy score is the same growth pushing the cost-of-living score down, and Charlotte is where both show up hardest. For anyone operating locally, the read is a tight labor market with rising input costs — clients have money and less patience for price increases at the same time.
