◆ The economy

Data Aug 26, 2026

Second-quarter GDP was left at 1.5% in the second estimate

The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the second estimate of second-quarter real GDP on August 26 at 8:30am ET. Growth came in at a 1.5% annual rate, identical to the advance estimate published in July and below the 2.1% recorded in the first quarter. Increases in consumer spending, exports and investment drove the quarter, partly offset by a decrease in government spending.

The revisions inside the unchanged headline are the detail worth keeping. Consumer spending was revised up, and that was offset by an upward revision to imports, which enter the GDP calculation as a subtraction. So domestic demand read a little stronger than first reported, and more of it was met from abroad. The release also carried the corporate profits data for the quarter.

Why it matters: A headline that does not move can still change the picture underneath it. Stronger consumer spending met by more imports means household demand was better than the July print implied, while the arithmetic of the trade line kept the top-line number flat. For anyone reading these releases to gauge their own customers, the spending revision is the line that describes the customer.

Earnings Aug 26, 2026

Nvidia reported a $96.2 billion quarter and guided to $108 billion without China

Revenue for the fiscal second quarter was $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier and 18% from the prior quarter. Data center revenue was $89.0 billion, up 117%. GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins were both 75.0%, and earnings per diluted share were $2.46 GAAP and $2.22 non-GAAP. Guidance for the current quarter is $108.0 billion plus or minus 2%, with gross margin of 74.0%.

Two details sit under the headline. The guidance explicitly assumes no data center compute revenue from China, so the number is built without a market the company used to count on. And the chief financial officer signaled strong sales growth continuing into fiscal 2028 on the call, which is what moved the shares after hours and lifted index futures on Thursday morning. Wednesday's regular session had been flat to lower, with chip names dragging the Nasdaq 100 down 0.5% ahead of the release.

Why it matters: A single company guiding to $108 billion in a quarter, with China excluded from the assumption, is now one of the larger inputs into how the market reads the whole economy. For a personal-investor lens, the number to watch is not the beat but the gross margin guide stepping from 75.0% to 74.0%, and how much of the demand is financed by customers the company also invests in. Concentration cuts both ways: the same line item that carries an index up carries it down.

Fed Aug 27–29, 2026

Jackson Hole opens today, on payments

The Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's annual economic policy symposium runs August 27 to 29 in Jackson Hole, Wyoming. This year's theme is "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy." Roughly 120 officials and economists from more than 70 countries are expected.

Chair Kevin Warsh, who took office as the seventeenth Chair of the Federal Reserve on May 22, delivers the keynote on Friday, August 28 at 10:00am ET. It is his first Jackson Hole address as Chair. The Kansas City Fed does not usually publish the full agenda until shortly before the event.

Why it matters: A symposium themed on payments puts the plumbing of how money moves in front of the people who regulate it, at a moment when the policy rate has been held steady for an extended run. Anyone whose business depends on payment rails, card processing or settlement timing has a direct reason to read Friday's remarks rather than the market reaction to them.

◆ Storms and response

Disaster Aug 25–26, 2026

A federal emergency declaration was approved for Hawaii County after Hurricane Lala

Lala passed just off the southern end of Hawaii Island as a Category 1 hurricane on August 15 and 16, after the state declared an emergency on August 13 and the county issued its own proclamation on August 14. The storm caused loss of life and damage to power, transportation, communications and private property. More than 250,000 customers lost power and entire communities were cut off from road access. Statewide damage is estimated at $81.4 million, concentrated on the Big Island with impacts on Oahu and Maui.

The presidential emergency declaration makes federal disaster assistance available for impacts between August 14 and 16 and lets FEMA coordinate relief and supply equipment and resources. Meanwhile the Pacific stayed active: the National Hurricane Center was tracking Tropical Storm Iselle in the Eastern Pacific and Tropical Storm Lala's successor systems in the Central Pacific, with Tropical Storm Moke passing south of the islands and a third disturbance forming.

Why it matters: Federal assistance flowing to Hawaii County sets the response baseline as the Atlantic season approaches its climatological peak in early September. NOAA's outlook remains below normal for the Atlantic — 7 to 13 named storms, 2 to 6 hurricanes, 0 to 2 major — and the season so far has produced two named storms and no hurricanes there.

◆ Science and space

Space Aug 26, 2026

The Roman Space Telescope is sealed in its fairing and cleared for an August 30 launch

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has been enclosed inside its Falcon Heavy payload fairing and cleared for final launch preparations, targeting liftoff on August 30. It carries two instruments: the Wide Field Instrument, a 300-megapixel infrared camera, and a coronagraph for direct imaging of faint objects near bright stars.

The mission is planned for a minimum of five years, imaging the sky at a far faster rate than Hubble. Separately, SpaceX launched 27 Starlink satellites from California in a predawn liftoff on August 26, and astronauts Anil Menon and Sophie Adenot completed a six-and-a-half-hour spacewalk on August 25 to install a replacement Space-to-Ground antenna on the International Space Station.

Why it matters: Roman is the largest NASA astrophysics launch of the year, and a five-year wide-field infrared survey produces a public data set that researchers and software builders outside the agency will use for a decade. The launch window is Sunday.
