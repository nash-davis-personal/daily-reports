◆ The economy

Markets September 23

The 10-year Treasury hit its highest yield since 2007

A stronger-than-expected economic report unsettled markets on September 23. The S&P Global flash composite PMI rose to 58.4 in September, up from 56.0 in August and the highest reading in more than five years; the manufacturing component hit 57.0, a 52-month high, and services came in at 58.7. Fast growth combined with Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr's comment that additional rate hikes are needed to bring down "sticky" inflation, and traders pushed the odds of an October hike to roughly 70%, up from about 55% a day earlier and under 9% a month ago.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.11%, up nearly 3% on the day and its highest close since 2007. All three major stock indexes fell: the Dow lost 352 points (0.68%) to 51,511.59, the S&P 500 dropped 0.75% to 7,706.03, and the Nasdaq fell 1.13% to 26,936.04, retreating from Monday's record close.

Why it matters: Mortgage and business-loan pricing track the 10-year more closely than the Fed's overnight rate, so a move like this reaches a 30-year mortgage or a small-business term loan within days. For anyone holding a portfolio concentrated in the handful of names that carried Monday's rally, Wednesday is a reminder that the same concentration cuts both ways.

Energy September 23

Crude snapped a five-day slide after Iran said it would not surrender

Brent crude rose 3.9% to close at $103.08 a barrel on September 23, and U.S. benchmark WTI gained 1.8% to $92.16, ending five straight sessions of declines. The rebound followed comments from Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian at the United Nations that Iran would not surrender to the United States, reviving uncertainty over whether Hormuz shipping would normalize as quickly as this week's earlier rally had priced in.

Why it matters: Diesel and gasoline still hadn't caught up to Monday's dip in crude, so a Wednesday reversal removes the case for near-term relief at the pump — a live cost line for any Charlotte-area trade, delivery or landscaping business running a truck fleet.

◆ Diplomacy and trade policy

State visit September 23-24

Xi arrived, and the trade truce got two more months

President Trump greeted Xi Jinping and Peng Liyuan at Joint Base Andrews on September 23, opening Xi's first state visit to Washington since 2015. Thursday's schedule includes a South Lawn arrival ceremony with 479 military personnel, a Rose Garden meeting and an evening state dinner; the visit concludes September 25 with tea at the White House and a stop at the National Archives.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two countries extended their existing trade truce, moving the deadline from November 10 to January 10, 2027. The truce builds on an agreement reached at last year's Busan summit, under which the U.S. eased some export controls on advanced technology in exchange for lower Chinese tariffs; unresolved issues include rare earths, technology curbs and Taiwan.

Why it matters: A two-month extension keeps existing tariff and export-control terms in place rather than letting them lapse into a harder deadline this fall. The practical effect for a business sourcing components from China is that today's cost and compliance picture holds through early January, not that it improves.

◆ Charlotte and North Carolina

Local September 22

CMS fired its superintendent, and the DA asked the SBI to investigate

The Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools Board of Education voted 5-4 on September 22 to terminate Superintendent Dr. Crystal Hill without cause. Hill, who was not present for the vote, is owed a year's salary in severance — $340,770 — under her contract. The vote followed months of turmoil: Hill was placed on paid leave over the summer and investigated over administrative and operational oversight, and an outside review released this week found no evidence of improper conduct by Hill herself.

Days before the vote, Hill sent board members a 14-page letter raising concerns about district finances, including payments of about $30,000 a month for a year to a consultant, Raki McGregor, with no contract or purchase order on file. Mecklenburg County District Attorney Spencer Merriweather has asked the State Bureau of Investigation to open a criminal inquiry into the district's financial transactions dating to fiscal year 2026.

Why it matters: CMS is North Carolina's second-largest school district and a major line item in the county budget. An SBI inquiry into district finances alongside a contested leadership change is the kind of instability that affects bond ratings and public trust well beyond this single personnel vote.

◆ Infrastructure and public safety

Cybersecurity September 22-23

A hacking group claims it breached the FBI's own job site

The hacking group ShinyHunters claimed this week that it breached FBIjobs.gov, the bureau's job-application portal, on September 21 and used that access to reach other agency systems, taking what it described as 2 to 3 terabytes of data on nearly all FBI agents and job applicants. The FBI said it is "aware of claims regarding unauthorized activity" and is investigating whether the breach originated with the FBI's own systems or a third-party provider that supports the site; FBIjobs.gov remained offline as of Wednesday morning. The group said the attack was retaliation for a May 2026 FBI advisory that detailed its methods and urged victims not to pay ransoms.

Why it matters: A breach at a federal law-enforcement agency's own recruiting system, even if it turns out to trace back to a third-party vendor, is a reminder that vendor risk reaches organizations with the most sensitive missions — a relevant lesson for any small business handing client or employee data to an outside SaaS tool.

◆ Weather and natural disasters

Weather September 23-24

A hurricane watch is up for Hawaii's Big Island

Tropical Storm Nolo was centered roughly 300 miles south of Hawaii's Big Island on September 23, prompting a hurricane watch. Forecasters expect Nolo to reach hurricane strength by Thursday night and make its closest approach to the Big Island Saturday night as a Category 3 storm. Forecasts call for 10 to 15 inches of rain across the Big Island through the weekend, with up to 35 inches possible in the steepest terrain — a setup forecasters say could bring catastrophic, life-threatening flooding and mudslides to the Big Island and Maui.

Why it matters: This is a forecast track, not a landfall. Rainfall totals in this range on the Big Island's terrain are the combination that has produced the islands' worst flooding events, so the watch is worth tracking through the weekend regardless of the exact path.
