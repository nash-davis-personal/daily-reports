◆ Economy and markets

Markets Friday, Sept 25

Stocks finished a volatile week higher despite the bond sell-off

The Dow rose 478.64 points (0.93%) to 51,828.62, the S&P 500 gained 0.51% to 7,743.41 and the Nasdaq added 0.48% to 27,068.72. All three posted weekly gains, and the Dow ended a three-week losing streak. Tech optimism helped, including Akamai's roughly 15% jump on its cloud deal with Anthropic.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed near 5.18%, up about 2bp, after touching its highest level in nearly two decades earlier in the week. WTI crude fell 2.33% to $92.41 and Brent slipped below $98 as traders weighed an Iranian offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: Stocks absorbed the rate shock this week, but borrowing costs did not come down. For a personal investor, bond yields near 5% make cash and short-term Treasuries a real alternative again; mortgage and business-loan rates stay elevated.

Data Friday, Sept 25

Consumer sentiment fell to 48.1 in September's final reading

The University of Michigan index came in at 48.1, down from 51.7 in August and slightly above the 47.5 economists expected. Expectations for personal finances weakened about 10%.

Why it matters: Sentiment in the 40s signals households bracing for tighter budgets. Small businesses that depend on discretionary spending should expect price-sensitive customers into the holidays.

◆ Weather

Storm Late Friday, Sept 25

Hurricane Nolo stalled south of Hawaii's Big Island

At 11 p.m. Friday, Nolo was a Category 2 hurricane with 105 mph winds, about 145 miles south of South Point and nearly stationary. Forecasters expect it to turn west Saturday and pass well south of the main islands without landfall, strengthening into a major hurricane with winds possibly reaching 140 mph by late Sunday.

Southeast Big Island could see 10 to 15 inches of rain, with isolated totals near 25 inches. A hurricane watch and tropical storm warning cover Hawaii County, and a tropical storm watch covers Maui County.

Why it matters: The main threat is flash flooding and landslides on the Big Island even without landfall. Anyone with travel or shipments to Hawaii this weekend should expect delays.

◆ Next week's calendar

Calendar Sept 28 – Oct 1

PCE inflation, a funded government and a Starship orbital attempt

August PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, is due Wednesday, Sept 30 at 8:30 a.m. ET. July's reading showed 3.7% headline and 3.3% core over 12 months.

Fiscal year 2026 ends Sept 30, but no shutdown is scheduled: the House passed a stopgap 370–48 and it was signed Sept 2, funding the government through Dec 11. SpaceX targets Monday, Sept 28 for Starship's first orbital flight from Texas.

Why it matters: Wednesday's PCE print is the week's main scheduled market mover given how sensitive yields are right now. Federal contractors and grant recipients have funding certainty until Dec 11.
