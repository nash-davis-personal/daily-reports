◆ The economy

Data Nov 25

Q3 growth is revised up to 4.3%, inflation runs above forecast, and jobless claims fall to 189,000

The BEA's second estimate put third-quarter growth at a 4.3% annual rate, up from 3.4%. Most of the upgrade came from business investment, led by software, and from goods spending; services spending was revised down. Real final sales to private domestic buyers rose 5.8%. The third estimate is due Dec 23.

October's PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 3.8% from a year earlier, and core PCE (excluding food and energy) rose 3.3%, above the 3.2% consensus. Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000 for the week ended Nov 21. Reuters described the job market as "low-hire, low-fire."

Why it matters: Faster growth with inflation above forecast leaves the Fed little room to ease. For anyone carrying a variable-rate loan or credit card balance, higher borrowing costs into 2027 are the base case markets are pricing.

Markets Nov 25

Stocks erase most of a steep morning slide before the holiday

Hot inflation data, the new tariff proposal and another drop in chip stocks sent indexes sharply lower at the open. The Dow fell to 48,913.74 in the first 20 minutes, then rallied to close at 50,182.95. The Nasdaq 100 recovered from a 4.28% loss to finish down 0.64%, the S&P 500 closed near 7,183, down about 0.25%, and the small-cap Russell 2000 rose 0.71%. Nvidia fell 3.9% and is down about 27% in November. Markets are closed Thursday, and Friday is a shortened session.

Why it matters: A 2% intraday swing on a holiday-shortened week is a reminder that thin trading magnifies moves. For a personal portfolio, the day's close says less than the trend in rates and credit below.
BBN Times Broad Spectrum Finance
Trade Nov 25

The US proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners

After an eight-month investigation into excess factory capacity, the US Trade Representative proposed tariffs on China, Bangladesh, Cambodia, the EU, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam. China's proposed rate is 20%, on top of about 25% already in place. Reported rates for others vary by outlet, from about 10% to as much as 35% for Vietnam. Mexico and Norway were investigated and left out for now. The proposals go through public comment and a hearing, and CNBC reports they likely won't take effect for months.

Why it matters: Small retailers that import goods, from ceramics to electronics, have a window to plan before any rate applies. The comment period is the point where affected businesses can file.

◆ Weather and travel

Weather Nov 25–26

Storms on both coasts slow near-record Thanksgiving travel, and the Macy's parade moves to Saturday

A coastal storm is bringing heavy rain, damaging wind gusts and flooding to the East Coast; New York and New Jersey declared states of emergency. Snow is falling in the Midwest, and king tides are flooding parts of the West Coast. More than 6,000 flights were delayed Wednesday and hundreds were canceled by Thursday morning. TSA expected about 17.6 million passengers from Nov 24 to 30. Macy's moved its 100th parade to Saturday, Nov 28, at 8:30 a.m. ET, because balloons must be grounded in high winds; it is the first weather postponement in the parade's history.

Why it matters: Anyone flying home this weekend should expect weather delays early on; forecasts call for the East Coast storm to mostly clear by Saturday.

◆ North Carolina

Local Nov 26

Charlotte businesses discount THC products ahead of a Dec 11 federal change to the hemp definition

WBTV reports that a federal redefinition of hemp taking effect Dec 11 could remove popular THC drinks and products from shelves. Charlotte sellers are marking down inventory. NoDa Brewing says its THC seltzer is about 10% of its revenue.

Why it matters: Local breweries, bottle shops and convenience stores carrying hemp products face a revenue hole and a menu change in about two weeks.
