◆ Markets & the economy

Markets June 26

A losing week, but the money rotated rather than ran

On June 26 the Nasdaq Composite fell, the S&P 500 finished roughly flat, and the Dow and small-cap Russell 2000 gained. Weakness in the big tech names led the major indexes to weekly losses on worries about AI demand and rising prices — yet advancing shares outnumbered decliners, a sign investors were rotating into sectors beyond tech rather than selling broadly.

Why it matters: For someone managing their own money, the tell is breadth: a market where most stocks rise while the index falls is narrowing its dependence on a handful of megacaps. It's a rotation, not a rout — context, not a trade signal.

Fed June 16–17

Rates on hold a fourth time as inflation runs hot

The FOMC left rates unchanged at its June meeting — its fourth consecutive hold. April PCE came in at +3.8% on the headline and +3.3% on the core, both well above the 2% target, and the committee revised its Q4 2026 core inflation projection up from 2.7% to 3.3%, signaling it sees price pressure lingering. A data-heavy stretch is ahead: a fresh PCE reading and the June jobs report land next week.

Why it matters: "Higher for longer" stays the base case — borrowing costs for a small business, a mortgage, or a car loan aren't easing soon. Consumer sentiment near historic lows (final UMich around 48.9) says households feel the squeeze, which shows up as cautious spending at local businesses.

Corporate June 23–25

FedEx caps a strong fiscal year and returns cash

FedEx reported strong fourth-quarter and full-year results on June 23 with year-over-year revenue and earnings growth, and issued a CY2026 outlook. It followed up June 25 with cash tender offers capped at $4.15 billion, funded largely by an approximately $4.1 billion dividend from the FedEx Freight business it spun off into a separately traded company (FDXF) on June 1.

Why it matters: FedEx is a read on the broader shipping economy. A solid quarter plus a large capital return suggests freight demand and margins held up better than the cautious consumer mood would imply.

◆ Weather & public safety

Wildfire June 26–28

The interior West faces extreme fire weather and a record warning

Heat, wind, and drought drove dangerous wildfire conditions across the Southwest. Utah's Cottonwood Fire, east of Beaver, has burned an area roughly the size of Salt Lake City in days and remains 0% contained — officials believe it may become the state's most destructive and costly fire on record. The National Weather Service in Salt Lake City issued the first "particularly dangerous situation" red-flag warning in its history. Smoke from Great Basin fires has drifted east across the Rockies toward Front Range cities.

Why it matters: Active evacuations, degraded air quality across several states, and another year of record-setting fire losses that feed into Western property-insurance costs. The "first in NWS office history" framing signals genuinely exceptional conditions.

Heat Next week

A widespread heat dome is forecast to build over the eastern U.S.

Forecasters expect a heat dome to bring dangerously hot, humid conditions to the eastern half of the country next week — the most widespread heat wave of the summer so far — while extreme fire conditions persist out West through the weekend.

Why it matters: For the Carolinas and most of the East, that means heat-advisory-level conditions ahead: strain on the power grid, real health risk for outdoor workers, and a spike in demand for HVAC and cooling-related services.
