◆ Energy and the economy

Record Sept 7

The most expensive Labor Day at the pump on record, and diesel broke its all-time high

AAA put the national average for regular gasoline at $4.15 a gallon on Labor Day. The national average had never been above $4 on the holiday before; the previous record was $3.82, set September 3, 2012. Diesel is the sharper number: $5.90 nationally, an all-time high for any date, not just for the holiday.

The state spread is wide. California averaged $5.86, up from $4.616 a year earlier. Washington ($5.515), Hawaii ($5.389), Alaska ($5.028) and Oregon ($5.019) also sat above $5. Diesel is the one that propagates: it moves freight, delivery fleets and agricultural equipment, so it shows up in the price of goods with a lag rather than at the pump immediately.

Why it matters: Diesel at a record feeds directly into shipping and delivery costs for every business that moves physical goods, which is a margin problem for retailers and contractors before it becomes a consumer price. For a household, the gap between the national average and a $5-plus state is now over $1.70 a gallon, so the same commute costs materially different amounts depending on where you live.

Breaking Sept 6

Iran says it will declare an exclusion zone outside the Strait of Hormuz

Mohsen Rezaei, the new head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, told Iranian state broadcasting on Sunday that Tehran plans to announce an exclusion zone in the coming days and weeks. In his description it "will begin from the line of the U.S. naval blockade, extend toward the Strait of Hormuz, and from this side continue into the Persian Gulf," and "any ship that enters this area with the intention of passing through the Strait of Hormuz and is identified will be placed on our sanctions list." Few other details were given.

The sequence matters for reading it. Rezaei spoke a day after US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers, which the US described as a response to Iranian ballistic missiles fired at US warships. Throughput through the strait has been running far below normal for months, and that constraint is the mechanism connecting this to the pump prices above.

Why it matters: Hormuz is the chokepoint for a large share of seaborne crude, so any further narrowing of safe transit tends to show up in US fuel prices within weeks. Fuel is also a live input to the inflation print due Friday, which means this is not only a foreign-policy story — it feeds the number the Fed reads before its September 16 decision.

Calendar Sept 7

Markets took the day off before the week that decides the Fed call

The NYSE, Nasdaq, the bond market and commercial bank branches were all closed Monday for Labor Day, with equities resuming at 9:30 a.m. Eastern Tuesday. USPS, UPS and FedEx suspended standard pickup and delivery.

The three-day weekend sits between Friday's August payrolls surprise — 162,000 jobs against a consensus near 53,000, unemployment steady at 4.1% — and the two events that resolve it. August CPI publishes September 11. The FOMC meets September 15 and 16, with the decision and statement on the 16th. CME FedWatch has been showing roughly 58% odds of a 25 basis point hike; the Kalshi strip has run several points lower.

Why it matters: A holiday close does not pause the setup, it compresses it. Two prints land inside five days, and the second one is a policy decision markets currently rate close to a coin flip. Anyone with a rate-sensitive decision pending — a mortgage lock, a variable-rate business line, a financing close — is deciding inside that window.

◆ Weather and public safety

Breaking Sept 7

Kauai County is under a hurricane warning as Lowell passes to the west

The National Hurricane Center upgraded Kauai and Niihau from a tropical storm warning to a hurricane warning on Monday as Lowell, a Category 3 with 115 mph sustained winds, closed on the islands. The forecast track carries the center just west of the main Hawaiian Islands Monday night, slowing by early Wednesday after it passes.

Expected impacts: 6 to 10 inches of rain on Kauai, 4 to 8 on the Big Island and 2 to 4 elsewhere; storm surge of 1 to 3 feet in Kauai County and up to 1 foot on Oahu; and surf over 20 feet on south- and west-facing shores of Kauai and Oahu, with flash flooding and landslides possible. Polihale State Park and the Na Pali Coast State Wilderness Park, including the full Kalalau Trail, are closed.

Why it matters: A hurricane warning means hurricane conditions are expected, not merely possible, and Hawaii's exposure is concentrated: Kauai's road network has limited redundancy and landslides can isolate communities for days. The heaviest impacts run Monday night into early Tuesday.

Update Sept 6–7

NTSB chair is leading the Miami runway-overrun investigation

21 Air Flight 7598, an Amazon-branded Boeing 767-300 arriving from Luis Munoz Marin International in San Juan, overran Runway 30 at Miami International around 2 p.m. Sunday, struck multiple vehicles and came to rest in a field beside the airport. Five people were killed and five injured, three of them critically. The aircraft was moving at nearly 130 mph when it left the usable portion of the runway.

The NTSB has dispatched a go-team led by chair Jennifer Homendy. The cause of the overrun has not been determined.

Why it matters: Miami International is one of the largest air-cargo gateways in the country, and a fatal accident involving ground vehicles outside the airport perimeter puts both runway-safety-area design and cargo-carrier oversight into the investigation's scope. NTSB preliminary reports typically arrive within about 30 days.

◆ Infrastructure

Infrastructure Sept 4–5

Part of the Kennedy Center's Grand Foyer ceiling came down during storms

A section of ceiling in the Kennedy Center's Grand Foyer collapsed Friday evening as heavy rain moved through the Washington area. No one was hurt. Photos released by the center show a large hole in the high ceiling and debris across the carpet in the hall that connects the Opera House, Concert Hall and Eisenhower Theater. The Grand Foyer is restricted to visitors and non-essential personnel through the week.

The center's own statement attributed the failure to conditions rather than the storm alone: "Decades of deferred maintenance made this inevitable." A legal dispute over the board's plan to close the building for renovations is ongoing.

Why it matters: This is a deferred-maintenance failure at a federally chartered national landmark, which is the same category of problem showing up across aging public buildings. The immediate effect is a closed circulation space in a venue that routes audiences for three theaters through it.
