◆ The economy & the Fed

Rates Jul 8, 2026

June Fed minutes confirm a hawkish turn under new Chair Warsh

The Fed released the minutes of its June 16–17 meeting on July 8. At that meeting the committee held the federal funds target at 3.50%–3.75% unanimously, but the tone ran more hawkish than markets expected: the policy statement was much shorter, stripped of the forward guidance markets had leaned on for years, in favor of pure data dependence. On the "dot plot," participants split — roughly nine saw at least one more hike this year, against those expecting no change or a cut. Markets currently price about a 76% chance rates hold at the next meeting.

Why it matters: Borrowing costs are more likely to rise than fall in the near term, and the Fed has removed the "hints" investors and small-business borrowers used to plan around. For anyone managing their own money or weighing a loan, the message is less certainty and more sensitivity to each new inflation and jobs print.

Markets Jul 2026

A narrow market, cooling hiring, and prices that won't fully settle

The bull market's leadership remains concentrated in AI and energy names, and the AI-capex story keeps drawing capital — Amazon just launched a bond sale of at least $25B to fund infrastructure (see today's Technology briefing). Underneath, the labor market is tight but slow: recent job gains landed around 57,000 with unemployment at 4.2%, while headline CPI rose 4.2% over the year to May, the largest 12-month increase since April 2023.

Why it matters: A market carried by a handful of sectors is more fragile than a broad one, and 4.2% inflation with cooling hiring is exactly the mix that keeps the Fed cautious. For households, it means real wages and financing costs both deserve a close look before big purchases.

◆ Public safety & science

Public safety Jul 7, 2026

A buckling Midtown high-rise forces mass evacuations near Grand Central

A 37-story Midtown Manhattan tower on East 42nd Street — the former Pfizer headquarters being converted into apartments — was evacuated the morning of July 7 after two structural support columns on the 21st floor began to buckle, with cracks and sagging floors found nearby. Officials cleared seven surrounding buildings and a school of about 400 students as a precaution. Contractors began installing temporary shoring by mid-afternoon; no injuries were reported, and the building had stopped moving by evening.

Why it matters: A near-collapse of a large occupied building in one of the densest parts of the country is a rare, serious public-safety event, and it will likely prompt scrutiny of structural work on the wave of office-to-residential conversions underway in major US cities.
NBC News — Midtown high-rise evacuations
Science Jul 3, 2026

NASA and a startup pull off a first-of-its-kind satellite rescue

On July 3, NASA and Katalyst Space Technologies launched the "Swift rescue" mission to save NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory by boosting the aging spacecraft to a higher orbit, using a LINK servicing spacecraft carried up on a Northrop Grumman Pegasus XL air-launched rocket. It's an early demonstration of commercial on-orbit servicing extending the life of a working science satellite rather than letting it decay.

Why it matters: On-orbit servicing turns satellites into repairable, upgradeable assets instead of one-shot hardware — a shift with real implications for the cost and longevity of the space infrastructure the US economy increasingly relies on.
