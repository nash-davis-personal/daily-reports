◆ Markets

Markets Aug 18–19, 2026

A semiconductor selloff pulled the indexes down and kept going overnight

The S&P 500 closed Tuesday down 0.69% at 7,692. The Nasdaq took the harder hit at 1.33%, closing at 26,290, and the Dow held its loss to 0.22% at 53,343. The driver was a broad reassessment of AI-related valuations concentrated in chip names, with memory and storage among the worst hit.

It did not stop at the U.S. close. Overnight the MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan index fell more than 3%, Japan's Nikkei dropped 4%, and Taiwan's market fell more than 6%, its steepest decline in months. Europe's STOXX 600 opened down about 0.5%. In fixed income the 10-year Treasury yield eased to roughly 4.71% while the 30-year sat near 5.28%, after touching a 19-year high above 5.33% on Tuesday. Brent traded around $88.88 and WTI around $83.11, both close to flat.

Why it matters: The long end of the curve near a 19-year high is the number that reaches households and small businesses, because 30-year yields set mortgage pricing and long-dated business borrowing. A market that is selling AI infrastructure while long rates stay elevated is repricing both the growth story and the cost of financing it at the same time.

Fed Aug 19, 2026

The July FOMC minutes land at 2 p.m. ET, with three dissents to explain

The Federal Reserve publishes the minutes of its July meeting Wednesday afternoon. The target range for the federal funds rate is 3.50% to 3.75%. Three officials dissented at that meeting in favor of raising rates, an unusually wide split, and the minutes are the first detailed look at how that disagreement was argued.

Why it matters: Three dissents pointing toward a hike is a signal that part of the committee reads inflation as unfinished. Anyone with a variable-rate business line, a construction loan, or a near-term refinance should read the minutes rather than the headline.

◆ The American consumer, measured

Earnings Aug 18, 2026

Home Depot beat on both lines and reaffirmed guidance anyway

Second-quarter sales were $47.9 billion, up 5.7% year over year. Comparable sales rose 1.7%, with U.S. comps up 1.3%. Net earnings were $4.8 billion, or $4.79 per diluted share, against $4.6 billion and $4.58 a year earlier; excluding one-time items, $4.92 per share versus the $4.73 analysts expected. The company reaffirmed full-year guidance of 2.5% to 4.5% sales growth and comparable sales flat to up 2%.

The commentary was the substance. Home Depot described broad engagement across categories in both its professional and do-it-yourself businesses and called its customer a healthy cohort, while saying shoppers have not returned to large projects. The framing management used was that customers have the means to spend and are hesitant, and that uncertainty is why guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest available read on discretionary big-ticket spending, and it says capacity is intact while willingness is not. Target, Lowe's, TJX and Analog Devices all report Wednesday morning, which will show whether the same hesitancy holds across general merchandise and off-price retail.

Labor Aug 19, 2026

Tracked tech layoffs in 2026 passed the full-year 2025 total before Labor Day

Layoffs.fyi counts 126,305 tech job cuts so far in 2026 as of mid-August. The comparable figure for all of 2025 was 122,606 across 278 companies. Reporting attributes much of the acceleration to corporate spending on AI tooling and to consolidation at large employers, with Oracle, Amazon, Dell and Meta accounting for a large share of the total.

Why it matters: The count crossed last year's full-year mark with more than four months left on the calendar, which means the trend line rather than the total is the story. For anyone hiring, the supply of experienced technical people looking for work is the deepest it has been in this cycle.

Policy Aug 18, 2026

Pennsylvania made its data-center standards binding and gave localities a veto

Governor Josh Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05 on Tuesday, converting the state's Governor's Responsible Infrastructure Development standards from a voluntary certification into legally binding requirements. The Department of Environmental Protection may now review a data-center permit application only if the developer has signed a Consent Order and Agreement committing to the GRID requirements and has already obtained all required local approvals.

The standards cover energy affordability, environmental protection, workforce and economic development, transparency and community engagement, and require developers to source rising shares of power from clean sources including solar, advanced nuclear and battery storage. The order also removes AI data-center proposals from the state's fast-track permitting process and prohibits nondisclosure agreements on these projects.

Why it matters: Pennsylvania sits on the PJM grid that serves much of the mid-Atlantic, so siting rules there affect regional electricity supply and pricing well beyond the state line. The NDA prohibition is the provision other states are most likely to copy, because it makes the terms of these deals public before local officials vote on them.

Carolinas Aug 18, 2026

Charlotte tied a 1911 record at 97 degrees, with relief arriving at week's end

Charlotte hit 97 degrees on Tuesday, matching the record for the date set in 1911, with feels-like readings near 105. The National Weather Service heat advisory covered Mecklenburg, Cabarrus, Gaston, Union, Anson and Stanly counties. Highs stay in the mid-90s midweek before storm chances arrive late in the week. The region is now running roughly 20 more days per year with a heat index above 90 than its historical average.

Why it matters: A sustained stretch at these levels drives peak electricity demand and cooling-system failures at the same time, which is when residential HVAC and electrical service capacity gets tested across the metro.
