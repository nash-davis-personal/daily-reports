◆ The economy

Markets Sep 2, 2026

The 10-year Treasury yield reached its highest level since November 2023

The benchmark 10-year yield rose roughly 8 basis points to 4.804%, with an intraday high of 4.818%. The 30-year rose 13 basis points to 5.28%. The move is part of a global selloff in government debt that has kept long US yields at levels not sustained since 2006. The S&P 500, Nasdaq and Dow snapped a three-day losing streak once the run-up paused.

On rate expectations, markets are pricing roughly 60 basis points of hikes through year-end. New York Fed President John Williams told CNBC there were "no clear signs right now" that a September hike would be needed to bring down inflation.

Why it matters: The 10-year sets the price of borrowing across the economy. At 4.8% the cost of a mortgage, a car loan, a small-business term loan and a corporate refinancing all move up together, independent of what the Fed does at its next meeting.

Housing Sep 2, 2026

The 30-year mortgage average climbed to about 6.70% and is being quoted near 7%

The average 30-year fixed rate rose four basis points to roughly 6.70% APR on September 2, with published surveys spanning 6.66% to 6.91% depending on methodology. Reporting attributes the increase to the Treasury move rather than lenders widening margins, describing pricing as passing through nearly one for one. Analysts expect rates to approach 7%.

Low inventory is already limiting sales and holding prices up, and higher yields raise construction costs for new rental supply, so the affordability squeeze runs on both sides of the transaction.

Why it matters: Housing-adjacent small businesses feel this before anyone else. Contractors, realtors, movers, home services and anyone whose customer takes out a mortgage will see volume soften first if 7% holds.

Labor Sep 2, 2026

ADP put August private hiring at 38,000, the smallest gain since January

Private employers added 38,000 jobs in August according to ADP, below the roughly 47,000 consensus. The composition is narrower than the headline. Education and health services added 45,000. Manufacturing shed 17,000, professional and business services shed 16,000, and both natural resources and mining and trade, transportation and utilities shed 5,000 each.

Three more data points land September 3: weekly jobless claims, where the prior week was 203,000 and consensus is around 205,000; the July trade balance; and the August ISM Services PMI at 10:00 a.m. ET, with consensus near 54.2 against 54.1 in July.

Why it matters: A labor market adding jobs in only one sector is a different picture than one adding 38,000 broadly. It also complicates the rate argument, since soft hiring usually argues for cuts while the bond market is currently pricing hikes.

Fed Sep 2, 2026

The Beige Book: modest growth, with data centers named as a driver

The Federal Reserve's survey of conditions across its twelve districts, published two weeks ahead of the next FOMC meeting, described US economic activity as increasing modestly since July. Demand from data centers was singled out as driving growth. The overall outlook was described as positive, with sentiment mixed across sectors amid uncertainty about energy prices and geopolitics.

Why it matters: When the Fed's own field survey names one industry as the growth driver, it tells you how concentrated the expansion is. It also connects directly to the interconnection-queue freezes now happening in Texas and Pennsylvania.

◆ Energy

Oil Sep 2, 2026

Crude topped $90 a barrel on Strait of Hormuz shipping risk

West Texas Intermediate traded above $90 after gaining more than 5% in the prior session, with traders weighing supply risk against evidence that barrels are still moving. The US Energy Secretary said 17 million barrels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, the highest daily volume since hostilities began. Tradingeconomics recorded crude at $90.72 on September 2.

The cause is a military conflict in the region: the United States conducted further airstrikes on Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in Iran following attempted attacks on commercial shipping in the strait and on US forces in the region. The domestic effect is the price of energy and its pass-through into inflation expectations and long-term yields.

Why it matters: Energy is the input every small business pays for twice, in fuel and in freight. A sustained move above $90 shows up in delivery surcharges and materials pricing within weeks, and it is one of the reasons the bond market is pricing hikes while hiring is slowing.
