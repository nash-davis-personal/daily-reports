◆ Markets & the economy

Earnings Jul 14–15

Big banks open earnings season above estimates

The largest US banks kicked off Q2 reporting with beats across the board. JPMorgan Chase earned $6.14 per share on $58.02B revenue (versus roughly $5.85 and $50.19B expected). Goldman Sachs reported $20.98 per share on $20.34B revenue, well above the ~$14.48 and $16.13B consensus. Bank of America came in at $1.21 per share on $31.7B revenue, both ahead of estimates. The strength was broad, spanning trading, dealmaking, and lending.

Why it matters: Bank results are an early read on the whole economy — strong trading and lending revenue signals healthy market activity and steady credit demand. For anyone managing their own money, it's a data point that the "soft landing" narrative is still intact heading into the rest of earnings season.

Inflation June data

CPI cooled more than expected; PPI due today

June's Consumer Price Index fell 0.4% on a seasonally adjusted basis after a 0.5% rise in May, pulling the annual rate to 3.5%. Core CPI (excluding food and energy) held steady at a 2.6% annual pace, below forecasts near 2.9%. Producer Price Index data — a read on wholesale inflation that often previews consumer prices — is scheduled for release today, July 15, and will be watched as another input to the Fed's outlook.

Why it matters: Cooler inflation eases the case for further rate hikes and keeps borrowing costs — mortgages, business loans, credit lines — from climbing. A soft PPI reading today would reinforce that; a hot one would reopen the debate. Either way, it lands two weeks before the Fed decides.

The Fed Late-July meeting

Rate hold looks near-certain as Warsh leans on data

The Federal Reserve is holding its target range at 3.50–3.75%. Since taking over as chair, Kevin Warsh has dropped traditional forward guidance in favor of pure data-dependence, so each release now carries more weight for market expectations. With inflation cooling and the June jobs report showing +57,000 payrolls and 4.2% unemployment, markets treat a hold at the end-of-July meeting as all but assured.

Why it matters: Steady rates mean steady financing conditions for households and small businesses — no near-term relief on rates, but no new squeeze either. The shift to data-dependence also means market swings around each CPI, PPI, and jobs print are likely to get sharper.

◆ Weather & the country

Weather Jul 14–15

A second heat dome puts 125M+ under alerts; Texas faces flood risk

The summer's second major heat dome, after record-challenging highs from the Great Basin to the Northern Plains, is shifting toward the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic. More than 125 million Americans are under some form of heat alert. The earlier late-June episode was linked to 44 deaths and pushed grid operators, including PJM Interconnection, toward record demand. Separately, southwest Texas — from the Hill Country to Big Bend — faces flash-flood risk, with some areas forecast to see more than 10 inches of rain.

Why it matters: Extreme heat strains the power grid (raising the risk of localized outages), pushes up cooling costs, and carries real public-health danger for vulnerable people. The Texas flood threat is the more acute short-term hazard. For the Charlotte area, the heat's eastward spread is worth watching for advisories and elevated electricity demand.
